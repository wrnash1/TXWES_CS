## Course: CIS-3321 Network Administration

## Overview

Strong posts demonstrate systematic thinking, reference specific methodology steps and diagnostic tools, and engage honestly with the challenges of real-world troubleshooting.

## Prompt A — Required for All Students

### Troubleshooting Case Analysis

A mid-sized accounting firm experiences the following situation on a Monday morning:

- 35 of 80 employees report they cannot log into the network file server
- The other 45 employees are working normally
- The affected employees are all located on the second floor
- Affected employees can browse the internet
- Affected employees can reach the file server by IP but cannot map the network drive by server name
- The problem started when employees arrived Monday morning — it worked fine Friday afternoon
- IT logs show a switch firmware update was pushed automatically to all switches over the weekend

Using the CompTIA seven-step methodology, work through this scenario. Your post must:

- State the precise problem (Step 1) — be specific, not generic
- List at least three theories of probable cause ranked by likelihood (Step 2) and identify which OSI layer each implicates
- Describe the specific commands or checks you would run to test your top two theories (Step 3)
- State your most likely root cause given the clues in the scenario and explain your reasoning

Your initial post should be 300–400 words and reference specific module concepts including diagnostic commands and OSI layers.

## Prompt B — Choose One

### Option 1: Personal Troubleshooting Experience

Describe a network or technology problem you have personally experienced — at home, at work, at school, or in a previous course. Apply the seven-step troubleshooting model retroactively to that experience.

For each of the seven steps: (a) describe what you did (or should have done) at that step, and (b) identify whether you actually followed the step or skipped it. Be honest — most real-world troubleshooting does not follow the ideal model perfectly. Conclude with: what would you do differently now that you know the formal methodology, and which step do you think is most commonly skipped and why?

### Option 2: Build vs. Buy — Cable Tester Decision

Your organization is wiring a new 200-node office building. The project manager asks whether to purchase a basic cable tester ($150) or a full cable certifier ($8,500). You need to make a recommendation with justification.

Write a technical recommendation memo that: (a) explains what each tool can and cannot test, (b) identifies the specific scenarios in which each is necessary, (c) explains the cost-benefit analysis — when is the $8,350 price difference justified?, and (d) makes a final recommendation for this specific project (new installation of structured cabling) with your reasoning.

### Option 3: Escalation Judgment

You are a Tier 1 help desk technician. A call center supervisor calls and reports that half the call center agents cannot make outbound calls on the VoIP system. The other half are working normally. The affected agents are all on a specific network switch. You have checked the switch and all ports show green link lights. You do not have access to the VoIP system administration console.

Analyze this scenario: (a) What steps can you perform at Tier 1 before escalating? (b) At what point should you escalate, and to which team (network, VoIP/telephony, vendor)? (c) What information would you provide when escalating? (d) What is the risk of escalating too early vs. too late, and how do you balance this in a call center environment where downtime directly costs revenue?
