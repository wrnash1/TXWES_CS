### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **ESD protection**: Electrostatic Discharge (ESD) protection refers to the tools and practices used to prevent static electricity from damaging sensitive electronic components. Common ESD protection tools include anti-static wrist straps, anti-static mats, and anti-static bags. Even a small static discharge that a human cannot feel (as low as 10 volts) can permanently destroy a microchip or circuit board.
*   **grounding**: Grounding is the practice of electrically connecting a person, component, or chassis to the earth (or a large neutral conductor) to safely dissipate built-up static charge. When working inside a PC, a technician grounds themselves by wearing an ESD wrist strap clipped to the metal chassis, preventing static buildup from transferring to components during handling.
*   **safety guidelines**: Safety guidelines for PC hardware work are a set of documented procedures designed to protect both the technician and the equipment. They include steps such as powering down and unplugging the PC before opening the case, never touching capacitors on a PSU (which retain charge even when unplugged), and using proper lifting techniques when handling heavy equipment.
*   **core internal components overview**: The major internal components of a desktop PC include the CPU (processor), RAM (system memory), storage drives (HDD/SSD), the motherboard (main circuit board), the power supply unit (PSU), and the GPU (graphics card). Each component is installed in a specific location on or connected to the motherboard, and understanding their physical locations is a foundational A+ skill.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Focus Area (A+ Core 1 — Domain 3.5):** The A+ exam tests your ability to identify PC components by sight. Expect scenario-based questions where you must select the correct component to install or replace given a specific symptom. Memorize the physical appearance and connectors of common components.
*   **Scenario Trap:** A common A+ distractor is confusing ESD risk with electrical shock risk. ESD damages the **PC components**, not the technician. The exam will offer "protects the user from electrocution" as a wrong answer when asking about ESD wrist straps — that answer is always incorrect.
*   **Study Resource:** Professor Messer's free CompTIA A+ video series covers safety procedures and component identification in detail. Use it to reinforce visual recognition: [Professor Messer's CompTIA A+ Core 1 Course — Safety and Professionalism](https://www.professormesser.com/free-a-plus-training/220-1101/220-1101-video/). These videos align directly with the 220-1101 and 220-1102 exam objectives.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will perform the following steps to apply these concepts:
*   **Identify safety risks in an opened PC case**: Locate capacitors on the PSU, exposed solder points, and sharp metal edges. Document which components pose ESD risk vs. electrical hazard.
*   **Equip an ESD strap and locate grounding points**: Attach an anti-static wrist strap to your wrist and clip it to the unpainted metal interior of a PC chassis. Verify the strap's resistor is intact (should measure ~1 MΩ).
*   **Locate internal components: PSU, Motherboard, RAM, CPU**: Open a desktop case and physically identify the power supply, motherboard, CPU socket, RAM slots, and storage connectors. Sketch a component map and label each part.
