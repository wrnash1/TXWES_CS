### Scenario A — The Multi-Monitor Workstation

An architecture firm is upgrading ten workstations to dual-monitor setups. Each workstation has a GPU with two DisplayPort 1.4 outputs, one HDMI 2.0 output, and one DVI-D output. Each employee will use a primary 27-inch 4K IPS monitor (DisplayPort input, HDMI 2.0 input) and a secondary 24-inch 1440p IPS monitor (DisplayPort input only). One employee in the print department has asked to also connect an old 22-inch VGA-only monitor as a third screen for viewing reference documents.

Respond to all three of the following:

1. Describe the optimal cable connections for the primary and secondary monitors on a standard employee workstation. For each monitor, specify which GPU port to use, which monitor input to use, and which cable version is required to achieve the monitor's native resolution at 60Hz. Justify each choice.
2. For the print department employee who wants to add the VGA-only third monitor using the DVI-D port, explain whether a passive DVI-D to VGA adapter will work. If it will not, describe what is required and why.
3. An IT administrator asks whether the firm could save money by purchasing cheaper HDMI 1.4 cables for the 4K primary monitors instead of HDMI 2.0 cables. Explain exactly what would happen at the display level and why this would not be acceptable for a professional architecture environment.

### Scenario B — The Gamer's Display Dilemma

A customer purchases a new gaming monitor rated at 1440p, 165Hz, with both HDMI 2.0 and DisplayPort 1.4 inputs. Their GPU has one HDMI 2.0 output and one DisplayPort 1.4 output. They connect the monitor using an HDMI 2.0 cable. After setup, Windows shows the maximum refresh rate as 144Hz, not 165Hz. The customer asks why they are not getting 165Hz and whether they need to buy a new GPU.

Respond to all three of the following:

1. Explain why the customer is seeing 144Hz maximum over HDMI 2.0 when the monitor is rated for 165Hz. Identify the limiting factor and the correct cable change needed to achieve 165Hz.
2. The customer also asks whether they should use the HDMI 2.0 or DisplayPort 1.4 connection for gaming going forward. Compare the two options for this specific use case (1440p, high refresh rate gaming) and recommend one. Include the bandwidth figures from the module in your reasoning.
3. The customer then asks about upgrading to a 240Hz monitor in the future. Explain what they would need — beyond just the monitor — to actually benefit from 240Hz. Address the GPU, the cable, and the game performance requirements in your answer.

### Scenario C — The Office Display Troubleshoot

A corporate IT technician is called to a user's desk. The user has a new 24-inch monitor connected to their laptop via an HDMI cable. The monitor displays an image, but the user reports two problems: first, the image appears blurry compared to their old monitor even at the same 1920x1080 resolution; second, they cannot get extended display mode — the laptop always mirrors to the monitor instead of extending.

Respond to all three of the following:

1. Diagnose the blurry image problem. The user confirms the monitor resolution in Windows is set to 1920x1080 and the monitor's native resolution is also 1920x1080. Explain two possible causes of a blurry or soft image at native resolution that are not related to cable version or resolution mismatch.
2. Diagnose the mirroring-only problem. Explain whether this is a hardware limitation of HDMI, a cable limitation, or a software configuration issue. Describe the specific steps the user should take in Windows to switch to extended display mode.
3. The IT technician notices the user has an IPS monitor but previously used a TN monitor. The user says the new IPS monitor looks "washed out" compared to the old one. Explain the perceptual difference between IPS and TN that might cause this complaint, and describe what display setting the technician should check and adjust to resolve it.

### Professor Nash's Note

Display troubleshooting is one of those areas where technicians who know their connector versions and panel types save clients a lot of money — because the answer is almost never "buy a new GPU" or "buy a new monitor." Usually it is a cable version, a display mode setting, or a brightness and contrast calibration. The scenarios in this discussion are all drawn from the kinds of calls IT departments actually receive. Think carefully about what the actual limiting factor is before you recommend hardware replacements. I look forward to reading your analysis.
