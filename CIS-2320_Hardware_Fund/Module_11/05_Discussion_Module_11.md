## Scenario A — The Wiring Closet Upgrade

A mid-size accounting firm is upgrading its office network. The building was wired with Cat5e in 2009 and all horizontal runs are between 60 and 95 meters. The IT director wants to deploy 10 Gbps switching to every workstation without running new cable. A vendor proposes that Cat5e can be "boosted" to 10 Gbps using a signal repeater at the midpoint of each run.

1. Evaluate the vendor's claim. Is it technically possible to achieve 10 Gbps on existing Cat5e runs using signal repeaters or any other method? Explain why or why not using the physical characteristics of Cat5e.
2. What cable category would you recommend for a full re-cable, and what specific factor about the run lengths in this building influences your recommendation?
3. During the re-cable, the installer discovers some horizontal runs pass through the air-return plenum above the drop ceiling. What cable jacket specification is required for those runs, and what happens if standard PVC cable is used instead?

## Scenario B — The Patch Cable Mystery

A help desk technician receives a ticket: "User in cubicle 14C has no network connection. Cable appears plugged in." The technician arrives and sees a cable running from the user's laptop NIC port to a wall jack. The switch port light is not lit. The technician swaps the cable with a known-good patch cable and the switch port immediately lights up and the user gets an IP address.

1. List three distinct physical or wiring faults that could have caused the original cable to fail. For each fault, describe how a cable tester would display that specific failure.
2. The original cable was a straight-through patch cable. A colleague suggests that a crossover cable would have worked instead since "the wiring is similar anyway." Is this correct? Explain the functional difference and when a crossover cable is the appropriate choice.
3. The technician notices the failed cable has RJ-45 plugs on both ends but one end appears slightly narrower than the other. What might this indicate, and what connection problem could it cause?

## Scenario C — The Inter-Building Fiber Link

A university campus needs a new fiber optic link between the library building and the computer science building 320 meters away. The link must support 10 Gbps. A purchasing agent found a great deal on multimode fiber with SC connectors and wants to place the order immediately.

1. Evaluate whether multimode fiber is the appropriate choice for this 320-meter, 10 Gbps requirement. What is the typical distance limitation of multimode fiber at 10 Gbps, and how does 320 meters compare to that limit?
2. What fiber type and connector would you recommend for this installation, and why is that connector preferred over SC in a new enterprise deployment?
3. The cable run will be buried in conduit between the two buildings. Does the choice between single-mode and multimode fiber affect the outdoor burial rating of the cable, and what other cable specification should the purchasing agent confirm before ordering?

## Professor Nash — Closing Note

Network cabling is one of the most visually tested topics on the A+ Core 1 exam. You will be shown a photograph of a connector or a cable and asked to identify it, or given a speed-and-distance scenario and asked to select the right cable. The scenarios in this discussion are designed to make you reason through those decisions the same way you will on the exam — and the same way you will in a real wiring closet. Read your classmates' responses carefully. The scenarios overlap in useful ways, and you will likely learn something from each one.
