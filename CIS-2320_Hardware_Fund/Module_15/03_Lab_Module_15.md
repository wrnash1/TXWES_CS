## Part 1: EP Process Sequencing and Symptom Mapping (40 Points)

### Part 1 Objective

Demonstrate mastery of the six-step laser printing EP process by correctly sequencing scrambled steps and matching print quality symptoms to the responsible EP stage or component.

### Part 1 Background

The CompTIA A+ Core 1 exam presents the EP process steps in a scrambled order and asks candidates to identify the correct sequence. It also presents a print quality symptom — a photograph or description of a defective print — and asks which EP step or component failed. This exercise replicates both question types.

### Part 1 Procedure

#### Exercise 1A: EP Process Sequencing (15 points)

The following six EP process steps are presented in scrambled order. In your lab report, write out the six steps in the correct sequence as defined by the CompTIA A+ certification objectives. After writing the sequence, write one sentence for each step that describes the physical action occurring and the component responsible.

Scrambled steps (do not use this order — rearrange them):

- Fusing
- Developing
- Transferring
- Exposing
- Charging
- Cleaning

After completing the sequence, answer the following in your lab report: What does the mnemonic "Could Children Ever Do That Fast?" represent? Write out each word and the EP step it corresponds to.

#### Exercise 1B: Print Quality Symptom Matching (25 points)

For each of the ten symptoms described below, identify: (a) the EP process step or printer component most likely responsible, (b) a brief technical explanation of why that step or component produces that specific symptom, and (c) the recommended corrective action.

Symptom 1: Pages exit the laser printer with toner that smears when rubbed with a finger. The image is fully visible and correctly positioned before touching.

Symptom 2: Printed pages are significantly lighter than expected across the entire page. Shaking the toner cartridge slightly improves output quality temporarily.

Symptom 3: A gray or dirty background appears behind all printed content. Text and images are present but set against a gray haze across the full page.

Symptom 4: A solid vertical black line runs from the top to the bottom of every printed page. The line is present in the same position on every page.

Symptom 5: Small black dots appear at regular intervals down the page — specifically every 94 millimeters — regardless of what content is being printed.

Symptom 6: Printed content is correctly formatted but significantly faint, with portions missing at the page edges. The toner cartridge was just replaced.

Symptom 7: A laser printer that previously worked normally now produces completely blank pages. The cartridge is not empty and the printer reports no errors.

Symptom 8: An inkjet printer's color output shows horizontal white bands through images. Cyan and magenta lines are present but yellow lines are missing throughout the print.

Symptom 9: A receipt printer at a POS terminal produces blank receipts consistently. The paper roll appears fully loaded and the printer reports no error codes.

Symptom 10: A 3D FDM printer's ABS print lifts off the build plate at the corners about one hour into a long print job.

#### Part 1 Grading Rubric

- Exercise 1A sequence (correct order): 8 points
- Exercise 1A one-sentence descriptions (six steps): 7 points
- Exercise 1B ten symptoms (responsible component/step, explanation, corrective action — 2.5 points each): 25 points

## Part 2: Printer Maintenance Procedures (35 Points)

### Part 2 Objective

Complete a structured printer maintenance checklist and document each maintenance step with observations, demonstrating practical knowledge of laser, inkjet, and thermal printer upkeep.

### Part 2 Background

Printer maintenance is a routine technician responsibility. Knowing the correct maintenance procedures for each printer type, the correct sequence for performing them, and how to document the work for a maintenance log are all skills tested on the A+ exam and required in the field.

### Part 2 Procedure

Complete at least two of the three maintenance exercises below. If you have access to a physical printer of the relevant type, perform the procedure on the actual device. If you do not have access, complete the documentation exercise using the scenario description provided.

#### Maintenance Exercise A: Laser Printer Toner Cartridge Replacement (12 points)

Perform or document the following procedure:

Step 1: Power off the laser printer and open the front or top access panel as directed by the printer's design. Record the printer make and model (or use "HP LaserJet P2035" as the scenario device if no physical printer is available).

Step 2: Locate the toner cartridge. Note the locking mechanism (tab, lever, or pull-out handle) used to secure it. Release the mechanism and remove the spent cartridge. Record what the cartridge looks and feels like — is it noticeably light?

Step 3: Remove the new toner cartridge from its packaging. Before inserting it, perform the redistribution step: hold the cartridge horizontally and gently rock it from side to side five times to distribute toner evenly across the developer roller. Record why this step is important.

Step 4: Remove the protective tape seal from the cartridge (if present). Insert the cartridge into the printer until it clicks into the locking position. Close the access panel.

Step 5: Power on the printer. Print a test page. Record the quality of the test page output. If a calibration page is required, allow it to complete before the test page.

Step 6: In your lab report, answer: What happens if the protective tape seal is not removed before inserting the cartridge? What is the correct disposal method for a spent laser toner cartridge?

#### Maintenance Exercise B: Inkjet Nozzle Check and Head Cleaning (12 points)

Perform or document the following procedure using an inkjet printer or the scenario description (Epson EcoTank ET-4760):

Step 1: Open the printer driver on a Windows PC. Navigate to the printer properties. Locate the Maintenance or Tools tab. Record the exact menu path used.

Step 2: Initiate a Nozzle Check print. Allow the test pattern to print. Examine the output: are all color channels printing continuous lines, or are there gaps? Record which colors, if any, show missing or broken lines.

Step 3: If the nozzle check shows gaps or missing lines, initiate one Head Cleaning cycle from the maintenance tab. Allow the cleaning cycle to complete fully — do not interrupt it.

Step 4: Run the Nozzle Check again. Compare the second test pattern to the first. Record whether the cleaning cycle improved the output.

Step 5: If the second nozzle check still shows problems, repeat the cleaning cycle once more and run another nozzle check. Record the total number of cleaning cycles required to restore acceptable output.

Step 6: In your lab report, answer: Why should a technician not simply run multiple cleaning cycles in succession without checking between each one? What is the risk of running too many cleaning cycles?

#### Maintenance Exercise C: Thermal Printer Maintenance (11 points)

Perform or document the following procedure using a direct thermal label printer or receipt printer, or the scenario description (Zebra GK420d label printer):

Step 1: Power off the printer. Open the media cover to access the printhead and paper path.

Step 2: Using a printhead cleaning pen or a cotton swab lightly dampened with 99% isopropyl alcohol (IPA), gently wipe along the full length of the printhead element. The printhead is the narrow bar with a row of tiny heating elements. Record the appearance of the swab after cleaning — was there visible residue, dust, or adhesive on the printhead?

Step 3: Allow the printhead to dry completely (approximately 60 seconds) before loading media.

Step 4: Reload the thermal paper or label stock. Confirm the coated side faces the printhead. For labels: the label side faces up toward the printhead. For receipt paper: the shinier or smoother side faces inward toward the printhead. Record how you confirmed which side is the coated side.

Step 5: Close the media cover. Power on the printer. Feed one label or run a short test print. Record the output quality.

Step 6: In your lab report, answer: What happens to a direct thermal printer's output if the printhead is never cleaned? At what frequency should printhead cleaning typically be performed (consult the manufacturer's recommendation in the reading guide or device documentation)?

#### Part 2 Grading Rubric

- Maintenance Exercise A completed with all six steps documented and questions answered: 12 points
- Maintenance Exercise B completed with nozzle check results, cleaning cycle documentation, and questions answered: 12 points
- Maintenance Exercise C completed with cleaning documentation, media loading confirmation, and questions answered: 11 points
- Minimum requirement: complete at least two of the three exercises. Maximum credit requires all three.

## Part 3: Network Print Queue Setup and Sharing (25 Points)

### Part 3 Objective

Configure a network print queue on a Windows PC and connect a second computer to the shared printer, demonstrating the steps a technician performs when deploying a shared printer in a small office environment.

### Part 3 Background

Shared printers are fundamental to small office and enterprise IT deployments. A technician must know how to enable sharing on a Windows host, assign a share name, and connect client computers through the shared queue. The A+ exam tests this as both a configuration task and a troubleshooting scenario (for example, identifying why a shared printer stops working after the host PC gets a new IP address or is renamed).

### Part 3 Procedure

If you have access to two Windows computers and a printer, perform this procedure on actual hardware. If working solo, complete Steps 1 through 4 on a single computer and document the configuration, then answer the scenario questions in Step 5 for the remaining points.

Step 1: On the Windows PC directly connected to or hosting the printer, open Settings > Bluetooth and Devices > Printers and Scanners. Select the printer you want to share. Click Printer Properties, then navigate to the Sharing tab. Check the box to Share this printer. Enter a share name (for example: OfficeHP). Click Apply and OK. Take a screenshot of the sharing configuration.

Step 2: Confirm that File and Printer Sharing is enabled in the network settings. Navigate to Control Panel > Network and Sharing Center > Advanced Sharing Settings. Ensure File and printer sharing is turned on for the active network profile. Take a screenshot.

Step 3: From a second Windows PC on the same network, open Settings > Bluetooth and Devices > Printers and Scanners > Add a printer or scanner. When Windows does not find the printer automatically, click "The printer that I want isn't listed." Select "Select a shared printer by name" and enter the UNC path: \\HostComputerName\OfficeHP (replace HostComputerName with the actual name of the sharing PC). Complete the wizard. Take a screenshot of the successfully added printer.

Step 4: Print a test page from the second PC through the shared queue. Confirm it prints successfully. Take a screenshot of the test page confirmation dialog.

Step 5: Answer the following scenario questions in complete sentences.

Scenario A — After working for two weeks, the shared printer stops responding to print jobs from client computers. The printer itself is online and prints a test page directly from the host PC. What is the most likely cause, and how would you resolve it?

Scenario B — A technician sets up a shared printer using the host computer's hostname in the UNC path. After the host computer is renamed during a domain join, client computers cannot find the printer. Explain what changed and how to reconnect the clients.

Scenario C — Management asks whether the office should switch from a single shared printer through a PC to a network printer with its own Ethernet port. Describe two advantages of a dedicated network printer compared to a PC-shared printer in an office environment.

#### Part 3 Grading Rubric

- Steps 1 through 4 completed with screenshots or equivalent documentation: 15 points
- Three scenario questions answered with technical accuracy and complete sentences: 10 points (3-4 points each)
