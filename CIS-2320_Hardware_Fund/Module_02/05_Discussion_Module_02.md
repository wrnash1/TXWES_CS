## Scenario A — The Upgrade That Won't Fit

A small business owner brings in a desktop PC with a standard ATX mid-tower case. She says the current motherboard died and she needs it replaced quickly. A junior technician at your shop orders a Mini-ITX replacement board because it was significantly cheaper and in stock. When the board arrives, the technician realizes it does not fit correctly in the existing case without modification.

Respond to all three of the following:

1. Explain why the Mini-ITX board does not fit correctly in a standard ATX mid-tower case and what physical standard determines case-to-board compatibility.
2. Which form factor should the technician have ordered instead, and why does that form factor work in this case while Mini-ITX does not?
3. If the business owner later asks whether she can add a dedicated graphics card to the replacement board you recommend, what would you tell her? Base your answer on the typical expansion slot count for that form factor.

## Scenario B — The Mystery Shutdown

A customer's desktop PC powers on, gets through POST, and boots into Windows — but every morning the clock is wrong and shows a date from several years ago. The customer says the problem started about two weeks ago and gets worse every day. A coworker suggests the issue is a failing power supply because "the PSU handles the clock." You disagree.

Respond to all three of the following:

1. Identify the most likely cause of the date/time reset problem and explain the role of the specific component involved.
2. Explain why your coworker's PSU theory is incorrect. What does the PSU actually provide, and how is the clock circuit powered differently?
3. Describe the repair procedure, including the component name, part number if applicable, and any precautions a technician should take when performing this replacement.

---

## Scenario C — The Expansion Slot Dilemma

A technician is upgrading a workstation and needs to install three cards: a dedicated GPU, a 10-gigabit network interface card (NIC), and a PCIe NVMe SSD adapter. The motherboard has one PCIe x16 slot, one PCIe x4 slot, and one PCIe x1 slot. The technician is unsure which card goes in which slot and whether all three can be installed at the same time.

Respond to all three of the following:

1. Assign each card to its correct slot and justify each assignment based on the bandwidth and physical size requirements of that card type.
2. The NIC card is a PCIe x4 card but the only remaining open slot after placing the GPU is a PCIe x1 slot. Can the NIC be installed in the x1 slot? Explain the physical and electrical compatibility rules that apply here.
3. If the motherboard only had two PCIe slots (x16 and x1), which two cards would you prioritize installing and why? What would the business impact be of leaving out the third card?
