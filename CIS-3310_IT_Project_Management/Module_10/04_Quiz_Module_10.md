**Question 1**
Which risk response strategy involves buying an insurance policy or outsourcing a database migration task to a vendor?
*   A) Avoid
*   B) Transfer
*   C) Mitigate
*   D) Accept
*   **Correct Answer:** B) Transfer shifts the financial or operational burden of a risk to a third party (such as an insurer or a specialized vendor), without eliminating the risk itself.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why B is correct:* Insurance and outsourcing are the classic examples of risk transfer. The risk still exists, but another party assumes the financial consequences if it occurs.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Avoidance eliminates the risk entirely—typically by changing the project plan or scope to remove the risk condition. Insurance does not eliminate the risk.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Mitigation reduces the probability or impact of the risk before it occurs through proactive measures (e.g., adding firewalls to reduce breach risk). It does not shift ownership to another party.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Acceptance acknowledges the risk and chooses not to take proactive action; the project team retains responsibility for the risk. Buying insurance is not "doing nothing"—it is actively moving the consequence to a third party.

**Question 2**
Which of the following best defines a **Risk Register** in project management?
*   A) A project document that identifies all known risks, their probability and impact ratings, prioritization, planned responses, risk owners, and current status.
*   B) A contract document signed between the project manager and the project sponsor to formally authorize risk contingency spending.
*   C) A financial spreadsheet that tracks all project expenditures against the approved cost baseline.
*   D) A quality tool that ranks defect categories by frequency to identify the most impactful issues for correction.
*   **Correct Answer:** A) The Risk Register is the central tracking document for all project risk information, created during Risk Identification and continuously updated throughout the project lifecycle.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is correct:* The Risk Register records everything the team knows about each risk: description, probability, impact, priority score, response strategy, trigger, risk owner, and current status. It is one of the most important project management documents.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* A contract authorizing contingency spending describes a procurement or financial governance document, not a risk register. Contingency reserves are managed through the project budget, not a separate contract.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Tracking expenditures against the cost baseline describes cost performance monitoring (e.g., using EVM). The Risk Register is a risk management tool, not a financial tracking tool.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Ranking defect categories by frequency describes a Pareto Chart, a quality management tool. Risk and quality are separate knowledge areas.

**Question 3**
A project team is evaluating a vendor for a complex cloud migration. The scope of work is well-defined but the team wants to protect the project budget from cost overruns. Which contract type best meets this need?
*   A) Time and Materials (T&M)
*   B) Cost-Plus-Fixed-Fee (CPFF)
*   C) Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP)
*   D) Cost-Plus-Incentive-Fee (CPIF)
*   **Correct Answer:** C) A Firm-Fixed-Price contract places the cost overrun risk entirely on the seller; the buyer pays a set price regardless of the vendor's actual costs, protecting the project budget.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why C is correct:* FFP is the most common contract type for well-defined scopes. Because the price is fixed, any vendor cost overrun is the vendor's problem, not the buyer's.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* T&M pays the vendor for actual time and materials used; if the work takes longer or requires more materials than estimated, costs escalate for the buyer. It offers the least budget protection for a well-defined scope.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* CPFF reimburses the vendor for all actual costs and adds a fixed fee; the buyer bears all cost risk. This is appropriate for uncertain-scope work, not well-defined work requiring budget protection.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* CPIF also reimburses actual costs and adds an incentive fee for meeting targets; the buyer still bears cost risk. Like all cost-reimbursable contracts, it is riskier for the buyer than a fixed-price arrangement.

**Question 4**
An organization wants to invite multiple vendors to submit their proposed approach, methodology, and pricing for a complex IT system integration. Which procurement document should be issued?
*   A) Request for Quote (RFQ)
*   B) Request for Proposal (RFP)
*   C) Invitation for Bid (IFB)
*   D) Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)
*   **Correct Answer:** B) A Request for Proposal (RFP) invites vendors to propose both their technical approach and pricing for complex, non-standard work where methodology matters—not just price.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why B is correct:* An RFP is used when the buyer wants to evaluate both the vendor's technical solution and their pricing. It is appropriate for complex IT work where multiple valid approaches exist and the buyer is choosing based on best value.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* An RFQ invites vendors to provide a price only; it is used for straightforward, commodity-type purchases where specifications are already fully defined and only cost matters.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* An IFB (Invitation for Bid) is similar to RFQ—used for well-defined, fixed-price work where the buyer selects the lowest compliant bid. It does not evaluate technical approach or methodology.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* An NDA (Non-Disclosure Agreement) is a legal document protecting confidential information during negotiations; it is not a procurement solicitation document used to invite vendor proposals.

**Question 5**
A project manager identifies a risk that, if it occurs, will require restoring a failed database from backup. Rather than changing the project plan, the team documents the risk and sets aside $8,000 in contingency reserve specifically for recovery costs. Which risk response strategy does this represent?
*   A) Avoid
*   B) Transfer
*   C) Mitigate
*   D) Active Accept
*   **Correct Answer:** D) Active Acceptance acknowledges the risk, chooses not to eliminate or reduce it, and prepares a contingency reserve or contingency plan to handle it if it occurs.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why D is correct:* Setting aside a contingency reserve specifically for a documented risk is the definition of active acceptance. The team knows the risk exists, cannot or will not eliminate it, but prepares financial resources to respond if it materializes.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Avoidance would require eliminating the database failure risk entirely—for example, by switching to a fully managed cloud database with automatic failover. Simply documenting the risk and reserving money does not avoid it.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* Transfer would involve purchasing a backup/recovery service contract or cyber insurance that covers database restoration costs. Holding internal contingency funds is not transfer.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Mitigation would involve reducing the probability of a database failure (e.g., implementing real-time replication) or reducing its impact (e.g., shortening the RTO). Reserving money for recovery does not reduce probability or impact—it plans for consequences.
