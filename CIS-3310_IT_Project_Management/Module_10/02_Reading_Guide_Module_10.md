### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 10 - Procurement Management**! Most IT projects involve some degree of procurement—buying hardware, licensing software, hiring consultants, or contracting cloud services. Procurement management covers how to plan what needs to be purchased, how to select vendors, which contract type to use, and how to manage and close vendor agreements. Understanding contract types and their risk allocation is a high-value exam topic.

This module's content directly maps to CompTIA Project+ Domain 3 (Project Execution and Delivery) and PMI CAPM Knowledge Area 12 (Procurement Management).

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Risk Register**: A project document that identifies all known risks, their probability and impact ratings, prioritization, planned response strategies, risk owners, and current status. The Risk Register is created during Risk Identification and updated continuously throughout the project. It is the central tracking tool for all project risk information.
*   **Qualitative Risk Analysis (Probability vs. Impact)**: A risk analysis technique that assesses and prioritizes risks by evaluating their probability of occurrence and their potential impact on project objectives using a Probability × Impact (P×I) matrix. Results are subjective (based on expert judgment) but allow the project team to quickly focus attention on the highest-priority risks without requiring statistical modeling.
*   **Risk Response Strategies (Avoid, Transfer, Mitigate, Accept)**: The four strategies for responding to negative risks (threats). Avoid = eliminate the risk by changing the project plan. Transfer = shift the financial burden to a third party (insurance, outsourcing). Mitigate = reduce the probability or impact before the risk occurs. Accept = acknowledge the risk and decide not to take proactive action (passive: do nothing; active: set aside contingency reserve).
*   **Risk Triggers**: Early warning signs or conditions that indicate a risk event is about to occur or has already occurred. Identifying risk triggers enables the project team to activate contingency plans before a risk causes significant damage. Triggers are documented alongside each risk in the Risk Register.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Project+ Tip – Contract Type Risk Allocation**: Know which party bears cost risk in each contract type: Fixed-Price (seller bears risk; buyer benefits if actual costs are low), Cost-Reimbursable (buyer bears risk; seller is paid actual costs plus a fee), Time & Materials (shared risk; buyer pays actual time and materials with no guaranteed cost ceiling). The exam frequently tests "which contract type is riskiest for the buyer."
*   **CAPM Tip – Procurement Documents**: Know the difference between an RFP (Request for Proposal—invites vendors to describe their approach and price), RFQ (Request for Quote—invites vendors to provide a price only), and IFB (Invitation for Bid—used for fixed-price, well-defined work). The CAPM may present a procurement scenario and ask which document is most appropriate.
*   **Scenario Trap**: A question describes a risk where "the project team decides to do nothing and accept any losses if it occurs." This is passive risk acceptance. If they say "we set aside $10,000 in contingency reserve for this risk," that is active risk acceptance. Both are forms of acceptance—know the difference.
*   **Study Resource**: For a clear breakdown of contract types with examples and risk allocation, search [YouTube: Project Management Contract Types Fixed Price Cost Reimbursable Time Materials](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=project+management+contract+types+fixed+price+cost+reimbursable+time+materials+exam). This is one of the most tested procurement topics on both Project+ and CAPM.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will perform the following steps to apply these concepts:
*   **Build a risk register table detailing threats**: For your IT deployment scenario, identify at least eight risks, and document each in a Risk Register table with columns for Risk ID, Description, Probability (H/M/L), Impact (H/M/L), Risk Score, Response Strategy, and Risk Owner.
*   **Calculate risk score rankings (Probability × Impact)**: Using a 1–5 scale for both probability and impact, calculate a numeric risk score for each risk in your register and sort them from highest to lowest priority.
*   **Draft risk mitigation workflows**: For the top three risks in your register, write a mitigation plan that describes the specific actions the team will take to reduce either the probability or impact of each risk before it occurs.
