## Section 2: Procurement Management Process Flow [03:00 – 07:00]

[SHOW SLIDE] Three procurement management processes

PMI defines three processes in Procurement Management.

Plan Procurement Management is the Planning process. The PM analyzes procurement needs, makes make-or-buy decisions, selects contract types, and creates the Procurement Management Plan and any Statements of Work.

Conduct Procurements is the Executing process. The PM distributes solicitation documents (RFP, RFQ, IFB) to potential sellers, receives and evaluates proposals, selects a seller, and awards a contract.

Control Procurements is the Monitoring and Controlling process. The PM monitors vendor performance, manages changes to the contract, administers payments, resolves disputes, and ultimately closes each procurement when the work is complete.

> **Project+ Exam Tip:** Know which process group each procurement process belongs to. This is tested frequently. Plan Procurement Management is Planning. Conduct Procurements is Executing. Control Procurements is Monitoring and Controlling.

## Section 3: Make-or-Buy Analysis [07:00 – 10:00]

Before signing any contracts, the project team performs a make-or-buy analysis — a decision-making process to determine whether a product, service, or component should be developed internally or procured externally.

Factors favoring make (internal):

Proprietary technology or competitive advantage if developed internally. Existing capacity and expertise available on the team. Cost analysis shows internal development is more economical. Need for direct control over the work.

Factors favoring buy (external):

Specialized expertise not available internally. Cost analysis shows purchasing is more economical than building. Speed to market is a priority. The work is not core to the organization's capabilities.

Make-or-buy analysis is a formal decision documented in the Project Management Plan. The output informs the procurement approach — if the decision is to buy, procurement processes continue. If the decision is to make, the work is planned as internal project work.

> **Project+ Exam Tip:** Make-or-buy analysis is a Planning activity, not an Executing activity. Students sometimes assume it happens during Conduct Procurements when vendors are being evaluated. The analysis and decision happen in Plan Procurement Management, before any solicitation documents are issued.

## Section 4: Contract Types [10:00 – 16:00]

[SHOW SLIDE] Contract types with risk distribution arrows

Contract type selection is one of the most heavily tested procurement concepts on the Project+ exam. There are three main categories.

Fixed-Price contracts set a defined price for a defined scope of work. The seller assumes the cost risk — if the work costs more than expected, the seller absorbs the overrun. The buyer assumes the risk of incomplete or unclear scope definition.

Fixed-Price Lump Sum (FPLS): A single price for all work. Requires a very well-defined scope.

Fixed-Price Incentive Fee (FPIF): Fixed price with a bonus structure for exceeding performance targets.

Fixed-Price with Economic Price Adjustment (FP-EPA): Fixed price with built-in adjustments for cost escalation over time; used on long-duration contracts.

Cost-Reimbursable contracts reimburse the seller for actual costs plus a fee. The buyer assumes the cost risk — if actual costs exceed the estimate, the buyer pays. These contracts are used when scope is uncertain or cannot be completely defined upfront.

Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF): Buyer reimburses costs plus a fixed fee regardless of performance.

Cost Plus Incentive Fee (CPIF): Buyer reimburses costs plus a fee that increases for better performance.

Cost Plus Award Fee (CPAF): Buyer reimburses costs plus a subjective award fee based on performance.

Time and Material (T&M): A hybrid — the buyer pays a fixed rate per hour of labor plus actual material costs. Risk is shared but tends to favor the seller unless the contract includes a not-to-exceed clause.

> **Project+ Exam Tip:** The risk distribution question is the most common contract-type exam trap. Fixed-Price = seller carries cost risk; Cost-Reimbursable = buyer carries cost risk; T&M = risk is shared. A question that describes "the buyer wants to limit cost risk" is pointing toward Fixed-Price. A question that describes "scope cannot be defined" is pointing toward Cost-Reimbursable.

## Section 5: Statement of Work and Procurement Closure [16:00 – 21:00]

[SHOW SLIDE] Statement of Work vs. contract

The Statement of Work (SOW) is a detailed description of the products, services, or results to be procured from a vendor. The SOW defines: the scope of work, deliverables, acceptance criteria, location of performance, and applicable standards. The SOW is included in or attached to the contract — it is the technical heart of the agreement.

The contract is the formal legal agreement between buyer and seller. It includes the SOW, payment terms, schedule, dispute resolution mechanisms, warranties, and change control provisions. Both are required — the SOW without a contract has no legal standing; the contract without a complete SOW lacks the specific work definition needed to manage performance.

[SHOW SLIDE] Source selection criteria matrix

Source selection criteria are the factors used to evaluate seller proposals. Common criteria include technical approach, relevant experience, management plan, past performance, price, and compliance with requirements. Source selection criteria are documented before proposals are received to ensure objective, consistent evaluation.

Procurement closure occurs when the PM confirms that all contracted work has been completed and accepted, all payments have been processed, and a formal contract closure document has been signed. Verbal confirmation is not sufficient — formal closure is required.

> **Project+ Exam Tip:** A completed deliverable is not the same as a closed contract. Procurement closure requires formal documentation that all work has been accepted and all financial obligations have been settled. Students sometimes confuse technical acceptance with contract closure.
