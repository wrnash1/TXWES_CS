### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Project vs. Operations**: A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result; it has a defined start and end date. Operations are ongoing, repetitive activities that sustain the business—such as running a help desk or managing a network. The Project+ exam distinguishes these precisely, so memorize that "temporary + unique = project."
*   **Triple Constraint (Scope, Time, Cost)**: The three interdependent boundaries that define a project's work. Changing any one constraint forces trade-offs in the other two—for example, compressing schedule (time) typically increases cost or reduces scope. A fourth element, quality, is often placed at the center as the outcome all three constraints affect.
*   **Project Lifecycle Phases**: The sequence of phases a project moves through from start to finish: Initiation, Planning, Execution, Monitoring & Controlling, and Closing. CompTIA Project+ maps to a similar phase model; understanding which activities belong in each phase is heavily tested.
*   **Stakeholders**: Any individual or group who has an interest in, or is affected by, the project. Stakeholders include the project sponsor, project manager, team members, end users, and external vendors. Early identification and engagement is a key success factor.
*   **Project Manager Role**: The individual responsible for leading the project team to achieve project objectives within the defined constraints. The PM is accountable for integration, communication, and decision-making—not necessarily for performing the technical work directly.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Project+ Tip – Know the PMI Knowledge Areas**: CompTIA Project+ aligns closely with PMI's 10 Knowledge Areas (Integration, Scope, Schedule, Cost, Quality, Resource, Communications, Risk, Procurement, Stakeholder). Expect scenario questions that ask which knowledge area or process is most applicable to a given situation.
*   **CAPM Tip – Memorize PMBOK Process Groups**: PMI CAPM tests the five Process Groups (Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring & Controlling, Closing) extensively. Be able to identify which process belongs in which group; don't confuse process groups with project phases.
*   **Scenario Trap**: A common exam trap presents a "project" that is actually ongoing operations (e.g., "the IT department maintains the company's email servers"). Recognize the keywords "temporary," "unique," and "defined end date" as markers of a true project.
*   **Study Resource**: To reinforce these concepts visually, watch the CompTIA Project+ overview series by Joseph Phillips—search [YouTube: CompTIA Project+ by Joseph Phillips](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=comptia+project%2B+PK0-005+joseph+phillips). Phillips covers the PMI framework in plain language with practical examples aligned to the PK0-005 exam objectives.
