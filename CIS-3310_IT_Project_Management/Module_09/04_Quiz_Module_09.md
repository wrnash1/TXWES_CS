## Question 1

According to PMI, which of the following best defines a project risk?

- A) Any event that will definitely cause a negative impact on the project schedule
- B) An uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on one or more project objectives
- C) A documented problem that has already occurred and requires corrective action
- D) A constraint that limits the project manager's ability to meet the project baseline

Correct Answer: B) PMI defines risk as an uncertain event or condition that may positively or negatively affect project objectives. Risk is forward-looking and includes both threats and opportunities.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why B is correct:* The PMI definition explicitly includes both positive and negative effects, and the word "uncertain" is essential — something that will definitely happen is not a risk, it is a certainty to be planned for.
- *Why A is incorrect:* This definition contains two errors: it restricts risk to negative outcomes only, and it says the event "will definitely" occur. Risk requires uncertainty. A certain negative event is a constraint or issue, not a risk.
- *Why C is incorrect:* An event that has already occurred is an issue, not a risk. Risk management is forward-looking. Once a risk event occurs, it transitions to issue management.
- *Why D is incorrect:* A constraint is a limitation on the project (fixed budget, mandatory deadline) — it is not a risk. Constraints are known conditions that affect planning; risks are uncertain future events.

## Question 2

A project team identifies a risk that their primary vendor may miss a delivery milestone. The PM decides to require the vendor to post a performance bond and include financial penalties for late delivery in the contract. Which risk response strategy is being used?

- A) Avoid
- B) Transfer
- C) Mitigate
- D) Exploit

Correct Answer: B) Transfer shifts the financial consequences of a risk to a third party (in this case, the vendor through contractual penalties and a performance bond). The risk still exists, but the financial impact is shifted.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why B is correct:* Contractual mechanisms such as performance bonds, insurance, and financial penalties are classic Transfer strategies. They do not eliminate the risk of vendor delay — they ensure the financial consequences fall on the vendor rather than the project.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Avoid would require changing the project plan to eliminate the risk entirely — for example, sourcing a different vendor or removing the dependency. Using contractual penalties does not eliminate the risk.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Mitigate would reduce the probability or impact of the vendor delay — for example, building in a schedule buffer or requiring weekly progress check-ins to catch delays early. Penalties after the fact do not reduce the likelihood of delay.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Exploit is an opportunity response strategy. Vendor delay is a threat, not an opportunity. Using an opportunity response on a threat is a category error.

## Question 3

After implementing additional automated testing to reduce the risk of critical defects reaching production, the project team discovers that the new automated test suite occasionally produces false-positive failures that halt the build pipeline. What is the false-positive failure risk called?

- A) Residual risk
- B) Known unknown
- C) Secondary risk
- D) Management reserve risk

Correct Answer: C) A secondary risk is a new risk that is created as a direct result of implementing a risk response. The false-positive failures from the automated test suite are a new risk introduced by the mitigation action itself.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why C is correct:* Secondary risks are specifically defined as risks created by the risk response. The team did not have the false-positive problem before implementing the automated testing solution — it is a direct product of the response.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Residual risk is the remaining portion of the original risk after the response is applied — the part of the original defect risk that automated testing did not fully eliminate. False-positive failures are a new, different risk, not a remnant of the original one.
- *Why B is incorrect:* A known unknown is an identified risk — any risk on the Risk Register. The classification does not describe the relationship between a risk and a response.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Management reserve is a budget category for unknown unknowns. It is not a type of risk.

## Question 4

A project manager reviews the Risk Register and identifies a low-probability, low-impact risk related to minor printer setup issues in the new office. The PM decides to document the risk and take no proactive action, simply noting that the team will address it if it occurs. Which response strategy is this?

- A) Mitigate
- B) Transfer
- C) Avoid
- D) Passive Accept

Correct Answer: D) Passive Accept acknowledges a low-priority risk and plans no proactive action. The team will address the issue if and when it occurs. No contingency reserve is set aside.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why D is correct:* Passive acceptance is appropriate for low-priority risks where the cost of a proactive response exceeds the likely impact. Simply noting the risk and deciding to address it reactively if it occurs is the definition of passive acceptance.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Mitigate requires taking a proactive action to reduce the probability or impact. The PM is taking no action, so this is not mitigation.
- *Why B is incorrect:* Transfer requires shifting the financial consequences to a third party — typically through a contract or insurance. No such arrangement is described.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Avoid requires changing the project plan to eliminate the threat. No plan change is described; the risk is simply acknowledged and left unaddressed proactively.

## Question 5

The project team identifies a risk that a competitor may release a similar product six months before the project's planned go-live date. To address this, the team proposes accelerating the project timeline and adding development resources to complete the go-live two months early. Which response strategy does this represent?

- A) Mitigate
- B) Transfer
- C) Avoid
- D) Escalate

Correct Answer: C) Avoid changes the project plan to eliminate the threat entirely. By accelerating the timeline to go live before the competitor's release, the team eliminates the risk that the competitor's launch will affect their own launch timing.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why C is correct:* Avoid requires changing the project plan so the risk no longer applies. If the project goes live before the competitor's product is released, the threat of competitive timing impact is eliminated — not just reduced.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Mitigate would reduce the probability or impact of the competitor's release affecting the project — for example, increasing marketing spend to differentiate the launch. Accelerating the timeline to eliminate the risk entirely is Avoid, not Mitigate.
- *Why B is incorrect:* Transfer would shift the financial consequences of competitive timing risk to a third party. Accelerating internal development does not involve a third party absorbing the risk.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Escalate transfers risk response authority to a higher organizational level. Accelerating the timeline is an internal team decision, not an escalation to the program manager or sponsor.

## Question 6

During a project risk workshop, a team member suggests that if the company's new data center comes online ahead of schedule, it could allow the project to move testing to the new infrastructure, reducing vendor hosting costs by $30,000. How should the PM classify and respond to this scenario?

- A) Threat — Transfer; purchase insurance against hosting cost overruns
- B) Opportunity — Exploit; take action to ensure the data center comes online ahead of schedule
- C) Threat — Mitigate; reduce the dependency on vendor hosting to lower cost exposure
- D) Opportunity — Accept; document the scenario and take no proactive action

Correct Answer: B) This is an opportunity — an uncertain event that would have a positive effect on project objectives. The Exploit strategy takes proactive action to ensure the opportunity occurs, which in this case means working with the infrastructure team to accelerate the data center timeline.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why B is correct:* Positive cost reduction is an opportunity. Exploit — the counterpart to Avoid for threats — attempts to make the opportunity definitely happen. Working to accelerate the data center schedule is a reasonable Exploit action.
- *Why A is incorrect:* This scenario is clearly an opportunity (cost reduction), not a threat. Classifying it as a threat and applying Transfer is a fundamental category error.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Mitigate is a threat response strategy. The scenario describes a potential benefit, not a problem to reduce. Even if the positive outcome does not materialize, the project is no worse off than planned.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Accept is valid for opportunities, but the scenario provides a specific and actionable path to capture $30,000 in savings. Passive acceptance would forego that benefit without reason.

## Question 7

Which of the following best distinguishes Qualitative Risk Analysis from Quantitative Risk Analysis?

- A) Qualitative analysis uses probability-impact scoring to prioritize risks; quantitative analysis uses numerical models to estimate the combined effect of risks on project objectives
- B) Qualitative analysis is performed only by the project manager; quantitative analysis requires a risk specialist
- C) Qualitative analysis is performed after quantitative analysis to validate numerical results
- D) Qualitative analysis applies only to schedule risks; quantitative analysis applies only to cost risks

Correct Answer: A) Qualitative risk analysis uses probability and impact ratings to rank and prioritize risks. Quantitative analysis uses numerical modeling techniques (such as Monte Carlo simulation or decision trees) to estimate the aggregate effect of prioritized risks on project objectives.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why A is correct:* The distinction between qualitative (ordinal scoring, prioritization) and quantitative (numerical modeling, probability distributions) is one of the most tested risk management concepts. Qualitative always precedes quantitative, and quantitative is applied only to the high-priority risks identified in qualitative analysis.
- *Why B is incorrect:* PMI does not assign qualitative analysis exclusively to the PM or quantitative analysis exclusively to specialists. Both can involve the full team and subject matter experts as appropriate.
- *Why C is incorrect:* The sequence is reversed. Qualitative analysis comes first and is used to prioritize risks for quantitative analysis — not the other way around.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Both qualitative and quantitative analysis apply to all risk dimensions — cost, schedule, scope, and quality. There is no domain restriction.

## Question 8

A project's Risk Register identifies a risk that a new regulatory requirement could affect the project. The PM assigns this risk to the compliance officer, who will monitor the regulatory environment and implement a response if the regulation is enacted. What role does the compliance officer hold in the Risk Register?

- A) Risk sponsor
- B) Risk owner
- C) Risk reviewer
- D) Risk accountable

Correct Answer: B) The risk owner is the team member assigned responsibility for monitoring a specific risk and implementing its response if triggered. Assigning the compliance officer to monitor and respond to the regulatory risk is the correct use of the risk owner designation.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why B is correct:* Risk ownership is explicitly defined in the Risk Register. Assigning a subject matter expert (the compliance officer) to own risks within their domain is PMI best practice — the PM does not personally own every risk.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Risk sponsor is not a standard PMI Risk Register role. The project sponsor has overall accountability for the project but is not listed as a risk-level owner in the register.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Risk reviewer is not a standard PMI role definition for the Risk Register. While reviews are part of monitoring, the designated role for ongoing responsibility is risk owner.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Risk accountable is not a PMI-defined Risk Register role. PMI uses risk owner for the person with monitoring and response responsibility.

## Question 9

The project team has set aside $25,000 within the project's Cost Baseline to cover risks that have been documented in the Risk Register. Who controls access to this money?

- A) The project sponsor, who must approve all risk expenditures
- B) The program manager, who oversees the project portfolio
- C) The project manager, who can release funds when a documented risk occurs
- D) The finance department, which releases funds through the annual budget cycle

Correct Answer: C) The $25,000 is a Contingency Reserve — held within the Cost Baseline for identified risks (known unknowns). Contingency reserves are controlled by the project manager, who can release them when a documented risk event occurs without requiring management approval.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why C is correct:* Contingency reserves sit inside the Cost Baseline and are under PM authority. When a risk on the Risk Register materializes, the PM accesses the contingency without escalating to leadership. This is the defining characteristic that distinguishes contingency from management reserve.
- *Why A is incorrect:* The sponsor's approval is required to access management reserves, not contingency reserves. Contingency is within the PM's authority precisely so risk responses can be implemented quickly.
- *Why B is incorrect:* The program manager does not control project-level contingency reserves. Those are managed at the project level by the PM.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Contingency reserves are project-level funds established during planning, not annual budget cycle funds managed by the finance department.

## Question 10

A project manager is reviewing the Risk Register six weeks into a 20-week project. She discovers three new risks that were not identified during initial planning. What action should she take?

- A) Add the new risks to the Risk Register immediately and perform qualitative analysis on them
- B) Wait until the next formal risk review meeting before adding new risks to the register
- C) Escalate the new risks to the sponsor before adding them to the register
- D) Close the Risk Register because the identification phase is complete

Correct Answer: A) Risk identification is an iterative process throughout the project lifecycle. New risks should be added to the Risk Register as soon as they are identified, followed by qualitative (and if appropriate, quantitative) analysis to prioritize them.

Distractor Analysis:

- *Why A is correct:* PMI explicitly describes risk identification as iterative and ongoing. Delaying the addition of new risks or treating the register as closed after initial planning is a risk management failure. New risks discovered at any point should be documented and analyzed immediately.
- *Why B is incorrect:* Waiting for a formal meeting before logging a newly identified risk creates a gap in the risk management system. The register should be updated as new risks are discovered, regardless of meeting schedules.
- *Why C is incorrect:* New risks do not require sponsor escalation before being added to the Risk Register. The PM adds risks to the register as part of normal project management duties. Escalation may be appropriate for very high-priority risks, but it does not precede documentation.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Closing the Risk Register before project completion is a serious risk management error. The register remains open and active throughout the project lifecycle until all risks have been resolved or the project is closed.
