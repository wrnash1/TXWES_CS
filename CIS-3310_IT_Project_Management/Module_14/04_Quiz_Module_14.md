## Question 1

A project manager is evaluating whether to develop a custom reporting module
internally or purchase a third-party tool. The internal team has the skills, but
the project schedule is very tight. Which project constraint most directly supports
the decision to purchase externally?

A. The team's technical skills are insufficient for the work
B. The schedule leaves insufficient time for internal development
C. The project sponsor prefers vendor relationships
D. The cost of the third-party tool is lower than internal labor

### Correct Answer: B

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — the scenario states the team has the skills, so capability is not
the issue. C is incorrect — sponsor preference alone is not a procurement driver in
the make-or-buy framework. D may or may not be true; the scenario does not provide
cost data. B is correct because schedule constraint (insufficient time) is the
explicit factor described that supports buying rather than building.

## Question 2

Under which contract type does the seller bear the greatest financial risk?

A. Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF)
B. Time and Materials (T&M)
C. Firm Fixed Price (FFP)
D. Cost Plus Incentive Fee (CPIF)

### Correct Answer: C

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — under CPFF the buyer reimburses all costs, so the buyer bears the
financial risk. B is incorrect — T&M risk is shared; the buyer pays actual hours and
materials, which can exceed estimates. D is incorrect — CPIF places cost risk primarily
on the buyer, though incentives share some risk with the seller. C is correct — under FFP
the seller commits to a fixed price regardless of actual costs, bearing all cost overrun risk.

## Question 3

A government agency is procuring research and development work. The exact scope cannot
be fully defined in advance, and the agency wants the vendor's fee to increase if costs
come in below a target amount. Which contract type should the agency use?

A. Firm Fixed Price (FFP)
B. Fixed Price Incentive Fee (FPIF)
C. Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF)
D. Cost Plus Incentive Fee (CPIF)

### Correct Answer: D

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — FFP requires well-defined scope, which this procurement does not have.
B is incorrect — FPIF is a fixed-price variant, still inappropriate for undefined R&D scope.
C is incorrect — CPFF reimburses costs and pays a fixed fee, but the fee does not vary
with performance, so it does not meet the agency's incentive requirement. D is correct —
CPIF reimburses all costs and adjusts the fee based on whether actual costs beat the
target cost, directly satisfying both requirements.

## Question 4

An IT manager issues a document to the market asking vendors to describe their
capabilities and general approach to cloud migration. No pricing is requested and
no commitment is made by either party. What type of solicitation document is this?

A. Request for Proposal (RFP)
B. Request for Quotation (RFQ)
C. Request for Information (RFI)
D. Statement of Work (SOW)

### Correct Answer: C

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — an RFP requests a full proposal including pricing and methodology; it
implies intent to procure. B is incorrect — an RFQ specifically requests pricing on
defined items. D is incorrect — a SOW is a contract document, not a solicitation. C is
correct — an RFI is used for market research, asks for capability information only, and
creates no obligation on either side.

## Question 5

A project manager is developing source selection criteria for a complex IT services
procurement. She finalizes the criteria weights after reading one of the submitted
proposals. What problem does this create?

A. It violates the T&M contract ceiling requirement
B. It introduces evaluator bias favoring the proposal already read
C. It requires a rebid because the SOW was not finalized first
D. It means the procurement cannot use an RFP format

### Correct Answer: B

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — contract ceiling requirements are unrelated to when criteria are defined.
C is incorrect — rebid is not automatically required, though the process is compromised.
D is incorrect — the RFP format is independent of when criteria are finalized. B is correct —
defining evaluation criteria after reading a proposal creates confirmation bias; the criteria
will unconsciously reflect what makes the read proposal look favorable, undermining fairness.

## Question 6

Which section of a Statement of Work defines the specific outputs the vendor must
deliver, along with the due date and quality threshold for each?

A. Background and Objectives
B. Period of Performance
C. Deliverables
D. Applicable Standards

### Correct Answer: C

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — Background and Objectives explains why the procurement is happening,
not what specific outputs are required. B is incorrect — Period of Performance specifies
the contract start and end dates, not individual deliverable details. D is incorrect —
Applicable Standards lists compliance frameworks, not deliverable outputs. C is correct —
the Deliverables section lists each specific output with due dates and acceptance criteria.

## Question 7

A vendor has completed all work items on an IT integration contract. The project manager
verbally tells the vendor "looks good, you're done" but no written acceptance is issued.
The vendor submits a final invoice. What risk does this situation create for the buyer?

A. The buyer may owe the vendor a performance bonus
B. The buyer loses the right to dispute defects discovered after payment
C. The contract automatically renews without written closure
D. The vendor can claim the work was never formally accepted and request additional payment

### Correct Answer: B

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — performance bonuses apply only to contracts with incentive fee provisions,
which are not described here. C is incorrect — contracts do not automatically renew; this
is not a standard contract mechanism. D is partially plausible but inverted; the vendor
has an incentive for acceptance, not against it. B is correct — releasing final payment
without formal written acceptance eliminates the buyer's primary leverage to require
rework, making it legally difficult to dispute defects discovered post-payment.

## Question 8

A project manager notices that a T&M vendor's weekly timesheets show 120 hours billed
for a two-developer team over seven days. The contract has no not-to-exceed ceiling.
What is the most appropriate immediate action?

A. Issue a stop-work order to the vendor
B. Request the vendor justify the hours and compare against milestone progress
C. Amend the contract to add a fixed-price clause retroactively
D. Notify the project sponsor that the contract should be terminated

### Correct Answer: B

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — a stop-work order is a drastic measure appropriate for serious breaches,
not an anomalous timesheet. C is incorrect — retroactive contract amendments are not
standard practice and would require vendor agreement. D is incorrect — termination is
not warranted based solely on one high timesheet without investigation. B is correct —
the PM should first validate the hours against deliverables and milestone progress before
escalating, consistent with proactive T&M contract control practices.

## Question 9

A project is winding down and the project manager needs to close out two vendor
contracts. She has received all deliverables but has not yet verified them against
the acceptance criteria in the SOW. The finance team is requesting she approve the
final invoices immediately so the accounting period can close. What should she do?

A. Approve the invoices immediately to support the finance team's deadline
B. Complete formal acceptance verification before approving final payment
C. Ask the vendor to resubmit the deliverables in the next accounting period
D. Close the contracts verbally and document acceptance retroactively next month

### Correct Answer: B

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — approving payment before acceptance removes all buyer leverage to
require rework if defects are found. C is incorrect — resubmission is unnecessary;
the deliverables have been received. D is incorrect — retroactive documentation of
acceptance that did not actually occur is a compliance risk and potentially fraudulent.
B is correct — formal acceptance must precede final payment regardless of finance
deadlines; the PM should communicate the verification timeline to finance and expedite
the acceptance process.

## Question 10

A project manager is conducting a bidder conference for an upcoming IT infrastructure
procurement. One vendor asks a clarifying question about the firewall specifications in
the RFP. The PM answers verbally during the conference. What must happen next to ensure
procurement integrity?

A. The answer should be documented in the meeting minutes shared only with that vendor
B. The answer should be issued as a formal RFP amendment distributed to all vendors
C. The PM should ask the vendor to resubmit its question in writing before responding
D. The answer should be recorded in the project risk register as a scope clarification

### Correct Answer: B

### Distractor Analysis

A is incorrect — providing clarification only to one vendor creates an unfair advantage
and may invalidate the procurement. C is incorrect — while written questions are good
practice, requiring resubmission before answering is not the key integrity mechanism.
D is incorrect — the risk register is not the vehicle for distributing RFP clarifications
to vendors. B is correct — all questions and answers from a bidder conference must be
issued as formal RFP amendments distributed simultaneously to all potential vendors to
preserve fairness and equal information.

## Question 11

Under which contract type does the BUYER bear the MOST cost risk?

- A) Firm Fixed Price (FFP)
- B) Fixed Price Incentive Fee (FPIF)
- C) Time and Materials (T&M)
- D) Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF)

**Correct Answer:** D) Cost Plus Fixed Fee (CPFF)

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why D is correct:* In a Cost-Reimbursable contract, the buyer reimburses all allowable costs plus a fee. Because scope is not fully defined, the seller has little incentive to control spending and the buyer absorbs all cost growth. CPFF is the purest cost-risk-to-buyer contract type.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Under FFP, the seller bears cost risk. If costs exceed the fixed price, the seller absorbs the overrun — the buyer's cost is capped at the agreed price.
- *Why B is incorrect:* FPIF has a price ceiling and shares savings between buyer and seller. The buyer retains some cost risk but less than in a cost-reimbursable structure.
- *Why C is incorrect:* T&M is a hybrid with moderate buyer risk — it is open-ended but can be capped with a Not-to-Exceed clause. Cost-reimbursable contracts expose the buyer to greater risk than T&M.

## Question 12

A project team needs specialized software development work but cannot precisely define all the technical requirements upfront. The work will be billed at hourly rates for labor plus actual material costs. What contract type is MOST appropriate?

- A) Firm Fixed Price (FFP)
- B) Fixed Price with Economic Price Adjustment (FPEPA)
- C) Time and Materials (T&M)
- D) Cost Plus Incentive Fee (CPIF)

**Correct Answer:** C) Time and Materials (T&M)

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why C is correct:* T&M is the hybrid contract type suited for work where scope is partially defined. Labor is billed at fixed hourly rates (reducing uncertainty) while materials are reimbursed at actual cost. It is commonly used in IT consulting and development.
- *Why A is incorrect:* FFP requires fully defined scope so both parties can agree on a price. With undefined technical requirements, a fixed price cannot be fairly established.
- *Why B is incorrect:* FPEPA adjusts a fixed price for economic factors (inflation, commodity price changes) over long-term contracts — not for undefined scope.
- *Why D is incorrect:* CPIF reimburses all allowable costs and adds a fee based on performance. It is appropriate for research or major system development where requirements evolve significantly — more complex than needed for the hourly-rate consulting work described.

## Question 13

What is the PRIMARY purpose of a bidder conference (also called a contractor conference or pre-bid meeting)?

- A) To allow the project team to evaluate which vendors are most financially stable
- B) To ensure all prospective vendors have the same understanding of procurement requirements and can ask questions before submitting proposals
- C) To negotiate contract terms with the leading vendor before proposals are submitted
- D) To present the project schedule to vendors so they can plan their resource commitments

**Correct Answer:** B) To ensure all prospective vendors have the same understanding of procurement requirements and can ask questions before submitting proposals.

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why B is correct:* Bidder conferences create a level playing field. All vendors attend together, hear the same clarifications, and receive the same written amendments. This maintains fairness and improves proposal quality by ensuring everyone understands the requirements.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Financial stability evaluation is part of vendor pre-qualification or source selection criteria, not the purpose of a bidder conference.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Negotiating with a leading vendor before proposals are submitted is unethical and violates competitive procurement principles. The bidder conference occurs before proposals are submitted.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Presenting the project schedule to inform vendor resource planning may be a minor element, but it is not the primary purpose of a bidder conference.

## Question 14

A project manager receives a Statement of Work (SOW) from the contracting officer describing the cybersecurity audit services to be procured. What does the SOW primarily define?

- A) The financial terms and payment schedule of the contract
- B) The work to be performed, deliverables expected, performance standards, and acceptance criteria
- C) The vendor selection criteria and weighting used to evaluate proposals
- D) The legal liability and indemnification clauses binding both parties

**Correct Answer:** B) The work to be performed, deliverables expected, performance standards, and acceptance criteria.

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why B is correct:* A Statement of Work is the technical document that defines the scope of the contracted work: what must be done, what must be delivered, when deliverables are due, and what standards must be met for acceptance. It is the foundation of the contract's scope.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Financial terms and payment schedules are in the contract terms and conditions or a separate payment schedule, not the SOW.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Vendor selection criteria and weighting are documented in the source selection plan or RFP evaluation criteria, not the SOW.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Legal liability and indemnification are contract legal clauses, typically in the terms and conditions section, not the SOW.

## Question 15

The Conduct Procurements process occurs in which PMI Process Group?

- A) Planning
- B) Initiating
- C) Executing
- D) Monitoring and Controlling

**Correct Answer:** C) Executing

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why C is correct:* Conduct Procurements is the Executing process where the project team advertises the procurement, holds bidder conferences, receives proposals, evaluates vendors, and awards contracts. It is the active work of finding and selecting a seller.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Planning contains Plan Procurement Management — the process of deciding what to procure, how to procure it, and developing the SOW. Conducting the actual procurement happens in Executing.
- *Why B is incorrect:* Initiating identifies the project and key stakeholders. Procurement planning and execution begin later.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Monitoring and Controlling contains Control Procurements — the process of managing an active contract relationship. Selecting and awarding the contract is Executing.

## Question 16

A vendor consistently delivers IT consulting services that exceed the contracted quality level, but their final invoices are always 15% above the agreed rates. How should the project manager handle this situation?

- A) Accept the overages because the quality is exceptional and worth the additional cost.
- B) Apply the contract terms: pay only the agreed rates, document the billing discrepancies, and formally notify the vendor of the contractual non-compliance.
- C) Terminate the vendor contract immediately for breach of payment terms.
- D) Renegotiate the contract to match what the vendor is actually billing.

**Correct Answer:** B) Apply the contract terms: pay only the agreed rates, document the billing discrepancies, and formally notify the vendor of the contractual non-compliance.

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why B is correct:* Contract terms are legally binding. The PM must enforce the agreed rates, document the discrepancy, and formally notify the vendor. Paying above contract rates without authorization sets a precedent and creates budget variance without approved change control.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Accepting unauthorized cost overages because quality is good violates budget governance and the contract. Quality performance does not retroactively authorize rate increases.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Termination for cause requires a material breach. Billing overages are a contractual non-compliance issue that should be formally addressed through the dispute resolution process before termination is considered.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Renegotiating to match unauthorized billing rewards non-compliant behavior and sets a negative precedent. If rate adjustments are warranted, they should be initiated through a formal contract modification, not in response to non-compliant invoicing.

## Question 17

Which solicitation document is BEST suited for procuring a commodity item where all specifications are fully defined and the award will go to the lowest compliant bidder?

- A) Request for Proposal (RFP)
- B) Request for Information (RFI)
- C) Request for Quotation (RFQ)
- D) Invitation for Bid (IFB)

**Correct Answer:** D) Invitation for Bid (IFB)

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why D is correct:* An IFB is used for formally defined procurements where the award is made solely on price. It is common in government contracting for commodity purchases. The lowest compliant bid wins — no technical evaluation is conducted.
- *Why A is incorrect:* An RFP invites vendors to propose both a technical approach and a price. It is used when the buyer wants innovative solutions or needs to evaluate methodology — not just price.
- *Why B is incorrect:* An RFI is a market research tool used before a formal solicitation to understand vendor capabilities. No contract award results from an RFI.
- *Why C is incorrect:* An RFQ requests price quotes on a defined item. While similar to an IFB, RFQs are less formal and do not always result in a binding contract award. For formal government-style lowest-bid procurement, IFB is the standard term.

## Question 18

The project team decides to build a custom reporting module in-house rather than purchasing a third-party reporting tool. This decision is the result of which procurement management activity?

- A) Source selection
- B) Make-or-buy analysis
- C) Contract closure
- D) Procurement audit

**Correct Answer:** B) Make-or-buy analysis

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why B is correct:* Make-or-buy analysis is the decision-making process for determining whether a product or service should be developed internally (make) or obtained from an external vendor (buy). Building the module in-house is the "make" outcome of this analysis.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Source selection is the process of evaluating and choosing among competing vendors — it occurs after the "buy" decision has already been made.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Contract closure is a Closing Process Group activity performed at the end of a procurement — it has nothing to do with the initial make-or-buy decision.
- *Why D is incorrect:* A procurement audit reviews the procurement process for effectiveness and compliance. It does not drive the initial build-vs-buy decision.

## Question 19

What is the key difference between "termination for convenience" and "termination for cause" in a procurement contract?

- A) Termination for convenience requires court approval; termination for cause does not.
- B) Termination for convenience allows the buyer to end the contract at will with payment for work completed; termination for cause occurs when the seller has breached contract terms and may result in remedies against the seller.
- C) Termination for cause is initiated by the seller; termination for convenience is initiated by the buyer.
- D) Both termination types result in the same payment obligations and legal remedies.

**Correct Answer:** B) Termination for convenience allows the buyer to end the contract at will with payment for work completed; termination for cause occurs when the seller has breached contract terms and may result in remedies against the seller.

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why B is correct:* These are the two standard contract termination types. Convenience termination protects the buyer's right to cancel when business needs change — the seller is compensated for work done. Cause termination responds to seller breach and may include financial penalties, liquidated damages, or other remedies.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Neither termination type routinely requires court approval. Both are contract rights exercised by the parties themselves. Disputes may escalate to court, but the termination itself does not require prior judicial authorization.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Either party may initiate termination depending on contract terms. Termination for cause is typically buyer-initiated when the seller breaches — not seller-initiated.
- *Why D is incorrect:* The two types have different payment and legal remedy implications. Convenience termination pays the seller for work completed; cause termination may reduce or deny payment and allow the buyer to pursue damages.

## Question 20

What must the project manager complete before the overall project can be formally closed when the project included multiple vendor contracts?

- A) The project can be closed as long as the final deliverable is accepted by the sponsor, regardless of contract status.
- B) Each procurement must be formally closed — all deliverables accepted, final payments made, and contract documentation archived — before overall project closure.
- C) Vendor contracts automatically close when the project charter expires.
- D) Contract closure only applies to Fixed Price contracts; cost-reimbursable contracts close automatically.

**Correct Answer:** B) Each procurement must be formally closed — all deliverables accepted, final payments made, and contract documentation archived — before overall project closure.

**Distractor Analysis:**

- *Why B is correct:* PMI's Close Procurements process must be completed for every active procurement before the project can be closed. Open contracts represent unresolved legal and financial obligations. Procurement closure is a prerequisite to project closure.
- *Why A is incorrect:* Sponsor acceptance of the final deliverable does not close vendor contracts. Open contracts have independent legal standing and must be formally closed regardless of project deliverable acceptance.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Contracts are legal agreements that do not expire automatically when a charter ends. They must be formally closed through the contractual process.
- *Why D is incorrect:* All contract types — fixed price, cost-reimbursable, and T&M — require formal procurement closure. Contract type does not determine whether formal closure is required.
