## Scenario A: The Stakeholder Who Was Never Told

A mid-size manufacturing company is six months into an ERP implementation project. The project manager has been sending weekly status reports to the steering committee and the technical team. At month six, the warehouse operations manager — who was not included in any status reports — discovers that the new ERP will replace his department's legacy inventory system. He raises an emergency escalation, claiming the change will disrupt warehouse operations during the busiest shipping season. The change was in scope from the beginning, was approved in the project charter, and was correctly reflected in the WBS. The problem is that no one communicated this impact to the warehouse manager during planning.

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Identify which Communications Management process was inadequate and explain the specific gap that caused the problem. Was this a failure of Plan Communications Management, Manage Communications, or Monitor Communications — or some combination? Explain using the process definitions.
2. The project manager argues that the warehouse manager should have read the project charter and known about the scope. Is this a reasonable position? Apply communications management principles to evaluate this argument.
3. Looking ahead, the project manager must now communicate the ERP impact to the warehouse manager's team with limited time before the go-live. Recommend the most appropriate communication method (push, pull, or interactive) for this urgent, complex, and sensitive message and justify your choice.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario B: The Meeting That Could Have Been an Email

A technology consulting firm runs a project with 14 stakeholders. The project manager holds a two-hour status update meeting every Monday morning with all 14 stakeholders present. The meetings cover schedule progress, budget summaries, open issues, and upcoming milestone decisions. After the fifth weekly meeting, the sponsor pulls the PM aside and says that four of the stakeholders are complaining that most of the meeting content is irrelevant to their work, and that they are losing four hours of productive time per week (including preparation). Two stakeholders have started skipping the meetings entirely.

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Calculate the total number of communication channels among 14 stakeholders. Then explain how this number relates to the PM's communication management challenge in this scenario.
2. Using the push/pull/interactive communication framework, redesign the PM's communication approach. Identify which parts of the weekly meeting content belong in each communication type and explain why. You do not need to eliminate meetings entirely — but you should right-size each communication type.
3. What section of the Communications Management Plan should have prevented this situation from developing? Describe two specific provisions that section should have included for this project.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario C: The Scope Change That Was Never Written Down

The project manager of a municipal broadband infrastructure project receives a call from the Deputy Mayor, who serves as the project sponsor. The Deputy Mayor verbally directs the PM to extend the fiber network coverage area by three additional neighborhoods — representing a 20% scope increase and an estimated $400,000 cost increase. The PM is new to the role and does not want to push back against the sponsor, so she verbally agrees to extend the scope and directs the engineering team to begin planning the additional work. Three weeks later, the original scope and budget are used to evaluate the project's performance, and the project shows a significant cost and schedule variance. The finance department is now questioning the project's viability.

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. Identify every communications management error made in this scenario. What should the PM have done when the Deputy Mayor called, and why does the formality rule exist for scope changes?
2. The PM now needs to document and legitimize the scope extension after the fact. Using change control and communications management principles, describe the steps she must take to bring this change into the formal project management system.
3. What provision in the Communications Management Plan, if it had been in place and followed, would have prevented this situation? Draft one specific policy statement (2–3 sentences) that could be added to the Communications Management Plan to address verbal scope change requests.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

Communications management is where the difference between a good plan and a good project manager shows up. A Communications Management Plan can specify exactly who gets what information and when — but if the PM does not enforce it, follow up when stakeholders go quiet, or insist on written documentation for material changes, the plan is just paper. The scenarios in this module are all variations of the same failure: someone who needed information did not receive it, or someone who requested a significant change did not submit it in writing. Both failures are preventable. They require the PM to be proactive, systematic, and willing to slow down when a stakeholder pushes for a quick informal answer to a question that deserves a formal written process.
