### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 04 - Schedule Management – Gantt Charts and CPM**! Once the WBS defines *what* must be done, schedule management answers *when* it will be done. This module covers how to sequence activities, assign durations, identify the Critical Path, and visualize your schedule using Gantt charts and network diagrams. The Critical Path Method (CPM) is one of the most calculation-intensive topics on both the CompTIA Project+ and PMI CAPM exams—plan to practice the forward and backward pass calculations by hand.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Activity Sequencing**: The process of identifying and documenting relationships (dependencies) between project activities to create a logical order of execution. The primary tool for activity sequencing is the Precedence Diagramming Method (PDM), which produces a network diagram showing which activities must come before others.
*   **Dependency Types (Finish-to-Start, FS)**: The four logical relationships between activities in a network diagram: Finish-to-Start (FS, the most common—B cannot start until A finishes), Start-to-Start (SS—B cannot start until A starts), Finish-to-Finish (FF—B cannot finish until A finishes), and Start-to-Finish (SF—rarely used). Knowing all four types and being able to identify them in diagrams is essential for the exam.
*   **Gantt Chart Configurations**: A bar chart that represents the project schedule on a horizontal timeline, with activities listed vertically and their durations shown as horizontal bars. Gantt charts are the most widely used scheduling visual in practice. Dependencies can be shown as arrows between bars. Milestones appear as diamond shapes with zero duration.
*   **Lead and Lag Times**: Lead time is the amount of time a successor activity can begin *before* its predecessor has finished (an overlap, expressed as a negative number in network calculations). Lag time is a deliberate delay added between the end of a predecessor and the start of a successor (e.g., "wait 3 days after concrete is poured before adding framing"). Both are applied to dependency relationships and affect the schedule's critical path calculations.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Project+ Tip – Know all four dependency types**: The Project+ exam tests all four Precedence Diagramming Method relationships (FS, SS, FF, SF). Create a simple mnemonic or flashcard for each. SF is the rarest and hardest to remember—it means the successor cannot *finish* until the predecessor *starts*.
*   **CAPM Tip – Critical Path and Float**: Any task on the critical path has zero total float. If you delay a critical path task, the entire project finish date slips. CAPM exam questions frequently give you a network diagram and ask you to identify which path is critical or what happens if a specific task is delayed. Practice forward and backward pass calculations.
*   **Scenario Trap**: Questions about schedule compression often pair "crashing" (adding resources to critical path tasks) with "fast-tracking" (overlapping tasks). Crashing always increases cost. Fast-tracking always increases risk. Know which technique to apply in which situation.
*   **Study Resource**: For visual CPM examples and forward/backward pass walkthroughs, search [YouTube: Critical Path Method CPM Project Management Tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=critical+path+method+CPM+forward+backward+pass+project+management). Practice at least two network diagram problems before the quiz.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will perform the following steps to apply these concepts:
*   **Create an activity list with estimated durations**: Using your Module 03 WBS, derive a list of at least ten schedule activities with estimated durations in days, assigned from the bottom-level work packages.
*   **Draw a Gantt chart mapping task timelines**: Enter your activity list into a free scheduling tool (such as GanttProject, ClickUp, or a spreadsheet) and produce a Gantt chart spanning at least four weeks, showing task start and end dates.
*   **Add Finish-to-Start dependencies**: Identify at least five FS dependencies in your Gantt chart and explain in one sentence why each predecessor must finish before the successor can start.
