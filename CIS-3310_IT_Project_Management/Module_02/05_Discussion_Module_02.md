## Overview

Choose one of the three scenarios below (A, B, or C). Write an initial post responding to all three sub-questions for your chosen scenario. Then reply to at least two classmates who chose different scenarios from yours.

## Scenario A: The Skipped Charter

A regional hospital's IT department is implementing a new electronic health records (EHR) system. The VP of Operations, under pressure to meet a compliance deadline, tells the IT project manager: "We don't have time for all the paperwork — just get the team started. We'll document things as we go." Six weeks in, two departments are demanding features that contradict each other, the budget is already 15% over initial estimates, and a key vendor is waiting for formal authorization to proceed.

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Identify at least three specific problems that would have been prevented or mitigated if a proper Initiating Process Group had been completed before work began. Be specific — reference the documents that would have been created.
2. The IT project manager now wants to retroactively create a Project Charter. Is this worth doing at this stage, or is it too late? Argue your position using PMI principles.
3. How does this scenario illustrate the relationship between the Initiating and Planning Process Groups? What should have happened first, and what would Planning have added on top of it?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario B: The Lifecycle Mismatch

A logistics company is evaluating two IT projects for the upcoming fiscal year. Project 1 is replacing a 10-year-old warehouse management system with a new off-the-shelf platform — the requirements are fully documented and the vendor has a fixed implementation methodology. Project 2 is developing a proprietary predictive analytics tool for their shipping routes — the company has never built analytics software before, stakeholders disagree on what "success" looks like, and the business environment changes weekly.

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Which lifecycle model (predictive, iterative, or adaptive) is most appropriate for each project? Justify each recommendation with at least two specific attributes of the project that support your choice.
2. Even though the two projects use different lifecycle models, both must still apply the five PMI Process Groups. Choose one Process Group and explain how its activities would look different in Project 1 vs. Project 2.
3. If the logistics company assigns the same project manager to both projects simultaneously, what challenges related to Monitoring and Controlling would that PM face? How should those challenges be managed?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario C: The Phase-Gate Review

A state government agency is modernizing its tax processing system through a three-phase project: Phase 1 (Requirements and Design), Phase 2 (Development and Testing), Phase 3 (Deployment and Training). At the end of Phase 1, senior leadership must review the Phase 1 deliverables and formally approve Phase 2 before work can continue. This review is called a phase-gate.

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. Explain how the Phase 1 Closing activities connect to the Phase 2 Initiating activities. What specifically must happen at the end of Phase 1 before Phase 2 can begin?
2. The agency's CFO questions whether repeating Initiating at the start of each phase is "wasteful bureaucracy." How would you respond? Make a clear case for why Initiating activities in Phase 2 serve a different purpose than Phase 1 Initiating activities.
3. Identify two risks specific to a phase-gated government IT project that would not exist in a private-sector project using an adaptive lifecycle. Explain why each risk is unique to this scenario.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

The Process Groups are not bureaucratic overhead — they are the GPS system for your project. Skipping Initiating is like starting a road trip with no destination entered. You may drive very fast and very confidently in completely the wrong direction. As you respond this week, think about a time you were involved in a project — at work, at school, or in a volunteer context — where a Process Group was skipped. What went wrong, and how does what you learned this week explain it?
