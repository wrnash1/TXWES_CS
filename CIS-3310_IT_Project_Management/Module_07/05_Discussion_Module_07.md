## Scenario A: The RACI That Nobody Read

A newly appointed project manager at a regional healthcare system has created a detailed RACI matrix for an EHR upgrade project with twelve team members and twenty deliverables. She distributes the matrix at kickoff and references it during the meeting. Six weeks into the project, two department directors are both approving change requests for the same deliverable — each assuming they are the Accountable party. The development team is confused about which approvals to follow. The PM discovers that both directors were listed as Accountable for that deliverable, which she realizes was an error in her original matrix. Work has halted pending resolution.

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Identify the specific RACI rule violation and explain why it caused the operational problem described. What is the immediate corrective action the PM should take on the matrix, and who should be designated as the sole Accountable party — and why?
2. Beyond fixing this one error, what process should the PM implement to validate the entire RACI matrix going forward? Reference at least one RACI design rule beyond the Accountable rule in your answer.
3. The PM is considering whether to assign herself as Accountable for several additional deliverables to prevent future ambiguity. Is this an appropriate use of the Accountable designation for a project manager? What organizational considerations should guide that decision?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario B: The Team That Skipped Storming

A software development company prides itself on its collaborative culture. When a new project team was formed for a major platform migration, the project manager worked hard during kickoff to minimize tension — she intervened immediately any time disagreement arose, redirected all technical debates to offline conversations, and kept every standup meeting positive and on-script. At Week 10, the team appeared to be in Norming: meetings ran smoothly, there was no visible conflict, and everyone seemed to get along well. At Week 12, the team entered the final development sprint and experienced a sudden, severe breakdown. Two senior developers revealed they had fundamentally different assumptions about the architecture. The rework required extended the project by four weeks.

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Using Tuckman's model, diagnose what actually happened to this team. What stage did the PM inadvertently prevent the team from completing, and what was the consequence of skipping it?
2. What specific PM actions during Storming would have been more appropriate than suppressing the conflict? Reference the correct PM leadership style for that stage.
3. Looking ahead to the remaining development work, what actions should the PM take now to help the team complete the stage they skipped and reach a genuine Performing stage?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario C: The Functional Manager Problem

Marcus is a project manager leading a data analytics platform project at a large financial services firm with a functional organizational structure. Three developers assigned to his project report to a functional IT manager named Rachel, who manages their performance reviews, promotions, and schedules. During Week 6, Rachel pulls all three developers off Marcus's project for two weeks to support a department priority — without informing Marcus in advance. When Marcus raises the issue, Rachel says the developers' primary obligation is to her department. Marcus has no formal authority to override Rachel's decision.

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. What organizational structure best describes Marcus's situation, and what does this structure mean for his authority over project resources? How should this have been anticipated during project planning?
2. Which section of the Project Management Plan should have defined the process for handling resource conflicts between the functional manager and the project? Describe two specific provisions that plan should have included.
3. Marcus must now address the immediate resource gap. Using PMI conflict resolution principles, recommend the most appropriate technique Marcus should use to resolve this situation with Rachel, and explain why it is preferable to the alternatives.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

Resource management is the discipline where planning meets reality. You can build a perfect RACI matrix and then discover nobody read it. You can create a team development plan and then discover you unintentionally skipped Storming — and paid for it in Week 12. You can negotiate resource agreements in your Project Management Plan and still have a functional manager reprioritize your team without notice. The tools and frameworks in this module — RACI, Tuckman's stages, conflict resolution techniques — are not guarantees. They are instruments for having the right conversations early, before the crises hit. The PM who understands organizational structures, names accountability explicitly, and lets teams work through productive conflict is far more likely to deliver than the one who avoids those conversations and discovers the gaps at the worst possible moment.
