**Question 1**
What is the main focus of Quality Assurance (QA) compared to Quality Control (QC)?
*   A) QA focuses on preventing defects in processes, while QC focuses on identifying defects in final products
*   B) QA runs automated unit tests; QC manages project budgets
*   C) QC manages project budgets; QA is only performed by external auditors
*   D) QA is done only by project managers; QC is done only by developers
*   **Correct Answer:** A) QA is process-oriented and proactive (preventing defects through process improvements); QC is product-oriented and reactive (inspecting deliverables to find defects).
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is correct:* The QA vs. QC distinction is one of the most tested quality concepts. QA audits processes to prevent defects from occurring. QC inspects outputs to detect defects after they have been produced.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* Running automated unit tests is a QC activity (testing a product), not QA; and QC has nothing to do with budget management.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* QC is product inspection, not budget management. QA can be performed by internal team members through process audits, not only external auditors.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Both QA and QC can be performed by different roles depending on the organization. Neither activity is restricted to a single role type.

**Question 2**
Which of the following best defines a **Check Sheet** in quality management?
*   A) A simple data collection form used to record the frequency of defects, errors, or observations in real time during quality control activities.
*   B) A hierarchical diagram that shows all the potential causes of a quality defect organized by major categories.
*   C) A bar chart that ranks defect categories from most to least frequent to help teams focus on the highest-impact issues.
*   D) A formal document that lists all quality standards applicable to the project and how the team will meet them.
*   **Correct Answer:** A) A check sheet is a simple, structured data collection form used to record how often specific defects or events occur as observations are made.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is correct:* Check sheets enable real-time data collection during QC activities. The tally marks or checkboxes are later analyzed using other quality tools such as histograms or Pareto charts.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* A hierarchical diagram showing categories of potential defect causes describes an Ishikawa (Cause-and-Effect or Fishbone) Diagram, not a check sheet.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* A bar chart ranking defect categories by frequency describes a Pareto Chart, a different quality tool.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* A document listing quality standards and compliance approaches describes the Quality Management Plan, not a check sheet.

**Question 3**
A software project team conducts a mid-project review of their development process to verify that all coding practices comply with the organization's security standards. What type of quality activity is this?
*   A) Quality Control (QC) – because they are reviewing the code itself
*   B) Quality Assurance (QA) – because they are auditing the process to ensure it meets defined standards
*   C) Scope Validation – because they are confirming deliverables are complete
*   D) Risk Monitoring – because they are checking for security vulnerabilities
*   **Correct Answer:** B) This is a Quality Assurance (QA) activity—auditing the development process to verify it conforms to defined standards is proactive, process-focused quality work.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why B is correct:* Process audits against defined standards are the hallmark of QA. The team is not inspecting a specific code deliverable for defects; they are checking whether the team's process itself is compliant.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* QC inspects finished products (e.g., running tests on completed code modules to find bugs), not the process used to build them.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Scope Validation is the process of formally accepting completed deliverables with the customer; it is different from an internal process audit.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Risk Monitoring tracks identified risks and watches for new ones; a process compliance review is a quality activity, not a risk response activity.

**Question 4**
A project manager is using a Pareto Chart to analyze help desk ticket categories after a system deployment. The chart shows that password reset (45%), access permission errors (30%), and printer issues (15%) together account for 90% of all tickets. What action does the Pareto analysis suggest?
*   A) Address all ticket categories equally to ensure comprehensive coverage
*   B) Focus first on resolving the password reset and access permission processes, as they account for 75% of the problem
*   C) Ignore the minor categories entirely, as they are too small to matter
*   D) Replace the help desk team, since a high ticket volume indicates poor performance
*   **Correct Answer:** B) Pareto analysis directs the team to focus on the "vital few" causes—here, password resets and access permissions together cause 75% of tickets, so fixing those processes delivers the most impact.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why B is correct:* The 80/20 rule underlying Pareto analysis says that roughly 80% of effects come from 20% of causes. Focusing resources on the highest-frequency categories delivers disproportionate improvement.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Spreading effort equally across all categories ignores the Pareto principle; it wastes resources on low-impact issues while high-impact ones persist.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Minor categories should not be completely ignored—they may still affect users and could grow over time. The Pareto principle prioritizes, not eliminates, lower-frequency items.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* High ticket volume after deployment may reflect a configuration issue or training gap, not necessarily poor help desk performance. Replacing staff is not a quality management response.

**Question 5**
Tuckman's model describes the stages of team development. In which stage do team members begin to challenge each other's ideas, resulting in conflict and competition for influence?
*   A) Forming
*   B) Storming
*   C) Norming
*   D) Performing
*   **Correct Answer:** B) Storming is the stage where team members begin to push against established boundaries, experience conflict, and compete for influence and roles within the team.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why B is correct:* Storming is a natural and expected phase in team development. The PM's role is to guide the team through conflict productively, establishing ground rules and encouraging open communication.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Forming is the initial stage where team members are polite, cautious, and getting to know each other; conflict has not yet emerged.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Norming occurs after storming—the team has resolved conflicts, established working norms, and is beginning to collaborate effectively.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Performing is the high-productivity stage where the team operates smoothly and independently; conflict has been resolved and the focus is on delivering results.
