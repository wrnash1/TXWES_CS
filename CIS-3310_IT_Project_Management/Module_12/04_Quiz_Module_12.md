**Question 1**
How does scope change management differ in Agile compared to traditional Waterfall project management?
*   A) Agile permits changes at any time by prioritizing the backlog, while Waterfall uses strict Change Control Boards to evaluate and approve scope modifications
*   B) Agile does not allow any changes once a sprint has started; changes require a formal change request
*   C) Waterfall updates requirements dynamically after each sprint based on customer feedback
*   D) Agile requires more documentation for changes than Waterfall because each change must be added to the backlog with a written description
*   **Correct Answer:** A) Agile welcomes change by re-evaluating and re-prioritizing the Product Backlog before each sprint; Waterfall manages change through formal change control boards and baseline adjustments.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is correct:* The Agile Manifesto values "responding to change over following a plan." Changes are welcomed and accommodated by adjusting backlog priorities. Waterfall freezes scope early and requires formal change control to revise it.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* While scope should remain stable within a sprint (to protect sprint goals), changes can be added to the Product Backlog at any time and will be addressed in future sprints. This is an Agile strength, not a restriction.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Waterfall does not update requirements dynamically after sprints—Waterfall does not use sprints at all. Sprints are an Agile/Scrum concept.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Agile actually requires less formal documentation for changes. A new user story added to the Product Backlog is far simpler than a Waterfall Change Request Form with full impact analysis and CCB approval.

**Question 2**
Which of the following best defines **Adaptive Planning** in Agile project management?
*   A) A planning approach in which the project plan is expected to evolve continuously in response to new information, stakeholder feedback, and changing requirements throughout the project.
*   B) A planning technique where the project manager creates a detailed fixed plan at the start of the project that all team members must follow without modification.
*   C) A risk management strategy that adjusts the project schedule automatically when critical path tasks are delayed.
*   D) A budgeting method that allocates funds to sprints based on the number of story points completed in the previous sprint.
*   **Correct Answer:** A) Adaptive planning is a core Agile principle: plans are living documents expected to change, not fixed commitments made at project initiation.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is correct:* Adaptive planning contrasts with predictive (plan-driven) planning. Agile teams plan at multiple levels—release planning, sprint planning, daily planning—and continuously update plans based on what they learn.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* Creating a detailed fixed plan followed without modification describes predictive (Waterfall) planning—the opposite of adaptive planning.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* Automatically adjusting the schedule when critical path tasks slip describes schedule compression or automated PM tool features; it is not a definition of Agile adaptive planning.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Allocating sprint budgets based on story point velocity is a real Agile budgeting concept but is much narrower than the broad definition of adaptive planning, which applies to all aspects of the project (scope, schedule, quality, team).

**Question 3**
In Scrum, who is responsible for maximizing the value of the product by managing and prioritizing the Product Backlog?
*   A) Scrum Master
*   B) Development Team
*   C) Project Sponsor
*   D) Product Owner
*   **Correct Answer:** D) The Product Owner owns and prioritizes the Product Backlog, ensuring the team always works on the highest-value items and that stakeholder needs are clearly represented in the backlog.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why D is correct:* The Product Owner is the single authority on what goes into the Product Backlog and in what priority order. They balance stakeholder needs, business value, and technical feasibility when ordering the backlog.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* The Scrum Master is a servant-leader who facilitates Scrum ceremonies, removes impediments, and coaches the team—they do not control the Product Backlog or make product decisions.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* The Development Team selects backlog items to work on during Sprint Planning and self-organizes to complete them; they do not prioritize the overall backlog.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* The Project Sponsor may fund the project and have input into product direction, but in Scrum, product decisions and backlog ownership are explicitly delegated to the Product Owner.

**Question 4**
A Scrum team completes Sprint 1 and holds a meeting to demonstrate the working product increment to stakeholders and receive feedback. What Scrum ceremony is this?
*   A) Sprint Planning
*   B) Daily Scrum
*   C) Sprint Retrospective
*   D) Sprint Review
*   **Correct Answer:** D) The Sprint Review is the ceremony at the end of each sprint where the Development Team demonstrates the completed increment to stakeholders and collects feedback to inform future backlog priorities.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why D is correct:* The Sprint Review is a working session (not a formal presentation) where the team shows what was built and discusses what to do next with the Product Owner and stakeholders. Feedback gathered here feeds into Product Backlog updates.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Sprint Planning occurs at the beginning of a sprint; the team selects items from the Product Backlog and creates the Sprint Backlog. No product demonstration occurs here.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute daily synchronization meeting where team members discuss what they completed, what they plan to do next, and any impediments. It is internal to the Development Team.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* The Sprint Retrospective is an internal team meeting focused on process improvement—what went well, what could be improved, and what actions to take next sprint. Stakeholders do not typically attend the Retrospective.

**Question 5**
A Scrum team's velocity over the last four sprints was 22, 18, 24, and 20 story points. The Product Backlog contains 120 story points of remaining work. Approximately how many sprints will it take to complete the backlog?
*   A) 4 sprints
*   B) 5 sprints
*   C) 6 sprints
*   D) 8 sprints
*   **Correct Answer:** C) Average velocity = (22 + 18 + 24 + 20) / 4 = 21 story points/sprint. 120 ÷ 21 ≈ 5.7 sprints, rounding up to approximately 6 sprints.
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why C is correct:* Velocity forecasting: calculate the average velocity, then divide the remaining backlog by the average. 84 / 4 = 21 avg; 120 / 21 ≈ 5.71, so 6 sprints are needed to complete the work.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* 4 sprints × 21 points = 84 story points completed, which does not cover the 120-point backlog.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* 5 sprints × 21 = 105 story points, which still leaves 15 story points unfinished. A sixth sprint is required.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* 8 sprints × 21 = 168 story points—significantly more than the 120-point backlog. This overestimates the required time by nearly two sprints.
