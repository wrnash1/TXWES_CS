### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 12 - Agile and Scrum Methodologies**! Agile represents a fundamentally different approach to project management than the plan-driven (Waterfall/PMBOK) methodology covered in previous modules. Rather than planning everything upfront and executing a fixed plan, Agile teams work in short iterative cycles (sprints), deliver working product incrementally, and adapt continuously to changing requirements. Understanding when Agile is most appropriate—and how it differs from traditional PM—is essential for the exam and for modern IT project work.

CompTIA Project+ PK0-005 added significant Agile content compared to previous exam versions. Expect scenario questions comparing Agile to Waterfall and asking about Scrum roles, ceremonies, and artifacts.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Agile Methodologies**: A family of iterative, incremental project management approaches guided by the Agile Manifesto (2001), which values: individuals and interactions over processes and tools; working software over comprehensive documentation; customer collaboration over contract negotiation; and responding to change over following a plan. Scrum, Kanban, SAFe, and XP are all Agile frameworks. Agile is best suited to projects with high uncertainty, evolving requirements, or frequent stakeholder feedback cycles.
*   **Sprint Cycles**: In Scrum, a sprint is a fixed-length iteration (typically 1–4 weeks) during which the development team completes a defined set of backlog items and delivers a potentially shippable product increment. Each sprint begins with Sprint Planning (selecting items from the Product Backlog) and ends with a Sprint Review (demonstrating work to stakeholders) and a Sprint Retrospective (team reflection on process improvements).
*   **Kanban Boards**: A visual workflow management tool that displays project tasks as cards moving through columns representing stages of work (e.g., To Do → In Progress → Review → Done). Kanban focuses on limiting Work in Progress (WIP) to identify and eliminate bottlenecks, maximizing flow efficiency without fixed-length sprints.
*   **Velocity Metrics**: In Agile, velocity is the average number of story points (or user stories) completed by a team per sprint. Velocity is used to forecast how many backlog items can be completed in future sprints and to plan the number of sprints needed to complete the entire product backlog. Higher velocity indicates greater throughput.
*   **Adaptive Planning**: A core Agile principle stating that project plans are expected to change and are continuously updated in response to new information, stakeholder feedback, and shifting priorities—in contrast to predictive (plan-driven) planning, where the scope, schedule, and cost are fixed at the start.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Project+ Tip – Scrum Roles, Ceremonies, Artifacts**: The Project+ exam tests Scrum-specific content. Know the three roles: Product Owner (defines and prioritizes the Product Backlog), Scrum Master (facilitates Scrum, removes impediments), and Development Team (self-organizing, cross-functional). Know the four ceremonies: Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective. Know the three artifacts: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment.
*   **CAPM Tip – Agile vs. Predictive**: CAPM tests when to use Agile vs. Waterfall. Agile is appropriate when: requirements are unclear or likely to change, customer involvement is high, rapid delivery of partial functionality is valuable, and the team is experienced with Agile. Waterfall is appropriate when: requirements are stable and well-defined, regulatory compliance requires detailed documentation, or the team lacks Agile experience.
*   **Scenario Trap**: The Daily Scrum (or Daily Stand-up) is a 15-minute team synchronization meeting—it is NOT a status report to management. The Scrum Master does not run it by asking each person to report to them; the Development Team members communicate with each other. Questions that describe a Daily Scrum incorrectly as a management status meeting are wrong.
*   **Study Resource**: For a clear introduction to Scrum with examples of sprints, backlogs, and ceremonies, search [YouTube: Scrum Agile Project Management Explained for Beginners](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=scrum+agile+project+management+explained+beginners+sprint+backlog). Many free resources include Scrum role and ceremony cheat sheets.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will perform the following steps to apply these concepts:
*   **Map Agile sprint cycles for a mobile app project**: For a provided mobile application scenario, create a Sprint Plan for two 2-week sprints, identifying the user stories to be completed in each sprint and the team capacity.
*   **Track task progress on a physical/digital Kanban board**: Using Trello (free) or a hand-drawn board, create a Kanban board with at least 10 task cards and move them through the workflow stages, demonstrating WIP limits.
*   **Verify project backlog items**: Create a Product Backlog of at least 8 user stories for your mobile app project, written in the format "As a [user type], I want [feature], so that [benefit]." Prioritize the backlog with the highest-value stories at the top.
