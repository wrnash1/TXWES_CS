## Scenario A: The Fast-Ship Decision

A startup has been developing a mobile banking application under intense investor pressure to ship by a fixed market window. The project manager, under direction from the CEO, approves the release after only partial testing — skipping the final security audit and the accessibility compliance review to save two weeks. Six weeks after launch, the app is pulled from both app stores after a security researcher publicly reveals an authentication vulnerability. The company faces regulatory fines, customer compensation costs, and a rebranding expense estimated at $800,000.

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Using the Cost of Quality framework, classify the skipped security audit as one type of quality cost and the resulting fines, compensation, and rebranding expense as another. Explain why the total Cost of Non-Conformance so dramatically exceeded the Prevention/Appraisal cost that was avoided.
2. The CEO argues that "a security audit would have cost $40,000 and only 1 in 10 apps has this kind of problem, so statistically it was the right business decision." Respond to this argument using quality management principles. What does this logic fail to account for?
3. How should the Quality Management Plan for the app's next version reflect the lessons from this incident? Specify at least two concrete QA activities and two concrete QC activities that would address the root causes.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario B: The Out-of-Control Chart

A cloud services company monitors the response time of its customer portal using a Control Chart. The Upper Control Limit is 2.2 seconds. In the past three weeks, the team observes: Week 1 (Monday–Friday): all five data points between 1.8 and 2.0 seconds. Week 2: all five data points between 1.9 and 2.1 seconds. Week 3, Day 1: 2.3 seconds (above UCL). The operations manager says: "One data point above the limit is probably just noise — let's ignore it."

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Is the operations manager correct that one point above the UCL can be ignored? Reference the appropriate Control Chart rule in your answer and explain what the correct response should be.
2. Looking at the 11 data points prior to the UCL violation, do they suggest a problem even though they were within the control limits? Explain using the Rule of Seven concept.
3. If the team decides to investigate the root cause of the rising response times, which of the Seven Basic Quality Tools would be most useful for the root cause investigation phase (after the data shows a trend) and which would be most useful for organizing the investigation team's brainstorming? Justify each choice.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario C: The QA vs. QC Argument

A healthcare IT company is deploying a new patient scheduling system. The Director of Development tells the project manager: "We don't need QA — we have a very strong QC team that tests every feature before release. Testing catches everything before it gets to the customer." The project manager disagrees and insists on maintaining both QA audits and QC testing in the project plan.

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. Who is correct, and why? Explain the difference between what QA and QC accomplish, and why having strong QC alone is insufficient for a healthcare application.
2. Give two specific examples of QA activities and two specific examples of QC activities that would be appropriate for a healthcare patient scheduling system. Explain why each is QA or QC.
3. The Director argues that QA audits slow down the development team and create overhead without directly reducing defects. How would you respond using the Cost of Quality framework? What is the financial argument for maintaining QA alongside QC?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

In IT, quality is not just about testing — and this is a lesson many organizations learn the hard way. Testing (QC) finds defects after they exist. Process discipline (QA) prevents many of them from being created in the first place. The most resilient software teams combine both: rigorous processes that reduce the error rate, and thorough testing that catches what slips through. The best quality investment is almost always prevention. Remember that when you are in your first PM role and someone asks you to skip the audit.
