### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 06 - Quality Management**! Quality management in project management is about ensuring that the project's deliverables meet the defined requirements—neither more nor less. This module covers quality planning, the difference between quality assurance and quality control, and the key tools used to measure and improve quality (such as control charts, Pareto diagrams, and checklists). Understanding the proactive vs. reactive distinction between QA and QC is essential for the exam.

On the Project+ and CAPM exams, quality questions often present a scenario and ask whether the described activity is QA (process-focused, proactive) or QC (product-focused, reactive inspection).

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Analogous vs. Parametric Estimating**: Analogous estimating uses the actual cost or duration from a similar past project as the basis for estimating the current project—it is quick and inexpensive but less precise. Parametric estimating uses a statistical relationship between historical data and project variables (e.g., cost per line of code, cost per server) to calculate estimates—more accurate than analogous but requires reliable historical data.
*   **Bottom-Up Estimation**: A highly accurate cost or schedule estimating technique that involves estimating the cost and duration of each individual work package and then rolling those estimates up through the WBS to arrive at a project total. Bottom-up estimating is time-consuming but produces the most reliable estimates because every component of work is individually assessed.
*   **Contingency Reserves**: Budget amounts (and sometimes schedule buffers) set aside to address identified risks—known unknowns—that may occur during the project. Contingency reserves are within the project manager's control and are included in the project cost baseline. They differ from management reserves, which are held by senior management for unknown unknowns.
*   **Cost Baseline**: The approved version of the time-phased project budget, used as the comparison point for measuring and reporting cost performance. The cost baseline is established at the end of the Planning process group and is a component of the performance measurement baseline. Changes to the cost baseline require formal change control approval.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Project+ Tip – Know the estimating techniques in order of accuracy**: From least to most accurate: Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM, −25% to +75%), Analogous (top-down), Parametric, Bottom-up (most accurate). The Project+ exam tests which technique is most appropriate given the information available and the required accuracy level.
*   **CAPM Tip – Contingency vs. Management Reserves**: CAPM questions distinguish between contingency reserves (in the cost baseline, under PM control, for known risks) and management reserves (outside the cost baseline, under management control, for unknown risks). If a question says the PM "needs to request additional funds from management," it is likely about management reserves.
*   **Scenario Trap**: A question may describe a project where "extra features were added at no extra cost." This sounds positive but is actually gold plating—and the correct answer is that the PM should have followed the change control process, even for beneficial changes, to protect the project baseline.
*   **Study Resource**: For a clear comparison of estimating techniques with examples, search [YouTube: Project Management Cost Estimation Techniques Analogous Parametric Bottom-Up](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=project+management+cost+estimating+analogous+parametric+bottom-up). Many Project+ prep channels include worked cost estimation examples.
