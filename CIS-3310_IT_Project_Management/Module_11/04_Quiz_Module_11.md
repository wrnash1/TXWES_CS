## Question 1

A project manager wants expert input on project risks but is concerned that one senior architect will dominate the discussion and discourage others from sharing dissenting views. Which risk identification technique is most appropriate?

- A) Brainstorming session with the full team
- B) SWOT analysis
- C) Delphi Technique
- D) Checklist analysis

**Correct Answer: C**

The Delphi Technique collects expert opinions through anonymous, iterative rounds facilitated by a coordinator. Because responses are anonymous, no single participant can dominate the discussion, which prevents groupthink and authority bias.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why C is correct:** The Delphi Technique was specifically designed to get honest, independent expert opinions. The anonymity eliminates social pressure and hierarchical influence, making it the right tool when one voice might otherwise suppress others.
- **Why A is incorrect:** Brainstorming is an open session where all participants share ideas openly. It is useful for broad idea generation but explicitly vulnerable to authority bias — senior voices naturally carry more weight in a room-based discussion.
- **Why B is incorrect:** SWOT analysis examines organizational strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats. It is a good risk identification framework but does not address the social dynamics concern about a dominant voice.
- **Why D is incorrect:** Checklist analysis reviews historical risk lists from past projects. It is passive and does not involve real-time expert interaction, so it does not address the facilitation challenge described in the question.

## Question 2

A risk has a probability of 0.70 and an impact of 0.40 on the project's cost. What is the risk score, and in which priority zone does it fall?

- A) Risk score 0.28 — Yellow (Medium) priority
- B) Risk score 1.10 — Red (High) priority
- C) Risk score 0.28 — Red (High) priority
- D) Risk score 0.07 — Green (Low) priority

**Correct Answer: C**

`Risk Score = Probability × Impact = 0.70 × 0.40 = 0.28`. A score of 0.28 falls in the Red (High) zone, which begins at 0.18 on the standard scale.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why C is correct:** The formula is `P × I = 0.70 × 0.40 = 0.28`. The Red zone threshold typically begins at 0.18 on standard scales. A score of 0.28 is well above 0.18, placing this risk in the high-priority zone requiring active response planning.
- **Why A is incorrect:** The score calculation is correct (0.28) but the zone classification is wrong. 0.28 is in the Red zone, not Yellow. Yellow typically covers the 0.05 to 0.17 range on standard scales.
- **Why B is incorrect:** 1.10 results from adding probability and impact instead of multiplying them. The P-I matrix formula always multiplies — adding produces a meaningless value that exceeds the maximum possible score of 0.81.
- **Why D is incorrect:** 0.07 would result from multiplying 0.70 by 0.10. The impact value in this question is 0.40, not 0.10. Double-check which row of the impact scale you are using.

## Question 3

The project team identifies a risk that a key hardware supplier may go out of business before delivering critical equipment. The PM decides to identify an alternate supplier and establish a parallel procurement agreement so delivery is guaranteed regardless of the primary supplier's status. Which risk response strategy is the PM using?

- A) Transfer
- B) Mitigate
- C) Avoid
- D) Accept

**Correct Answer: B**

Establishing an alternate supplier reduces the probability or impact of the supplier failure risk. The PM is not eliminating the risk or shifting financial consequences — they are taking proactive steps to reduce the risk's likelihood of causing harm. This is mitigation.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why B is correct:** Mitigation reduces the probability or impact of a threat before it materializes. A parallel procurement agreement does not eliminate the supplier failure risk but ensures the project has a backup path that reduces impact significantly.
- **Why A is incorrect:** Transfer shifts the financial consequence to a third party — for example, purchasing delivery insurance or using a fixed-price contract with liquidated damages. Setting up an alternate supplier is a direct protective action, not a financial shift.
- **Why C is incorrect:** Avoidance eliminates the risk by changing the project plan — for example, sourcing the hardware from a completely different technology that requires no single-source supplier. The PM has not removed the dependency; they have reduced its impact.
- **Why D is incorrect:** Acceptance means no action is taken (passive) or a contingency fund is set aside (active). The PM is taking specific proactive action, which defines mitigation, not acceptance.

## Question 4

After implementing a risk response strategy, the project manager discovers that the response has created a new risk: the alternate supplier has never worked with the project's integration standards and may introduce compatibility issues. What is this new risk called?

- A) Residual risk
- B) Secondary risk
- C) Trigger risk
- D) Accepted risk

**Correct Answer: B**

A secondary risk is a new risk that arises as a direct result of implementing a risk response. The compatibility concern did not exist before the alternate supplier was engaged — it was created by the response action itself.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why B is correct:** Secondary risks are a recognized output of risk response planning. They must be added to the Risk Register and managed like any primary risk. The fact that this risk was created by the PM's own response action is the defining characteristic.
- **Why A is incorrect:** Residual risk is the portion of the original risk that remains after the response has been applied. It is the same risk type — just at a reduced level. The compatibility concern is an entirely new and different risk category.
- **Why C is incorrect:** A trigger risk is not a standard PMI term. Risk triggers are early warning indicators that a risk is about to materialize, not a category of risk.
- **Why D is incorrect:** An accepted risk is one the PM has consciously decided not to actively respond to. The compatibility risk was not accepted — it was newly discovered and requires its own analysis and response planning.

## Question 5

A project team has identified a potential opportunity: if the development work is completed three weeks early, the organization could capture a competitive advantage worth $80,000 in new contracts. The PM assigns two additional senior developers to the module to ensure early delivery definitely happens. Which opportunity response strategy is this?

- A) Accept
- B) Enhance
- C) Share
- D) Exploit

**Correct Answer: D**

Exploit is the strategy that takes action to ensure an opportunity definitely occurs. By assigning resources specifically to guarantee early completion, the PM is removing the uncertainty and making the positive outcome certain.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why D is correct:** Exploitation is the aggressive pursuit of an opportunity to guarantee it occurs. Adding senior resources to make early delivery certain — not just more likely — is the defining characteristic of exploitation. The goal is certainty, not mere probability improvement.
- **Why A is incorrect:** Accept means acknowledging the opportunity but taking no specific action to pursue it. The PM is clearly taking deliberate, resource-intensive action — this is not acceptance.
- **Why B is incorrect:** Enhance increases the probability or impact of an opportunity without guaranteeing it. Adding a junior developer to "help things along" would be enhance. Adding two senior developers specifically to ensure the outcome is exploit.
- **Why C is incorrect:** Share involves partnering with another party to jointly capture an opportunity — for example, teaming with a subcontractor to jointly pursue the contract. No partnership or external party is described here.

## Question 6

A project manager sets aside $75,000 in reserve for two specific identified risks in the risk register. A separate $40,000 reserve is held by the sponsor for risks that cannot be anticipated. Which correctly identifies both reserves?

- A) Both are management reserves controlled by the sponsor
- B) The $75,000 is a management reserve; the $40,000 is a contingency reserve
- C) The $75,000 is a contingency reserve controlled by the PM; the $40,000 is a management reserve
- D) Both are contingency reserves split between the PM and sponsor

**Correct Answer: C**

Contingency reserves are set aside for identified risks (known unknowns) and are controlled by the project manager. Management reserves cover unidentified risks (unknown unknowns) and require higher authorization — typically sponsor or executive approval — to access.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why C is correct:** The $75,000 covers two specific, identified risks from the register — this is a contingency reserve. The $40,000 held by the sponsor for unidentifiable risks is a management reserve. The control authority distinction (PM vs. sponsor) is a key exam test point.
- **Why A is incorrect:** The $75,000 is specifically tied to identified register risks and is controlled by the PM — the definition of a contingency reserve. Management reserves cover unknowns and require sponsor authorization.
- **Why B is incorrect:** This reverses the definitions. The reserve for identified risks is always the contingency reserve (PM-controlled). The reserve for unidentified risks is always the management reserve (sponsor/executive-controlled).
- **Why D is incorrect:** Splitting a single reserve type between two authority levels does not reflect PMI's reserve classification. The difference between the two reserves is what they cover (identified vs. unidentified risks) and who controls them.

## Question 7

A risk identified early in the project was classified as low priority and placed on the watchlist. Six months later, a regulatory change significantly increases both the probability and impact of that risk. What should the project manager do?

- A) Leave the risk on the watchlist since it was already reviewed
- B) Remove the risk from the register because regulatory risks are outside the PM's control
- C) Escalate the risk to the sponsor as an unmanageable organizational risk
- D) Perform a risk reassessment to update the probability, impact, and response plan for the risk

**Correct Answer: D**

Risk reassessment is the process of reviewing identified risks at intervals to update their status, probability, and impact. When new information changes a risk's profile, the register must be updated and the response plan adjusted accordingly.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why D is correct:** The Risk Register is a living document. When circumstances change — such as a regulatory update — the affected risks require reassessment. The PM must update the scores, reclassify the priority zone, and develop an active response plan if the risk has moved to medium or high priority.
- **Why A is incorrect:** The watchlist is for monitoring low-priority risks, not permanently archiving them. Leaving the risk unchanged when its profile has materially changed is a failure of risk monitoring responsibilities.
- **Why B is incorrect:** Removing a risk from the register because it is external is incorrect. External risks are among the most impactful categories. Regulatory risks in particular are standard RBS entries. Uncontrollable does not mean unmanageable.
- **Why C is incorrect:** Escalation is a response strategy for risks that are outside the PM's authority — but escalation should follow reassessment and re-scoring, not replace it. Escalating without updating the analysis skips a required process step.

## Question 8

A project team has identified a risk that the primary software vendor may not deliver a critical module on time. The PM purchases a vendor performance bond that requires the vendor to pay $100,000 if delivery is more than 30 days late. Which response strategy is the PM using?

- A) Avoid
- B) Mitigate
- C) Transfer
- D) Escalate

**Correct Answer: C**

Transfer shifts the financial consequence of a risk to a third party. A performance bond does not prevent the delay — if it occurs, the vendor pays. The risk itself still exists, but the financial impact has been transferred.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why C is correct:** Transfer does not eliminate or reduce the probability of the risk. The delivery delay could still happen. What changes is who pays for it. The performance bond contractually shifts the financial burden to the vendor — the classic definition of transfer.
- **Why A is incorrect:** Avoidance would require eliminating the dependency — for example, building the module internally or switching to a vendor with guaranteed delivery. The PM has not removed the risk of late delivery.
- **Why B is incorrect:** Mitigation would involve actions that reduce the probability of late delivery — such as adding milestone checkpoints, requiring bi-weekly progress reports, or maintaining a development shadow team. The bond does not affect probability.
- **Why D is incorrect:** Escalation passes the risk to a higher organizational authority. Purchasing a performance bond is a contractual action within the PM's authority — it does not transfer risk management responsibility upward.

## Question 9

At the 70% completion mark, a project manager discovers a significant data migration error that was not identified during risk planning. The team must immediately develop an improvised solution to prevent data loss. What is this type of response called, and what does it indicate about the risk management process?

- A) A contingency plan; indicates the risk register was properly maintained
- B) A workaround; indicates the risk was either unidentified or a known risk that materialized unexpectedly
- C) A risk audit; indicates the PM is conducting a scheduled review
- D) A fallback plan; indicates the primary response strategy has already been attempted

**Correct Answer: B**

A workaround is an unplanned response to a risk event that was either not identified in the risk register or was not anticipated to occur at this severity level. It indicates a gap in the risk identification or monitoring process.

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why B is correct:** Workarounds are improvised responses — they are reactive, not proactive. Their occurrence signals that either risk identification missed this risk entirely, or that a low-priority watchlist risk escalated faster than monitoring detected. Both indicate process gaps.
- **Why A is incorrect:** A contingency plan is pre-planned for a specific identified risk. The scenario explicitly states the risk was not identified during planning — there was no contingency plan prepared. The improvised nature of the response is the defining indicator.
- **Why C is incorrect:** A risk audit is a structured review of risk process effectiveness, typically scheduled and conducted with some formality. An improvised real-time response to an emerging crisis is not an audit.
- **Why D is incorrect:** A fallback plan is triggered when a primary contingency response is insufficient — it assumes a contingency plan existed. Since the risk was unidentified, there was no primary response to have already attempted.

## Question 10

A project's Risk Register contains 24 identified risks. The PM wants to apply quantitative analysis to determine the probability distribution of the total project cost at completion. Which technique is most appropriate?

- A) SWOT analysis
- B) Delphi Technique
- C) Monte Carlo simulation
- D) Probability and Impact Matrix

**Correct Answer: C**

Monte Carlo simulation runs the project's cost model thousands of times with randomly sampled values for each risk variable to produce a probability distribution of total project cost outcomes (e.g., an 80% confidence interval for final cost).

Distractor Analysis:

- **Why C is correct:** Monte Carlo simulation is the standard quantitative technique for generating cost and schedule probability distributions. It models the combined effect of all identified risks across thousands of iterations, producing a range of outcomes with associated confidence levels.
- **Why A is incorrect:** SWOT analysis is a qualitative risk identification technique. It examines internal and external factors to surface potential risks — it does not produce cost probability distributions.
- **Why B is incorrect:** The Delphi Technique is a qualitative risk identification method using anonymous expert input. It identifies and qualitatively assesses risks but does not perform statistical cost modeling.
- **Why D is incorrect:** The Probability and Impact Matrix is a qualitative prioritization tool. It ranks risks by P-I score to determine response priority. It does not aggregate risk impacts into a statistical probability distribution of project cost.
