## SEGMENT 2: Project+ Exam Overview (2:00–4:30)

[SHOW SLIDE: CompTIA Project+ PK0-005 Exam Structure]

The CompTIA Project+ exam, exam code PK0-005, consists of a maximum of 95 questions.
You have 90 minutes to complete it. The passing score is 710 on a scale of 100 to 900.

[PAUSE]

Questions include multiple-choice single answer, multiple-choice multiple answer,
drag-and-drop, and performance-based items. Performance-based questions appear early
in the exam and require you to apply knowledge in a simulated scenario, not just
recall a definition.

Domain 1: Project Management Concepts — 19%

## SEGMENT 3: Domain 1 Review — Project Management Concepts (4:30–7:30)

[SHOW SLIDE: Domain 1 — Project Management Concepts]

Domain 1 covers foundational concepts: project characteristics, the project lifecycle,
organizational structures, and roles and responsibilities.

[PAUSE]

Key knowledge checks for Domain 1:

A project is a temporary endeavor with a defined beginning and end, producing a unique
product, service, or result. Operations are ongoing and repetitive — they are not projects.

The project lifecycle has five process groups: Initiating, Planning, Executing,
Monitoring and Control, and Closing. These are not phases — they are groups of processes
that can occur across multiple phases.

[SHOW SLIDE: Organizational Structures Comparison]

Organizational structures affect the project manager's authority. In a functional
organization, the PM has little authority — functional managers control resources. In
a projectized organization, the PM has maximum authority — the team is dedicated to the
project. In a matrix organization, authority is shared — balanced matrix splits authority
evenly; strong matrix favors the PM; weak matrix favors the functional manager.

[PAUSE]

The Project Charter is the document that formally authorizes the project and gives the
PM authority to apply resources. No charter means no formal project authority.

The key roles: Project Sponsor provides funding and executive support. Project Manager
plans and executes. Stakeholders are affected by or can affect the project outcome.
The Project Management Office, or PMO, provides governance standards and support.

## SEGMENT 4: Domain 2 Review — Project Planning (7:30–12:00)

[SHOW SLIDE: Domain 2 — Project Planning — The Heaviest Domain]

Domain 2 is worth 28% of the exam. It covers the work breakdown structure, scheduling,
budgeting, risk planning, quality planning, communication planning, and resource planning.

[PAUSE]

WBS key facts: The WBS decomposes project scope into work packages. It is deliverable-
oriented, not activity-oriented. The WBS dictionary defines each element. The lowest
level of the WBS is the work package. A WBS does not show sequence or dependencies —
that is the network diagram's job.

[SHOW SLIDE: Critical Path Method Review]

Scheduling key facts: The network diagram shows activity sequence and dependencies.
Four dependency types: Finish-to-Start (FS), Start-to-Start (SS), Finish-to-Finish (FF),
Start-to-Finish (SF). FS is the default.

The critical path is the longest path through the network — it determines the earliest
project completion date. Activities on the critical path have zero float. Float, also
called slack, is how long an activity can be delayed without delaying the project end date.

[PAUSE]

Three-point estimating uses Optimistic, Most Likely, and Pessimistic values.
PERT formula: (O + 4M + P) divided by 6.
Triangular average: (O + M + P) divided by 3.

[SHOW SLIDE: Earned Value Management Reference]

Earned Value key formulas — these are heavily tested:

SV = EV minus PV. Positive SV means ahead of schedule.
CV = EV minus AC. Positive CV means under budget.
SPI = EV divided by PV. Greater than 1.0 means ahead of schedule.
CPI = EV divided by AC. Greater than 1.0 means under budget.
EAC = BAC divided by CPI. Estimate at Completion.
ETC = EAC minus AC. Estimate to Complete.
VAC = BAC minus EAC. Variance at Completion.

[PAUSE]

Risk planning key facts: Risk identification produces the risk register. Qualitative
risk analysis uses probability-impact matrix. Quantitative risk analysis uses numerical
methods like Monte Carlo simulation. Risk responses for threats: avoid, transfer,
mitigate, accept. Risk responses for opportunities: exploit, share, enhance, accept.

## SEGMENT 5: Domain 3 Review — Project Execution and Delivery (12:00–15:30)

[SHOW SLIDE: Domain 3 — Project Execution and Delivery]

Domain 3 covers how work gets done: team management, quality management, procurement,
vendor management, and Agile execution.

[PAUSE]

Team management key facts: Tuckman's stages are Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing,
and Adjourning. Conflict resolution methods in priority order for most situations:
Collaborate/Problem Solve first, then Compromise, then Accommodate, then Avoid. Force
is a last resort. Withdraw is the same as Avoid.

Motivation theories: Maslow's hierarchy goes from physiological needs at the base to
self-actualization at the top. Herzberg's two-factor theory distinguishes hygiene factors
(eliminate dissatisfaction) from motivators (drive satisfaction). McGregor's Theory X
assumes workers need control; Theory Y assumes workers are self-motivated.

[SHOW SLIDE: Quality Management Key Concepts]

Quality management: Quality planning defines standards. Quality assurance evaluates
whether processes are being followed — it is proactive. Quality control inspects outputs
against standards — it is reactive. Cost of Quality includes prevention costs, appraisal
costs, and failure costs (internal and external). Prevention is always cheaper than failure.

[PAUSE]

Procurement key facts: Contract types — FFP puts risk on seller, CPFF puts risk on buyer,
T&M shares risk. RFI is for market research. RFQ is for pricing. RFP is for complex
proposals. The SOW defines what the vendor must deliver.

Agile execution key facts: Scrum has three roles, five events, three artifacts. Velocity
is the average story points per sprint. Definition of Done is the quality gate for
every increment. Kanban uses WIP limits and continuous flow.

## SEGMENT 6: Domain 4 Review — Change, Control, and Communication (15:30–18:30)

[SHOW SLIDE: Domain 4 — Change, Control, and Communication]

Domain 4 covers integrated change control, performance reporting, and stakeholder
communication.

[PAUSE]

Change control key facts: All project changes must go through the integrated change
control process. A change request is submitted, assessed for impact on scope, schedule,
cost, quality, and risk, reviewed by the Change Control Board, and either approved,
deferred, or rejected. Approved changes update the project management plan. No verbal
change authorizations.

The Change Control Board, or CCB, is the group with authority to approve or reject
change requests. Depending on the organization, the CCB may include the PM, sponsor,
key stakeholders, and subject matter experts.

[SHOW SLIDE: Communication Planning and Methods]

Communication planning: The communication management plan defines who needs what
information, when, in what format, and through what channel. The number of communication
channels in a project is calculated as N times (N minus 1) divided by 2, where N is
the number of stakeholders.

[PAUSE]

A project with 10 stakeholders has 10 times 9 divided by 2 = 45 communication channels.
This formula is tested on every Project+ exam administration. Know it cold.

Reporting: Status reports communicate current state. Progress reports show cumulative
performance. Forecast reports project future performance based on current trends.

## SEGMENT 7: Domain 5 Review — Project Closing (18:30–20:30)

[SHOW SLIDE: Domain 5 — Project Closing]

Domain 5 covers administrative closure, procurement closure, lessons learned, and
transition and knowledge transfer.

[PAUSE]

Administrative closure key facts: Formal acceptance from the customer or sponsor
officially ends the project. The project manager must obtain written acceptance — verbal
acceptance is not sufficient for closure documentation.

Lessons learned: Captured throughout the project, not just at the end. Stored in the
organizational process assets or lessons learned repository. Used to improve future
projects. Also called retrospectives in Agile environments.

[SHOW SLIDE: Project Closure Checklist]

Procurement closure: Each contract must be formally closed separately from project
administrative closure. Steps: verify all deliverables accepted, authorize final payment,
archive records, document vendor performance.

[PAUSE]

Transition and knowledge transfer: Before a project is closed, operational teams must
receive training, documentation, and support arrangements for the deliverable. Handing
off a system without training and documentation is an incomplete closure.

## SEGMENT 8: Exam Strategy and Final Tips (20:30–22:30)

[SHOW SLIDE: Exam Strategy — How to Approach Project+ Questions]

Here are my top exam strategy tips, developed from working with hundreds of students
who have passed Project+.

[PAUSE]

First, read every answer choice before selecting one. Project+ questions are designed
to have two plausible answers. The difference is usually in the nuance.

Second, eliminate obviously wrong answers first. On a four-choice question, you can
usually eliminate two quickly. Then apply your knowledge to the remaining two.

Third, trust the process. When a scenario asks what the PM should do, the answer that
follows the correct process almost always wins over the answer that seems practical
but skips a step.

[SHOW SLIDE: Common Exam Traps]

Fourth, watch for "first," "next," and "best" in question stems. These words change
the correct answer. "What is the first thing the PM should do?" is asking about process
sequence. "What is the best response?" is asking about judgment.

Fifth, for change control questions, the answer is almost always "go through the change
control process." Vendors asking for more money, stakeholders requesting scope additions,
team members discovering risks — all of these go through change control.

[PAUSE]

Sixth, for communication questions: more stakeholders equals more complexity. When
a new stakeholder is added, update the communication management plan.
