## Overview

## Scenario A: The Misleading Progress Report

A software development project at a financial services firm has a BAC of $600,000 and is in Month 8 of a 12-month schedule. The development manager submits a status report stating that the team is "on time and on budget" because actual spending ($300,000) equals exactly 50% of the total budget. The project manager reviews the report and realizes the team has only completed 40% of the planned work, even though 50% of the budget has been spent.

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Calculate PV, EV, AC, CV, SV, CPI, and SPI for this project using the data provided. Show all formulas and values.
2. Is the development manager's claim that the project is "on time and on budget" accurate? Explain specifically where the claim is wrong, using the EVM metrics you calculated.
3. The project manager must present the corrected status to the project sponsor. What EAC would the PM forecast using the CPI-based formula? What does this forecast mean for the project budget? What corrective action options should the PM recommend?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions. Show calculations clearly.

## Scenario B: The Reserve Dispute

A university is replacing its library management system. The project budget is $350,000. The project manager has documented 8 risks in the risk register, for which $35,000 in contingency reserve has been allocated. The provost's office holds an additional $25,000 for completely unexpected situations. Midway through the project, two documented risks materialize simultaneously: a vendor delay that costs $12,000 and a data migration error that costs $9,000. The project manager requests $21,000 from the provost's office. The provost's office replies: "Use your own reserve — that is what it is there for."

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Who is correct in this dispute — the project manager or the provost's office? Explain your reasoning using the correct PMI terminology for each type of reserve.
2. After using the contingency reserve for the two materialized risks, how much contingency reserve remains? If a third documented risk were to materialize costing $20,000, what would the PM's options be?
3. A team member suggests that the PM should have set aside more contingency reserve at the start of the project to avoid running out. How is the size of the contingency reserve determined during Cost Management planning? What inputs drive that decision?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario C: The Sinking Infrastructure Project

A city government is upgrading its emergency dispatch system. The BAC is $1,200,000. At the Month 10 status date, the project reports: EV = $480,000, PV = $600,000, AC = $560,000. The project has 14 months remaining on its original 24-month schedule.

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. Calculate CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC, ETC, and VAC. Show all formulas and values. Based on your calculations, write a two-sentence plain-language project status summary suitable for a city council briefing.
2. The project director proposes crashing several critical path activities to recover the schedule delay, at an estimated additional cost of $80,000. Based on the current financial performance metrics, is adding $80,000 in cost a responsible recommendation? Explain your reasoning using the VAC and CPI results.
3. If the project sponsor approves the additional $80,000 as a change to the project budget, does this money come from the contingency reserve, the management reserve, or require a formal change request and baseline update? Explain the correct process.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions. Show calculations clearly.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

EVM is one of those topics that seems intimidating until you realize it is just arithmetic with meaning attached. CPI of 0.80 means you are getting eighty cents of work for every dollar you spend. That is not abstract — that is a real problem with a real dollar impact. Once you internalize what each metric means, the formulas become natural. Practice the calculations until you can set up any EVM problem in under 60 seconds. That speed will serve you both on the Project+ exam and in your career.
