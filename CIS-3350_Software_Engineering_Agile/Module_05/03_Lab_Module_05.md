# Lab Activity: Module 05 – Sprint Review and Retrospective

**Course:** CIS-3350 Software Engineering and Agile
**Certification Alignment:** PSM I (Professional Scrum Master I) – Scrum.org
**Instructor:** Professor Nash | Texas Wesleyan University
**Points:** 100

## Overview

This lab simulates a Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective for the CampusConnect mobile app team introduced in Module 04. You will write a Sprint Review agenda and stakeholder feedback summary, then facilitate a written Sprint Retrospective using a standard format and produce a formal improvement plan. No programming is required.

Estimated time: 90–120 minutes

---

## Sprint Context

The CampusConnect team has just completed Sprint 3. Here is what happened:

Completed and meeting the Definition of Done:

- PBI-01: Students can create a study group with name, subject, and meeting time (8 pts)
- PBI-02: Students can invite others to a study group by username or email (5 pts)
- PBI-03: Students can view campus events on a calendar view (8 pts)
- PBI-04: Students can RSVP to a campus event (3 pts)

Not completed (did not meet DoD):

- PBI-06: Search for study groups by course name (5 pts) — 70% complete; search indexing not finished

Total: 24 of 29 committed story points delivered as Done Increments.

Sprint events and issues that occurred:

- The PBI-03 calendar library licensing issue from Module 04 was resolved on Day 8 by switching to an open-source alternative, but this caused a two-day delay
- PBI-06 was started late because two Developers were both pulled into PBI-03's library migration
- One Developer, Alex, mentioned at a Daily Scrum that they felt the Sprint Planning stories were not detailed enough — they had to ask the Product Owner for clarification multiple times during the Sprint
- The team consistently finished the Daily Scrum in 10–12 minutes
- The Sprint Review is scheduled for today; six stakeholders (two academic advisors, one IT administrator, two students from the beta tester group, and the university's Dean of Students) will attend

## Part 1 — Sprint Review Simulation (40 points)

### Part 1 Instructions

Prepare and document the Sprint Review for Sprint 3.

Task A — Sprint Review Agenda (15 points): Write a structured agenda for the Sprint Review. Your agenda must:

- Include all recommended Sprint Review components from the Reading Guide
- Account for the Done items, the not-Done item, and the context for each
- Allocate time for the Increment demonstration (list which features will be demonstrated and in what order)
- Include time for stakeholder feedback and Product Backlog discussion
- Fit within the two-hour timebox for a two-week Sprint (show estimated time for each agenda item)

Task B — Stakeholder Feedback Summary (25 points): Write a realistic stakeholder feedback summary from the Sprint Review. Imagine the six stakeholders reacted to the demonstration and provided feedback. Your summary must include:

- At least four distinct pieces of stakeholder feedback (one per stakeholder type)
- For each piece of feedback, a statement of how it would affect the Product Backlog (new item added, existing item modified, item de-prioritized, or no change)
- One piece of feedback that raises a requirement the team had not previously considered
- One piece of feedback that simplifies a planned feature
- A brief statement of what the updated top five Product Backlog priorities are after incorporating the feedback

### Part 1 Grading (40 points)

- Task A — Sprint Review Agenda: 15 points (structure 5, Done/not-Done handling 4, timebox fit 3, stakeholder time 3)
- Task B — Stakeholder Feedback Summary: 25 points (4 feedback items 12, PB impact statements 8, new requirement 3, simplification 2)

## Part 2 — Sprint Retrospective Simulation (45 points)

### Part 2 Instructions

Conduct a written Sprint Retrospective for Sprint 3 of the CampusConnect team. Use the Start, Stop, Continue format.

Task A — Retrospective Data Collection (20 points): Write at least three items for each of the three categories below, based on the Sprint 3 events described in the scenario. Each item should be specific and actionable — not generic.

Start (things the team should begin doing):

- Item 1: [Specific practice to start]
- Item 2: [Specific practice to start]
- Item 3: [Specific practice to start]

Stop (things the team should stop doing):

- Item 1: [Specific practice to stop]
- Item 2: [Specific practice to stop]
- Item 3: [Specific practice to stop]

Continue (things that are working and should be maintained):

- Item 1: [Specific practice to continue]
- Item 2: [Specific practice to continue]
- Item 3: [Specific practice to continue]

Task B — Root Cause Analysis (10 points): Two issues from Sprint 3 are worth deeper analysis:

Issue 1: PBI-06 was not completed — the team underestimated how much cross-team dependency existed between PBI-03 and PBI-06.

Issue 2: The team had to ask the Product Owner for clarification on stories multiple times during the Sprint.

For each issue, apply the Five Whys technique. Ask "Why?" five times to trace the issue to its root cause. Present your Five Whys chain for each issue.

Task C — Improvement Plan (15 points): Based on your Start/Stop/Continue data and root cause analysis, write a formal improvement plan for Sprint 4. Your plan must include:

- Exactly two high-priority improvements (quality over quantity)
- For each improvement: a clear statement of the change, the person or role responsible for implementing it, and how you will know it worked (a measurable indicator)
- A statement of whether either improvement will be added to the Sprint Backlog as a formal work item, with justification

### Part 2 Grading (45 points)

- Task A — Start/Stop/Continue (9 items minimum): 20 points (specificity and grounding in scenario events)
- Task B — Five Whys for two issues: 10 points (5 pts each — chain quality and root cause plausibility)
- Task C — Improvement Plan: 15 points (two improvements 8, measurable indicators 4, Sprint Backlog decision 3)

## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

### Challenge 1: Retrospective Format Comparison

The CampusConnect team has been using the Start/Stop/Continue format for three Sprints. Design an alternative Retrospective for Sprint 4 using the Sailboat format (Wind = what helped us, Anchors = what slowed us, Rocks = risks ahead, Island = our goal):

1. Write at least two items for each of the four Sailboat categories, grounded in the Sprint 3 events from the lab scenario.
2. Compare the two formats (Start/Stop/Continue vs. Sailboat): identify one type of problem each format surfaces more effectively than the other.
3. Write a two-to-three sentence recommendation for which format the CampusConnect team should use in Sprint 5 and why, given the specific process issues identified so far.
4. Identify one situation where the Five Whys format would be more appropriate than either format and describe the situation in two to three sentences.

### Challenge 2: Sprint Review Stakeholder Engagement Plan

The CampusConnect Product Owner notices that stakeholders attend Sprint Reviews but rarely provide actionable feedback — they mostly nod and say "looks good." Design a stakeholder engagement plan to change this:

1. Write three specific techniques the Product Owner can use before the Sprint Review to prime stakeholders for meaningful feedback (not just observation).
2. Write two facilitation questions the Scrum Master can ask during the Increment demonstration that force stakeholders to think critically rather than passively observe.
3. Write a follow-up protocol: what should happen with stakeholder feedback in the 24 hours after the Sprint Review ends? Name the roles responsible and the artifact updated.
4. Identify one risk of too much stakeholder feedback (e.g., conflicting priorities, scope explosion) and describe how the Product Owner manages it without dismissing stakeholder input.

### Reflection Questions

1. The Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective both produce feedback that changes future behavior — one at the product level, one at the process level. In your view, which feedback loop is harder to act on in a real organization and why? What organizational structures or incentives tend to undermine each loop?
2. Psychological safety is a prerequisite for an effective Sprint Retrospective. Identify three specific Scrum Master behaviors that would increase psychological safety in a Retrospective and three behaviors that would destroy it.
