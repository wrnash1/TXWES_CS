# Reading Guide: Module 05 – Sprint Review and Retrospective

**Course:** CIS-3350 Software Engineering and Agile
**Certification Alignment:** PSM I (Professional Scrum Master I) – Scrum.org
**Instructor:** Professor Nash | Texas Wesleyan University

## Introduction

The Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective are the two events that close every Sprint and feed the empirical feedback loops that make Scrum work. They are frequently confused with each other — the Review inspects the product, the Retrospective inspects the team's process — and both are frequently misexecuted in practice. This guide covers both events in depth, including their distinct purposes, participants, outputs, common failure modes, and PSM I exam traps.

---

## 1. Sprint Review — Complete Coverage

### Sprint Review Overview

The Sprint Review is an informal working session — not a formal presentation or approval gate — in which the Scrum Team and stakeholders inspect the outcome of the Sprint and determine future adaptations. The primary output is a revised Product Backlog that reflects what was learned during the review.

### Sprint Review Participants

- Scrum Team (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers): all attend
- Stakeholders: key stakeholders invited by the Product Owner
- Others: the Scrum Guide allows "other people" to be invited to provide advice; this is at the Scrum Team's discretion

The Product Owner is responsible for inviting the right stakeholders and ensuring the Sprint Review is productive. The Scrum Master facilitates if useful.

### Sprint Review Timebox

| Sprint Length | Sprint Review Timebox |
|---|---|
| 1 week | 1 hour |
| 2 weeks | 2 hours |
| 3 weeks | 3 hours |
| 4 weeks (1 month) | 4 hours |

### Sprint Review Agenda (Typical)

1. The Product Owner explains what Product Backlog items have been Done and what has not been Done during the Sprint
2. The Developers discuss what went well during the Sprint, what problems were encountered, and how those problems were solved
3. The Developers demonstrate the Done Increment and answer stakeholder questions
4. The Product Owner discusses the Product Backlog as it stands and projects likely target dates given current velocity
5. The entire group collaborates on what to do next — stakeholder feedback is gathered and the Product Backlog is updated
6. Review of market conditions, timelines, budget, or capability that may influence the product direction

### Sprint Review Outputs

- Revised Product Backlog: updated to reflect stakeholder feedback, new insights, and changed priorities
- Stakeholder feedback formally incorporated into future planning
- A shared understanding of the Increment's current state among all participants

### What the Sprint Review Is Not

The Sprint Review is not a status meeting. The Scrum Team does not report upward to management; stakeholders are partners in a collaborative inspection.

The Sprint Review is not an approval gate. Stakeholders do not approve or reject the Increment. The Product Owner decides whether to release based on the Definition of Done and release strategy.

The Sprint Review is not the only stakeholder feedback mechanism. The Scrum Guide encourages stakeholder engagement throughout the Sprint, not only at the Sprint Review.

## 2. Sprint Retrospective — Complete Coverage

### Sprint Retrospective Overview

The Sprint Retrospective is the Scrum Team's dedicated time to inspect how they worked during the last Sprint and create a plan for at least one high-priority improvement to be enacted during the next Sprint. It focuses on people, relationships, processes, and tools — not on the product.

### Sprint Retrospective Participants

The Sprint Retrospective is for the Scrum Team: the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and all Developers. Stakeholders do not attend. This privacy is intentional — the team needs psychological safety to discuss dysfunctions and interpersonal issues honestly.

The Scrum Master participates as a team member and often facilitates. The Scrum Master also brings Scrum expertise to discussions about process improvement.

### Sprint Retrospective Timebox

| Sprint Length | Sprint Retrospective Timebox |
|---|---|
| 1 week | 45 minutes |
| 2 weeks | 90 minutes |
| 3 weeks | 2 hours 15 minutes |
| 4 weeks (1 month) | 3 hours |

### Sprint Retrospective Focus Areas

The Scrum Guide identifies five areas for retrospective inspection:

- Individuals: each team member's experience, workload, and professional growth
- Interactions: how team members communicate and collaborate
- Processes: how the team plans, executes, and reviews their work
- Tools: whether development, testing, and collaboration tools are serving the team well
- Definition of Done: whether the current DoD appropriately captures quality requirements

### Sprint Retrospective Output

The primary output is a plan containing at least one high-priority process improvement the team will implement in the next Sprint. The Scrum Guide notes that the most impactful improvements may be added to the Sprint Backlog as formal work items, giving them the same visibility and accountability as product work.

### Common Retrospective Formats

Format A — Start, Stop, Continue: Team members identify practices to start (new ideas), stop (things not working), and continue (things working well). Simple, widely used.

Format B — Four Ls: Team reflects on what they Liked, Learned, Lacked, and Longed for during the Sprint.

Format C — Sailboat (or Speed Boat): Wind = what helped us move forward; Anchors = what slowed us down; Rocks = risks ahead; Island = our destination/goal.

Format D — Five Whys: The team identifies a problem and asks "Why?" five times to trace it to its root cause. Useful when the same issue keeps appearing Sprint after Sprint.

## 3. Sprint Review vs. Sprint Retrospective — Side-by-Side Comparison

| Attribute | Sprint Review | Sprint Retrospective |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | Product Increment | Team process and relationships |
| Who attends | Scrum Team + stakeholders | Scrum Team only |
| Output | Revised Product Backlog | Process improvement plan (1+ items) |
| Timebox (1-month Sprint) | 4 hours | 3 hours |
| Order in Sprint | Fourth event | Fifth (final) event |
| Manifesto Principle | Principle 7 (working software as measure) | Principle 12 (regular team reflection) |
| Empirical pillar | Inspection and Adaptation (product) | Inspection and Adaptation (process) |

## 4. The Empirical Feedback Loops

Scrum creates two overlapping feedback loops through its events:

Product feedback loop: Sprint Planning → Sprint execution → Sprint Review → updated Product Backlog → next Sprint Planning. This loop ensures the team builds the right product by continuously incorporating stakeholder feedback.

Process feedback loop: Sprint execution → Sprint Retrospective → process improvement → next Sprint execution. This loop ensures the team continuously improves how they build, reducing waste and increasing quality over time.

Both loops operate at the Sprint cadence — every one to four weeks. This is Scrum's core claim: frequent, short feedback loops in both dimensions (product and process) produce better outcomes than infrequent, long-cycle planning and review processes.

## 5. Common Failure Modes

### Sprint Review Failure Modes

Failure Mode 1: The Sprint Review as a management demo. The team presents polished slides and a scripted demo to an approving audience. Stakeholder feedback is not solicited, and the Product Backlog does not change as a result. This is a status meeting masquerading as a Sprint Review.

Failure Mode 2: Only the Developers attend. If stakeholders are not invited or do not attend, the team loses the external feedback that gives the Sprint Review its value. The team is inspecting in a vacuum.

Failure Mode 3: Items not meeting the Definition of Done are shown. Presenting unfinished work at the Sprint Review misrepresents the Increment's state and erodes stakeholder trust.

Failure Mode 4: The Sprint Review is cancelled because "we didn't finish much." This is exactly when the Sprint Review is most needed — to understand why less was accomplished and to adapt the Product Backlog accordingly.

### Sprint Retrospective Failure Modes

Failure Mode 1: The Retrospective is skipped. "We're too busy to do a Retrospective" is the team saying they are too busy to improve. This leads to compounding dysfunction.

Failure Mode 2: Retrospective outputs are never acted on. If the team identifies improvements Sprint after Sprint but never implements them, the Retrospective becomes a venting session with no improvement value. Improvements must be tracked and held to account.

Failure Mode 3: The Retrospective is dominated by management concerns. If the Scrum Master or Product Owner uses the Retrospective to push a management agenda, Developers disengage and stop contributing honestly.

Failure Mode 4: Surface-level retrospectives. "Everything went well" is rarely an honest assessment. Effective retrospectives require psychological safety, skilled facilitation, and genuine curiosity about what could be better.

## 6. PSM I Exam Tips

Tip 1: The Sprint Review inspects the product; the Sprint Retrospective inspects the process. This distinction is tested directly on PSM I.

Tip 2: The Sprint Retrospective occurs after the Sprint Review and before the next Sprint begins. The order is: Sprint Review, then Retrospective, then next Sprint Planning.

Tip 3: Stakeholders attend the Sprint Review but not the Retrospective. If an exam answer puts stakeholders in the Retrospective, it is wrong.

Tip 4: The output of the Sprint Review is a revised Product Backlog, not approval to release. The Product Owner decides on release based on the Definition of Done.

Tip 5: The Sprint Retrospective produces at least one improvement item — not zero, not "a long list." One high-priority improvement that the team commits to implementing in the next Sprint.

Tip 6: The Scrum Guide says improvement items from the Retrospective "may" be added to the Sprint Backlog. This is optional, not mandatory, but it is a best practice for accountability.

Tip 7: Skipping the Sprint Review or Retrospective is never the correct answer on PSM I. No matter how busy the team is, these events are required elements of the Scrum framework.

Tip 8: The Sprint Review is for the product Increment — only Done items are shown. Items that do not meet the Definition of Done are not part of the Increment and are not shown at the Sprint Review.
