# Reading Guide: Module 05 – Sprint Review and Retrospective

## Course: CIS-3350_Software_Engineering_Agile (Professional Scrum Master (PSM I))

---

### Introduction

Welcome to **Module 05 – Sprint Review and Retrospective**! These two events close every Sprint and are among the most misunderstood in Scrum practice — and therefore among the most tested on the PSM I. Students frequently confuse their purposes, audiences, and outputs.

The Sprint Review is about the *product* — stakeholders inspect the Increment and collaborate on what to do next. The Sprint Retrospective is about the *team* — the Scrum Team inspects its own processes and creates an improvement plan. Getting this distinction wrong is a common PSM I failure point.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary

Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

* **Sprint Review:** The fourth Scrum event, held at the end of the Sprint, where the Scrum Team presents the Increment to stakeholders and collaboratively inspects the outcome. The Product Owner and stakeholders discuss what was done and what to do next; the Product Backlog may be adjusted based on this conversation. Timeboxed to 4 hours for a one-month Sprint. The Sprint Review is *not* a demo or approval ceremony — it is a working session.

* **Sprint Retrospective:** The fifth and final Scrum event, held after the Sprint Review and before the next Sprint Planning. The Scrum Team inspects how the last Sprint went with regard to individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done. The output is at least one actionable improvement to be implemented in the next Sprint. Timeboxed to 3 hours for a one-month Sprint.

* **Increment:** A concrete stepping stone toward the Product Goal — the sum of all Product Backlog items completed during the Sprint plus all previous Increments. The Increment must meet the Definition of Done to be considered complete. Multiple Increments may be created and released within a Sprint.

* **Definition of Done (DoD):** A formal description of the state a product Increment must reach to be considered releasable. It creates transparency and a shared understanding of what "done" means. If an item does not meet the DoD it is not included in the Increment and is returned to the Product Backlog.

* **Inspection and adaptation:** Two of Scrum's three pillars of empiricism (alongside transparency). The Sprint Review inspects the product Increment and adapts the Product Backlog. The Sprint Retrospective inspects the team's processes and adapts the way of working. Both events embody the inspect-adapt cycle.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips

* **PSM I Focus — Sprint Review is not a demo:** The Scrum Guide explicitly states the Sprint Review "should not be considered a gate to releasing value" and is not simply a demo. It is a collaborative working session where the team and stakeholders together examine the Increment and adapt the Product Backlog. Questions that describe it as a formal approval meeting are describing it incorrectly.
* **Scenario Trap — Who attends each event:** Sprint Review includes stakeholders (invited by the Product Owner). Sprint Retrospective is for the Scrum Team only — stakeholders do not attend the Retrospective. This distinction appears frequently on the PSM I.
* **Sprint Retrospective output:** The Scrum Guide states the Retrospective should produce at least one improvement to be implemented in the next Sprint. Questions that describe a Retrospective with no actionable output are describing an incomplete Retrospective.
* **Order of events matters:** Sprint Review comes before Sprint Retrospective. Both come after the Sprint ends and before the next Sprint Planning. Getting this order wrong is a trap on scenario questions.
* **Study Resource:** Read the "Sprint Review" and "Sprint Retrospective" sections of [The Scrum Guide (2020)](https://scrumguides.org/). They are short but each word is exam-relevant.
