## Course: CIS-3350_Software_Engineering_Agile (Professional Scrum Master (PSM I))

### Introduction

Welcome to **Module 15 – Software Project Metrics and Velocity Tracking**! Metrics in Agile and Scrum serve one purpose: to support empirical decision-making through transparency, inspection, and adaptation. This module covers the metrics Scrum teams use to assess their health, predict delivery, and improve — and the critical distinction between metrics that serve the team versus metrics that are weaponized against it.

The PSM I regularly tests whether students understand which metrics are appropriate in Scrum, who uses them, and what happens when metrics are misused. Velocity, burndown charts, and cycle time are all valid tools; the exam tests whether you apply them correctly.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary

Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

* **Velocity:** The average number of story points (or Product Backlog items) a Scrum Team completes per Sprint over recent Sprints, used to forecast future Sprint capacity. Velocity is a team-internal planning tool — not a performance benchmark and not comparable across teams. It stabilizes after several Sprints and naturally fluctuates.

* **Sprint Burndown chart:** A graph showing remaining Sprint Backlog work (in story points or tasks) over time within the Sprint. Ideally it trends toward zero by Sprint end. Burndown charts help Developers visualize daily progress toward the Sprint Goal and identify if they are ahead of or behind the forecast pace.

* **Release burndown chart:** A higher-level chart tracking remaining Product Backlog items (or story points) Sprint by Sprint, projected toward a release target. It shows whether the team is on track to deliver a specific set of features by a given date and guides Product Backlog re-ordering decisions.

* **Cumulative Flow Diagram (CFD):** A Kanban and flow-based metric that shows the number of items in each workflow stage (Backlog, In Progress, Done) over time. Widening bands indicate work piling up in a stage — signaling a bottleneck. CFDs provide more nuanced flow visibility than a simple burndown chart.

* **Lead time vs. cycle time:** Lead time is the elapsed time from when a customer requests a feature to when it is delivered. Cycle time is the elapsed time from when active work begins on an item to when it is complete. Lead time includes queue time; cycle time does not. Both are flow metrics from Lean and Kanban.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips

* **PSM I Focus — Velocity is not a performance metric:** The most heavily tested velocity concept is that it should not be used to rank teams, compare teams, or evaluate individual Developer performance. Velocity is a planning input for the team. If management uses it as a KPI, the Scrum Master should coach against it.
* **Scenario Trap — Forcing velocity to increase:** A common trap presents management demanding that the team increase their velocity by 20% next Sprint. In Scrum, the Developers set their own Sprint capacity. Management cannot mandate velocity increases. Doing so creates pressure to inflate story point estimates — "velocity gaming."
* **Burndown charts are optional:** The Scrum Guide does not require burndown charts. They are a useful practice, not a Scrum rule. Questions that describe a team being forced to maintain a burndown chart by their Scrum Master are describing an anti-pattern.
* **Metrics serve transparency, not control:** All Scrum metrics exist to make work visible so the team can inspect and adapt. They are not surveillance tools. A Scrum Master who uses metrics to report on individual Developer productivity is misusing them.
* **Study Resource:** [The Scrum Guide (2020)](https://scrumguides.org/) does not prescribe specific metrics. Supplement with [Scrum.org's Evidence-Based Management guide](https://www.scrum.org/resources/evidence-based-management-guide) — a free framework for measuring Agile team and organizational health with four key value areas.

### Lab & Command Integration

In this week's hands-on lab, you will:

* **Calculate and chart velocity:** Given six Sprints of completed story point data, calculate average velocity, identify Sprint outliers, and produce a simple Sprint burndown chart for the most recent Sprint.
* **Build a release burndown:** Using average velocity and a Product Backlog of 180 story points, project the number of Sprints needed to complete the backlog and sketch a release burndown chart showing the projected completion trajectory.
* **Identify metrics misuse:** Review three provided organizational scenarios and identify which Agile metric is being misused in each (e.g., velocity used for team ranking, burndown used for individual surveillance), explain why the use is harmful, and state the correct application.
