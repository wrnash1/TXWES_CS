### Introduction

Welcome to **Module 14 – Scaled Agile: SAFe and LeSS Overview**! Single-team Scrum is powerful, but most enterprise software products require multiple teams working in coordination. Scaled Agile frameworks address the challenge of maintaining Agile principles — fast feedback, self-organization, continuous delivery — at the organizational level.

The PSM I tests awareness of scaling challenges and the core principles behind major frameworks. Students are not expected to be SAFe or LeSS practitioners, but they are expected to understand why scaling is needed, what problems each framework addresses, and how they preserve (or occasionally diverge from) the Scrum Guide.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary

Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

* **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe):** A comprehensive, prescriptive framework for applying Agile and Scrum across large enterprises. SAFe organizes teams into Agile Release Trains (ARTs) — groups of 50–125 people aligned to a common Program Increment (PI) planning cadence of 8–12 weeks. SAFe adds roles (Release Train Engineer, Product Manager, Business Owner) and events (PI Planning, Inspect and Adapt) beyond standard Scrum.

* **Large-Scale Scrum (LeSS):** A minimalist scaling framework that applies Scrum to multiple teams by expanding the roles and events of a single Scrum Team rather than adding new roles and processes. LeSS has one Product Owner with one Product Backlog for up to eight teams; all teams run synchronized Sprints and share a single Sprint Review and Sprint Retrospective.

* **Agile Release Train (ART):** SAFe's fundamental unit of delivery — a long-lived, self-organizing team-of-teams that delivers a continuous flow of value aligned to a common business mission. ARTs synchronize across teams using a fixed Program Increment cadence.

* **Program Increment (PI) Planning:** A SAFe ceremony where all teams in an ART gather (in person or virtually) for two days to align on the next 8–12 weeks of work, identify dependencies, set PI Objectives, and commit to a Program Board. PI Planning is analogous to a scaled Sprint Planning event.

* **Scrum of Scrums:** A coordination technique (used in LeSS and independently) where representatives from multiple Scrum teams meet briefly — typically daily or several times a week — to synchronize cross-team dependencies, share impediments, and align on shared goals. It is not a formal Scrum event but a widely used scaling practice.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips

* **PSM I Focus — Scaling does not change core Scrum:** A key PSM I principle is that adding teams should extend Scrum, not replace it. Both SAFe and LeSS preserve individual Scrum Teams with their own Sprints, Sprint Events, and Definition of Done. Questions that describe scaled teams eliminating Sprint Retrospectives or Daily Scrums are describing frameworks that have departed from Scrum.
* **Scenario Trap — SAFe and "project managers":** SAFe adds many roles not in the Scrum Guide, including the Release Train Engineer. PSM I questions that present a "Release Train Engineer" and ask what Scrum role this corresponds to — the closest analog is a Scrum Master at the program level, but it is not the same role.
* **LeSS keeps it simple:** LeSS's design philosophy is to keep Scrum intact and resist adding new roles and processes. If a question describes a scaling approach that adds the fewest new roles and artifacts while preserving the Scrum Guide's structure, it is likely describing LeSS.
* **PI Planning vs. Sprint Planning:** PI Planning operates at the program level over multiple Sprints; Sprint Planning operates within a single Sprint. They are not the same event and should not be confused in scenario questions.
* **Study Resource:** [Scaled Agile Framework overview — scaledagileframework.com](https://scaledagileframework.com/about/) and [LeSS framework overview — less.works](https://less.works/less/framework/index) both provide free introductory material. Read the one-page overview of each.
