## Question 1

Sprint Planning for a two-week Sprint is timeboxed to a maximum of:

- A) 2 hours
- B) 4 hours
- C) 6 hours
- D) 8 hours

Correct Answer: B — The Scrum Guide timeboxes Sprint Planning to eight hours maximum for a one-month Sprint. For shorter Sprints the timebox is proportionally shorter; a two-week Sprint has a four-hour maximum.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: 2 hours is the timebox for Sprint Planning for a one-week Sprint, not a two-week Sprint.
- Why C is incorrect: 6 hours corresponds to a three-week Sprint.
- Why D is incorrect: 8 hours is the maximum for a one-month (four-week) Sprint, not a two-week Sprint.

## Question 2

Which of the following is the MOST important output of Sprint Planning?

- A) A detailed task list with hour estimates for every story
- B) A Sprint Goal and Sprint Backlog
- C) An updated Product Backlog ordered by business value
- D) A project schedule showing which story will be completed on which day

Correct Answer: B — Sprint Planning's primary outputs are the Sprint Goal (the commitment for the Sprint) and the Sprint Backlog (the selected items and plan). The Sprint Goal is the single most important output because it gives the Sprint coherence and direction.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Detailed hour-level task estimates are useful but are not the primary output of Sprint Planning; the Sprint Goal takes precedence.
- Why C is incorrect: The Product Backlog is an input to Sprint Planning, not its primary output; refinement of the Product Backlog is a separate ongoing activity.
- Why D is incorrect: A day-by-day project schedule is a Waterfall artifact; Scrum does not produce task-level schedules in Sprint Planning.

## Question 3

During Sprint 3, the Developers discover that one of the selected Product Backlog items is technically more complex than estimated. They cannot complete it without jeopardizing the Sprint Goal. What should the Developers do?

- A) Work overtime to complete all committed items regardless of the Sprint Goal
- B) Cancel the Sprint because the original commitment cannot be met
- C) Collaborate with the Product Owner to negotiate scope, potentially swapping or reducing items while protecting the Sprint Goal
- D) Add the item back to the Product Backlog and remove it from the Sprint without informing the Product Owner

Correct Answer: C — The Sprint Goal is fixed, but the Sprint Backlog scope is flexible. When unexpected complexity arises, the team collaborates with the Product Owner to negotiate scope trade-offs that protect the Sprint Goal.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Working overtime to fulfill a commitment regardless of sustainability violates Agile Principle 8 (sustainable pace) and does not address the root problem.
- Why B is incorrect: Sprint cancellation is reserved for situations where the Sprint Goal itself becomes obsolete, not for individual items being more complex than estimated.
- Why D is incorrect: Removing items from the Sprint without informing the Product Owner violates transparency — one of Scrum's three empirical pillars.

## Question 4

Who determines how much work the Developers will take into a Sprint?

- A) The Product Owner, based on business priorities
- B) The Scrum Master, based on historical velocity data
- C) The Developers, based on their own assessment of their capacity and past performance
- D) A combination of the Product Owner (what) and Scrum Master (how much)

Correct Answer: C — The Scrum Guide is explicit: only the Developers can assess their own capacity and determine how much they can accomplish in a Sprint. The Product Owner orders the backlog but does not set the commitment.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Product Owner orders the Product Backlog and proposes what could create value, but cannot force a commitment on the Developers.
- Why B is incorrect: The Scrum Master may coach the team on using velocity data, but does not determine the team's Sprint commitment.
- Why D is incorrect: The "what" and "how much" distinction is not how Scrum divides this responsibility; the Developers hold the capacity assessment.

## Question 5

What does the 2020 Scrum Guide say about the three-question format for the Daily Scrum ("What did I do yesterday? What will I do today? Any blockers?")?

- A) It is the mandatory format for all Daily Scrums as defined in the Scrum Guide
- B) It is one common approach but is not required; teams may use any structure that focuses on the Sprint Goal
- C) It was removed from Scrum entirely in 2020 and should never be used
- D) It is required for teams of more than five Developers but optional for smaller teams

Correct Answer: B — The 2020 Scrum Guide removed the prescriptive three-question format. Teams may use any structure that creates focus on the Sprint Goal and allows for adaptation of the Sprint Backlog.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The 2020 revision specifically removed this as a mandatory format to allow teams more flexibility.
- Why C is incorrect: The three-question format is still a valid and widely used approach; it is not prohibited, just no longer mandated.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Guide makes no size-based distinction about Daily Scrum format.

## Question 6

A manager attends the Daily Scrum and asks each Developer to report their status directly to him. The Scrum Master observes this happening. What should the Scrum Master do?

- A) Allow it because the manager is a stakeholder and transparency requires sharing information with stakeholders
- B) Take over facilitation of the Daily Scrum to protect the team from the manager
- C) Speak with the manager after the Daily Scrum to explain that the event is for Developer synchronization and that his participation is changing its purpose
- D) Cancel the Daily Scrum and reschedule it without the manager present

Correct Answer: C — The Scrum Master serves the organization by helping people understand Scrum. The appropriate response is to coach the manager outside the event, not to disrupt the event or exclude stakeholders by decree.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Transparency in Scrum means making the work visible, not turning the Daily Scrum into a management status meeting. This behavior undermines the event's purpose.
- Why B is incorrect: Taking over the Daily Scrum removes the Developers' ownership of it and may create conflict in the moment; coaching after the event is more effective.
- Why D is incorrect: Cancelling the Daily Scrum denies the Developers their inspection event; the problem is the manager's behavior, not the event itself.

## Question 7

During Sprint Planning, the Product Owner insists the team must commit to 50 story points even though the team's velocity has been 34 points over the last three Sprints. The Developers say 50 points is not realistic. What should happen?

- A) The team should commit to 50 points to satisfy the Product Owner's business needs
- B) The Scrum Master should set the commitment at the team's historical velocity of 34 points
- C) The Developers' assessment of their capacity takes precedence; they commit to what they believe they can accomplish
- D) The Product Owner should remove lower-priority items to bring the total to 50 points of only high-value work

Correct Answer: C — The Scrum Guide is clear that only the Developers can determine how much work they can take on. Forcing a commitment beyond the team's assessed capacity leads to partially done work and Definition of Done shortcuts.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Committing to more than the team can accomplish creates a predictable failure and erodes trust over time.
- Why B is incorrect: The Scrum Master coaches and facilitates but does not set the team's commitment; velocity is a guide, not a dictate.
- Why D is incorrect: Removing items to hit a point target does not address the team's realistic capacity; the issue is the commitment number, not which items are included.

## Question 8

What is the primary difference between the Sprint Goal and the Sprint Backlog?

- A) The Sprint Goal is written by the Product Owner alone; the Sprint Backlog is written by the Scrum Master
- B) The Sprint Goal is fixed for the duration of the Sprint; the Sprint Backlog scope can be renegotiated with the Product Owner as the team learns more
- C) The Sprint Goal contains all user stories; the Sprint Backlog contains only technical tasks
- D) The Sprint Goal is optional if the team has a clear roadmap; the Sprint Backlog is always required

Correct Answer: B — The Sprint Goal is the commitment for the Sprint and does not change once set. The Sprint Backlog is the current best plan for achieving the Sprint Goal and can be adapted as Developers learn more during the Sprint.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Sprint Goal is created collaboratively by the entire Scrum Team; the Sprint Backlog belongs to the Developers, not the Scrum Master.
- Why C is incorrect: The Sprint Backlog includes both selected Product Backlog items (user stories) and the technical tasks needed to deliver them.
- Why D is incorrect: The Sprint Goal is not optional; it is a required commitment that gives the Sprint coherence according to the Scrum Guide.

## Question 9

The Daily Scrum is timeboxed to 15 minutes. A Developer raises a complex technical issue that cannot be resolved in the remaining 5 minutes. What is the best course of action?

- A) Extend the Daily Scrum until the issue is fully resolved to maintain team alignment
- B) Note the issue, end the Daily Scrum on time, and have the relevant team members hold a separate discussion immediately after
- C) Skip the Daily Scrum for the day and address the issue first
- D) The Scrum Master resolves the issue during the Daily Scrum so the team can continue

Correct Answer: B — The Daily Scrum ends at the timebox. Detailed problem-solving conversations happen in follow-on discussions ("breakouts") with only the people who need to participate. This protects everyone else's time and keeps the Daily Scrum focused.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Extending the Daily Scrum indefinitely to resolve every issue undermines its timebox and its core purpose of daily inspection and adaptation.
- Why C is incorrect: Skipping the Daily Scrum denies all Developers their daily synchronization point; one technical issue does not justify cancelling the event.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Master does not resolve technical issues; that is the Developers' domain.

## Question 10

Which of the following Sprint Goals is written in the best Scrum style?

- A) "Complete PBI-01, PBI-03, PBI-04, and PBI-07 by end of Sprint"
- B) "Allow students to create study groups and browse campus events so that the beta launch audience can begin using core social features"
- C) "Improve application performance by 20% and fix all critical bugs"
- D) "The team will work on the calendar and notifications features this Sprint"

Correct Answer: B — This Sprint Goal states a user-facing outcome, is achievable within a Sprint, allows flexibility in implementation, and can be evaluated at the Sprint Review. It explains both what will be delivered and why it matters.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Listing PBI numbers is a task list, not a Sprint Goal. It provides no meaningful direction and no user-facing outcome.
- Why C is incorrect: A 20% performance improvement is a metric target, not a Sprint Goal as described in the Scrum Guide. It also does not explain why the Sprint is valuable.
- Why D is incorrect: "Working on features" describes activity, not an outcome. It is vague, not testable, and gives no indication of what success looks like at the Sprint Review.
