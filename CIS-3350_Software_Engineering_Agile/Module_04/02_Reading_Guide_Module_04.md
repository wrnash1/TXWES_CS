### Introduction

Welcome to **Module 04 – Scrum in Practice: Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum**! This module moves from Scrum theory into its two most frequently occurring events. Sprint Planning sets the direction for the entire Sprint; the Daily Scrum keeps the team aligned every single day. Together, they account for the majority of a team's Scrum overhead and are heavily tested on the PSM I.

Understanding not just the mechanics but the *purpose* of each event is critical. The PSM I exam frequently presents scenarios where a team is running these events incorrectly — too long, wrong attendees, wrong focus — and asks you to identify the problem or prescribe the correct Scrum behavior.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary

Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

* **Sprint Planning:** The event that initiates every Sprint, where the entire Scrum Team collaborates to define the Sprint Goal and select Product Backlog items the Developers forecast they can complete. Sprint Planning addresses three topics: why the Sprint is valuable (Sprint Goal), what can be done, and how the work will be accomplished. Timeboxed to 8 hours for a one-month Sprint.

* **Sprint Goal:** The single objective the Scrum Team commits to achieving during the Sprint. It is created during Sprint Planning and provides the Developers with flexibility in how they achieve it. The Sprint Goal is the commitment associated with the Sprint Backlog and must not be changed mid-Sprint unless the Sprint is cancelled.

* **Sprint Backlog:** The Sprint Goal plus the set of Product Backlog items selected for the Sprint, plus the Developers' plan for delivering the Increment. The Sprint Backlog is owned exclusively by the Developers and is updated daily as more is learned. No one outside the Developers changes the Sprint Backlog during the Sprint.

* **Daily Scrum:** A 15-minute event held every working day of the Sprint, at the same time and place, exclusively for the Developers. Its purpose is to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as needed. The three classic questions (what did I do, what will I do, any impediments) are not required by the Scrum Guide but are a common format.

* **Impediment:** Any obstacle that prevents a Developer from making progress toward the Sprint Goal. Developers identify impediments at the Daily Scrum; the Scrum Master is accountable for helping remove organizational impediments, while Developers resolve team-level blockers themselves.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips

* **PSM I Focus — Who attends the Daily Scrum:** The Scrum Guide states the Daily Scrum is "for the Developers." The Product Owner and Scrum Master are not required attendees unless they are also doing development work. Questions frequently ask who the Daily Scrum is for — the answer is always the Developers.
* **Scenario Trap — Manager in the Daily Scrum:** If a scenario describes a manager attending and asking status questions in the Daily Scrum, this is a Scrum violation. The Scrum Master should protect the team from this interruption. The Daily Scrum is for the Developers to self-organize, not to report upward.
* **Sprint Goal is not a list of tasks:** The Sprint Goal is a single, cohesive objective — a "why." Questions sometimes present a Sprint Goal that is merely a list of features and ask if it is a valid Sprint Goal. A list of tasks is not a Sprint Goal; it lacks the unifying purpose the Scrum Guide requires.
* **Sprint Planning output:** The output of Sprint Planning is the Sprint Backlog, which includes the Sprint Goal, the selected Product Backlog items, and the Developers' plan. Questions sometimes omit the Sprint Goal as an output — that is incorrect.
* **Study Resource:** Read the "Sprint Planning" and "Daily Scrum" sections of [The Scrum Guide (2020)](https://scrumguides.org/). These two sections are short but dense with exam-testable details.
