## Overview

This lab simulates the Sprint Planning and Daily Scrum events for a hypothetical software product team. You will write a Sprint Goal, build a Sprint Backlog from a provided Product Backlog, and document a Daily Scrum exchange. No programming is required.

These simulation exercises mirror the scenario-based thinking tested on the PSM I exam.

Estimated time: 90–120 minutes

---

## Scenario Background

You are a Developer on the Scrum Team for CampusConnect — a mobile app that helps university students find study groups, share notes, and schedule campus events. The app has been in development for two Sprints. The current Product Backlog (ordered by the Product Owner) contains the items below.

Sprint length: 2 weeks. Team velocity over the last two Sprints: approximately 34 story points per Sprint. Today is the start of Sprint 3.

### Current Product Backlog (top 12 items)

| ID | Item Description | Story Points | Priority Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| PBI-01 | As a student, I can create a study group with a name, subject, and meeting time | 8 | 1 |
| PBI-02 | As a student, I can invite other students to my study group by username or email | 5 | 2 |
| PBI-03 | As a student, I can view all upcoming campus events on a calendar view | 8 | 3 |
| PBI-04 | As a student, I can RSVP to a campus event | 3 | 4 |
| PBI-05 | As a student, I can upload a class notes document to a study group | 8 | 5 |
| PBI-06 | As a student, I can search for existing study groups by course name | 5 | 6 |
| PBI-07 | As a student, I receive a push notification when a new event is added to my calendar | 5 | 7 |
| PBI-08 | As an administrator, I can create and publish campus events to all users | 8 | 8 |
| PBI-09 | As a student, I can download notes documents from my study group | 3 | 9 |
| PBI-10 | As a student, I can set my availability for study group meetings | 5 | 10 |
| PBI-11 | As a student, I can message other members of my study group | 13 | 11 |
| PBI-12 | As a student, I can rate the quality of shared notes documents | 3 | 12 |

## Part 1 — Sprint Planning Simulation (50 points)

### Part 1 Instructions

Conduct a written Sprint Planning session for Sprint 3 of the CampusConnect project. Complete the four tasks below.

Task A — Select Sprint Backlog Items (10 points): Based on the team's velocity of approximately 34 story points, select the Product Backlog items to include in Sprint 3. List each selected item by ID and story point value. Justify your selection in 2–3 sentences — why did you stop where you did? Did you stay strictly under 34 points, go slightly over, or make a different trade-off?

Task B — Write the Sprint Goal (15 points): Write a Sprint Goal for Sprint 3 that reflects the selected items. Your Sprint Goal must:

- State a meaningful user-facing outcome (not a task list)
- Be achievable within the two-week Sprint
- Be testable — you can evaluate yes or no at the Sprint Review
- Be written as a single sentence of 15–30 words

Then write 2–3 sentences explaining why your Sprint Goal is a good one using the criteria from the Reading Guide.

Task C — Sprint Backlog Task Decomposition (15 points): Choose two of your selected Product Backlog items and decompose each into tasks of one day or less. For each PBI, list at least four subtasks with estimated hours. Format:

- PBI-XX: [Item description]
  - Task 1: [description] — estimated X hours
  - Task 2: [description] — estimated X hours
  - Task 3: [description] — estimated X hours
  - Task 4: [description] — estimated X hours

Task D — Capacity Question (10 points): One Developer on the team will be out for three days during Sprint 3. The Sprint has 10 working days. Write 3–4 sentences explaining how you would adjust the Sprint's capacity planning to account for this. What would you do about the story points already committed to Sprint 3?

### Part 1 Grading (50 points)

- Task A — Item selection with justification: 10 points
- Task B — Sprint Goal quality and explanation: 15 points
- Task C — Task decomposition (2 PBIs, 4+ tasks each): 15 points
- Task D — Capacity adjustment reasoning: 10 points

## Part 2 — Daily Scrum Simulation (30 points)

### Part 2 Instructions

It is Day 6 of Sprint 3 (the midpoint). The team has the following status:

- PBI-01 (create study group, 8 pts): In progress. Backend API is complete; UI is 50% done. On track.
- PBI-02 (invite students, 5 pts): Not started yet. Developers plan to begin today.
- PBI-03 (calendar view, 8 pts): In progress. The third-party calendar library the team planned to use has a licensing issue that will take 2–3 days to resolve or require finding an alternative.
- PBI-04 (RSVP to event, 3 pts): Complete and meeting Definition of Done.

Write out the full transcript of a Daily Scrum for this team on Day 6. Your transcript must:

- Have each Developer (name them yourself — at least three) give an update
- Focus explicitly on the Sprint Goal, not just task completion
- Identify the calendar library impediment and surface it clearly
- Stay within the spirit of 15 minutes (do not write a 45-minute status meeting)
- Show the team adapting: what will they do about the PBI-03 impediment?
- Demonstrate that the Scrum Master is not running the meeting — the Developers are

Minimum length: 400 words. Format as a realistic conversation transcript.

### Part 2 Grading (30 points)

- Sprint Goal focus in updates (not just task reporting): 8 points
- Impediment surfacing and team adaptation response: 8 points
- Scrum Master not running the meeting: 5 points
- Realistic 15-minute scope (no status report to management): 5 points
- Minimum word count and transcript format: 4 points

## Part 3 — Reflection Questions (20 points)

### Part 3 Instructions

Answer both questions in 100–150 words each.

Reflection Question 1: In your Sprint Planning simulation, what was the hardest decision you made and why? What information would have helped you make a better decision, and how does Scrum's sprint cadence create opportunities to get that information?

Reflection Question 2: In the Daily Scrum transcript you wrote, the calendar library impediment was surfaced on Day 6 — the midpoint of the Sprint. What is the cost of discovering this impediment on Day 6 rather than Day 1? How does the Daily Scrum reduce the risk of late impediment discovery, and what additional practice could help surface it even earlier?

### Part 3 Grading (20 points)

- Reflection Question 1: 10 points (decision identification 3, information need 3, sprint cadence connection 4)
- Reflection Question 2: 10 points (cost of late discovery 4, Daily Scrum mechanism 3, additional practice 3)

## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

### Challenge 1: Sprint Goal Quality Audit

Review the following five Sprint Goals written by fictional teams and evaluate each one:

1. "Complete PBI-12, PBI-14, PBI-15, and PBI-18."
2. "Make the app faster."
3. "Enable campus event organizers to publish events and students to RSVP so that the spring activities fair can use CampusConnect as its official registration system."
4. "Fix all critical bugs found in Sprint 5 testing."
5. "Improve the backend so developers are happy with the codebase."

For each Sprint Goal: identify one specific flaw (or confirm it is strong), rewrite it as an improved Sprint Goal if it is flawed, and cite the specific quality criterion from the Reading Guide that it violates or satisfies.

### Challenge 2: Sprint Planning Meeting Design

Design a complete two-hour Sprint Planning agenda for a two-week Sprint for the CampusConnect team. Your agenda must:

1. Allocate time in minutes to each of the three Sprint Planning topics and justify the allocation.
2. Identify two specific inputs that should be prepared before Sprint Planning begins and who is responsible for each.
3. Write the facilitation steps the Scrum Master should take to keep the event productive if the team begins debating a technical approach that could consume the entire timebox.
4. Define what "done" looks like at the end of the Sprint Planning event — what must exist for the team to leave the room confidently?

### Reflection Questions

1. The Scrum Guide says velocity is a guide, not a guarantee. Describe a scenario where blindly following historical velocity would lead a team to make a poor Sprint Planning decision. What additional information should Developers consider beyond velocity when determining Sprint capacity?
2. The Daily Scrum is described as a Developer event, yet research consistently shows that teams whose Scrum Masters attend the Daily Scrum passively (without speaking) have better outcomes than teams whose Scrum Masters skip it entirely. Why might passive Scrum Master presence improve Daily Scrum quality even when the Scrum Master says nothing?
