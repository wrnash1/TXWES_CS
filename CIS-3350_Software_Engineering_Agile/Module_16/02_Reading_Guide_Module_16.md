## Introduction

This reading guide is a comprehensive Scrum Guide review designed specifically for the PSM I exam. It covers the 2020 Scrum Guide in full — the three accountabilities, five events, three artifacts with commitments, Scrum values, and the pillars of empiricism. It also covers the Agile Manifesto values and principles as tested on the exam. Use this guide alongside the Scrum Guide itself (free at scrum.org/resources/scrum-guide) in your final preparation.

## 2. Scrum Theory

### Empiricism

Scrum is founded on empirical process control — the belief that knowledge comes from experience and decisions should be based on what is observed. The three pillars:

| Pillar | Description | Supported By |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Transparency | Work and progress must be visible | Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Increment, burndown charts |
| Inspection | Scrum artifacts and progress must be regularly examined | Sprint Review, Daily Scrum, Sprint Retrospective |
| Adaptation | If inspection reveals deviation, the process must adjust | Retrospective actions, Sprint Backlog updates, Product Backlog refinement |

### Lean Thinking

The 2020 Scrum Guide added explicit reference to Lean: Scrum incorporates Lean thinking to reduce waste and focus on essentials.

### Scrum Values

Five values: Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage.

When people embody these values, the three pillars of empiricism come to life. Exam questions that describe violations of Scrum values (lack of openness in Retrospectives, lack of courage to raise impediments) are testing whether candidates can connect values to empiricism.

## 3. The Scrum Team

One Scrum Team: no sub-teams, no hierarchies. Small enough to remain nimble, large enough to complete significant work in a Sprint. Typically ten or fewer people.

### Product Owner

- Single person accountable for maximizing the value of the product and managing the Product Backlog
- May represent stakeholder interests but holds final prioritization authority
- Decisions must be respected by the organization — the Product Owner is not a committee
- Collaborates with Developers on Sprint Backlog refinement
- Is not accountable for how Developers accomplish their work

### Scrum Master

- Accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness
- Serves the Product Owner: helps with Product Goal, backlog management, empirical product planning
- Serves the Developers: coaches self-management, removes impediments, facilitates Scrum events when needed
- Serves the organization: coaches Scrum adoption, advocates for empirical approaches
- Is not a project manager, does not assign work, does not direct Developers

### Developers

- Create the Increment; accountable for creating a plan for the Sprint (Sprint Backlog), adherence to the Definition of Done, and adapting their plan daily
- Self-managing — no one outside tells Developers how to turn PBIs into Increments
- Cross-functional — they have all the skills needed to create a Done Increment
- No titles other than Developer regardless of the work performed
- No sub-teams within the Developers

## 4. The Five Scrum Events

### Sprint

- Container for all other events
- Fixed length: one to four weeks; consistent length throughout development
- No changes that endanger the Sprint Goal during the Sprint
- Scope may be clarified with the Product Owner as more is learned
- Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint; only when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete

### Sprint Planning

- Entire Scrum Team attends
- Addresses three topics: Why (Sprint Goal), What (PBIs selected), How (initial plan)
- Sprint Goal is created during Sprint Planning by the entire Scrum Team
- Developers determine how much they can do — Product Owner does not commit on their behalf
- Timebox: 8 hours maximum for one-month Sprint; proportionally shorter for shorter Sprints

### Daily Scrum

- 15-minute event for Developers
- Held daily at the same time and place during the Sprint
- Inspects progress toward Sprint Goal; creates plan for next 24 hours
- Not a status report to management
- Scrum Master does not run it; Developers self-organize it
- Scrum Master may attend to ensure the event occurs and stays within its timebox

### Sprint Review

- Inspects the Increment and adapts the Product Backlog
- Entire Scrum Team + invited stakeholders attend
- A working session — stakeholders interact with the Increment
- Not a presentation or approval ceremony; not a gate before the Increment can be released
- Timebox: 4 hours for one-month Sprint

### Sprint Retrospective

- Inspects the Scrum Team's processes, practices, and interactions
- Creates a plan for meaningful improvement in the next Sprint
- Scrum Team only — stakeholders do not attend
- High-impact improvements often target the Definition of Done
- Timebox: 3 hours for one-month Sprint
- Last event of every Sprint (occurs after Sprint Review, before next Sprint Planning)

## 5. The Three Artifacts and Their Commitments

### Product Backlog

- Commitment: Product Goal
- Single source of work for the Scrum Team
- Ordered by Product Owner — highest value items at top
- Refined continuously (ongoing, not a formal event; no more than 10% of Sprint capacity)
- Items near the top are more detailed; items further away are less refined

### Sprint Backlog

- Commitment: Sprint Goal
- Created during Sprint Planning; owned by Developers
- Highly visible, real-time picture of work for the Sprint
- Updated daily by Developers
- Only Developers can change it during the Sprint (scope clarification with PO is possible)

### Increment

- Commitment: Definition of Done
- Sum of all PBIs completed in the current Sprint plus all previous Increments
- Must meet the Definition of Done to be considered an Increment
- May be delivered at any point during the Sprint — does not have to wait for Sprint Review
- Multiple Increments may be created within a Sprint

### Definition of Done

- Formal description of the state of the Increment when it meets required quality measures
- If organizational DoD standards exist, the team's DoD must include them as a minimum
- If no organizational standard, the Scrum Team creates their own
- Used to assess completion of PBIs
- Developers adhere to the DoD; if a PBI does not meet it, it is not released and not included in the Increment

## 6. Agile Manifesto

### Four Values

1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
4. Responding to change over following a plan

Items on the right are not without value; items on the left are valued more.

### Twelve Principles — Exam-Critical Summary

| Principle | Number | Key Connection |
|-----------|--------|---------------|
| Deliver working software frequently, prefer shorter timescale | 1 | Sprint cadence |
| Welcome changing requirements even late | 2 | Empirical process; Product Backlog as living document |
| Business and developers work together daily | 4 | Product Owner proximity; Daily Scrum |
| Build projects around motivated individuals; give them support and trust | 5 | Self-management; Scrum Master as servant-leader |
| Working software is the primary measure of progress | 7 | Increment as the unit of value; no partial credit |
| Sustainable development — maintain constant pace indefinitely | 8 | Sprint cadence consistency; anti-utilization pressure |
| Continuous attention to technical excellence enhances agility | 9 | TDD, design patterns, CI/CD, refactoring |
| Simplicity — maximize work not done | 10 | YAGNI; avoid speculative features; thin vertical slices |
| Best architectures emerge from self-organizing teams | 11 | Developer self-management of technical approach |
| Regular reflection and adjustment | 12 | Sprint Retrospective |

## 7. Most Frequently Tested PSM I Concepts

### Concept 1: What the Scrum Master Does NOT Do

- Does not assign tasks to Developers
- Does not manage the team's schedule
- Does not own the Sprint Backlog
- Does not run the Daily Scrum (facilitates if needed, but does not own it)
- Does not report team performance metrics to management
- Does not decide what goes in the Sprint Backlog

### Concept 2: What the Product Owner Owns

- Product Backlog ordering (priority) — sole authority
- Product Goal — accountable for creating and communicating it
- Sprint Goal — co-creates with the Scrum Team during Sprint Planning
- Does NOT decide how Developers do their work
- Does NOT add work to the Sprint Backlog during the Sprint without Developers' agreement

### Concept 3: Sprint Cancellation

- Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint
- Only valid reason: Sprint Goal has become obsolete
- Any completed Done PBIs are reviewed; incomplete ones return to the Product Backlog

### Concept 4: Definition of Done vs. Acceptance Criteria

- Definition of Done: applies to every Increment, every PBI; owned by the Scrum Team
- Acceptance Criteria: specific conditions for a particular PBI; typically defined with the Product Owner during refinement
- An item can meet acceptance criteria but still fail the Definition of Done

### Concept 5: Sprint Backlog Changes During Sprint

- Scope can be negotiated with the Product Owner during the Sprint
- The Sprint Goal does not change
- Developers own the Sprint Backlog — they decide how to work within it

## 8. Common PSM I Exam Traps

| Trap | Why It's Wrong |
|------|---------------|
| "The Scrum Master should ensure the team meets velocity targets" | Velocity is a forecasting tool; Scrum Masters do not set performance targets |
| "The Product Owner approves the Increment before it can be released" | The Increment may be released at any point; Sprint Review is not an approval gate |
| "The Daily Scrum is for reporting status to the Scrum Master" | The Daily Scrum is for Developers; it is not a status report |
| "Sprint 0 is used for architecture and planning before Sprints begin" | Sprint 0 is not a Scrum concept; there is no prescribed pre-Sprint phase |
| "Scrum requires story points for estimation" | The Scrum Guide does not prescribe estimation units; story points are a common practice, not required |
| "The Scrum Guide requires a Sprint Retrospective after each Sprint Review" | The order is: Sprint Review, then Sprint Retrospective. Retrospective is the last event of the Sprint. |
| "The team can extend the Sprint if the Sprint Goal is not met" | Sprints have fixed length; they end on time regardless of completion status |
| "The Scrum Master runs the Sprint Retrospective" | The Scrum Master facilitates the Retrospective for their own team but does not own or control it |

## 9. PSM I Exam Reasoning Strategy

When you are unsure of a PSM I answer:

1. Ask: "What does the Scrum Guide say about this?" If you know the Guide, you know the answer.
2. Eliminate any answer that describes a Scrum Master doing project management tasks.
3. Eliminate any answer that describes the Product Owner controlling how Developers work.
4. Eliminate any answer that describes a team extending a Sprint or suspending the DoD.
5. When two answers seem correct, choose the one that most completely aligns with the 2020 Scrum Guide language and the three pillars of empiricism.

## 11. Supplemental Resources

The following free, open-access resources are essential for PSM I exam preparation:

**1. "The Scrum Guide (2020)" — Scrum.org**
<https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html>
The official and only authoritative source for the PSM I exam. The 2020 version is 13 pages. Read it at least twice — once for content and once to understand the reasoning behind each element. Every PSM I question traces back to language or principles in this document. Available free in multiple languages.

**2. "Scrum Open Assessment" — Scrum.org**
<https://www.scrum.org/open-assessments/scrum-open>
The official free practice assessment from Scrum.org. The question bank rotates; take it multiple times until you score above 95 percent consistently. The questions are representative of PSM I difficulty and style. No registration required for the free Scrum Open.

**3. "Agile Manifesto Principles" — Agilemanifesto.org**
<https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html>
The original twelve Agile Manifesto principles. PSM I questions occasionally cite specific principles — particularly Principles 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9, 10, and 12. Reading these alongside the Scrum Guide clarifies the philosophical foundation that the Scrum Guide's practices are built upon.
