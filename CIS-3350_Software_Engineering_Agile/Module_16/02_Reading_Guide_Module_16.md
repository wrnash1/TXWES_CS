Welcome to **Module 16 – Final Exam Prep and PSM I Certification**! This final module integrates everything you have learned across the course and prepares you for both the CIS-3350 final exam and the Scrum.org Professional Scrum Master I (PSM I) certification assessment.

The PSM I is an 80-question, 60-minute online assessment with a passing threshold of 85%. It tests Scrum knowledge at a depth that requires understanding *why* Scrum works the way it does — not just memorizing rules. This guide consolidates the highest-value topics, most common trap areas, and exam strategy to maximize your success.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary

Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

* **Empiricism:** Scrum's theoretical foundation — the belief that knowledge comes from experience and that decisions should be based on what is known rather than what is planned. Scrum's three pillars of empiricism are Transparency (visible work and process), Inspection (frequent examination of progress), and Adaptation (adjusting when deviation is detected). All five Scrum events and three artifacts are designed to implement these pillars.

* **Scrum values:** The five values the Scrum Guide states must be embodied by the Scrum Team: Commitment, Courage, Focus, Openness, and Respect. These values give life to the Scrum pillars. Questions on values often ask which behavior exemplifies or violates a specific value.

* **Definition of Done (DoD):** The formal quality standard every Increment must meet before it can be considered released. The DoD creates transparency and a shared understanding of completeness. If the organizational DoD is stronger than the team's DoD, the organizational standard applies.

* **Self-managing teams:** The Scrum Guide's description of the Scrum Team's internal authority — the team decides who does what, when, and how. No external manager, project manager, or Scrum Master directs the Developers' work assignments. Self-management is not the same as self-organizing (which the 2017 Scrum Guide used); the 2020 Guide uses self-managing.

* **PSM I assessment format:** 80 questions, 60-minute time limit, 85% passing score (68 of 80 correct), available online 24/7 at Scrum.org, $200 USD. Question types include multiple choice, multiple answer (select all that apply), and matching. Multiple-answer questions do not penalize partial credit — you must select all correct options to receive credit.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips

* **The Scrum Guide is the only source:** PSM I questions are based exclusively on the 2020 Scrum Guide. If your answer matches a real-world practice but contradicts the Scrum Guide, choose the Scrum Guide answer. Phrases like "In Scrum..." or "According to the Scrum Guide..." always signal that the Scrum Guide is the authoritative source.
* **"Correct" vs. "best" questions:** Many PSM I questions present multiple plausible-sounding answers. The correct answer is always the one most consistent with the Scrum Guide's principles — not merely the one that sounds reasonable in practice.
* **Most common exam topics:** Scrum Team accountabilities (Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers), Sprint event timeboxes, artifact-commitment pairs, the Sprint Cancellation rule, who attends each event, and the Definition of Done. Collectively these account for a large portion of the exam.
* **Sprint Cancellation rule:** Only the Product Owner can cancel a Sprint, and only when the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete. This is a rare and extreme action. Questions about who can cancel a Sprint — the answer is always the Product Owner, never the Scrum Master or Developers.
* **Study Resource:** [Open Assessments — Scrum.org](https://www.scrum.org/open-assessments) — take the free Scrum Open assessment (30 questions, no time limit) at least three times before the real exam. The questions are representative of PSM I difficulty and topic distribution.
