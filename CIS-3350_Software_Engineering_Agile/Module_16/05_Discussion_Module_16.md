### Scenario A: The "We Already Do Agile" Team

You have just been hired as a Scrum Master at a 200-person software company. During your first week, you observe the following:

- The team holds a "standup" every morning where each person says what they did yesterday and what they will do today. The meeting takes 45 minutes. The Scrum Master (your predecessor) asked clarifying questions throughout.
- Sprint Reviews are 30-minute demos where the team walks through a slide deck — no live software demonstration. Stakeholders are not invited because "they don't have time."
- The Definition of Done has not been updated in two years. It contains three items: "Code complete," "QA tested," and "Approved by manager."
- The team tells you: "We've been doing Scrum for four years. We know how it works."

Write an initial post (175–225 words) addressing the following:

- Identify three specific Scrum principle violations in the scenario, citing the relevant Scrum Guide concepts.
- How should you approach coaching a team that believes they already know Scrum correctly? What Scrum value is critical here?
- Which violation would you prioritize addressing first, and why?
- As a PSM I certified professional, how do you balance coaching toward the Scrum Guide with respecting the team's four years of practice and culture?

### Scenario B: Your PSM I Preparation Plan

You have completed all sixteen modules of CIS-3350. Your PSM I exam is scheduled in three weeks. You scored 72% (29/40) on the Module 16 mock assessment. You missed questions in the following categories: Sprint events and time-boxes (3 missed), Product Backlog management (4 missed), Scrum Master accountability (2 missed), and Definition of Done (2 missed).

Write an initial post (175–225 words) addressing the following:

- Design a specific three-week study plan. Which Scrum Guide sections will you focus on, in what sequence, and for how much time per week?
- For the two categories where you missed the most questions (Sprint events and Product Backlog management), describe the specific misconception you most likely held and how you now understand the correct concept.
- The PSM I passing score is 85%. If you score 72% on the mock, what score would you need on the remaining questions to achieve 85% on the real exam? (Show your math.)
- What is your single most important preparation action for the 24 hours before the exam?

### Scenario C: Applying the Full Course — A Capstone Reflection

Throughout this course you have covered: Agile Manifesto and principles, Scrum framework, Sprint events and artifacts, Kanban, user stories and estimation, Definition of Done, CI/CD and DevOps, technical debt, retrospectives, scaling frameworks, and PSM I preparation.

Write an initial post (175–225 words) as a capstone reflection addressing the following:

- Which two concepts from this course will most directly change how you approach software work in your career, and why?
- Describe one connection between a Module 1–10 concept and a Module 11–16 concept that you did not see clearly at the start of the course but now understand deeply.
- If you were explaining Scrum to someone with no software background — a family member, a potential employer — what would be your two-sentence explanation?
- Looking at the full arc of the course, what is the single most important thing a professional Scrum Master does that separates an effective one from an ineffective one?
