# Quiz: Module 03 – Scrum Framework: Roles, Events, Artifacts

**Course:** CIS-3350 Software Engineering and Agile
**Certification Alignment:** PSM I (Professional Scrum Master I) – Scrum.org
**Instructor:** Professor Nash | Texas Wesleyan University
**Total Questions:** 10 | **Points:** 10 (1 point each)

## Question 1

According to the Scrum Guide, who is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team?

- A) The Scrum Master
- B) The Developers
- C) The Product Owner
- D) The Project Sponsor

Correct Answer: C — The Scrum Guide explicitly states that the Product Owner is accountable for maximizing the value of the product resulting from the work of the Scrum Team.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Scrum Master is accountable for the Scrum Team's effectiveness, not for maximizing product value.
- Why B is incorrect: Developers are accountable for creating the Increment each Sprint, not for product value decisions.
- Why D is incorrect: Project Sponsor is not a Scrum accountability; stakeholders influence product direction through the Product Owner, not directly.

## Question 2

What is the maximum timebox for a Sprint Review for a one-month Sprint?

- A) 1 hour
- B) 2 hours
- C) 3 hours
- D) 4 hours

Correct Answer: D — The Scrum Guide timeboxes the Sprint Review at four hours for a one-month Sprint, with shorter Sprints having proportionally shorter Sprint Reviews.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: 1 hour is far too short for the inspection and adaptation work of a Sprint Review.
- Why B is incorrect: 2 hours is not the Scrum Guide timebox for Sprint Review.
- Why C is incorrect: 3 hours is the Sprint Retrospective timebox, not the Sprint Review timebox.

## Question 3

What is the commitment associated with the Sprint Backlog?

- A) The Product Goal
- B) The Sprint Goal
- C) The Definition of Done
- D) The Release Plan

Correct Answer: B — Each Scrum artifact has a commitment. The Sprint Backlog's commitment is the Sprint Goal, which gives the Sprint coherence and focus.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Product Goal is the commitment associated with the Product Backlog, not the Sprint Backlog.
- Why C is incorrect: The Definition of Done is the commitment associated with the Increment, not the Sprint Backlog.
- Why D is incorrect: The Release Plan is not a Scrum artifact or commitment.

## Question 4

A Product Backlog item is completed during the Sprint but does not meet the Definition of Done. What happens to this item?

- A) It is presented at the Sprint Review with a note that it is partially complete
- B) It is returned to the Product Backlog for future consideration
- C) It is released immediately so the team can move on to new work
- D) The Scrum Master decides whether it meets a modified standard

Correct Answer: B — An item that does not meet the Definition of Done cannot be included in the Sprint Increment and must be returned to the Product Backlog. It cannot be presented as Done at the Sprint Review.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Sprint Review only presents Done Increments; presenting partially complete work misrepresents the team's output.
- Why C is incorrect: Releasing software that does not meet the Definition of Done bypasses the quality standard Scrum is built on.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Master does not modify the Definition of Done to accommodate incomplete work; that would undermine transparency.

## Question 5

Who can cancel a Sprint?

- A) The Scrum Master
- B) The Developers
- C) Any member of the Scrum Team
- D) Only the Product Owner

Correct Answer: D — The Scrum Guide states that only the Product Owner has the authority to cancel a Sprint, and only if the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Scrum Master does not have authority to cancel a Sprint.
- Why B is incorrect: Developers do not have authority to cancel a Sprint.
- Why C is incorrect: Sprint cancellation is explicitly reserved for the Product Owner, not the Scrum Team collectively.

## Question 6

Which Scrum event is specifically for the Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog?

- A) Sprint Planning
- B) Daily Scrum
- C) Sprint Review
- D) Sprint Retrospective

Correct Answer: B — The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute event for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt the Sprint Backlog as necessary.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Sprint Planning occurs at the start of the Sprint and creates the Sprint Backlog; it is not an ongoing inspection event.
- Why C is incorrect: The Sprint Review inspects the product Increment with stakeholders, not the Sprint Backlog.
- Why D is incorrect: The Sprint Retrospective inspects the team's process and interactions, not daily progress toward the Sprint Goal.

## Question 7

What does the Scrum Guide say about the size of a Scrum Team?

- A) Exactly 7 people, plus or minus 2
- B) At least 5 people, with no maximum
- C) Typically 10 or fewer people, including the Product Owner and Scrum Master
- D) Exactly 5 Developers, 1 Product Owner, and 1 Scrum Master

Correct Answer: C — The 2020 Scrum Guide states that the Scrum Team is typically 10 or fewer people, including the Product Owner and Scrum Master.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: "7 plus or minus 2" was a common informal guideline but is not in the current 2020 Scrum Guide.
- Why B is incorrect: There is a practical upper limit — the Scrum Guide warns that larger teams require too much coordination.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Guide does not prescribe a fixed number of Developers; this varies based on the work.

## Question 8

The Scrum Master is attending the Daily Scrum and taking notes to share with the VP of Engineering afterward. Is this consistent with the Scrum Master's accountability?

- A) Yes — the Scrum Master should facilitate the Daily Scrum and report outcomes to management
- B) Yes — transparency requires that management be informed of the team's daily progress
- C) No — the Scrum Master serves the team, and sharing Daily Scrum content with management without team consent undermines psychological safety
- D) No — the Scrum Master should not attend the Daily Scrum at all

Correct Answer: C — Sharing the team's internal coordination conversations with management without team consent undermines the psychological safety needed for honest communication during the Daily Scrum, violating the Scrum values of Openness and Respect.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Scrum Master does not report Daily Scrum content to management; this is a project management behavior, not Scrum.
- Why B is incorrect: Transparency in Scrum refers to making work visible to those responsible for outcomes, not to surveillance of team conversations.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Master may attend the Daily Scrum to ensure it happens and is productive, but does not run or report on it.

## Question 9

What is the output of Sprint Planning?

- A) A fully documented requirements specification and a project schedule
- B) A Sprint Goal and a Sprint Backlog
- C) A release plan and a product roadmap
- D) A risk register and a staffing plan

Correct Answer: B — Sprint Planning produces two things: a Sprint Goal (the objective for the Sprint) and a Sprint Backlog (the selected Product Backlog items and a plan for delivering the Increment).

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Requirements specifications and project schedules are Waterfall artifacts, not Scrum outputs.
- Why C is incorrect: Release plans and product roadmaps may be complementary tools but are not the defined outputs of Sprint Planning.
- Why D is incorrect: Risk registers and staffing plans are project management artifacts; Scrum does not prescribe them as Sprint Planning outputs.

## Question 10

What is the purpose of the Sprint Retrospective?

- A) To demonstrate the Sprint's Increment to stakeholders and collect product feedback
- B) To plan the work for the next Sprint by selecting Product Backlog items
- C) To inspect how the last Sprint went and create a plan for team process improvements
- D) To update the Product Backlog based on new stakeholder priorities

Correct Answer: C — The Sprint Retrospective is specifically for the Scrum Team to inspect the Sprint regarding individuals, interactions, processes, tools, and the Definition of Done, and to create a plan for implementing improvements.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Demonstrating the Increment to stakeholders and collecting product feedback is the Sprint Review, not the Retrospective.
- Why B is incorrect: Planning the next Sprint's work is Sprint Planning, not the Retrospective.
- Why D is incorrect: Updating the Product Backlog is an output of the Sprint Review, not the Retrospective.
