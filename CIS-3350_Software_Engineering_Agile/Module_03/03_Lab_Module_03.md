## Overview

This lab is a simulation and documentation exercise centered on the complete Scrum framework. You will fill in a Scrum framework diagram with all roles, events, artifacts, timebox durations, and artifact commitments. You will then analyze a realistic scenario to identify Scrum rule violations and explain corrections. No programming is required.

Mastering the framework diagram is a foundational PSM I exam skill — every scenario question assumes you know this structure cold.

Estimated time: 90–120 minutes

## Part 1 — Scrum Framework Diagram (40 points)

### Part 1 Instructions

Reproduce the Scrum framework diagram below using a drawing tool (Lucidchart, draw.io, Microsoft Visio, PowerPoint, or a clearly photographed hand drawing). Your diagram must include every labeled element listed below. Accuracy and completeness are graded; visual polish is not.

### Required Diagram Elements

Your diagram must show:

Accountabilities (label each with name and one-line responsibility):

- Product Owner: accountable for maximizing product value; owns Product Backlog
- Scrum Master: accountable for team effectiveness; serves through coaching
- Developers: accountable for creating the Increment each Sprint

Events (label each with name and timebox for a one-month Sprint):

- Sprint: 1 week to 1 month container
- Sprint Planning: 8 hours maximum
- Daily Scrum: 15 minutes
- Sprint Review: 4 hours maximum
- Sprint Retrospective: 3 hours maximum

Artifacts (label each with name and its commitment):

- Product Backlog — commitment: Product Goal
- Sprint Backlog — commitment: Sprint Goal
- Increment — commitment: Definition of Done

Flow arrows showing:

- Product Backlog feeds into Sprint Planning
- Sprint Planning produces Sprint Backlog
- Sprint Backlog drives Sprint execution by Developers
- Developers produce Increment(s) each Sprint
- Sprint Review inspects the Increment with stakeholders
- Sprint Retrospective outputs an improvement plan for the next Sprint
- Sprint Review and Retrospective feed back into next Sprint Planning

### Part 1 Grading (40 points)

- All three accountabilities labeled with responsibility: 9 points (3 each)
- All five events labeled with correct timebox: 15 points (3 each)
- All three artifacts labeled with correct commitment: 12 points (4 each)
- Flow arrows present and directionally correct: 4 points

## Part 2 — Framework Scenario Analysis (35 points)

### Part 2 Instructions

Read the team scenario below and answer the five analysis questions that follow. Each answer should be 75–125 words.

### Team Scenario: Omega Team

Omega Team is a seven-person software team that has been using Scrum for three months. Observe the following behaviors:

Behavior 1: The team's Sprint length changes each Sprint based on how much work the Product Owner wants to include. Sprint 1 was two weeks, Sprint 2 was three weeks, Sprint 3 was one week.

Behavior 2: The Scrum Master runs the Daily Scrum, going around the room asking each Developer "What did you do yesterday? What will you do today? Any blockers?" The meeting typically runs 25–30 minutes.

Behavior 3: Halfway through Sprint 4, the VP of Engineering walks into the team room and tells two Developers to stop working on their Sprint Backlog items and fix a production bug instead. The Developers comply without telling the Scrum Master or Product Owner.

Behavior 4: At the Sprint Review, only the Scrum Team attends — no stakeholders are invited because "we don't want external feedback confusing the team's direction."

Behavior 5: The team's Definition of Done requires code review and unit testing, but the team skips both for a "quick fix" item in Sprint 4 because they are behind schedule.

### Analysis Questions

Question 1: What Scrum rule does Behavior 1 violate, and what should Omega Team do instead? Cite the specific Scrum Guide principle.

Question 2: What two problems are present in Behavior 2? What does the Scrum Guide say about how the Daily Scrum should work?

Question 3: Behavior 3 involves work being injected into the Sprint from outside the team. What Scrum principle does this violate? Who should the VP have spoken to instead, and what is the correct process for addressing this?

Question 4: What is wrong with the Sprint Review setup in Behavior 4? What is the stated purpose of the Sprint Review, and why are stakeholders essential to it?

Question 5: What are the consequences of violating the Definition of Done as described in Behavior 5, according to Scrum? Can this item be presented at the Sprint Review?

### Part 2 Grading (35 points)

- 7 points per question: rule identification (3), specific Scrum Guide reference (2), correction recommendation (2)

## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

### Challenge 1: Scrum Event Chain Simulation

Simulate one full Sprint cycle on paper or in a document, treating yourself as both the Product Owner and a Developer on a fictional three-person team building a campus event-scheduling app. Complete all of the following:

1. Write a Product Goal (one sentence) and a Product Backlog of at least eight items (title + one-line description each), ordered by priority.
2. Conduct a written Sprint Planning: select four to five items, write a Sprint Goal that unifies them, and decompose at least two items into sub-tasks of one day or less.
3. Write three Daily Scrum entries (Day 1, Day 5, Day 9 of a two-week Sprint) showing realistic progress, one impediment discovered, and how the Sprint Backlog was adapted.
4. Write a Sprint Review summary (three to five sentences) describing what was demonstrated, one stakeholder question raised, and how the Product Backlog was updated.
5. Write a Sprint Retrospective summary identifying one process problem observed and one concrete improvement action for the next Sprint.

### Challenge 2: Definition of Done Ladder

A "DoD Ladder" is a structured way to grow quality standards incrementally across Sprints. Build one for the campus event-scheduling app:

1. Write a Minimum DoD for Sprint 1 (at least three criteria a brand-new team could realistically achieve).
2. Write an Intermediate DoD for Sprint 6 (add at least two new criteria the team could add after establishing basic rhythm).
3. Write an Advanced DoD for Sprint 12 (add at least two more criteria requiring automated tooling or higher technical maturity).
4. For each new criterion added after Sprint 1, identify which Scrum value (Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, or Courage) it most directly supports and explain why in one sentence.

### Reflection Questions

1. After simulating the Sprint cycle, which event felt most difficult to do authentically (Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Review, or Retrospective) and why? What does that difficulty reveal about the skill or discipline that event requires in practice?
2. The Scrum Guide says the Retrospective's most impactful improvement items can be added directly to the Sprint Backlog. What is the risk of doing this, and what is the risk of not doing it?
