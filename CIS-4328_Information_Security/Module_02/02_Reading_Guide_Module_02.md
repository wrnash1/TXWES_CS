# Reading Guide: Module 02 - Social Engineering and Phishing

### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 02 – Social Engineering and Phishing**! This module covers one of the most heavily tested domains on the SY0-701 exam. The vast majority of real-world breaches begin with social engineering — manipulating people rather than exploiting software. You will learn to identify attack types, understand psychological principles attackers exploit, and apply defenses that go beyond technical controls.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Phishing**: A mass-delivery social engineering attack sent via email that tricks recipients into clicking malicious links, submitting credentials on fake sites, or opening malware-laden attachments. Phishing relies on urgency, authority, and fear to bypass critical thinking. It is the most common initial access vector tested on SY0-701.
*   **Spear Phishing**: A targeted variant of phishing directed at a specific individual or organization, using personalized details (name, job title, recent events) to increase believability. Because the message appears legitimate, spear phishing is far more effective than generic phishing and is frequently associated with APT (Advanced Persistent Threat) campaigns.
*   **Whaling**: A form of spear phishing that specifically targets high-value executives such as CEOs, CFOs, or legal counsel — the "big fish" of an organization. Whaling emails often reference board meetings, wire transfers, or legal matters to appear credible to the target.
*   **Vishing**: Voice phishing — social engineering conducted over telephone calls. Attackers impersonate IT support, bank fraud departments, or government agencies to extract credentials, PINs, or sensitive information verbally. SY0-701 frequently pairs vishing with pretexting scenarios.
*   **Smishing**: SMS phishing — social engineering via text message, often containing shortened URLs leading to credential-harvesting sites. Smishing exploits the trust users place in text messages and the difficulty of inspecting URLs on mobile devices.
*   **Pretexting**: Creating a fabricated scenario (pretext) to manipulate a victim into disclosing information or performing an action. For example, an attacker calls an employee claiming to be from IT and needs their password to resolve a "system outage." Pretexting is the psychological foundation underlying most social engineering attacks.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Domain Weight:** Social engineering maps to **Domain 2 – Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Mitigations (22%)** of SY0-701. Expect multiple scenario-based questions describing an attack and asking you to name the technique.
*   **Master the Phishing Variants:** The exam distinguishes phishing (mass/generic), spear phishing (targeted individual), whaling (executive-targeted), vishing (voice), and smishing (SMS). Identify which channel the attack uses — email, phone, or text — and whether it is targeted or mass-delivered.
*   **Psychological Principles Trap:** SY0-701 tests the six social engineering principles: authority, urgency, scarcity, social proof, familiarity, and intimidation. A question describing an email that says "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" exploits urgency — know each principle by name.
*   **Pharming vs. Phishing:** Pharming poisons DNS to redirect legitimate traffic to a fake site — the user types a real URL but lands on an attacker's page. Phishing requires the user to click a malicious link. These are frequently used as distractors for each other.
*   **Study Resource:** Professor Messer's free [CompTIA Security+ SY0-701 study notes and video course](https://www.professormesser.com/) include dedicated sections on social engineering techniques with scenario walkthroughs that match exam question style.
