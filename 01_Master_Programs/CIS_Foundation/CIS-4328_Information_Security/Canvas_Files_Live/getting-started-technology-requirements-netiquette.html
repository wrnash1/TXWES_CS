Technology Requirements

To succeed in this hybrid course, you will need:

  • A computer running Windows, macOS, or Linux, capable of running a virtual machine. A tablet or phone alone is not sufficient for the hands-on labs in this course.
  • Reliable high-speed internet access, both for the asynchronous portion of each week and for downloading lab tools.
  • A Kali Linux virtual machine (free, official images at kali.org) — used across multiple labs (cryptography, threat analysis, application security). Install and test this before Module 03.
  • A local intentionally-vulnerable web application for lab use (e.g., OWASP Juice Shop or DVWA, both free) — install locally; never test against a live production site.
  • A modern web browser (Chrome, Firefox, or Edge, kept up to date) for Canvas.
  • Your Texas Wesleyan email account — this is the official channel for all course and university communication. Check it regularly.

A note on lab safety and ethics: every hands-on lab in this course uses tools against systems you own or that are explicitly provided for the exercise (your own VM, a local vulnerable application). Never point any tool taught in this course at a system you do not own or do not have explicit written authorization to test — doing so may violate the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act and other laws, in addition to university policy.

If you don't have reliable access to a personal computer capable of running a VM, contact the TXWES IT Help Desk or your instructor as early as possible — do not wait until a lab is due.

Canvas Basics

If you're new to Canvas, review Canvas's own student guides (linked from the Canvas Help menu, the "?" icon in the global navigation). At minimum, know how to:

  • Check the Modules page for weekly content and due dates.
  • Submit an assignment (upload a file or type a text entry, depending on the assignment).
  • Post to and reply within a Discussion.
  • Take a Quiz and view your results afterward.
  • Check your Grades page.

Netiquette (Online Communication Guidelines)

Because a significant part of this course happens asynchronously in discussion boards, clear and respectful communication matters. Please follow these guidelines:

  • Be respectful. Disagreement on technical points is normal and encouraged — personal criticism of classmates is not.
  • Be specific and substantive. "I agree" or "Good post" does not meet participation requirements in this course; explain why you agree, add a new example, or extend the discussion with your own research.
  • Proofread before posting. You don't need perfect prose, but a quick read-through avoids confusing typos in technical terms.
  • Cite your sources. If you reference a fact, statistic, or specific technique from outside the course materials, cite it (see the APA 7 Citation Guide in this module).
  • Assume good faith. Written text lacks tone of voice — if a classmate's post seems harsh, consider that it may not have been intended that way before responding critically.
  • No sharing of exam or quiz content outside the course, including on external study sites — this is both an academic integrity issue and, in some cases, a certification-vendor policy violation for CompTIA-aligned material.
  • No discussion of real, non-lab systems' actual vulnerabilities you may discover outside this course in a public forum — report those responsibly through proper channels instead, as covered in Module 01.

Getting Help

If you're stuck on a technical problem, a lab step, or a concept, don't wait until the deadline — post a question in the course, email your instructor, or bring it to the in-person class meeting. Office hours are available by appointment (see the Syllabus for contact information).
