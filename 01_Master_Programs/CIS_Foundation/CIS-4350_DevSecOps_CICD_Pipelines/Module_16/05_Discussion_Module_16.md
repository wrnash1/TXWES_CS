## Discussion Prompt

This final discussion asks you to reflect on the complete arc of CIS-4350 and connect your learning to a real-world DevSecOps challenge. Rather than analyzing a single tool or concept, this prompt asks you to synthesize across domains — the same skill tested on the DSOE exam and required in a professional DevSecOps role.

### Scenario

You have been offered a DevSecOps Engineer role at a mid-size SaaS company. In your final interview, the engineering director asks you: "We have fifteen development teams, about 200 engineers, and we want to build a real DevSecOps program over the next 18 months. We have no security tooling in the CI/CD pipeline today. Where do you start, and how do you know when you have succeeded?"

This is the question this discussion asks you to answer.

---

### Your Tasks

**Initial Post (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM)**

In 250–300 words, respond to the engineering director's question. Your answer must:

1. Identify the first three initiatives you would implement, in priority order, and explain why you chose that sequence. Your sequence must reflect a deliberate logic — not just "do everything at once." Consider: what must be in place before other things can work? What failure modes are you trying to avoid from day one?

2. Name at least four specific tools from the course (with the correct tool name — Semgrep, not "a SAST tool"; Trivy, not "a container scanner") that would be part of your 18-month program, and explain the gap each one closes.

3. Answer the "how do you know when you have succeeded" part of the question using at least two specific metrics from the course. Do not say "when the program is mature" — give quantitative targets.

---

**Peer Responses (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM)**

Write substantive replies of at least 75 words each to at least two classmates. Your replies must engage with your peer's reasoning, not just their conclusions. Choose at least one of the following angles for each reply:

- Does your peer's sequencing logic hold up? Would a different first initiative avoid a failure mode they have not accounted for? Explain your reasoning.
- Did your peer's tool choices cover all four pipeline layers (code, dependencies, container, runtime)? If not, identify the gap and suggest the missing tool.
- Do your peer's success metrics actually measure program effectiveness (outcomes) or just program activity (inputs like "tool deployed" or "training completed")? Explain the difference.

---

## Instructor Notes for Grading

Strong initial posts will:

- Show deliberate sequencing logic — for example, establishing champion program and process before enabling mandatory gates (to avoid the tool-accumulation-without-process failure mode), or starting with a pilot team before organization-wide rollout
- Use correct tool names from the course material
- Cite specific, quantitative success metrics — for example, "Critical Escape Rate trending to zero within 12 months" or "DORA Change Failure Rate below 10%" — not vague qualitative statements
- Reflect awareness of at least one potential failure mode from Module 15 and how the proposed sequence avoids it

Strong peer responses will engage with the peer's reasoning at a technical and strategic level — not just affirm the post or add a tool that was missed. The goal is professional-level peer review: would this plan work, and why or why not?
