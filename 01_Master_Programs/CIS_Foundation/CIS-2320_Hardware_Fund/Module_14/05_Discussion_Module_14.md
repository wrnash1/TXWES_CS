## Scenario A — The New Employee Setup

A new employee arrives on their first day and hands you their corporate-issued Android smartphone. The phone needs to be connected to the corporate Wi-Fi network, which uses WPA2-Enterprise with 802.1X/PEAP authentication, and configured with the company's Exchange email using IMAP on port 993. The employee tells you their last job used a simple Wi-Fi password and they just typed their email address into their old phone and it configured itself automatically.

Address all three of the following in your post:

1. Explain to this employee why they cannot simply type in a Wi-Fi password for the corporate network, and describe the configuration steps you would perform to connect the device.
2. Describe the IMAP settings you would configure (server type, port, encryption method) and explain why you chose IMAP over POP3 for a user who also checks email on a tablet and a desktop computer.
3. Identify one security risk associated with corporate mobile device configuration and recommend one mitigation strategy a technician or IT department should implement.

## Scenario B — Bluetooth Troubleshooting in the Field

You are providing on-site support at a law firm. A senior partner is frustrated because their Bluetooth headset worked perfectly yesterday but today refuses to connect to their iPhone. The headset powers on, but the iPhone does not show it as available. You also notice the partner's car audio system is nearby and was used this morning during their commute.

Address all three of the following in your post:

1. Identify the most likely cause of the Bluetooth connection failure and explain the technical reason behind it, referencing how Bluetooth device pairing and connection bonding work.
2. Walk through the step-by-step troubleshooting procedure you would use to diagnose and resolve this issue, including what you would check first and why.
3. The partner asks whether they could use their headset and car audio system simultaneously with the same phone. Explain why or why not, referencing Bluetooth profile and connection limitations, and suggest a practical workaround if simultaneous use is not possible.

## Scenario C — Cellular Data and Hotspot Policy

Your company has issued field technicians smartphones with unlimited cellular data plans. Management has asked you, as the senior technician, to draft a one-page policy brief covering acceptable use of mobile hotspots and an explanation of 5G vs LTE expectations for technicians working in rural service areas.

Address all three of the following in your post:

1. Explain the technical difference between LTE and 5G sub-6 GHz coverage so that a non-technical manager can understand why rural field technicians may not see 5G speeds even on 5G-capable devices.
2. Describe two security risks of using an unsecured or poorly secured mobile hotspot in a client's facility and recommend specific technical controls to mitigate each risk.
3. One technician argues that because the plan is "unlimited" there are no concerns about hotspot use. Identify at least one legitimate concern that still exists even on an unlimited data plan and explain your reasoning.

## A Note from Professor Nash

Mobile device configuration is one of the first things you will do as a help desk technician or field support specialist. On your first week at almost any IT job, someone will hand you a phone and ask you to set up their email or get them on the Wi-Fi. The scenarios in this discussion are drawn directly from real help desk tickets. When you can explain to a confused user why their home Wi-Fi password will not work on the corporate network — or why deleting an email on their phone did not delete it on their laptop — you are doing the job.
