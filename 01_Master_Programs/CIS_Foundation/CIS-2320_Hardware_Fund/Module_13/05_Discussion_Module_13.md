## Scenario A — The Field Technician's Repair Bag

A field technician at a managed services company is dispatched to a customer site to perform three separate laptop repairs on the same day: (1) replace a failed Wi-Fi card in a business laptop, (2) replace a cracked LCD display panel in a user's personal laptop, and (3) diagnose a laptop that reports no battery charging.

1. For the Wi-Fi card replacement, the technician needs to disconnect two antenna cables. Describe the correct technique for removing the antenna connectors and explain what specifically can go wrong if the wrong technique is used. Name the connector type used on laptop antenna cables.
2. For the LCD replacement, the technician discovers the laptop uses CCFL backlighting. How does CCFL differ from LED backlighting, and what additional component must the technician inspect (beyond the panel itself) that is specific to CCFL-backlit laptops? Name the additional component.
3. The no-charging laptop shows a different behavior depending on the angle of the AC adapter cable. What does this angle-dependent symptom specifically indicate, and which component should the technician replace? Why does a consistent no-charge symptom (angle-independent) point to a different component?

## Scenario B — The IT Department Laptop Fleet

A university IT department manages 150 student laptops that are four years old. The department wants to extend the life of the fleet by upgrading RAM and replacing worn keyboards. The IT director asks the technician for a plan before purchasing any parts.

1. Before ordering 150 SO-DIMM upgrade kits, what must the technician verify about each laptop model? Name at least three things to check in the service manual, and explain what outcome would prevent the upgrade entirely regardless of budget.
2. Keyboard replacement will require accessing the ZIF connector on each laptop. Explain how a ZIF connector works and describe what happens if a technician attempts to remove the flex cable while the lock bar is in the closed position. What is the repair consequence if the lock bar breaks?
3. The IT director asks whether the technician should remove the battery before replacing keyboards, given that "it's just the keyboard and it's not electrical work." Provide a clear explanation of why battery removal is mandatory for keyboard replacement — not optional — and describe the specific risks of skipping this step.

## Scenario C — The Student's Broken Laptop

A computer science student brings in their personal laptop with two issues: the display is visibly cracked in the top-right corner after the laptop was dropped, and the laptop no longer charges when plugged in. The student says charging worked normally until about two weeks ago when the charging became "finicky" and then stopped entirely.

1. The cracked display requires panel replacement. Walk through the display removal procedure in logical sequence, starting from the closed laptop. Name the tool used to safely remove the bezel without cracking the plastic clips, and explain why powering on the laptop before closing the bezel is a best practice.
2. Based on the described charging history — "finicky" that progressed to "no charge" — which component is most likely failing: the AC adapter, the DC power jack, or the battery? Justify your answer using the progression of symptoms. What distinguishing symptom would help the technician confirm the diagnosis?
3. The student asks if both repairs can be done at the same time in a single disassembly. Explain whether this is practical, whether the disassembly steps for both repairs overlap, and which repair should be completed and tested first before moving on to the second.

## Professor Nash — Closing Note

Laptop service is the area where I have seen the most damage caused by technicians who were in a hurry. Stripped screw heads from using the wrong bit size. Snapped ZIF lock bars from pulling the cable before opening the lock. Torn antenna cables from pulling instead of prying. Cracked bezels from forcing the clips instead of finding the hidden screw under the rubber plug. Every one of those mistakes costs time, money, and sometimes the entire device. The procedures in this module are not suggestions — they are the result of the industry learning those lessons so you do not have to.

When you write your discussion post, write it as if you are explaining the procedure to a first-day technician who is about to pick up a screwdriver. Precision and completeness matter.
