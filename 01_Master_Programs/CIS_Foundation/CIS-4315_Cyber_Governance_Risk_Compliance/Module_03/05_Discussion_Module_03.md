## Scenario A — Choosing a Framework for a Community College

Westfield Community College in central Texas serves 12,000 students and operates with a cybersecurity team of three full-time employees and a modest annual security budget of $180,000. The college's CIO has been tasked with conducting a comprehensive risk assessment of the student records system, which contains Social Security numbers, financial aid data, and academic transcripts for all enrolled and alumni students. The CIO has heard of NIST RMF, ISO 31000, OCTAVE, and FAIR but has never formally applied any of them. The college has no federal contracts and no existing risk management documentation.

In 175–225 words, respond to the following questions in complete sentences.

Which of the four frameworks would you recommend to Westfield Community College, and why is it the best fit for this specific situation? Identify at least two characteristics of the college's situation that make your chosen framework more appropriate than the alternatives. Then identify one significant limitation of your chosen framework in this context, and explain how the college could address that limitation. Finally, explain what the primary deliverable or output of the recommended framework would be for the CIO, and how that output would help the college make better security decisions.

## Scenario B — Framework Integration at a Defense Contractor

Meridian Aerospace Solutions is a mid-sized defense contractor with 2,200 employees that manufactures guidance systems for U.S. Air Force contracts. Meridian is required by DFARS (Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement) to comply with NIST SP 800-171, which maps to the NIST RMF. However, the company's new Chief Risk Officer — who came from the insurance industry — wants to implement ISO 31000 as the enterprise risk management philosophy across all risk domains, not just cybersecurity. The CISO is concerned that running two frameworks simultaneously will create confusion and duplication of effort.

In 175–225 words, respond to the following questions in complete sentences.

Is the CISO's concern valid? Can NIST RMF and ISO 31000 coexist productively in the same organization, or do they conflict? Explain the role each framework would play if both were adopted. Identify at least one area where the two frameworks reinforce each other and one area where they might create tension or ambiguity for the security team. Conclude with a recommendation: should Meridian adopt both frameworks, and if so, how should the relationship between them be defined in the organization's governance documents?

## Scenario C — Communicating Risk to a Hospital Board

St. Catherine's Regional Hospital experienced a ransomware attack 18 months ago that encrypted its EHR system for 11 days, forcing the diversion of emergency patients to other facilities and costing an estimated $4.2 million in recovery, lost revenue, and regulatory response. The hospital's board of directors has asked the CISO to present the current cybersecurity risk posture at the next quarterly board meeting. The board is composed of physicians, community leaders, and business executives — none of whom have a technical cybersecurity background. The CISO has risk assessment data from an OCTAVE Allegro assessment completed six months ago, and is considering supplementing it with a FAIR analysis of the top three identified risks.

In 175–225 words, respond to the following questions in complete sentences.

Why would the CISO's instinct to supplement the OCTAVE output with a FAIR analysis be valuable for a board audience? What specific type of information does FAIR provide that OCTAVE alone cannot? Describe what the board presentation might look like if the CISO uses FAIR outputs — what data would be presented and in what format. Then address the potential risk of relying too heavily on FAIR's financial estimates: what assumptions or limitations should the CISO disclose to the board so they interpret the numbers responsibly? Conclude with your assessment of whether combining OCTAVE and FAIR is an effective strategy for this situation.

## A Note from Professor Nash

The goal of these discussion scenarios is to develop your professional judgment — the ability to look at an organization's situation and recommend the right approach with confidence and clear reasoning. On the CISM exam, you will see scenario questions that test exactly this skill. There is rarely a single "perfect" answer; what matters is the quality of your reasoning and whether your recommendation fits the facts of the scenario.

When you write your peer responses, practice respectful disagreement. If a classmate chose a different framework than you would have, engage with their reasoning. Ask yourself: what do they see that I might have missed? What do I see that they might not have considered? This kind of critical dialogue is how security professionals develop sound judgment.
