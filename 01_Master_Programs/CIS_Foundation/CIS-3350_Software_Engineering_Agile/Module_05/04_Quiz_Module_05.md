# Quiz: Module 05 – Sprint Review and Retrospective

**Course:** CIS-3350 Software Engineering and Agile
**Certification Alignment:** PSM I (Professional Scrum Master I) – Scrum.org
**Instructor:** Professor Nash | Texas Wesleyan University
**Total Questions:** 10 | **Points:** 10 (1 point each)

## Question 1

What is the primary output of the Sprint Review?

- A) A signed acceptance document from stakeholders approving the Increment
- B) A revised Product Backlog reflecting what was learned during the review
- C) A team process improvement plan for the next Sprint
- D) A detailed test report verifying that all items meet the Definition of Done

Correct Answer: B — The Scrum Guide states that the output of the Sprint Review is a revised Product Backlog. Stakeholder feedback during the Sprint Review is incorporated into the Product Backlog as updates to existing items or new items.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Sprint Review is not an approval gate; stakeholders do not sign off on the Increment. The Product Owner decides on release independently.
- Why C is incorrect: A team process improvement plan is the output of the Sprint Retrospective, not the Sprint Review.
- Why D is incorrect: Verifying items against the Definition of Done occurs during the Sprint, not at the Sprint Review.

## Question 2

Who attends the Sprint Retrospective?

- A) The Scrum Team only (Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers)
- B) The Scrum Team and all invited stakeholders
- C) The Developers and Scrum Master only; the Product Owner does not attend
- D) The Scrum Master only, who then reports findings to the team

Correct Answer: A — The Sprint Retrospective is for the Scrum Team only. Stakeholders do not attend, which protects the psychological safety needed for honest process discussion.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is incorrect: Stakeholders attend the Sprint Review, not the Retrospective.
- Why C is incorrect: The Product Owner is part of the Scrum Team and attends the Retrospective as a full participant.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Master does not conduct the Retrospective alone and report findings; the entire Scrum Team participates.

## Question 3

In what order do the final three Scrum events occur within a Sprint?

- A) Sprint Retrospective → Sprint Review → Sprint Planning
- B) Sprint Review → Sprint Planning → Sprint Retrospective
- C) Sprint Review → Sprint Retrospective → (next) Sprint Planning
- D) Sprint Retrospective → Sprint Planning → Sprint Review

Correct Answer: C — The Sprint Review occurs first (product inspection with stakeholders), then the Sprint Retrospective (team process inspection), then the next Sprint begins with Sprint Planning.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: The Retrospective comes after the Review, not before it.
- Why B is incorrect: Sprint Planning begins the next Sprint; it does not occur between the Review and Retrospective of the current Sprint.
- Why D is incorrect: This reverses the Review/Retrospective order and incorrectly positions Sprint Planning within the current Sprint.

## Question 4

A Scrum Team has completed Sprint 5. One Product Backlog item was not finished — it is 80% complete but does not meet the Definition of Done. What happens to this item at the Sprint Review?

- A) It is presented at the Sprint Review as "nearly done" to show the team's progress
- B) The Product Owner can waive the Definition of Done for this item given how close it is to completion
- C) The item is not shown at the Sprint Review; it is returned to the Product Backlog for future Sprints
- D) The Scrum Master can approve partial release of the item if stakeholders request it

Correct Answer: C — An item that does not meet the Definition of Done is not part of the Done Increment and cannot be shown at the Sprint Review. It returns to the Product Backlog.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Presenting unfinished work as progress misrepresents the Increment's state and violates transparency.
- Why B is incorrect: The Product Owner cannot waive the Definition of Done; it is a quality commitment that applies to every Increment.
- Why D is incorrect: The Scrum Master cannot approve partial release; the Definition of Done governs what constitutes a releasable Increment.

## Question 5

The Sprint Retrospective is timeboxed to a maximum of how long for a one-month Sprint?

- A) 1 hour
- B) 2 hours
- C) 3 hours
- D) 4 hours

Correct Answer: C — The Scrum Guide timeboxes the Sprint Retrospective at three hours for a one-month Sprint.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: 1 hour is insufficient for a full team process inspection and improvement planning session for a one-month Sprint.
- Why B is incorrect: 2 hours is not the Scrum Guide timebox for the Retrospective.
- Why D is incorrect: 4 hours is the Sprint Review timebox, not the Retrospective timebox.

## Question 6

During a Sprint Retrospective, the Scrum Team identifies five potential improvements. According to the Scrum Guide, what should the team commit to?

- A) Implementing all five improvements in the next Sprint regardless of capacity
- B) Presenting all five improvements to management for prioritization
- C) Selecting at least one high-priority improvement to implement in the next Sprint
- D) Documenting all five improvements and revisiting them in three months

Correct Answer: C — The Scrum Guide says the Retrospective should produce "a plan for implementing at least one way to improve its quality and effectiveness." One focused, committed improvement is more valuable than a list of intentions.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Committing to five improvements simultaneously may overwhelm capacity and lead to none being fully implemented.
- Why B is incorrect: Team process improvements are the team's accountability, not management's. Presenting them for external prioritization undermines team self-management.
- Why D is incorrect: Deferring improvements to a three-month review contradicts the Retrospective's purpose of creating a faster, Sprint-cadence improvement loop.

## Question 7

What is the key difference between what the Sprint Review inspects and what the Sprint Retrospective inspects?

- A) The Sprint Review inspects the team's process; the Sprint Retrospective inspects the product
- B) The Sprint Review inspects the product Increment; the Sprint Retrospective inspects the team's process and interactions
- C) The Sprint Review inspects test results; the Sprint Retrospective inspects stakeholder satisfaction
- D) Both events inspect the same things; the difference is only in who attends

Correct Answer: B — The Sprint Review focuses on the product (what was built), while the Sprint Retrospective focuses on the process (how the team worked). This is the fundamental distinction between the two events.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: This reverses the two events. The Review is for the product; the Retrospective is for the process.
- Why C is incorrect: While test results may be part of the Increment discussion, "test results" is not the Scrum Guide's description of what the Sprint Review inspects.
- Why D is incorrect: The two events have fundamentally different inspection targets, not just different attendance lists.

## Question 8

A company's Product Manager attends the Sprint Retrospective because they want to hear the team's feedback about the product requirements they wrote. Is this appropriate?

- A) Yes — the Product Manager's presence ensures that product feedback is captured accurately
- B) Yes — the Retrospective is open to anyone who wants to participate and observe
- C) No — the Sprint Retrospective is for the Scrum Team only; external participants undermine psychological safety
- D) No — product feedback should be shared at the Sprint Review, not the Retrospective

Correct Answer: C — The Sprint Retrospective is attended by the Scrum Team only. External participants — even well-intentioned ones — change the dynamics and can prevent team members from speaking candidly about process dysfunction.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Product feedback is collected at the Sprint Review, not the Retrospective; product requirement quality is a valid topic but belongs in a different forum.
- Why B is incorrect: The Scrum Guide specifically limits the Retrospective to the Scrum Team; it is not an open event.
- Why D is incorrect: While product requirement quality could be raised in the Retrospective as a process issue, the primary reason the Product Manager should not attend is the need for Scrum Team privacy, not a strict topic boundary.

## Question 9

Agile Manifesto Principle 12 states that teams should "at regular intervals, reflect on how to become more effective, then tune and adjust their behavior accordingly." Which Scrum event directly implements this principle?

- A) Sprint Planning
- B) Sprint Review
- C) Daily Scrum
- D) Sprint Retrospective

Correct Answer: D — The Sprint Retrospective directly implements Principle 12. It is the formal, recurring interval at which the Scrum Team reflects on their effectiveness and creates a plan to improve.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Sprint Planning focuses on creating the Sprint Goal and Sprint Backlog, not on team process reflection.
- Why B is incorrect: The Sprint Review focuses on the product Increment and stakeholder feedback, not on team process improvement.
- Why C is incorrect: The Daily Scrum is a 15-minute daily inspection of Sprint progress, not a team process reflection event.

## Question 10

A Scrum Team skips the Sprint Review for Sprint 8 because "we didn't finish many stories and don't want to show stakeholders an empty Sprint." What is wrong with this decision?

- A) Nothing — it is reasonable to skip the Sprint Review when there is little to show
- B) The Sprint Review should still occur; even when little was Done, the event provides valuable information about why and what should change
- C) The Scrum Master should show the unfinished work instead to maintain stakeholder relationships
- D) The Product Owner should cancel the Sprint retroactively rather than hold an unproductive Sprint Review

Correct Answer: B — The Sprint Review is required at the end of every Sprint. When little was accomplished, the Sprint Review is arguably most valuable — it creates the forum for the Product Owner and stakeholders to understand what happened and how to adapt the Product Backlog and approach.

Distractor Analysis:

- Why A is incorrect: Skipping the Sprint Review deprives the team and stakeholders of a critical inspection and adaptation opportunity.
- Why C is incorrect: Showing unfinished work that does not meet the Definition of Done misrepresents the Increment and violates transparency.
- Why D is incorrect: Sprint cancellation requires the Sprint Goal to be obsolete; a low-output Sprint does not meet that criterion, and retroactive cancellation is not a Scrum construct.

### Question 11 (5 points)

The Scrum Guide says that improvement items identified at the Sprint Retrospective "may" be added to the Sprint Backlog. Why is this considered a best practice?

- A) It ensures the improvements are completed before product work begins in the next Sprint
- B) It gives process improvements the same visibility, prioritization, and accountability as product work
- C) It transfers ownership of the improvements from the team to the Product Owner
- D) It replaces the need to hold another Retrospective until all improvements are implemented

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — Improvements are not necessarily done before product work; they are integrated into the Sprint alongside product backlog items.
  - C) Incorrect — Process improvements remain the team's accountability; adding them to the Sprint Backlog does not transfer ownership to the Product Owner.
  - D) Incorrect — Retrospectives continue every Sprint regardless of whether prior improvements are fully implemented.

### Question 12 (5 points)

A stakeholder attending the Sprint Review says "I approve this Increment — you can release it." What is wrong with this statement from a Scrum perspective?

- A) Nothing — stakeholder approval is required before any Increment can be released
- B) Stakeholders do not approve releases; the Product Owner decides whether and when to release the Increment
- C) The Scrum Master must countersign the approval before it is valid
- D) Approval is only granted at Sprint Planning, not the Sprint Review

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — The Sprint Review is not an approval gate; the Product Owner holds release authority, not stakeholders collectively.
  - C) Incorrect — The Scrum Master has no release authority; their role is coaching and facilitation.
  - D) Incorrect — Sprint Planning does not involve release approval; it creates the Sprint Goal and Sprint Backlog.

### Question 13 (5 points)

Which of the following best describes the "product feedback loop" created by the Sprint Review?

- A) Sprint Planning → execution → Sprint Review → updated Product Backlog → next Sprint Planning
- B) Sprint Review → Sprint Retrospective → next Daily Scrum → updated Sprint Backlog
- C) Daily Scrum → Sprint Review → Sprint Retrospective → next Sprint Planning
- D) Sprint Retrospective → updated Definition of Done → Sprint Review → stakeholder sign-off

- **Correct Answer:** A
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - B) Incorrect — This sequence mixes product and process feedback loops and incorrectly connects Retrospective output to Daily Scrum.
  - C) Incorrect — The Daily Scrum is an intra-Sprint event; it is not part of the inter-Sprint product feedback loop.
  - D) Incorrect — The Definition of Done update is a process feedback output from the Retrospective, not a step in the product feedback loop.

### Question 14 (5 points)

A Scrum Team holds its Sprint Retrospective and produces a list of twelve improvements. The Scrum Master asks the team to commit to implementing all twelve in the next Sprint. Is this good practice?

- A) Yes — more improvements committed means faster team growth
- B) Yes — the Scrum Guide requires all identified improvements to be actioned immediately
- C) No — the Scrum Guide recommends committing to at least one high-priority improvement; twelve simultaneous commitments risk spreading the team too thin and implementing none well
- D) No — the Scrum Master does not have authority to ask for improvement commitments; that is the Product Owner's job

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — Committing to too many improvements simultaneously leads to none being done well; focused improvement is more effective.
  - B) Incorrect — The Scrum Guide says "at least one" improvement, not all identified improvements.
  - D) Incorrect — Process improvements are the Scrum Team's collective accountability; the Scrum Master appropriately facilitates that commitment.

### Question 15 (5 points)

At the Sprint Review, the Product Owner announces that based on stakeholder feedback, two previously high-priority Product Backlog items are now low-priority and three new items have been added to the top of the backlog. Is this appropriate?

- A) No — the Product Backlog order cannot change until the next Sprint Planning
- B) Yes — the Sprint Review exists precisely to inspect the Increment and adapt the Product Backlog based on new information
- C) No — stakeholders cannot influence the Product Backlog; only the Scrum Team can propose changes
- D) Yes — but only if the Developers agree to the reprioritization before the Sprint Review ends

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — The Product Backlog is dynamic and can be updated at any time; the Sprint Review is specifically intended to produce Product Backlog updates.
  - C) Incorrect — Stakeholders are invited to the Sprint Review specifically to provide feedback that informs Product Backlog changes; the Product Owner incorporates that feedback.
  - D) Incorrect — Product Backlog ordering is the Product Owner's sole accountability; Developer agreement is not required to reorder the backlog.

### Question 16 (5 points)

The "Four Ls" retrospective format asks the team to discuss what they Liked, Learned, Lacked, and Longed for. Which of the four categories most directly surfaces process improvements?

- A) Liked — because it identifies what to continue doing
- B) Learned — because new knowledge drives future changes
- C) Lacked — because it identifies gaps in process, support, or resources that should be addressed
- D) Longed for — because it describes aspirational practices the team has never tried

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — "Liked" surfaces practices to continue, not improvements to make; it is backward-looking in a positive way.
  - B) Incorrect — "Learned" captures insights but does not directly identify what was missing from the process.
  - D) Incorrect — "Longed for" is aspirational but may not map to a concrete, actionable improvement for the next Sprint.

### Question 17 (5 points)

Which Sprint event most directly embodies the Scrum empirical pillar of "Adaptation" at the product level?

- A) Sprint Planning, because the team decides what to build next
- B) Daily Scrum, because the team adapts the Sprint Backlog daily
- C) Sprint Review, because stakeholder feedback causes the Product Backlog to be updated
- D) Sprint Retrospective, because the team changes its process based on what it learned

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — Sprint Planning is an adaptation event, but it works from an already-updated Product Backlog; the Sprint Review is where the product-level adaptation occurs.
  - B) Incorrect — The Daily Scrum enables process-level adaptation within the Sprint, not product-level adaptation.
  - D) Incorrect — The Sprint Retrospective drives process-level adaptation; the Sprint Review drives product-level adaptation.

### Question 18 (5 points)

The CampusConnect team skips their Sprint Retrospective after Sprint 7 because "the Sprint went great and there's nothing to improve." What is the primary risk of this decision?

- A) The team will violate the Scrum Guide's timebox rules
- B) The team misses an opportunity to reinforce what worked well and discover subtle issues before they become larger problems in future Sprints
- C) The Scrum Master will be penalized for failing to schedule the event
- D) Stakeholders will lose confidence in the team's ability to self-manage

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — Timebox rules define the maximum duration, not a penalty for skipping; the risk is loss of improvement opportunity, not a rule violation penalty.
  - C) Incorrect — The Scrum Guide does not define penalties for individuals; the team collectively is accountable for conducting Scrum events.
  - D) Incorrect — Stakeholders do not attend the Retrospective; they would not directly observe its absence.

### Question 19 (5 points)

At a Sprint Review, a Developer begins explaining at length why a feature took longer than expected. The stakeholders are disengaged. What is the Scrum Master's most appropriate action?

- A) Interrupt the Developer and redirect the meeting to the product demonstration
- B) Allow the explanation to continue because transparency requires sharing all relevant information
- C) Ask the Developer to hold detailed technical explanations for the Retrospective and refocus the Sprint Review on the Increment and stakeholder feedback
- D) Cancel the Sprint Review and reschedule when the Developers are better prepared

- **Correct Answer:** C
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — Interrupting the Developer is disrespectful; coaching toward the appropriate forum is more effective.
  - B) Incorrect — Transparency is important, but the Sprint Review's purpose is product inspection and stakeholder collaboration, not internal process debriefs.
  - D) Incorrect — Cancelling the Sprint Review for this reason is disproportionate; facilitation coaching is the correct intervention.

### Question 20 (5 points)

The Scrum Guide describes the Sprint Retrospective as creating a plan for improving quality and effectiveness. Quality improvements specifically could include which of the following?

- A) Reducing the number of Sprint Backlog items to ensure the Sprint Goal is always achieved
- B) Tightening the Definition of Done to require additional automated test coverage
- C) Increasing the Sprint timebox to give the team more time to complete quality work
- D) Moving testing responsibilities to a separate QA team to improve test quality

- **Correct Answer:** B
- **Distractor Analysis:**
  - A) Incorrect — Reducing Sprint Backlog items is a capacity planning adjustment, not a quality improvement; it does not change the team's quality standards.
  - C) Incorrect — Sprints have a fixed maximum length of one month and are not extended to accommodate quality; this would also reduce delivery frequency.
  - D) Incorrect — Delegating testing to a separate team contradicts the cross-functional, self-managing Scrum Team principle and typically reduces quality by introducing handoffs.
