## Instructions

Choose one of the three scenarios below (A, B, or C). Write an initial post responding to all three sub-questions within your chosen scenario. Then respond to at least two classmates who chose different scenarios whenever possible.

Initial post length: 175–225 words. Peer responses: at least 75 words each, adding substance beyond agreement.

## Scenario A — The Story That Launched a Debate

During Sprint Planning for a healthcare scheduling app, the team is reviewing a user story: "As a patient, I can book an appointment." The Product Owner says: "This is straightforward — just build a booking form." The lead Developer says: "We need to know: can patients book same-day appointments? What happens if no slots are available? Can they book for a family member? What are the cancellation rules?" The meeting is now 30 minutes over timebox because the team is trying to resolve these questions in Sprint Planning rather than during refinement.

Sub-questions:

1. What is the core problem revealed by this situation, and which component of the Three Cs model is missing from this story?
2. Write 4 Given/When/Then acceptance criteria that the team should have agreed on during refinement to prevent this Sprint Planning bottleneck. Address at least two of the scenarios the Developer raised.
3. What process change should the team and Product Owner adopt to prevent this pattern in future Sprints?

## Scenario B — Technical Stories in the Backlog

The DataFlow team's Product Backlog contains several items that are written as technical tasks rather than user stories, including: "Migrate authentication service from legacy OAuth to OAuth 2.0," "Upgrade PostgreSQL from version 12 to 15," and "Refactor the API gateway to use async processing." The Product Owner argues these should not be in the Product Backlog at all — "only user-facing features belong in the backlog." A Developer argues that all team work should be visible in the backlog.

Sub-questions:

1. Who is correct — the Product Owner or the Developer? What does the Scrum Guide say about what types of work belong in the Product Backlog?
2. The technical items as written do not follow user story format and have no stated user value. Does this mean they are invalid Product Backlog items? How should they be handled?
3. Rewrite one of the three technical items as a user-value-framed story (you do not need to eliminate the technical nature — reveal the value it creates for users or the business).

## Scenario C — Acceptance Criteria Ambiguity

A checkout story has been in the Sprint for four days: "As a customer, I can complete a purchase so that I can receive the product I want." At the Sprint Review, the Product Owner says the story is not Done because "the confirmation email should show the order total with tax broken out separately." The Developer says: "We sent a confirmation email — the story is Done." The Scrum Master says: "The acceptance criteria just say 'customer receives a confirmation email.'"

Sub-questions:

1. Who bears primary responsibility for the ambiguity in this acceptance criterion, and at what point in the Sprint lifecycle should this ambiguity have been resolved?
2. Rewrite the acceptance criterion for the confirmation email in Given/When/Then format that would have prevented this dispute. Include at least two criteria covering the email content requirements.
3. Beyond this specific story, what systemic practice should this team adopt to prevent acceptance criteria ambiguity from becoming a recurring source of Sprint Review disputes?

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

User stories and acceptance criteria are the most written artifacts in Agile practice, and they are among the most frequently written poorly. A vague story delays Sprint Planning. Missing acceptance criteria create Sprint Review disputes. Technical stories without stated value get deprioritized unfairly. The scenarios in this discussion represent the exact failure modes you will encounter in your first Agile job. Mastering this craft now — before you are under the pressure of a Sprint deadline — is one of the most valuable things you can do in this course.
