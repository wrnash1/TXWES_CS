## Scenario 1: The SLA Nobody Reads (175–225 words)

Vector Analytics is a data science consulting firm with 400 employees. Their IT department
created an SLA for their core analytics platform three years ago. The SLA is a 22-page
document that was signed by the CTO and filed in a SharePoint folder. Nobody has reviewed
it since.

Last month, the analytics platform experienced two outages. The IT department considered
both compliant with the SLA based on their interpretation of the availability target. The
Head of Analytics considers both to be unacceptable failures based on her understanding
of what IT had promised.

Using ITIL 4 Service Level Management principles, explain what systemic failures are
evident in this situation — beyond the obvious "the SLA was not reviewed." What does a
living, functional SLA require that this one lacks? What role should the service review
meeting have played in the preceding three years? Propose two specific, actionable
changes that would prevent this situation from recurring at Vector Analytics. Your
response should reference at least two ITIL 4 SLM concepts.

## Scenario 2: When Green Metrics Mean Nothing (175–225 words)

Hallmark Healthcare's IT service desk reports the following metrics to the executive team
every month:

- Availability: 99.8% (target: 99.5%) — Green
- P2 resolution: 91% within 4 hours (target: 90%) — Green
- First-call resolution: 83% (target: 80%) — Green

Every metric is green. The CIO presents these results proudly at the quarterly board meeting.

However, a staff satisfaction survey conducted independently by HR reveals that 64% of
clinical staff rate IT support as "poor" or "very poor." Common complaints include: "I
always have to call multiple times," "No one tells me what's happening," and "The fix
never lasts."

Using ITIL 4 concepts, explain the gap between the green dashboard and the 64% poor
rating. What is technically happening, and what important dimension of service quality
is being missed? How does the concept of Experience Level Agreements (XLAs) address
this gap? What specific XLA measurement would you recommend Hallmark implement first,
and why? Reference at least two specific concepts from Module 10.

## Scenario 3: The Honest Report Problem (175–225 words)

Dominic is the IT Service Manager at a regional bank. In September, his team missed the
availability SLA for the online banking portal by 0.3% due to an unplanned database
outage. The breach was identified internally, the outage was resolved, and a problem
record was raised.

Dominic's manager suggests that the September SLA report should describe the availability
as "99.5% excluding the database maintenance event on September 14" — technically
reclassifying an unplanned outage as maintenance to avoid reporting a breach.

Dominic refuses. His manager argues: "The client doesn't need to know every time we
have a bad month. Building confidence matters more than perfect honesty."

Using ITIL 4 guiding principles and Service Level Management best practices, construct
Dominic's argument for why the breach must be reported honestly. Address specifically:
What are the risks of the approach Dominic's manager is suggesting — both to the
customer relationship and to the organization's internal service management maturity?
What does ITIL 4 say about transparency in the context of service management? How does
honest breach reporting, done well, actually strengthen rather than damage the customer
relationship?
