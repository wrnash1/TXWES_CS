## Scenario A: The Most Important Concept

Across the sixteen modules of this course, you have covered the ITIL 4 Service Value System, the Four Dimensions, the Guiding Principles, the Service Value Chain, and fifteen practices from Incident Management to Risk and Compliance to DevOps integration.

**Prompt:** Argue for one concept, practice, or principle from this course that you believe is the most universally important — the one that, if an IT organization deeply understood and correctly applied, would have the greatest positive impact on service quality, user experience, and business outcomes. Make a specific case with evidence or examples. Anticipate the counterargument (what would someone who chose a different concept argue?) and respond to it.

---

## Scenario B: What ITIL Gets Wrong

ITIL 4 is not a perfect framework. No framework is.

**Prompt:** Identify one limitation, weakness, or criticism of ITIL 4 that you believe is valid and significant. This could be a gap in scope, an area where the framework provides insufficient guidance, a concept that is harder to implement than ITIL suggests, or a tension with modern delivery practices that ITIL does not fully resolve. Be specific and fair — acknowledge what ITIL 4 gets right in the area you are critiquing, but argue clearly for where it falls short and what you would add or change.

---

## Scenario C: Your Career Trajectory

You are about to complete an ITIL 4 Foundation certification course. This is the beginning of a professional development journey, not the end.

**Prompt:** Describe the role you aspire to in IT over the next 5 years — specific enough to be meaningful (not just "IT manager"). Explain which ITIL 4 practices or concepts are most relevant to that role. What certification or learning path would you pursue next (ITIL Managing Professional, DevOps certifications, cloud certifications, PMP, etc.)? What specific skill or knowledge gap do you need to close first? Make this a thoughtful professional development plan, not a generic answer.
