## Scenario A: The Hidden Data Center

An internal IT audit at a 500-person marketing firm discovered a fully operational server rack in a storage room on the third floor. The rack holds 6 servers running a customer analytics database that the marketing department has been operating for two years without IT's knowledge. The servers contain 3 years of customer behavioral data including purchasing history and location data. None of the servers have received security patches in 14 months. IT leadership is alarmed; the marketing VP says the servers support a mission-critical analytics workflow.

**Prompt:** This is a shadow IT situation with significant security and compliance dimensions. Analyze the risks — both the technical security risks and the regulatory risks related to unmanaged customer data. What immediate steps should IT take? How should the organization structure a formal ITAM process to prevent similar situations from arising? Should the marketing department be penalized, accommodated, or both?

## Scenario B: The License Audit Letter

A mid-size architecture firm receives a formal license audit notification from Autodesk, a major design software vendor. The firm has 80 Autodesk Architecture licenses purchased three years ago. An internal review, prompted by the audit notice, reveals 94 installations across staff computers, including 11 on personal laptops used by remote workers (the firm did not authorize software installation on personal devices). The audit is scheduled for six weeks from now.

**Prompt:** Walk through the steps the IT team should take immediately upon receiving the audit notice. What is the risk if they do nothing? What is the risk if they uninstall the excess copies before the audit? Is pre-audit self-remediation ethical and legally advisable? How should the firm use this experience to build a SAM program that prevents future audit exposure?

## Scenario C: End-of-Life Infrastructure

A university's central IT department discovers that 40% of its server infrastructure runs operating systems that have reached end-of-life — primarily Windows Server 2012 R2, for which Microsoft ended Extended Support in October 2023. Migrating or upgrading these servers would cost an estimated $2.1 million. The university's IT budget for the year is $3.4 million and is already fully allocated. Some servers run administrative systems used daily by faculty, students, and staff.

**Prompt:** From an ITAM perspective, how should the university quantify and communicate the risk of running end-of-life infrastructure to non-technical leadership? What are the specific threats that EOS/EOL status creates? Propose a prioritization framework: if only 30% of affected servers can be addressed this budget cycle, how should you decide which ones to address first? What compensating controls could reduce risk for servers that cannot be migrated immediately?
