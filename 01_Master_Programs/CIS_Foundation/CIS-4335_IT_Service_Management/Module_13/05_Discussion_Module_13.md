## Overview

IT Asset Management connects to almost every other dimension of IT operations — security, finance, compliance, change management, and incident response. The scenarios below ask you to reason through real ITAM problems from the perspective of an IT Asset Manager. Focus on applying ITIL 4 principles and ITAM concepts rather than providing generic answers.

## Scenario 1: The Shadow IT Discovery

A healthcare organization conducts its first automated network discovery scan after two years of managing assets via spreadsheet. The results reveal 134 devices on the network that have no corresponding record in any IT-managed inventory. Investigation reveals that departmental managers have been purchasing their own tablets, small servers, and wireless access points using departmental procurement cards — bypassing the IT department entirely. Some of these devices are actively processing patient scheduling data.

**Initial Post Prompt (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM):**

In 175–225 words, respond to the following:

- What ITAM and security risks does this shadow IT discovery create — be specific about at least two distinct risk categories?
- Using ITIL 4 principles, what process or governance change would you recommend to prevent future unauthorized asset acquisition?
- How should the organization handle the 134 discovered devices in the short term — can they simply be added to the CMDB, or are additional steps required?

**Peer Response Prompt (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM):**

Read at least two classmates' posts. In each reply (minimum 75 words):

- Evaluate whether their recommended governance change addresses the root cause or only controls the symptom
- Add one specific regulatory risk they may not have mentioned (consider HIPAA in the context of patient scheduling data)
- Share whether you think the shadow IT problem is primarily a people, process, or technology failure

## Scenario 2: The Software Audit Notice

A law firm receives an unexpected audit notice from Oracle. The firm uses Oracle Database on 14 production servers. The IT manager pulls the license records and discovers: the firm purchased per-processor licenses for 8 servers three years ago, but over time the database was expanded to 14 servers as the firm grew. No additional licenses were purchased. The 6 additional servers were added by the infrastructure team during a capacity expansion and were never evaluated for license compliance.

**Initial Post Prompt (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM):**

In 175–225 words, respond to the following:

- Classify this situation using SAM terminology and explain how the gap occurred based on what the scenario describes.
- What immediate actions should the IT manager take before responding to Oracle's audit notice?
- What ongoing SAM process would have detected this gap before the audit notice arrived? Be specific about the process steps and how often they should run.

**Peer Response Prompt (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM):**

Respond to at least two classmates' posts (minimum 75 words each):

- Evaluate whether their proposed SAM process would realistically catch this type of gap — consider how the expansion was triggered
- Add one consideration about negotiating with Oracle during the audit that they may not have addressed
- Discuss whether this situation represents a technology governance failure, a process failure, or both

## Scenario 3: The Disposal Dilemma

A financial services company is replacing its entire fleet of 850 workstations. The old machines will be sent to a certified electronics recycler who offers three service options: (A) Certificate of Recycling with no sanitization documentation, (B) Department of Defense 5220.22-M software overwrite with certificate, and (C) Physical shredding with witnessed destruction certificate. The machines contain client financial records, employee HR data, and locally cached login credentials. The recycler's cheapest option is A; the most expensive is C. The CFO is pushing for Option A to minimize disposal costs.

**Initial Post Prompt (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM):**

In 175–225 words, respond to the following:

- Evaluate each option against the data protection requirements for this specific scenario.
- Make a recommendation and justify it using both ITIL 4 IT Asset Management principles and at least one relevant regulatory or legal consideration.
- How would you frame the cost argument to the CFO — what is the financial risk of choosing Option A compared to the cost difference between the options?

**Peer Response Prompt (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM):**

Respond to at least two classmates' posts (minimum 75 words each):

- Challenge or affirm their recommendation with a specific data point or regulatory reference
- Discuss whether Option B and Option C are equally acceptable or if the data types in this scenario make one clearly superior
- Add a consideration about vendor due diligence — what should the company verify about the recycler before engaging any of the three options?
