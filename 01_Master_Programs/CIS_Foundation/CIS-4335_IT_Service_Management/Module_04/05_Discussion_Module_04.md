**Course:** CIS-4335 IT Service Management — Texas Wesleyan University

**Total Points:** 10

## Overview

This discussion asks you to analyze real IT management situations through the lens of the seven ITIL 4 Guiding Principles. Choose one scenario from the three options below. Write an initial post identifying and applying the relevant principles, then reply constructively to at least two classmates.

Identify principles by their exact ITIL 4 names. Paraphrasing principle names or describing principles without naming them will not earn full credit.

---

## Scenario A: The Big-Bang ERP Implementation

A retail chain with 180 stores is replacing its enterprise resource planning (ERP) system, which manages inventory, purchasing, HR, and finance. The project team, under pressure from the CEO to show results quickly, decides to deploy the new ERP to all 180 stores simultaneously on the same weekend. No pilot was run. Staff training consists of a 2-hour online module released one week before go-live. The existing ERP workflows were not mapped before the new system was designed. On go-live weekend, critical inventory transactions fail, payroll processing stalls, and store managers resort to spreadsheets. The rollout is declared a disaster and the project manager is removed.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Identify three specific Guiding Principles that were violated in this scenario. For each, describe exactly what the team did (or failed to do) that constitutes the violation. Use the exact principle names.
2. Redesign the implementation approach using at least three Guiding Principles to produce a different outcome. Be specific about what would change in the plan and why.
3. The CEO justified the big-bang approach by saying: "We need to show results fast — incremental implementations take too long and lose momentum." Using ITIL 4 Guiding Principles, explain why this reasoning is flawed and what the CEO should have said instead.

## Scenario B: The Ignored Monitoring Data

A cloud services company has an excellent monitoring platform that collects performance data from all 47 of its production services in real time. However, the monitoring platform generates 3,400 alerts per day. The operations team has become so overwhelmed with alert volume that they have begun ignoring all but the highest-severity alerts. As a result, two services experienced gradual degradation over a period of three weeks that eventually caused a major outage — all of which would have been visible from the monitoring data if anyone had been reviewing it.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Identify which Guiding Principles are most relevant to the alert-flooding problem and to the team's response to it. Explain how each principle you identify would have changed the situation if applied earlier.
2. The operations manager argues: "More monitoring data is always better. We should add even more alert sources to get a more complete picture." Identify which Guiding Principle conflicts most directly with this reasoning and explain why.
3. Propose a resolution to the alert flooding problem using at least two Guiding Principles. Describe specific actions, not just principles in the abstract.

## Scenario C: The Perfect Process That No One Uses

A logistics company's IT department spent eight months designing a comprehensive change enablement process. The process document is 47 pages, includes 22 workflow steps, requires input from seven stakeholders for every change, and uses a custom-built form with 62 fields. Six months after launch, the compliance team discovers that 71% of changes are being submitted as "emergency changes" to bypass the normal workflow, and that the change advisory board is approving 98% of all changes submitted to it without substantive review.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Identify which Guiding Principles were violated during the design of this change enablement process. Be specific about the evidence from the scenario that demonstrates each violation.
2. The 98% approval rate by the change advisory board suggests the board is not performing substantive review. Identify which Guiding Principle explains why this is a problem and what the principle would recommend.
3. Design a simplified change enablement process using the Keep It Simple and Practical and Optimize and Automate principles. Describe what the process would look like, how many steps it would have, and what would be automated vs. what would require human judgment.

## A Note from Professor Nash

One of the things I want you to notice in these scenarios is that the Guiding Principles rarely fail in isolation. Organizations that violate Progress Iteratively with Feedback typically also violate Collaborate and Promote Visibility and Think and Work Holistically. When a principle is ignored, others tend to follow. Your goal in this course is not to memorize seven labels but to develop a diagnostic instinct — the ability to read a situation and identify which principles, if applied, would have changed the outcome.
