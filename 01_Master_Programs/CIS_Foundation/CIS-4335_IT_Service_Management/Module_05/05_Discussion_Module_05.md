# Discussion Forum: Module 05 — Service Value Chain Activities

**Course:** CIS-4335 IT Service Management — Texas Wesleyan University
**Instructor:** Professor Nash
**Total Points:** 10

---

## Overview

This discussion asks you to map real IT service scenarios to Service Value Chain activities and analyze how value streams work in practice. Choose one scenario from the three options below. Write an initial post applying the SVC framework, then reply constructively to at least two classmates.

Use the exact names of the SVC activities in your post. Describing what happens without naming the activity will not earn full credit.

---

## Scenario A: The Hospital Patient Portal Launch

A regional hospital is launching a new patient portal that allows patients to view lab results, request prescription refills, schedule appointments, and message their care team. The project has been in development for 14 months. The portal is built on a third-party platform. Clinical staff participated in a beta test three months ago and submitted 47 feedback items, 18 of which were marked as critical. The hospital's IT team has configured the platform, integrated it with the electronic health record system, and trained 80 front-desk staff on the new workflow.

On launch day, the IT service desk receives 200 calls from patients who cannot log in because the multi-factor authentication system does not work with several popular email domains. The issue is resolved in 6 hours, but patient satisfaction surveys conducted one week later show significant frustration with the login experience.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Map the 14-month project to the appropriate SVC activities. Which activities were involved in which phases of the project? Be specific about which activity handled the beta feedback, which handled the go-live support, and which should be activated after the launch failure.
2. The 18 critical beta feedback items should have triggered action before go-live. Which SVC activity was responsible for processing this feedback, and which Guiding Principle describes why acting on it was the right course of action?
3. Design a value stream for the ongoing operation of the patient portal after launch. Include at least four SVC activities and explain what happens in each.

## Scenario B: The Retail IT Modernization

A national retail chain with 240 locations is modernizing its IT infrastructure. The project involves replacing the point-of-sale systems at all locations, upgrading the network equipment, migrating from on-premise servers to a cloud infrastructure, and implementing a new IT service management platform.

The CIO describes the situation: "We are rebuilding while the plane is flying. Every day those 240 stores are open, they need their systems to work. We cannot take anything down during store hours. But we also cannot wait — our current systems are five years past their refresh cycle."

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Identify which SVC activities are most critical in this scenario and explain why. Which activity is most critical for ensuring that the POS replacement does not disrupt store operations? Which activity is most important for ensuring the new IT service management platform is adopted by IT staff?
2. The CIO describes working on modernization while stores are live — "rebuilding while the plane is flying." Which ITIL 4 Guiding Principle most directly addresses how to manage this kind of parallel operation? Connect your answer to a specific SVC activity.
3. What value stream would you design for replacing the POS system at a single store? List the SVC activities in order, describe what happens in each, and identify one risk to manage at each step.

## Scenario C: The University IT Team's Backlog

A university IT department has a documented backlog of 340 unresolved items: 112 open incidents more than 30 days old, 89 service requests that have not been fulfilled in more than two weeks, 67 known problems with no assigned owner, and 72 improvement ideas submitted through the staff suggestion system that have never been reviewed.

The new IT director says: "We have a lot of data here. The challenge is that we are so busy delivering services day-to-day that we never have time to step back and work on improvement. The urgent always beats the important."

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Map the four categories of backlog items to the SVC activities that should be handling them. Which activity is responsible for each category?
2. The IT director describes being trapped in day-to-day delivery with no time for improvement. Which ITIL 4 Guiding Principle explains why this is a dangerous pattern, and which SVC activity is being systematically neglected?
3. Design a practical approach for the IT director to begin clearing the backlog without hiring additional staff. Use at least two Guiding Principles and at least two SVC activities in your recommendation.

Write 175–225 words. Your post must:

* Identify which scenario (A, B, or C) you are responding to at the top of your post.
* Address all three numbered prompts for your chosen scenario.
* Use the exact names of at least four SVC activities in context.
* Be written in your own words — do not copy definitions verbatim from the reading guide.

Initial posts are due Wednesday at 11:59 PM.

## Peer Response Requirements

Reply to at least two classmates. Each response must be at least 75 words and must do one or more of the following:

* Challenge an SVC activity assignment your classmate made — if you believe the work described belongs to a different activity, explain why.
* Add a Guiding Principle connection your classmate did not mention.
* Compare the value stream your classmate designed to your own — what is different, and what does that reveal about how the SVC is used differently in different contexts?

## A Note from Professor Nash

The SVC is one of those concepts that becomes genuinely intuitive with practice. The first time you apply it to a scenario it feels mechanical. By the fifth time, you start to notice naturally which activities are in play when you read about an IT situation. That intuition is exactly what the Foundation exam is testing. These discussions are your practice reps — take them seriously.
