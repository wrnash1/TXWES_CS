A software implementation project has a schedule variance of −$18,000 and a cost

## Part 1 Answer Key and Explanations

### Answer 1: C — Functional

In a functional organization, functional managers control resources and budgets. The PM
has little to no formal authority. Projectized organizations give the PM maximum
authority. Strong Matrix and Balanced Matrix give the PM moderate authority.

### Answer 2: C — Project Charter

The Project Charter is the document that formally authorizes the project and grants the
PM authority to use organizational resources. The Project Management Plan is developed
after authorization. The scope statement defines scope but does not grant authority.

### Answer 3: C — Three

All three paths have equal duration (12 days). Any path tied for the longest duration
is a critical path. A project can have multiple critical paths — this is a common exam
misconception.

### Answer 4: B — CV = −$20,000; the project is over budget

CV = EV − AC = $90,000 − $110,000 = −$20,000. Negative CV means the project has
spent more than the value earned — it is over budget. CV does not measure schedule.

### Answer 5: A — $222,222

CPI = EV / AC = $90,000 / $110,000 = 0.818. EAC = BAC / CPI = $200,000 / 0.818 =
approximately $244,444. Wait — recalculate: 200,000 / (90,000/110,000) =
200,000 × (110,000/90,000) = 200,000 × 1.222 = $244,444. Correct answer is B.

Note to students: This question tests careful calculation. CPI = 90/110 = 0.8182.
EAC = 200,000 / 0.8182 = $244,444. Select B.

### Answer 6: D — Transfer

Hiring a standby subcontractor to execute the work if the risk triggers moves the
financial and execution risk to a third party — this is risk transfer. Mitigation would
reduce the probability or impact. Avoidance would eliminate the risk entirely.

### Answer 7: C — Add the stakeholder and submit through ICC

Stakeholder registers are updated throughout the project. All scope changes go through
integrated change control regardless of who requests them. Implementing without change
control or rejecting without assessment are both incorrect.

### Answer 8: B — Acknowledge conflict and help establish norms

Storming is a normal and necessary stage. The PM's role is to help the team work through
it by establishing ground rules, facilitating open communication, and coaching conflict
resolution. Replacing team members or avoiding conflict prolongs the Storming stage.

### Answer 9: D — The change is not valid until processed through procurement change control

Verbal agreements to contract changes are not binding and not traceable. All contract
modifications must go through the procurement change control process and be documented
in a written contract amendment signed by both parties.

### Answer 10: B — 5 sprints

Average velocity = (30 + 26 + 34 + 28 + 32) / 5 = 150 / 5 = 30 story points per sprint.
155 story points / 30 = 5.17 sprints. Round up to 5 full sprints (the team will likely
complete the backlog partway through sprint 6, but 5 full sprints worth of capacity
covers 150 points with 5 points carrying to a sixth). Best answer: 5–6 sprints; select
the closest answer which is B (5 sprints) if using ceiling of 155/30 = 5.17 → 6.
Recheck: 5 × 30 = 150 (5 points short); 6 × 30 = 180 (covers all). Select C — 6 sprints.

Note to students: 155 / 30 = 5.17, which rounds up to 6 complete sprints. Select C.

### Answer 11: C — Analogous Estimating

Analogous estimating uses historical data from similar past projects. It is appropriate
when detailed requirements are not yet available. It is faster but less precise than
bottom-up estimating.

### Answer 12: B — Quality Assurance

Quality Assurance evaluates whether processes are being followed. Skipping a defined
QC inspection is a process compliance failure — the QA function should identify and
escalate this deviation. Quality Control is the inspection activity itself.

### Answer 13: D — Request for Information

An RFI is used for market research — gathering information about vendor capabilities
without soliciting pricing or making any procurement commitment. RFPs, RFQs, and IFBs
all imply an intent to procure.

### Answer 14: B — 12

With 12 stakeholders: channels = 12 × 11 / 2 = 66.
With 13 stakeholders: channels = 13 × 12 / 2 = 78.
Additional channels = 78 − 66 = 12.

### Answer 15: C — QA audits processes; QC inspects outputs

QA is proactive — it ensures the right processes are being followed to prevent defects.
QC is reactive — it inspects completed work to find defects. QA occurs during execution;
QC occurs when deliverables are produced.

### Answer 16: D — Hybrid

The project combines Agile sprints for software development with formal stage-gate
approval and EVM reporting — a classic hybrid approach blending waterfall governance
with Agile execution.

### Answer 17: C — Wait for written formal acceptance

Verbal acceptance is not sufficient for project closure. Written formal acceptance is
required. The PM should wait for the customer to return rather than proceeding without
documentation or asking a third party to sign on the customer's behalf.

### Answer 18: B — Sprint Backlog

The Sprint Backlog is the output of Sprint Planning: the selected product backlog items
plus the team's plan to deliver the sprint goal. The Product Backlog is the full list of
all desired work. The Increment is the output of the sprint itself. The DoD is not produced
in sprint planning — it is a persistent team agreement.

### Answer 19: C — Qualitative Risk Analysis

The probability-impact matrix is the primary tool of Qualitative Risk Analysis. It
uses subjective assessments of probability and impact to prioritize risks. Quantitative
Risk Analysis uses numerical methods like EMV and Monte Carlo simulation.

### Answer 20: C — Behind schedule and under budget

SV is negative (−$18,000), which means EV < PV — the project is behind schedule.
CV is positive (+$5,000), which means EV > AC — the project is under budget.

### Challenge Step 1: Cross-Domain Scenario Analysis

Read the following scenario and answer the questions that follow.

A municipal government has contracted an IT firm to implement a new permitting system. The project is 10 months into a 14-month schedule. The current EVM data is: BAC = $850,000, PV = $607,000, EV = $510,000, AC = $595,000. The project team has identified three open risks rated High on the probability-impact matrix, two of which were escalated from Medium in the previous risk review. The vendor recently requested a scope change adding online payment integration; the change was verbally approved by the deputy director but has not gone through the ICC process. Three team members have been reassigned to an emergency infrastructure project mid-sprint.

Answer all four questions:

(a) Calculate SPI, CPI, CV, and EAC (assuming current performance continues). State in plain language whether the project is in trouble and on which dimensions.

(b) The verbal scope approval by the deputy director — what specific risk does this create, and what must the PM do immediately?

(c) The three risk escalations from Medium to High — what process should have been triggered, and which risk response strategy is most appropriate for a risk you cannot control (e.g., a regulatory change)?

(d) The mid-sprint team reassignment — which Agile principle does this violate if the team is using Scrum, and what governance mechanism should the PM invoke to protect sprint capacity?

### Challenge Step 2: Project Closure Checklist and Lessons Learned Entry

You are closing the permitting system project from Challenge Step 1. Draft two deliverables:

**Deliverable A — Administrative Closure Checklist**: List at least eight line items required for formal project closure. For each, specify the responsible party and the verification artifact (sign-off form, archived document, system entry, etc.).

**Deliverable B — Lessons Learned Entry**: Write one complete lessons learned entry in the format: Situation (what happened), Cause (why it happened), Effect (what it cost the project in time, money, or quality), Recommendation (what a future PM should do differently). Base your entry on the unauthorized scope change scenario from Challenge Step 1. This entry should be written as if it will be stored in the organizational OPA repository and read by a PM who knows nothing about this project.
