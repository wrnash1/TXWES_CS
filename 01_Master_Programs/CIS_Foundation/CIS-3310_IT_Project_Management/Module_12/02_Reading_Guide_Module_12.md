## Introduction

Communication management and stakeholder management are two of the most interpersonally demanding knowledge areas in IT project management. Together, they govern how a PM builds relationships, distributes information, manages expectations, and maintains engagement across a diverse audience. This reading guide provides the complete reference set for both areas including the communications formula, RACI rules, stakeholder analysis frameworks, and all Project+ exam tips.

---

## Section 1 — High-Yield Glossary

### Communication Management Plan

A subsidiary project management plan that documents the information needs of all stakeholders: what information each stakeholder needs, in what format, through which channel, at what frequency, and who is responsible for delivering it.

### Communications Formula

The formula for calculating the number of potential communication channels on a project: `n(n-1)/2`, where n equals the number of people on the project team. Used to quantify communication complexity.

### Push Communication

Information sent directly to specific recipients who receive it without actively seeking it. Examples: email updates, status reports, meeting minutes. Use when recipients must receive the information regardless of whether they seek it.

### Pull Communication

Information made available in a central location for stakeholders to retrieve when needed. Examples: SharePoint sites, document repositories, project portals. Use for reference materials, large files, or information with varying audience interest.

### Interactive Communication

Real-time, two-way communication between two or more parties. Examples: meetings, phone calls, video conferences, instant messaging. Use when discussion, decisions, or problem-solving are required.

### Stakeholder

Any individual, group, or organization that may affect, be affected by, or perceive themselves to be affected by a project decision, activity, or outcome.

### Stakeholder Register

The primary stakeholder management document. Records each stakeholder's name, role, contact information, interests, expectations, influence level, potential impact, and engagement strategy. Created during identification; updated throughout the project.

### Power-Interest Grid

A stakeholder analysis tool that plots stakeholders on two axes — power (authority to influence the project) and interest (level of concern about outcomes) — to determine the appropriate engagement strategy for each quadrant.

### Salience Model

A stakeholder classification model based on three attributes: power (ability to impose will), legitimacy (appropriateness of involvement), and urgency (time sensitivity of interests). Stakeholders with all three attributes are highly salient.

### RACI Matrix

A responsibility assignment matrix using four roles: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome — exactly one per task), Consulted (provides input before completion), and Informed (notified after completion).

### Stakeholder Engagement Assessment Matrix

A tool that compares each stakeholder's current engagement level to their desired engagement level. Engagement levels: Unaware, Resistant, Neutral, Supportive, Leading.

### Kickoff Meeting

A formal meeting at the start of the project or project phase that introduces the team, reviews the charter and scope, establishes communication norms, and creates shared understanding of goals and roles.

### Issue Log

A document that records raised concerns, open issues, their current status, the person responsible for resolution, and the resolution date. Provides accountability and transparency for stakeholder concerns.

### Noise

Any interference that distorts or interrupts a communication signal. Noise exists at every stage of the sender-receiver model: encoding, transmission, decoding, and feedback.

## Section 2 — Communications Formula Reference

### Formula

`Number of communication channels = n(n-1)/2`

Where n = total number of people on the project.

### Common Values

| Team Size (n) | Channels `n(n-1)/2` |
|---------------|---------------------|
| 3 | 3 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 8 | 28 |
| 10 | 45 |
| 12 | 66 |
| 15 | 105 |
| 20 | 190 |

### Adding One Person

When one person is added to a team of size n, the number of new channels added equals n (the new total size minus 1). Example: adding a member to an 8-person team creates 8 new channels (36 - 28 = 8).

### Why This Matters

Communication channels scale faster than team size. Doubling the team from 5 to 10 increases channels from 10 to 45 — a 4.5x increase. This explains why large teams require formal communication plans and why adding members to a late project increases overhead.

## Section 3 — Sender-Receiver Communication Model

| Stage | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Sender | Originates the message; selects channel |
| Encoding | Converting thoughts or data into a transmittable message |
| Channel/Medium | The transmission method (email, meeting, phone, report) |
| Decoding | Receiver's interpretation of the received message |
| Receiver | The intended audience of the message |
| Feedback | Receiver's response confirming understanding or requesting clarification |
| Noise | Any interference at any stage that distorts the message |

## Section 4 — Communication Methods Comparison

| Method | Direction | Best Use | Examples |
|--------|-----------|----------|---------|
| Push | One-way outbound | Required updates; time-sensitive information | Status reports, emails, meeting minutes |
| Pull | Self-service | Reference materials; large audiences with varying needs | SharePoint, wikis, project portals |
| Interactive | Two-way real-time | Decisions, problem-solving, relationship-building | Meetings, calls, video conferences |

## Section 5 — Power-Interest Grid Strategies

| Quadrant | Power | Interest | Strategy |
|---------|-------|----------|---------|
| Manage Closely | High | High | Frequent engagement; two-way communication; keep well-informed and involved |
| Keep Satisfied | High | Low | Regular updates; respect their time; avoid surprises; minimal detail |
| Keep Informed | Low | High | Regular informational updates; acknowledge their concerns; engage in feedback collection |
| Monitor | Low | Low | Minimal engagement; periodic check-ins; watch for changes in position |

## Section 6 — Stakeholder Engagement Levels

| Level | Description | Action Required |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| Unaware | Does not know about the project | Inform and introduce the project |
| Resistant | Aware but actively opposed | Understand root cause; address concerns directly; build trust |
| Neutral | Aware but neither supportive nor opposed | Engage and build interest; explain benefits |
| Supportive | Aware and supportive | Maintain engagement; leverage as advocate |
| Leading | Actively engaged and driving success | Empower as champion; involve in key decisions |

## Section 7 — RACI Matrix Rules

### Role Definitions

| Role | Symbol | Definition | Rule |
|------|--------|-----------|------|
| Responsible | R | Person(s) who perform the work | Can be multiple per task |
| Accountable | A | Person who owns the outcome | Exactly one per task — never zero, never two |
| Consulted | C | Person whose input is needed before completion | Two-way communication |
| Informed | I | Person notified after completion | One-way communication |

### RACI Construction Rules

1. Every task must have exactly one A (Accountable)
2. Every task must have at least one R (Responsible)
3. A single person can hold A and R for the same task
4. C and I roles are optional but should reflect real communication needs
5. If a person has no role on a task, leave their cell blank — do not use N/A

### Common RACI Errors

- Two people listed as Accountable for the same task (accountability diffusion)
- No Accountable assigned (accountability vacuum)
- Everyone listed as Accountable (same as no one being accountable)
- Confusing Consulted (two-way, before) with Informed (one-way, after)

## Section 9 — Status Report Components

A well-structured status report includes:

1. Report date and reporting period
2. Overall project health (green/yellow/red) with brief explanation
3. Milestones completed since last report
4. Milestones planned for next reporting period
5. Schedule status (on track, behind, ahead — quantified)
6. Budget status (planned vs. actual — include CPI if available)
7. Open issues and decisions needed from sponsor or stakeholders
8. Top risks and current status
9. Key accomplishments
10. Upcoming activities and next steps

## Section 10 — Project+ Exam Tips

**Tip 1 — Memorize the communications formula:**
`n(n-1)/2` is tested in multiple formats. Direct calculation: "how many channels with 12 people?" Incremental calculation: "a new person joins a 7-person team — how many new channels?" The increment = n-1 of the new team size.

**Tip 2 — Exactly one A per task in RACI:**
Any question describing accountability confusion on a project is signaling a RACI error. Two A's = accountability diffusion. Zero A's = accountability vacuum. One A always.

**Tip 3 — Consulted vs. Informed:**
Consulted is two-way communication before the work is done (you ask for their input and use it). Informed is one-way communication after the decision or action (you tell them what happened). The exam distinguishes these with scenario language.

**Tip 4 — Push, Pull, Interactive — match the scenario:**
"The PM sends weekly status reports to all stakeholders" = Push. "Project documents are stored in SharePoint for team access" = Pull. "The team holds a decision-making meeting" = Interactive.

**Tip 5 — Power-Interest grid: High Power/High Interest = Manage Closely:**
The exam will describe a stakeholder and ask for the appropriate strategy. High Power/High Interest stakeholders must be closely and actively managed. Do not confuse with High Power/Low Interest (keep satisfied — they need respect but not constant engagement).

**Tip 6 — The kickoff meeting occurs after project planning, before execution:**
Kickoff is the transition point from Planning to Executing. It is not the first project meeting — it is the formal launch meeting that signals execution has begun.

**Tip 7 — Stakeholder engagement is ongoing, not one-time:**
The exam describes scenarios where a PM creates the stakeholder register and does nothing more. This is a process failure. Engagement must be actively managed throughout the project lifecycle.

**Tip 8 — Resistant stakeholders need root cause analysis, not more information:**
A resistant stakeholder who receives more emails or reports does not become supportive — they become more annoyed. Resistance requires conversation, active listening, and addressing the underlying concern.
