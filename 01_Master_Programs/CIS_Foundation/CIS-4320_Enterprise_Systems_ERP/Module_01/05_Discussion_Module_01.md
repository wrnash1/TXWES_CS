## Overview

This discussion forum asks you to apply Module 01 concepts to realistic business scenarios. You will read one of three scenarios, write an original post analyzing it, and then respond substantively to at least two classmates who analyzed different scenarios. The goal is not just to restate definitions — it is to demonstrate that you can think like an enterprise systems consultant.

---

## Instructions

### Initial Post (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM)

Choose **one** of the three scenarios below (A, B, or C). Write a response of **175-225 words** that directly addresses the questions posed for your chosen scenario. Begin your post by stating which scenario you selected.

Your post must:

- Reference at least one concept from the Module 01 reading guide or video by name (e.g., functional silo, integrated data, modular architecture, master data, single source of truth)
- Apply that concept to the specific details of the scenario — do not just define it in the abstract
- Propose a concrete recommendation supported by your reasoning

### Peer Responses (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM)

Reply to at least **two classmates** who chose **different scenarios** from yours. Each reply must be substantive — at minimum 60 words — and must do one of the following:

- Build on your classmate's recommendation with an additional supporting point
- Respectfully challenge an assumption in their analysis with an alternative perspective
- Connect their scenario to one you have encountered in a job, internship, or case study

Replies that simply say "Good post!" or restate what the classmate already wrote will receive zero points for that response.

## Scenarios

### Scenario A: The Inventory Discrepancy

A regional grocery chain with 14 stores uses a separate inventory spreadsheet at each location. Each store manager updates the spreadsheet manually at the end of each day. Corporate headquarters aggregates all 14 spreadsheets every Monday morning to produce a weekly inventory report. Last quarter, the company over-ordered $340,000 worth of perishable goods because Monday's report reflected Saturday's inventory levels, and several stores had received large weekend deliveries that were not yet recorded. The over-ordered goods spoiled before they could be sold.

**Your task:** Identify the specific functional silo problem causing this financial loss. Explain how an integrated ERP system with a centralized inventory module would eliminate the root cause. Would a daily email summary from each store solve the same problem? Why or why not?

### Scenario B: The New Employee Gap

A mid-size law firm hires 12 new employees each quarter. HR enters each new hire into an Excel roster and then emails the IT department to request system access, emails the payroll service provider to add the employee to the pay run, and emails the facilities manager to assign a desk. On average it takes 9 business days for a new employee to have all three items completed. During that time the employee cannot access systems, may miss a payroll cycle, and is working from a temporary workspace.

**Your task:** Describe the silo architecture problem this scenario illustrates. Explain what a Human Capital Management (HCM) module integrated with IT provisioning and payroll would change about this process. What concept from today's module most directly describes the improvement — and why?

### Scenario C: The Month-End Reporting Battle

A consumer electronics distributor closes its books on the last day of each month. The finance team uses QuickBooks. The sales team uses a standalone CRM. The warehouse uses a barcode scanning system with its own database. At month-end, the finance director, sales director, and operations director each present different revenue figures — because QuickBooks shows shipped-and-invoiced orders, the CRM shows booked orders (some not yet shipped), and the warehouse system shows picked orders (some not yet invoiced). The CEO spends three days each month arbitrating which number is correct before approving the board report.

**Your task:** Name the ERP design principle that would resolve the discrepancy among these three numbers. Describe specifically what would be different about month-end close if the company implemented an integrated ERP system. What is the business risk of allowing this discrepancy to continue for another year?

## Professor Nash's Note

Enterprise systems thinking is the skill that separates a junior IT technician from a business systems analyst. When you write your post, try to think like the consultant in the room — the person who has to explain to a CFO why a $2 million software project is worth doing. That is the mindset both the Salesforce and SAP certification programs are testing when they ask you scenario-based questions. Your discussion posts are practice for exactly that kind of thinking.
