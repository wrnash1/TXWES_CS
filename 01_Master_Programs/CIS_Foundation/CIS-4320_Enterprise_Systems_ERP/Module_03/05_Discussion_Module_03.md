## Overview

This forum puts you in the role of a technology advisor helping an organization navigate the ERP selection process. Choose one scenario, write an original post applying vendor landscape and selection concepts, then respond substantively to two classmates who chose different scenarios.

---

## Instructions

### Initial Post (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM)

Choose **one** of the three scenarios below (A, B, or C). Write a response of **175-225 words** directly addressing the questions in your chosen scenario. State your scenario choice at the start of your post.

Your post must:

- Name at least one specific ERP vendor and explain why it is or is not a good fit for the scenario
- Reference at least one concept from the Module 03 reading guide by name (TCO, functional fit, multi-tenancy, RFP, etc.)
- Make a concrete recommendation with a reason grounded in the scenario's specific details

### Peer Responses (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM)

Reply to at least **two classmates** who chose **different scenarios** from yours. Each reply must be at least 60 words and must:

- Agree with, refine, or respectfully challenge the vendor recommendation your classmate made
- Add one specific supporting fact (about a vendor, a deployment model, or a TCO factor) that your classmate did not mention
- Ask a clarifying question that would improve the analysis if it were a real client engagement

## Scenarios

### Scenario A: The Cloud Skeptic

A 900-employee regional bank is evaluating ERP vendors. Their CFO has been reading industry articles about cloud data breaches and insists that any new ERP system must be installed on the bank's own servers in their private data center to guarantee data control. The bank currently uses a 15-year-old on-premise financial system that requires a $400,000 upgrade project every three years just to stay supported. The bank's IT team has three employees: a network administrator, a desktop support specialist, and a part-time DBA contractor.

**Your task:** Is the CFO's insistence on on-premise deployment well-reasoned given this company's IT capacity? Evaluate the TCO implications of continuing with an on-premise deployment versus a SaaS alternative. Name one specific ERP vendor whose cloud deployment model would address the CFO's data control concern while reducing operational burden on the small IT team. What specific feature or certification would you reference when addressing the CFO's security concern?

### Scenario B: The Ecosystem Decision

A $500 million logistics company already uses Salesforce Sales Cloud for their sales team and Microsoft Azure for all cloud infrastructure. They are replacing a legacy Oracle ERP system with a modern platform. The selection is down to two finalists: SAP S/4HANA Cloud Private Edition and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain. The logistics operations team wants strong transportation and warehouse management capabilities. The IT director wants to minimize integration complexity.

**Your task:** Compare the two finalists specifically on integration fit with Salesforce and Azure. Which vendor do you lean toward recommending for this company's profile and why? What selection criterion should be weighted most heavily for this specific decision, and why does it override the others?

### Scenario C: The Budget-First Buyer

A 200-employee specialty food distributor has received RFP responses from three vendors. The CFO's only directive is: "Pick the lowest cost option." The three finalists quoted as follows: Vendor A (NetSuite) at $85,000 per year subscription with $320,000 implementation. Vendor B (SAP Business One) at a $220,000 perpetual license with $280,000 implementation and $44,000/year maintenance. Vendor C (Microsoft Business Central) at $72,000 per year subscription with $290,000 implementation.

**Your task:** The CFO is using Year 1 cost to choose. Explain why comparing Year 1 costs alone is the wrong approach. Calculate a simplified 5-year total for at least two of the three vendors (use the numbers provided, assume no upgrade projects for this exercise). Which vendor do you recommend for a 5-year horizon and why? What risk are you accepting with the lowest-TCO option that the CFO should understand before signing?

## Professor Nash's Note

Real ERP selection engagements almost always have a budget constraint, a political preference, and a technical constraint pulling in different directions. The consultant's job is to make the best recommendation based on evidence — even when the client doesn't want to hear it. In Scenario C, the CFO is making a common mistake. Your ability to articulate why that mistake matters is exactly the kind of business reasoning that makes the difference between a technician and an advisor.
