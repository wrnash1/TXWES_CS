3. Course Overview & Learning Outcomes

Catalog Description: CSC-6361 Advanced Computer Networks is an intensive graduate-level course designed for students pursuing careers in senior network engineering, cloud architecture, and infrastructure management. The curriculum examines enterprise-scale networking technologies at the depth and rigor aligned with the Cisco Certified Network Professional (CCNP) Enterprise track (ENCOR 350-401 and ENARSI 300-410). Students analyze, configure, and troubleshoot complex multi-site routed and switched topologies using industry-standard protocols, command-line interfaces, and network automation frameworks.

Course Prerequisites: Undergraduate coursework in networking (e.g., CIS-3321 Network Administration or equivalent), Cisco CCNA-level certification, or instructor approval. Graduate standing in MS in Computer Science or MS in Information Technology.

Student Learning Outcomes (SLOs / CLOs)

Upon successful completion of this course, graduate students will be able to:

# Course Learning Objective (CLO) Professional / Credential Alignment
1 Design, configure, and optimize multi-area OSPFv2/v3 and EIGRP Named Mode topologies, implementing mutual redistribution with route-tag loop prevention. CCNP ENARSI (300-410) Domain 1.0
2 Architect resilient campus switching infrastructures integrating 802.1Q VLAN trunking, Rapid PVST+ / MSTP spanning tree, and LACP EtherChannels. CCNP ENCOR (350-401) Domain 2.0
3 Evaluate enterprise WAN architectures, configuring BGP path attributes, MPLS Label Distribution Protocol (LDP), and site-to-site IPsec VPN tunnels. CCNP ENCOR (350-401) Domain 3.0
4 Implement infrastructure security hardening including Control Plane Policing (CoPP), Dynamic ARP Inspection (DAI), and DHCP Snooping. CCNP ENCOR (350-401) Domain 5.0
5 Formulate Quality of Service (QoS) DiffServ marking/queuing policies and First-Hop Redundancy Protocols (HSRP/VRRP), and inspect RESTCONF/YANG automation. CCNP ENCOR (350-401) Domain 6.0
6 Synthesize advanced networking principles through scholarly technical writing (5–7 page Graduate Research Paper) and a comprehensive multi-site troubleshooting capstone. SACSCOC Graduate Rigor Standards

7. Institutional & Departmental Policies

Academic Integrity & Code of Conduct

Academic integrity is foundational to graduate scholarship. All submitted lab configurations, packet traces, research analyses, and exam submissions must reflect solely your own intellectual work. Copying topologies, exchanging configuration scripts, or utilizing unauthorized artificial intelligence generation without proper attribution constitutes academic dishonesty. Violations will be immediately referred to the Academic Integrity Committee in accordance with the Texas Wesleyan University Graduate Catalog, resulting in a minimum penalty of zero on the deliverable and possible course failure (grade of F) or administrative dismissal.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) may be used as supplementary tutors to clarify protocol behaviors or troubleshoot syntax errors. However, all submitted text, analysis reports, and research papers must represent your own original writing. Submitting AI-generated text verbatim without disclosure is treated as academic plagiarism.

Graduate Late Submission Policy

  • Initial Discussion Posts: Must be posted by Thursday 11:59 PM CT to enable peer critique. No late initial posts accepted.
  • Weekly Labs & Quizzes: Submissions after Sunday 11:59 PM CT incur a 10% penalty per 24-hour period up to 72 hours maximum. Submissions after 72 hours receive a grade of zero.
  • Graduate Research Paper & Final Exam: Absolutely no late submissions accepted due to strict registrar grade deadline constraints.
