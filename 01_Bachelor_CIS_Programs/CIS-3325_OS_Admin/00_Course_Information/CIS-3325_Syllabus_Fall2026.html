Course Introduction & Excitement

Welcome to OS Admin! In this 3-credit-hour undergraduate course, you will develop practical and analytical competencies in os admin. This course is delivered in a 100% online asynchronous format, providing maximum scheduling flexibility for adult and working learners while delivering rigorous academic content.

Course Description & Prerequisites

This course covers operating system administration, including Linux commands, bash scripting, file systems, package management, and system services.

Prerequisite: CIS-1310 or instructor approval.

Course Learning Objectives (CLOs)

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

Instructional & Learning Methods

The learning methods used in this online course include:

  • ☑ Direct Online Instruction: Structured interactive Reading Guides, visual architecture SVGs, and recorded video walkthroughs.
  • ☑ Hands-On Simulation Labs: Practical exercises, code implementation, and command-line diagnostics.
  • ☑ Discussion Forums: Peer-to-peer technical inquiry, real-world case analysis, and collaborative troubleshooting.
  • ☑ Auto-Graded Quizzes: Timed scenario assessments with instant distractor analysis and explanatory feedback.
  • ☑ Comprehensive Final Assessment: Proctored / comprehensive course examination.

Student Workload Expectation (SACSCOC / THECB Compliance)

3-Credit-Hour Workload Policy: In accordance with federal regulations and the Texas Higher Education Coordinating Board (THECB), a 3-credit-hour course represents approximately 135 total hours of student engagement across a 15-week semester (approximately 9 hours per week):
  • 3.0 Hours/Week: Direct instructional engagement (Reading Guides, visual models, video walkthroughs).
  • 3.5 Hours/Week: Hands-on simulation labs, coding projects, and technical reporting.
  • 2.5 Hours/Week: Discussion board participation, scenario quizzes, and exam preparation.

Academic Integrity & Generative AI Policy

Cheating, plagiarism (submitting another person’s material as one’s own), or completing assignments for another person who will receive academic credit are strictly prohibited at Texas Wesleyan University in accordance with the University Academic Integrity Policy.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy: Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot) may be utilized as supplementary tutors to explain complex technical concepts or brainstorm approaches. However, you may NOT submit AI-generated text, lab worksheets, discussion responses, or quiz answers as your own original work. Any authorized AI assistance must be explicitly cited. Submitting undisclosed AI-generated content is treated as plagiarism.

Class Attendance & Hybrid Course Engagement Policy

Mandatory Weekly In-Person Attendance: This is an officially scheduled Hybrid course. Students are required to attend class in person every Tuesday from 6:00 PM to 8:30 PM. During these classroom sessions, Professor Nash presents PowerPoint lecture decks, conducts comprehensive weekly review of the online materials, answers student technical questions, and provides live guidance on lab setups.

Arriving prepared with questions on the week's reading guide and active laboratory participation are required. Attendance is recorded at every Tuesday meeting.

In addition to in-person attendance, students must maintain continuous online academic engagement in Canvas throughout each week by submitting weekly hands-on labs, participating in online discussion forums, and completing module quizzes prior to the Sunday 11:59 PM deadline.

University Authorized Absences

University regulations authorize absences for students representing Texas Wesleyan in official capacities (e.g., intercollegiate athletics, fine arts, student government, or university-sponsored academic events). Faculty-Initiated Student Withdrawal from a Course

When a student exceeds the permissible number of absences or fails to meet the course participation expectations, the instructor may drop the student using the Faculty Initiated Student Withdrawal process. The Registrar will record a grade of “DP” on the student’s transcript if dropped prior to the deadline, or “WF” (withdrawal failing) if dropped afterward. Note: The last day to receive a “DP” for Fall 2026 is Friday, November 20, 2026.
