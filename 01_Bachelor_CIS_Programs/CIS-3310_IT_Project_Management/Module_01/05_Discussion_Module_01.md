## Overview

Choose one of the three scenarios below (A, B, or C). Write an initial post responding to all three sub-questions for your chosen scenario. Then reply to at least two classmates who chose different scenarios from yours.

## Scenario A: The Endless Help Desk

A regional insurance company employs 12 IT staff members. Most of their time is spent responding to help desk tickets, applying patches, maintaining servers, and managing the company's network. The IT manager tells her director: "We've been running the same processes for years — there's never a finish line." The director, who just completed a project management course, replies: "Some of what you described is operational work. But I think we also have a few projects buried in there."

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Using PMI's definition, identify at least two activities in the IT manager's description that are clearly operational, and explain the characteristic that makes each one operational.
2. Propose one IT initiative the insurance company could launch that would qualify as a true project under PMI's definition. Justify your answer using the terms "temporary," "unique," and "defined end date."
3. If the company decides to deploy a new cloud-based ticketing platform, how does the Triple Constraint apply from day one? Identify the most likely constraint to be fixed and explain why.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

---

## Scenario B: The Scope Negotiation

A mid-sized bank is replacing its online banking platform. The project has a hard deadline of December 1 (a regulatory requirement) and a fixed budget of $1.2 million. Four months into the project, the VP of Digital Banking sends an email to the project manager requesting that bill-pay and mobile check deposit features be added to the scope — features that were explicitly excluded in the approved Project Charter.

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Using the Triple Constraint model, explain what must happen if the VP's requested features are added without changing the deadline or the budget.
2. What should the project manager do before agreeing to or refusing the request? Reference the appropriate PMI process or document in your answer.
3. Explain why a signed Project Charter is the project manager's most important tool in this negotiation. What would the PM lose without one?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

---

## Scenario C: The New PM's First Week

Jordan was just promoted from senior network engineer to project manager for a campus-wide Wi-Fi upgrade project at a private university. The project will affect all 14 academic buildings, must be complete before fall semester, and has a $650,000 budget. Jordan has never managed a project before and is not sure where to start.

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. Which PMI Process Group should Jordan be working in during the first week, and what is the single most important document Jordan should produce? Explain the purpose of that document.
2. Jordan's manager tells her, "Just start working — we'll figure out the formal documentation later." Using PMI principles, explain why Jordan should push back and insist on completing the proper initiation process first.
3. Identify two Enterprise Environmental Factors (EEFs) and two Organizational Process Assets (OPAs) that could realistically influence Jordan's Wi-Fi upgrade project. Explain how each would affect the project.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

The distinction between projects and operations is not just an exam question — it shapes how organizations allocate resources, assign accountability, and measure success. Many IT professionals spend years doing excellent operational work without realizing that the biggest opportunities for career growth lie in leading projects. Learning to recognize that difference, and to articulate it in PMI language, is your first step toward thinking like a project manager. I look forward to reading your posts.
