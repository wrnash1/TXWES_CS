## Scenario A: The Risk That Was Not in the Register

A financial services company is two months into a nine-month core banking system replacement project. The project team conducted a thorough risk identification workshop at kickoff and documented fifteen risks in the Risk Register. At month two, the project's primary third-party integration vendor announces it is being acquired by a competitor. The acquisition creates uncertainty about whether the vendor will continue supporting the APIs the project depends on. This risk was not identified during the initial workshop. The project manager is now under pressure from the sponsor to explain how this was missed and what the team will do about it.

Sub-questions for Scenario A:

1. Is the PM responsible for failing to identify this risk? Evaluate this using PMI's description of risk identification as an iterative process. What does this principle mean for how the team should have been managing the Risk Register during Months 1 and 2?
2. Classify the vendor acquisition scenario as a specific risk category (technical, external, organizational, or project management) and explain your reasoning. Then identify the most appropriate PMI threat response strategy and describe the specific action the PM should take.
3. Implementing your chosen response strategy may create secondary risks. Identify at least two secondary risks that could emerge from the response you recommended and explain how the team should handle them.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario B: The High-Probability Risk Nobody Wanted to Address

An e-commerce company is eight weeks into a platform migration project. The Risk Register contains a risk labeled "Key developer may leave before go-live" with a probability rating of High and an impact rating of High — resulting in a maximum risk score. The project sponsor reviewed the Risk Register four weeks ago and commented: "We can't do anything about employee turnover — just accept it." The PM has passively accepted the risk with no contingency reserve and no response plan. Six weeks later, the key developer gives two weeks' notice. The project is now severely understaffed three months before go-live.

Sub-questions for Scenario B:

1. Evaluate the sponsor's reasoning that "we can't do anything about employee turnover." Is passive acceptance appropriate for a High-High risk? Apply the probability-impact matrix and PMI's guidance on risk prioritization to your answer.
2. What threat response strategies could have been applied to this risk before the developer resigned? Describe at least two specific, actionable responses — using the correct strategy names — that were available to the team.
3. Now that the developer has left, the risk has become an issue. What is the difference between a risk and an issue in PMI terminology, and what should the PM do now that the risk event has occurred?

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Scenario C: The Opportunity That Slipped Away

A healthcare IT company is developing a patient engagement mobile application. During the project, the team learns that a large regional hospital network — not the client — is actively looking for an app vendor and issuing a contract in 60 days. The product being built is highly aligned with the hospital network's stated requirements. One team member proposes that the company pursue the contract in parallel, potentially using the current project's deliverables as a demonstration. The project manager acknowledges the scenario but says: "That is not our problem — we are focused on delivering this project. Let's just accept the opportunity and move on."

Sub-questions for Scenario C:

1. Evaluate the PM's decision to use passive acceptance for this opportunity. Is this the most appropriate response given the nature and potential value of the opportunity? Reference PMI's opportunity response strategies in your answer.
2. Identify the opportunity response strategy that would best capture the value described in the scenario. Name the strategy, explain what it means, and describe two specific actions the PM or organization could take to implement it.
3. What risks (threats) might be created by actively pursuing this opportunity? Identify two potential secondary threats and describe how the PM should balance the pursuit of the opportunity against the protection of the current project.

Your initial post should be 175–225 words and address all three sub-questions.

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

Risk management is the discipline that separates reactive project managers from proactive ones. Every project has risks. The question is whether your team finds them before they become issues or after. A Risk Register that was built once at kickoff and never updated is not a risk management system — it is a historical document. Real risk management is continuous: you update the register as the environment changes, you assign ownership so someone is watching each risk, and you build responses before you need them. The projects that fail spectacularly — the ones that make the case study books — almost always had identifiable risks that were either not documented or not acted on. Your job as a PM is to make sure that never happens on your watch.
