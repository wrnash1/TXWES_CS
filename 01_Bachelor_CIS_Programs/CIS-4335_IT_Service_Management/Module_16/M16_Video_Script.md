## Slide 2: Exam Format and Requirements (1:00–3:00)

Let's start with what you are walking into.

### Exam Specifications

The ITIL 4 Foundation exam is administered by PeopleCert on behalf of Axelos. Key facts:

- **Question count:** 40 multiple-choice questions.
- **Passing score:** 26 out of 40 correct — 65%.
- **Time allowed:** 60 minutes (90 minutes for non-native English speakers with approved accommodation).
- **Question format:** All questions are single best answer — four options (A, B, C, D), one correct.
- **Open book:** No. The exam is closed book.
- **Calculator:** Not required or permitted.
- **Question weighting:** All questions are equally weighted — 1 point each.

### Exam Blueprint

The exam tests across five syllabus areas with approximate weightings:

- **Key concepts of ITIL (15%):** Approximately 6 questions on value, utility, warranty, services, service management, stakeholders, and relationships.
- **ITIL Guiding Principles (15%):** Approximately 6 questions on all seven principles and their application.
- **Four Dimensions (10%):** Approximately 4 questions on Organizations & People, Information & Technology, Partners & Suppliers, Value Streams & Processes.
- **Service Value System and Service Value Chain (20%):** Approximately 8 questions on SVS components, SVC activities, and demand/value concepts.
- **ITIL Management Practices (40%):** Approximately 16 questions distributed across the 15 Foundation-scope practices.

### Which Practices Are In Scope?

The ITIL 4 Foundation exam requires understanding of 15 specific practices at two levels:

**7 practices — "purpose and key terms" level:**

- Continual Improvement
- Change Enablement

**8 additional practices — "purpose only" level:**

## Slide 3: Exam Strategy (3:00–5:00)

Passing the ITIL 4 Foundation exam requires both content knowledge and test-taking strategy.

### Strategy 1: Understand, Don't Memorize

The exam tests application, not recall. Questions are scenario-based — they describe a situation and ask which principle, practice, or concept best applies. Memorizing definitions without understanding application will leave you uncertain on 50% of questions.

Study by asking: "Why does this concept exist? What problem does it solve?"

### Strategy 2: Identify the ITIL Language

ITIL has precise vocabulary. The exam uses exact ITIL terms. When a question mentions "an unplanned interruption to an IT service or reduction in quality," the answer involves Incident Management — because that is the ITIL definition of an incident. Know the definitions.

### Strategy 3: Apply the Process of Elimination

For every question, eliminate obviously wrong answers first. ITIL 4 Foundation questions typically have two clearly wrong distractors and two plausible options. Getting to two options and then reasoning carefully will get you to the right answer.

### Strategy 4: Beware the "Most" Trap

Some questions ask for the "best" or "most appropriate" action. Multiple options may be partially correct. The right answer is the most aligned with ITIL 4 thinking — which typically means the most value-focused, the most collaborative, the most iterative, and the least bureaucratic.

### Strategy 5: Flag and Return

If you encounter a difficult question, mark it and move on. Do not spend more than 90 seconds on any question on the first pass. Return to flagged questions after completing the rest. You have 60 minutes for 40 questions — 90 seconds average per question is comfortable.

### Strategy 6: The Night Before

Do not cram new material the night before. Review your key terms, sleep well, and trust the preparation you have already done.

## Slide 4: Key Concepts Consolidation (5:00–8:00)

Let us walk through the most important conceptual building blocks.

### Value, Utility, and Warranty

- **Value** — the perceived benefit from using a service relative to its cost.
- **Utility** — "what it does" — the functionality that meets the customer's need ("fit for purpose").
- **Warranty** — "how it performs" — assurance that the service is available, capable, and secure ("fit for use").
- **Both utility AND warranty are required to create value.** A service that does what you need but is down 50% of the time has no warranty — no real value.

### Service Relationship Concepts

- **Service provider** — an organization that provides services.
- **Service consumer** — an organization that uses services. May be a **customer** (defines requirements), **user** (uses the service), or **sponsor** (authorizes budget).
- **Service offering** — a formal description of services, including goods, service actions, and service resources.
- **Service relationship** — cooperation between a provider and consumer, involving provision, consumption, and management.

### The Four Dimensions

Every service must be designed and managed considering all four:

- **Organizations and People** — roles, responsibilities, culture, skills.
- **Information and Technology** — data, knowledge, tools, automation.
- **Partners and Suppliers** — vendors, contracts, integrations.
- **Value Streams and Processes** — workflows and activities that create value.

The dimensions are surrounded by external factors: PESTLE (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental).

## Slide 5: Service Value System Consolidation (8:00–10:00)

The SVS is ITIL 4's model of how all components work together to create value.

### SVS Components

- **Guiding Principles** — the seven principles that guide decisions at all levels.
- **Governance** — the means by which the organization directs and controls its activities.
- **Service Value Chain** — the core operating model with six activities.
- **Practices** — 34 sets of organizational resources for performing work.
- **Continual Improvement** — applies to all SVS components.

### The Seven Guiding Principles (memorize all seven)

1. Focus on Value
2. Start Where You Are
3. Progress Iteratively with Feedback
4. Collaborate and Promote Visibility
5. Think and Work Holistically
6. Keep It Simple and Practical
7. Optimize and Automate

### The Six Service Value Chain Activities (memorize all six)

1. **Plan** — ensures shared understanding of direction and continual improvement.
2. **Improve** — applies continual improvement across all activities.
3. **Engage** — understands stakeholder needs and ensures transparency.
4. **Design and Transition** — ensures services meet quality and cost expectations.
5. **Obtain/Build** — acquires and develops service components.
6. **Deliver and Support** — ensures services are delivered and supported per agreements.

### Inputs and Outputs

The SVC takes in **demand** (from customers) and **opportunities** (from the environment) and produces **value** for customers and stakeholders. Value flows through combinations of SVC activities — not a sequential process.

## Slide 6: Practice Consolidation — Core Seven (10:00–12:00)

The seven practices tested at full depth are the heart of the Foundation exam.

### Incident Management

- **Purpose:** Minimize the negative impact of incidents by restoring normal service as quickly as possible.
- **Incident:** An unplanned interruption or quality reduction.
- **Major incident:** Highest impact; requires separate procedure and post-incident review.
- **Key terms:** Incident classification, prioritization, escalation (functional / hierarchical), workaround, resolution.

### Problem Management

- **Purpose:** Reduce likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual and potential causes.
- **Problem:** Unknown cause of one or more incidents.
- **Known error:** A problem with a documented root cause and workaround.
- **Three phases:** Problem identification, control, error control.

### Change Enablement

- **Purpose:** Maximize successful changes by ensuring risks are properly assessed.
- **Change types:** Standard (pre-authorized), Normal (requires authorization), Emergency (expedited).
- **CAB:** Change Advisory Board — provides advice on Normal changes.
- **Change authority:** Person or group who authorizes changes (varies by change type and risk).

### Service Level Management

- **Purpose:** Set clear targets for service performance and ensure services are monitored against targets.
- **SLA:** Service Level Agreement — commitment between provider and customer.
- **OLA:** Operational Level Agreement — internal commitment between IT teams.
- **UC:** Underpinning Contract — agreement with external supplier.
- **Service review:** Regular review of SLA performance with customers.

### Continual Improvement

- **Purpose:** Align practices with changing organizational needs through ongoing improvement.
- **Continual Improvement Register (CIR):** Tracks improvement ideas with priority, owner, status.
- **The CI Model:** What is the vision → Where are we now → Where do we want to be → How do we get there → Take action → Did we get there → How do we keep the momentum going.

### Service Request Management

- **Purpose:** Support agreed service quality by handling service requests efficiently and user-friendly.
- **Service request:** Request from a user for something they are entitled to (not an incident, not a change requiring authorization).
- **Examples:** Password reset, software installation, information request.

### Service Desk

- **Purpose:** Single point of contact between service provider and users.
- **Functions:** Incident logging, service request handling, escalation, communication.
- **Formats:** Local, centralized, virtual, follow-the-sun.
- **Key principle:** Empathy and communication matter as much as technical skill.

## Slide 7: Practice Consolidation — Additional Eight (12:00–13:30)

The additional eight practices are tested at "purpose only" level — know what each practice does, but not detailed processes.

- **IT Asset Management:** Full lifecycle management of IT assets to maximize value and control costs.
- **Service Configuration Management:** Ensures accurate information about CIs and their relationships is available.
- **Release Management:** Makes new or changed services available for use.
- **Deployment Management:** Moves new or changed components to live environments.
- **Monitoring and Event Management:** Systematically observes services and records events; determines action.
- **Information Security Management:** Protects the organization's information from threats.
- **Relationship Management:** Establishes and nurtures links between the organization and stakeholders.
- **Supplier Management:** Manages suppliers and contracts to support service quality.

## Slide 8: Commonly Missed Topics (13:30–14:30)

Based on common exam performance patterns, pay extra attention to these areas:

### Incident vs. Problem vs. Change

These three are frequently confused in scenario questions:

- **Incident:** Something broken right now — restore service.
- **Problem:** Why things break — find and fix root cause.
- **Change:** Planned modification to the environment — requires authorization.

A question like "the service desk closes an incident but the same failure recurs next week" is about Problem Management — the incident was resolved but the problem was not.

### Utility vs. Warranty

"The application processes payroll correctly" = **utility** (fit for purpose).
"The application is available 99.9% of the time" = **warranty** (fit for use).
You need BOTH for value.

### Service vs. Product

A **product** is a configuration of resources that creates value. A **service** helps a customer achieve an outcome without the customer managing the underlying complexity. Services are delivered using products.

### The Watermelon Effect

The watermelon effect occurs when SLA metrics report "green" (passing) while the customer experience is actually "red" (poor). It is named because the outside is green but the inside is red. Caused by measuring the wrong things or measuring averages that hide outliers. Relevant to Service Level Management.
