# Discussion Forum: Module 01 — Introduction to ITIL 4 and Service Management

**Course:** CIS-4335 IT Service Management — Texas Wesleyan University
**Instructor:** Professor Nash
**Total Points:** 10

## Overview

This discussion asks you to connect the foundational ITIL 4 concepts from Module 01 to real or realistic organizational situations. Choose one scenario from the three options below. Write an initial post responding to that scenario, then reply constructively to at least two classmates who may have chosen the same or different scenarios.

Use correct ITIL 4 terminology throughout. Vague responses that restate the prompt without applying framework concepts will not earn full credit.

---

## Scenario A: The University Email Outage

The campus email system goes down on a Sunday night during finals week. Students cannot submit papers, faculty cannot send grade updates, and the IT help desk phone line is flooded with calls. The IT director says, "The servers are technically fine — it's a configuration issue we can fix in two hours." A frustrated faculty member responds, "I don't care about the servers. My students are failing because of this."

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Explain the difference between an output and an outcome as ITIL 4 defines them, and apply that distinction to this scenario.
2. Identify whether this situation represents a utility failure, a warranty failure, or both. Justify your answer using ITIL 4 definitions.
3. Identify which ITIL 4 stakeholder roles are represented in this scenario and describe the primary concern of each role.

---

## Scenario B: The New Streaming Service Launch

A media company launches a new video streaming service for corporate training content. The service allows employees to search, stream, and bookmark training videos from any device. On launch day, 3,000 employees attempt to access the service simultaneously. The platform buckles under the load, delivering choppy video and login failures. The development team notes that the underlying content library and search features work perfectly in testing.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Classify the launch-day failures as utility problems, warranty problems, or both. Use ITIL 4 definitions to support your classification.
2. Identify which of the ITIL 4 Four Dimensions was most likely neglected during the planning and launch of this service. Explain your reasoning.
3. Select one ITIL 4 Guiding Principle that, if applied earlier in the project, could have prevented or reduced the impact of the launch failures. Explain specifically how that principle would have changed the team's actions.

---

## Scenario C: The IT Department as a Service Provider

A hospital's internal IT department supports clinical staff, administrative teams, and patients accessing an online portal. The department's CIO says: "We don't have customers — we have users. IT is a cost center, not a service business." A consultant hired to assess the department disagrees and presents the ITIL 4 framework as evidence that IT departments are, in fact, service providers with all the obligations that entails.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Using ITIL 4 definitions, explain whether the CIO's claim — that the IT department has users but not customers — is accurate. Identify who the customers, users, and sponsors are in this hospital scenario.
2. Explain in your own words what it means for value to be co-created in this context. Give a specific example involving the hospital IT department and one of its consumer groups.
3. Explain why viewing IT as a "cost center" rather than a service provider might lead to poor outcomes, using at least one ITIL 4 concept from Module 01 to support your argument.

## A Note from Professor Nash

The scenarios in this discussion are designed to surface the tensions that real IT organizations face every day. There are no trick questions, but there are nuanced ones. When you write your initial post, resist the urge to give a textbook answer. Connect the ITIL 4 concepts to the human situation in the scenario — the frustrated faculty member, the employees who cannot access training, the clinical staff who need the portal to work. That connection between framework and reality is what ITIL 4 is fundamentally about, and it is what the Foundation exam will expect you to demonstrate.
