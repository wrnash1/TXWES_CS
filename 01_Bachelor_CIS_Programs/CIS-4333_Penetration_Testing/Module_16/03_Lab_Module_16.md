## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

### Challenge 1: Cross-Domain Scenario Analysis

Select any two findings from your labs across Modules 10–15 that you confirmed in an authorized lab environment. For each finding:

1. Write a complete finding entry using the professional report format: unique finding ID, affected asset, CVSS severity rating with justification, description of the vulnerability, evidence reference (screenshot or terminal output filename), business impact statement, and remediation recommendation specific to the vulnerable component.
2. Map each finding to its MITRE ATT&CK technique ID (for example, T1190 for Exploit Public-Facing Application or T1059 for Command and Scripting Interpreter) and explain in one sentence how that mapping would help a blue team validate their detection coverage.
3. Identify which PT0-002 exam domain each finding primarily belongs to and explain why.

### Challenge 2: Scope and Authorization Decision Review

Review the following three scenarios and for each one write a two-to-three sentence professional response describing the correct action and the specific rule or principle that governs it:

1. During an authorized external assessment, your scanner returns results for an IP address that falls inside the authorized CIDR range but resolves to a hostname belonging to a cloud provider's shared infrastructure — not the client's dedicated instance.
2. You are performing a retest of FIND-004 (an authenticated SQL injection in the client's admin portal). While retesting, you discover that the admin portal now has a new unauthenticated endpoint that was not present during the original engagement.
3. The client's IT Director calls you directly and asks you to verbally confirm whether a specific server was found vulnerable during the engagement, before the final report has been delivered to the CISO.

### Reflection Questions

1. A candidate who scores 749 out of 900 on PT0-002 does not pass. What study methodology adjustment — specifically related to which domain or topic area — would you recommend for a retake, and how does the domain weighting structure inform that recommendation?
2. You are a professional penetration tester asked to sign a client contract that requires you to permanently retain all engagement evidence indefinitely "for the client's future reference." What professional and ethical concerns does this clause raise, and what contractual language would you propose as an alternative?
