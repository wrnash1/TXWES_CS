# Discussion: Module 12 — Digital Forensics

## Course: CIS-4328 Information Security

**Certification Alignment:** CompTIA Security+ (SY0-701)

---

## Overview

Digital forensics sits at the intersection of technical science and legal process. The decisions investigators make — what to collect, how to collect it, who is authorized to do so, and how to document it — can determine whether a criminal is prosecuted, whether a company is protected or exposed in litigation, and whether the evidence holds up under cross-examination. This discussion explores those tensions through a realistic scenario.

---

## Discussion Prompt

Read the following scenario and respond to all three parts.

### Scenario

A mid-sized logistics company, Cascadia Freight, suspects that a departing senior logistics analyst, Marta Weiss, has stolen proprietary customer routing algorithms and client contracts before resigning. The security team receives a tip on her last day that she transferred large files to a personal Dropbox account from her work laptop.

Marta returns her company laptop at the exit interview at 3:00 PM. By 3:15 PM, the security team has the device in hand. The CISO wants to image the laptop immediately. The following constraints are known:

- Marta signed Cascadia's AUP at onboarding, which states that company devices are subject to monitoring and forensic investigation.
- The laptop is company-owned and was issued to Marta.
- The CISO wants to conduct the investigation entirely internally to avoid external cost and publicity.
- Legal counsel has not yet been consulted — it is 3:20 PM on a Friday.
- HR is not present — the HR director left early.
- The laptop is still powered on with Marta's Windows session active.

---

## Part 1 — Forensic Prioritization (Required)

Based on the scenario, the laptop is powered on and Marta's session is active. Apply the order of volatility to answer:

1. What should be collected first, and why?
2. What information specifically might be in RAM that would be valuable to this investigation?
3. Should the laptop be shut down before or after imaging? Justify your answer.

Your response to Part 1 should be approximately 150 to 200 words.

---

## Part 2 — Legal and Process Concerns (Required)

The CISO wants to proceed immediately without waiting for legal counsel. You are the security analyst assigned to assist.

Write a concise memo (150 to 200 words) to the CISO explaining:

- Which aspects of this investigation the AUP supports.
- What risks exist in proceeding without legal counsel on a Friday afternoon.
- At minimum, what step you recommend before imaging begins.

Be direct and professional. The CISO is technically capable but may be under pressure to act fast.

## Instructor Note

The tension in this scenario — act fast to preserve evidence vs. pause to ensure legal authorization — is real and recurring in corporate security teams. Speed matters in forensics (volatile data evaporates), but an improperly conducted investigation can expose the company to legal liability and render evidence inadmissible. The professional answer threads both concerns. Your discussion should demonstrate that you understand both the technical urgency and the legal obligation, and can communicate that balance clearly.
