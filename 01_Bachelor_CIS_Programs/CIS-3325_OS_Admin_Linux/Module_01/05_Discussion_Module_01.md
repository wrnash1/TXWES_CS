## Discussion Prompt

This discussion has two required parts. Address both in a single, organized post.

### Part 1 — Your Linux Background and Goals

Briefly introduce yourself to the class. Address the following:

- What is your prior experience with Linux, if any? Have you used it before, even briefly? This can include using a Raspberry Pi, running a Linux server at home, using the Windows Subsystem for Linux, or using macOS (which is Unix-based).
- What is drawing you to Linux administration specifically? What career path, job role, or personal interest motivated you to take this course?
- What does the CompTIA Linux+ certification mean for your goals — is it a requirement for a job, a personal milestone, a stepping stone to a more advanced certification, or something else?

Be genuine. A student who has never touched Linux and a student who has been self-hosting servers for five years are both exactly where they should be for this course.

### Part 2 — Open Source Philosophy Reflection

The FOSS philosophy — software freedom, open source development, and copyleft licensing — is the foundation that Linux is built on. Consider the following question and respond thoughtfully:

The GPL requires that anyone who distributes GPL-licensed software must also distribute the source code and license any derivative works under the GPL. Some companies view this as a restriction that makes GPL software difficult to use commercially. Others view it as essential protection for the community. Red Hat has built a billion-dollar company on GPL-licensed software.

In your view: Is the GPL's copyleft requirement a strength or a weakness for open-source software? Consider both the perspective of individual developers contributing to an open-source project and the perspective of a business that wants to use or build on open-source code. There is no single right answer — I am looking for thoughtful reasoning backed by what you learned in this module.

Your response should be at least two paragraphs and demonstrate that you understand what the GPL actually requires.
