**Course:** CIS-3350 Software Engineering and Agile

## Instructions

Choose one of the three scenarios below (A, B, or C). Write an initial post responding to all three sub-questions within your chosen scenario. Then respond to at least two classmates who chose different scenarios whenever possible.

Initial post length: 175–225 words. Peer responses: at least 75 words each, adding substance beyond agreement.

---

## Scenario A — The Documentation Battle

A large enterprise software team has just adopted Agile after years of Waterfall. The project manager still requires a complete functional specification, a detailed design document, and a test plan document before each Sprint begins. She argues: "The Agile Manifesto says documentation has value — so we're still Agile." A developer on the team argues back: "This is just Waterfall with shorter phases."

Sub-questions:

1. Who is more correct — the project manager or the developer? Use exact Manifesto language in your argument.
2. What is the practical consequence of requiring complete documentation before each Sprint begins? Which Manifesto principle does this most directly violate?
3. What would a minimal, Manifesto-aligned documentation approach look like for this team? Describe it specifically.

---

## Scenario B — The Principle in the Wild

You are interviewing for a Software Engineer position at a company that describes itself as "fully Agile." During the interview, you ask about their development process. They tell you: teams work in two-week Sprints, but the architecture is designed upfront by a separate Architecture Team and handed to the development teams; the customer sees a product demo once per quarter; and developers are expected to "surge" for the last two weeks of each quarter to ensure on-time delivery.

Sub-questions:

1. Identify two Agile Manifesto principles this company is violating. For each, name the principle (paraphrase is fine) and explain specifically which stated practice violates it.
2. Is this company "fully Agile" as they claim? Justify your answer using Manifesto values.
3. If you took the job, what three changes would you advocate for in your first 90 days, and which Manifesto principle justifies each change?

---

## Scenario C — Values in Conflict

A Scrum team is under contract to deliver a fixed feature set by a specific date. The customer has signed a contract specifying exactly which features will be delivered. Midway through the project, the customer realizes that three of the contracted features are no longer needed and wants to add two new ones instead. The legal team at the vendor says: "We cannot change the contract scope without a formal amendment, which takes 6 weeks." Meanwhile, the development team has capacity and could easily make the swap.

Sub-questions:

1. Which Agile Manifesto value is most in tension with the legal team's position? Quote the value and explain the tension.
2. The Manifesto acknowledges that "contract negotiation" has value. How do you reconcile this acknowledgment with the situation described?
3. What practical mechanism could this organization put in place for future contracts to prevent this conflict, and which Manifesto value or principle supports it?

## Professor Nash's Closing Note

The Agile Manifesto is deceptively simple. Four values, twelve principles — most students read them and think "of course, this is obvious." The test of understanding is not whether you can agree with the principles in the abstract. It is whether you can apply them correctly when they conflict with organizational inertia, legal constraints, or managerial anxiety. The scenarios in this discussion are designed to surface those real tensions. Push your classmates — and yourself — to reason through the friction rather than around it.
