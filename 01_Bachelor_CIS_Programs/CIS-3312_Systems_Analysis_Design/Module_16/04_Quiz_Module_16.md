**Question 9**
An ECBA candidate reads the following in a practice question: "The BA has completed elicitation and must now produce a model that shows the entities the system must store data about, their attributes, and their relationships." Which modeling technique does this describe?
*   A) BPMN process model — showing the flow of activities and decisions in a business process
*   B) Use case diagram — showing the interactions between actors and system functions
*   C) Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) — showing data entities, their attributes, and the relationships between them
*   D) Data Flow Diagram (DFD) — showing how data moves through a system and is transformed
*   **Correct Answer:** C) Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) — showing data entities, their attributes, and the relationships between them
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* BPMN models activity flow, decision logic, and process participants — it does not model data storage structure or entity relationships.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* Use case diagrams show which actors interact with which system functions; they do not model data structure or entity relationships.

    *   *Why C is correct:* An ERD is the standard technique for modeling the data structure of a system — it identifies entities (things the system must store information about), attributes (properties of those entities), and relationships (how entities are associated) with cardinality notation.

A BA notices that two requirements in the approved requirements baseline conflict with each other: FR-044 states that all users must authenticate with a single factor, while NFR-009 states that users accessing financial data must authenticate with two factors. *   A) Retain both requirements and let the development team resolve the conflict during implementation

**Question 16**
A BA is preparing to elicit requirements from a diverse group of stakeholders that includes end users, IT staff, and senior executives. The BA knows that executives have limited availability, that end users need to demonstrate their current workflow, and that IT staff need to provide technical constraints in writing. Which combination of elicitation techniques best matches each stakeholder group to the most appropriate method?

*   A) Conduct a single JAD session with all three groups simultaneously so all perspectives are captured in one meeting
*   B) Use job shadowing with executives for workflow observation, send a written survey to IT staff, and hold one-on-one interviews with end users

*   **Distractor Analysis:**

    *   *Why A is incorrect:* A joint session with all three groups creates power imbalances — executives dominate, end users defer, and IT staff may withhold technical concerns. Mixing authority levels and information types in one session is an elicitation anti-pattern.

**Question 19**
A BA is developing a stakeholder analysis for a large enterprise system replacement project. The BA has identified 22 stakeholders across six departments. To prioritize engagement effort, the BA uses a power/interest grid. In this grid, which quadrant represents stakeholders who have HIGH organizational power but LOW interest in the project, and what engagement strategy does BABOK® recommend for this group?

*   A) High power / Low interest — Manage Closely: schedule frequent detailed status meetings and involve them in all requirements decisions
*   B) High power / Low interest — Keep Satisfied: keep them informed of major decisions and developments without burdening them with operational detail, and engage them when their authority or approval is needed
*   C) High power / Low interest — Monitor: track them passively, as their low interest means they will never need to be engaged
*   D) High power / Low interest — Keep Informed: send weekly detailed project status reports covering all requirements and design decisions

*   **Correct Answer:** B) High power / Low interest — Keep Satisfied: keep them informed of major decisions and developments without burdening them with operational detail, and engage them when their authority or approval is needed
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why B is correct:* In the power/interest (or influence/interest) stakeholder grid, the High Power / Low Interest quadrant requires a "Keep Satisfied" strategy. These stakeholders have authority that can impact the project (they can block resources, escalate issues, or mandate direction) but are not engaged in day-to-day details. The BA should communicate major milestones, decisions, and issues concisely — satisfying their need for high-level awareness without overwhelming them with detail they do not want.
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* "Manage Closely" (frequent detailed meetings, involvement in all decisions) is the strategy for High Power / High Interest stakeholders — the key project champions, sponsors, and domain owners who are both influential and actively engaged. Applying this to low-interest stakeholders wastes their time and erodes their goodwill.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* "Monitor" (minimal engagement) is the strategy for Low Power / Low Interest stakeholders, who have neither the authority nor the motivation to significantly impact the project. Passively monitoring high-power stakeholders is risky — they can rapidly become blockers or disruptors if they feel uninformed about decisions that affect their area.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Sending weekly detailed reports to high-power, low-interest stakeholders contradicts the "Keep Satisfied" strategy. Detailed weekly communications will be ignored, may create negative impressions of the BA's communication judgment, and do not reflect the stakeholder's preference for concise, high-level updates.
