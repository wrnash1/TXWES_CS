## Course: CIS-3312 Systems Analysis & Design (IIBA ECBA)

---

### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 16 – Final Exam Prep & IIBA ECBA Certification**! This final module serves a dual purpose: it is your comprehensive review of the full course and your preparation guide for the IIBA Entry Certificate in Business Analysis (ECBA) exam. The ECBA is the entry-level professional credential from the IIBA, designed for individuals new to business analysis who can demonstrate knowledge of the BABOK® Guide v3.

This module reviews all seven BABOK® Knowledge Areas tested on the ECBA, provides exam-taking strategies, covers the certification application process, and concludes with a practice exam aligned to the ECBA question format.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **IIBA ECBA (Entry Certificate in Business Analysis)**: The ECBA is the IIBA's foundational business analysis credential, targeting individuals with fewer than two years of BA experience who have completed at least 21 hours of professional development related to business analysis. It validates knowledge of the BABOK® Guide v3 across all seven knowledge areas through a 50-question, 60-minute online proctored exam. Passing the ECBA demonstrates that a candidate understands BA concepts, techniques, and terminology at the entry level.

*   **BABOK® Guide v3 (Business Analysis Body of Knowledge)**: The BABOK® Guide is the IIBA's globally recognized reference standard for the business analysis profession. Version 3 (2015) defines six core concepts (the Business Analysis Core Concept Model: Change, Need, Solution, Stakeholder, Value, Context), seven knowledge areas (KA 2–8, with KA 1 being the introduction), and over 50 techniques. The ECBA exam is based on the BABOK® Guide v3 and the IIBA ECBA Exam Blueprint.

*   **Business Analysis Core Concept Model (BACCM)**: The BACCM is BABOK® v3's foundational framework consisting of six interrelated concepts: Change (the transformation a BA enables), Need (the problem or opportunity the change addresses), Solution (the means of satisfying the need), Stakeholder (a party with a relationship to the need or solution), Value (the worth of a solution to stakeholders), and Context (the circumstances that influence the need and solution). Every BABOK® knowledge area is framed within the BACCM.

*   **Knowledge Area (KA)**: A Knowledge Area in the BABOK® is a logical grouping of related BA tasks. The seven ECBA-tested knowledge areas are: KA 2 (Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring), KA 3 (Strategy Analysis), KA 4 (Elicitation and Collaboration), KA 5 (Requirements Analysis and Design Definition), KA 6 (Requirements Life Cycle Management), KA 7 (Solution Evaluation), and the underlying competencies in Perspectives. Know which key tasks and techniques belong to each KA.

*   **Requirements Life Cycle Management (BABOK® KA 6)**: Requirements Life Cycle Management covers the ongoing management of requirements from their creation through their retirement. Tasks include: Trace Requirements (maintaining the RTM), Maintain Requirements (keeping them current as the project evolves), Prioritize Requirements (ranking by business value using MoSCoW, weighted ranking, or other methods), Assess Requirements Changes (evaluating the impact of proposed changes), and Approve Requirements (obtaining formal stakeholder sign-off). This knowledge area is foundational to controlled, traceable requirements management.

*   **MoSCoW Prioritization**: MoSCoW is a requirements prioritization technique that classifies requirements into four categories: Must Have (essential for the solution to be viable), Should Have (important but not critical for the initial release), Could Have (desirable if resources allow), and Won't Have (explicitly excluded from the current release scope). MoSCoW is one of the most commonly used prioritization methods in both predictive and Agile contexts and is directly testable on the ECBA exam.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **ECBA Exam Format**: The ECBA exam consists of 50 multiple-choice questions with a 60-minute time limit (72 minutes with accommodations). All questions are scenario-based — they present a situation and ask which BABOK® task, technique, or concept is most appropriate. There are no trick questions; always choose the most appropriate BABOK®-aligned answer over a "common sense" answer that does not align with BABOK® terminology.
*   **Knowledge Area Weight Distribution**: The ECBA exam weights knowledge areas approximately as follows: KA 4 Elicitation (18%), KA 5 Requirements Analysis (18%), KA 2 Planning (16%), KA 6 Lifecycle Management (16%), KA 3 Strategy Analysis (14%), KA 7 Solution Evaluation (12%), Perspectives (6%). Study heavier-weighted areas more intensively if time is short.
*   **Technique vs. Task**: On ECBA scenario questions, always check whether the question asks for a *task* (what the BA does — e.g., "Define Transition State") or a *technique* (how the BA does it — e.g., "Gap Analysis"). These are different BABOK® concepts. Reading the question stem carefully for this distinction prevents choosing a correct concept in the wrong category.
*   **ECBA Application Process**: The ECBA requires 21 hours of professional development (BA-related education). Upon passing, you receive the credential and must maintain it with 20 CDUs (Continuing Development Units) every three years. Full application and exam registration information is at [https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/](https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/).
*   **Study Resource**: The IIBA ECBA Exam Study Guide and the official practice exam questions are available through IIBA membership at [https://www.iiba.org/](https://www.iiba.org/). Additionally, the free BABOK® v3 Online Student Edition is available for student members — register at [https://www.iiba.org/membership/join/student-membership/](https://www.iiba.org/membership/join/student-membership/) for reduced fees and free access to the BABOK® digital edition.

### Required Readings & Videos
*   **Required Reading**: Review all BABOK® v3 chapter summaries for KA 2 through KA 7. Focus especially on the task inputs, outputs, and recommended techniques for each knowledge area — these are the most direct ECBA exam targets. Also re-read the BABOK® v3 Chapter 2 (Business Analysis Key Concepts) to refresh the BACCM framework.
*   **Supplemental Reading**: The IIBA ECBA Exam Blueprint (downloadable at [https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/](https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/)) lists exactly which topics are covered and at what percentage weight. Comparing your self-assessed knowledge against the blueprint is the most efficient final exam preparation strategy.

## Supplemental Resources

The following open educational resources support your ECBA exam preparation and final course
review. All are freely accessible without login or purchase unless noted.

1. **IIBA ECBA Certification Overview**
   <https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/>
   Focus: The official IIBA page for the ECBA credential, including eligibility requirements,
   the exam blueprint (knowledge area weights), application process, and renewal requirements.
   This is the single most important reference for any candidate preparing to sit the ECBA
   exam — download the exam blueprint and use it to guide your final study effort.

2. **BABOK® Guide v3 — IIBA Online Student Edition**
   <https://www.iiba.org/membership/join/student-membership/>
   Focus: IIBA student membership provides free access to the BABOK® Guide v3 digital edition.
   The BABOK® is the primary source document for every ECBA exam question. Use the KA chapter
   summaries (Chapter 4–11) to review task inputs, outputs, and recommended techniques for all
   seven knowledge areas tested on the exam.

3. **Business Analysis Core Concept Model — IIBA Reference**
   <https://www.iiba.org/standards-and-resources/babok/>
   Focus: The BACCM (Change, Need, Solution, Stakeholder, Value, Context) is the conceptual
   foundation for all BABOK® v3 content and is explicitly tested on the ECBA. This resource
   explains how the six core concepts interrelate and how they frame every knowledge area,
   task, and technique in the BABOK® Guide.

4. **MoSCoW Prioritization Technique — Agile Business Consortium**
   <https://www.agilebusiness.org/dsdm-project-framework/moscow-prioririsation.html>
   Focus: Authoritative explanation of the MoSCoW prioritization method — Must Have, Should
   Have, Could Have, Won't Have — with definitions, usage guidelines, and common mistakes.
   MoSCoW is a high-frequency technique on the ECBA exam and is used across predictive and
   Agile project approaches.

5. **Requirements Traceability and Requirements Lifecycle — IIBA Learning Series**
   <https://www.iiba.org/training-and-events/learning-solutions/>
   Focus: IIBA's free learning resources covering KA 6 Requirements Life Cycle Management —
   the knowledge area responsible for tracing, maintaining, prioritizing, and approving
   requirements. KA 6 accounts for approximately 16% of the ECBA exam and is one of the
   areas most frequently missed by candidates who conflate lifecycle management tasks with
   requirements analysis tasks.
