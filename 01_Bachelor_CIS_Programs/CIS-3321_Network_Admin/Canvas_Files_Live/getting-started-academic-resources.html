Academic Resources

Texas Wesleyan University

Texas Wesleyan offers a number of free support resources to help you succeed in this course and throughout your degree program.

University Library

The TXWES library provides access to academic databases, research assistance, and citation support relevant to your discussion posts and lab write-ups. Visit the library's website or contact a librarian for help locating credible sources on networking and IT topics.

Tutoring & Learning Center (TLC)

The Tutoring & Learning Center offers free peer tutoring for computer science, information technology, and mathematics courses. If you're struggling with a specific concept (subnetting, binary math, routing logic, etc.), the TLC is a strong first stop.

Writing Center

The Writing Center can help you structure written responses, lab reports, and discussion posts more clearly — useful even in a technical course, since several assignments in this course are graded partly on clarity of written explanation.

Office of Disability Services

Texas Wesleyan University is committed to providing equal educational opportunities to all students. In accordance with Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973 and the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), if you have a documented disability and require academic accommodations, contact the Office of Disability Services (located in the Eunice and James L. West Library) as early in the semester as possible.

Title IX & Harassment Reporting

Texas Wesleyan University is committed to maintaining a learning environment free from all forms of discrimination, harassment, and sexual misconduct. If you experience or witness discrimination, sexual harassment, or assault, report it to the Title IX Coordinator or consult the student handbook for confidential support resources.

IT Help Desk

For Canvas login issues, TXWES email problems, or general technology support, contact the TXWES IT Help Desk. Don't wait until an assignment is due to report a technical problem — reach out as soon as you notice an issue.

If you're ever unsure which resource applies to your situation, ask your instructor — I'm happy to point you in the right direction.
