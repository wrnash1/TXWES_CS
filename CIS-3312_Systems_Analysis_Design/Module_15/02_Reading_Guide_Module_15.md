## Course: CIS-3312 Systems Analysis & Design (IIBA ECBA)

### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 15 – Enterprise Architecture and Business Strategy Alignment**! As business analysts advance in their careers, they are increasingly asked to work at the enterprise level — understanding how IT investments align with and enable the organization's strategic goals, not just solving individual project problems. This module covers Enterprise Architecture (EA) frameworks, business capability mapping, and BABOK® Guide v3 Knowledge Area 7: Solution Evaluation, which assesses whether deployed solutions actually deliver the expected business value.

Understanding EA gives BAs a broader lens through which to evaluate solution options, manage organizational complexity, and ensure that individual system changes integrate coherently with the overall technology landscape.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Enterprise Architecture (EA)**: Enterprise Architecture is a discipline that describes the structure and operation of an organization's business processes, information systems, technology, and data — and defines how these elements align to support the organization's strategy. EA provides a shared framework for making technology investment decisions consistently. Major EA frameworks include TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework), Zachman Framework, and FEAF (Federal Enterprise Architecture Framework).

*   **TOGAF (The Open Group Architecture Framework)**: TOGAF is the most widely used enterprise architecture framework globally. Its core methodology is the Architecture Development Method (ADM), a cycle of phases (Preliminary → Architecture Vision → Business Architecture → Information Systems Architecture → Technology Architecture → Opportunities & Solutions → Migration Planning → Implementation Governance → Architecture Change Management) that guides the development of enterprise architecture. TOGAF is maintained by The Open Group at [https://www.opengroup.org/togaf](https://www.opengroup.org/togaf).

*   **Business Capability Map**: A business capability map is a visual representation of what an organization does (its capabilities) independent of how it does it (processes or technology). Each capability represents a bounded area of business ability — for example, "Customer Onboarding," "Financial Reporting," or "Supply Chain Management." Capability maps are used in EA to assess which capabilities are underinvested, which have redundant systems, and which need strategic investment to close gaps between the current state and future goals.

*   **Gap Analysis**: A gap analysis compares the organization's current state (existing capabilities, processes, and systems) against the desired future state (strategic goals and capability targets) to identify the gaps that must be closed. In BA terms, a gap analysis is the core activity of BABOK® KA 3 (Strategy Analysis) and is the evidence base for recommending new systems or process improvements. Gaps may be in capabilities, data, processes, or technology.

*   **Solution Evaluation**: Solution Evaluation (BABOK® KA 7) is the knowledge area focused on assessing the value delivered by a solution once it is in use. BAs measure performance against success metrics defined before implementation, identify limitations of the deployed solution, and make recommendations to increase value (e.g., additional features, process improvements, user training). Solution Evaluation closes the BA lifecycle loop — from need identification through to confirmed value delivery.

*   **Business Strategy Alignment**: Business strategy alignment is the practice of ensuring that IT systems, process changes, and capability investments support and advance the organization's strategic objectives rather than existing in isolation. Misaligned IT investments are a major source of wasted organizational resources. BAs contribute to alignment by mapping requirements to strategic goals, using enterprise architecture frameworks to assess fit, and presenting solution options in terms of their strategic value contribution.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **BABOK KA 3 and KA 7**: The ECBA exam covers both Strategy Analysis (KA 3 — defining current and future states, identifying gaps) and Solution Evaluation (KA 7 — assessing whether deployed solutions deliver the expected value). Know what each knowledge area covers and when each is performed. KA 3 happens before/during development; KA 7 happens after deployment.
*   **Gap Analysis Output**: The output of a gap analysis is a list of specific capability or process gaps with their root causes, not just a comparison table. ECBA questions may ask what a BA should produce after comparing current and future state — the answer is a gap analysis report that informs the solution recommendation.
*   **TOGAF ADM Recognition**: You do not need to memorize every TOGAF ADM phase for the ECBA exam, but you should recognize that TOGAF is an EA framework used for enterprise-level architecture decisions, and that "Architecture Vision" and "Business Architecture" are early ADM phases that align closely with BA strategy analysis work.
*   **Study Resource**: The Open Group publishes a free TOGAF overview and introduction at [https://www.opengroup.org/togaf](https://www.opengroup.org/togaf) — the "TOGAF in Brief" document is the appropriate depth for an ECBA candidate and covers the ADM cycle and its BA-relevant phases.
