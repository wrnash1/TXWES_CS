## Course: CIS-3312 Systems Analysis and Design

## Texas Wesleyan University | Professor Nash

## Lab Overview

In this lab you will develop the key implementation and change management artifacts a business analyst produces during the transition phase of a systems project. You will select and justify a deployment strategy, apply the ADKAR model to a change resistance scenario, build a structured training plan, and design a post-implementation review. These artifacts directly map to ECBA competencies in Solution Evaluation and change management.

**Estimated time:** 75–90 minutes

**Deliverable:** Submit as a single PDF or Word document with all parts clearly labeled.

---

## Scenario Background

Brazos Valley Medical Center is replacing its fifteen-year-old paper-based patient intake and registration system with a new electronic health record (EHR) platform. The new system will be used by 340 staff members across four departments: Admissions (85 staff), Emergency Department (120 staff), Outpatient Clinics (95 staff), and Medical Records (40 staff).

The project has cleared UAT successfully. Go-live is planned for six weeks from today. The system is hosted in the cloud and the legacy paper process will be retired. There is no legacy electronic system to run in parallel.

Key facts:

- Emergency Department operates 24 hours, 7 days per week with no downtime tolerance
- Medical Records staff are highly experienced with paper processes and have expressed skepticism about the new system
- Admissions staff skew younger and have informally expressed enthusiasm
- Outpatient Clinics staff vary widely in technology comfort level
- The VP of Clinical Operations has publicly stated go-live will happen as scheduled regardless of readiness concerns
- A HIPAA compliance audit is scheduled ninety days after go-live

## Part 1 — Deployment Strategy Selection (20 points)

### Part 1A — Strategy Analysis

Evaluate all four deployment strategies — big bang, phased rollout, parallel operation, and pilot — against the Brazos Valley scenario. For each strategy, write two to three sentences assessing its feasibility given the specific constraints described above. Consider the Emergency Department's 24/7 requirement, the absence of a legacy electronic system for parallel operation, and the departmental variation in readiness.

### Part 1B — Recommendation and Justification

Select one deployment strategy or a justified hybrid approach. Write a 150–200 word recommendation that includes:

- The strategy you recommend
- The specific scenario characteristics that drove your selection
- The primary risk of your chosen strategy and how you would mitigate it
- What rollback procedures you would require to be in place before go-live

### Part 1C — Cutover Plan Outline

For your recommended strategy, outline the key events and milestones in the cutover plan. Present this as a timeline with at least seven entries, starting from "T minus 2 weeks" through "T plus 1 week." Each entry should have a date reference, an activity description, and a responsible party (you may use role titles rather than names).

## Part 2 — ADKAR Change Analysis (25 points)

### Part 2A — Stakeholder Change Assessment

For each of the four staff groups below, identify which ADKAR stage is most likely to be the primary adoption barrier and explain your reasoning in two to three sentences.

Group 1: Medical Records staff — experienced with paper processes, skeptical of the new system, have said publicly "the paper system never crashes."

Group 2: Emergency Department nurses — concerned that any system slowdown during a critical patient event could be dangerous. Have asked whether there is a downtime procedure.

Group 3: Admissions staff — enthusiastic about the new system but have never used an EHR before. Their supervisor says they are "excited but undertrained."

Group 4: Outpatient Clinics physicians — have not attended any of the informational sessions held over the past two months. Their assistants report that the physicians "don't know the go-live date."

### Part 2B — Intervention Design

For each group identified in Part 2A, design one specific intervention that directly addresses the ADKAR barrier you identified. For each intervention, specify:

- The target group
- The ADKAR stage being addressed
- The specific intervention (what will be done, by whom, and when)
- How you will measure whether the intervention was effective

### Part 2C — Executive Communication Challenge

The VP of Clinical Operations has stated that go-live will proceed as scheduled regardless of readiness. From an ADKAR perspective, identify which stage this executive communication approach risks undermining for front-line staff, and write a one-paragraph recommendation for how change communications should be adjusted.

## Part 3 — Training Plan Development (30 points)

Develop a structured training plan for the Brazos Valley EHR implementation.

### Part 3A — Audience Segmentation Table

Create a training audience segmentation table with the following columns: Department, Staff Count, Primary System Functions Used, Technology Comfort Level (High/Medium/Low — based on scenario context), Recommended Training Track, and Recommended Training Modality. Complete one row for each of the four departments.

### Part 3B — Training Track Design

Design two training tracks in detail: one for Admissions staff and one for Medical Records staff. For each track, specify:

- Learning objectives (three to five objectives)
- Training modalities to be used
- Sequence and timing of training activities (at least three activities)
- Materials to be developed
- Competency verification method
- Go-live support provisions specific to this group

### Part 3C — Training Schedule

Create a training schedule covering the six weeks from today to go-live. Present this as a table with columns: Week, Target Audience, Training Activity, Format, Duration, and Location or Platform. The schedule must reflect the staggered training approach needed for a 340-person workforce across four departments.

### Part 3D — Go-Live Support Plan

Describe the go-live support structure for the first five days after cutover. Address:

- Staffing levels (how many support resources, where deployed)
- Support channels available to staff (phone, chat, floor walkers, etc.)
- Escalation path for critical issues during Emergency Department hours
- Criteria for declaring a support-level incident vs. a system-level incident

## Part 4 — Post-Implementation Review Plan (25 points)

### Part 4A — Business Value Metrics

The original business case for the EHR project projected the following outcomes. For each projected outcome, define a specific, measurable metric and describe how you would collect the data at the thirty-day and ninety-day PIR points.

Projected outcomes:

1. Reduce patient registration time from an average of 12 minutes to 7 minutes
2. Eliminate paper record storage costs (estimated $180,000 per year)
3. Achieve HIPAA audit readiness by the ninety-day audit date
4. Reduce duplicate patient record creation by 90%

### Part 4B — PIR Agenda

Design a structured PIR meeting agenda for the thirty-day review. The meeting is 90 minutes and will include the VP of Clinical Operations, department managers from all four departments, the IT director, and you as the BA facilitator. Include:

- Meeting objectives
- Agenda items with time allocations
- Who presents or leads each agenda item
- How findings will be documented and distributed

### Part 4C — Lessons Learned Categories

Based on the Brazos Valley scenario, identify five likely lessons-learned topics that this project should generate findings on. For each topic, write one sentence describing the nature of the lesson and one sentence describing how the organization could apply it to a future EHR expansion or upgrade project.

## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

This section is optional and not separately graded. It extends the lab into advanced
implementation and change management practice aligned with ECBA exam competencies.

### Challenge Step 1: Resistance Escalation Scenario

Three weeks after go-live at Brazos Valley Medical Center, the BA receives a report that
Medical Records staff are printing EHR screens and maintaining paper shadow files in
addition to entering data in the new system. The department supervisor says: "We need
the paper backup in case the system goes down." Design a comprehensive intervention
plan that addresses this reversion using the ADKAR framework. Your plan must: diagnose
which ADKAR stage or stages are involved (justify your diagnosis), design a minimum of
three specific interventions targeting the identified stages, define measurable success
criteria for each intervention, specify who is responsible for each intervention
(by role), and establish a thirty-day re-assessment checkpoint with criteria for
escalating to executive leadership if the reversion continues. Finally, write a one-
paragraph reflection on the ethical dimension of this situation: the supervisor's concern
about downtime is legitimate — how does the BA balance honoring that concern while still
driving adoption of the intended solution?

### Challenge Step 2: Ninety-Day PIR Report

The Brazos Valley HIPAA compliance audit is scheduled ninety days after go-live. Using
the business value metrics you defined in Part 4A, write a complete ninety-day PIR
report as if you had collected real data. Fabricate plausible but realistic results —
for example, patient registration time reduced from 12 to 8.5 minutes (target was 7
minutes), or paper storage costs eliminated at 85% of projected savings. Your report
must include: an executive summary with a traffic-light status for each projected
outcome (Green = met, Yellow = partial, Red = missed), a root cause analysis for any
Yellow or Red outcomes using the 5 Whys technique, a prioritized corrective action
register with owner and target date for each action, a HIPAA audit readiness assessment
covering the specific audit-relevant outcomes, and a recommendation on whether the EHR
system should be considered a successful implementation or requires a formal remediation
plan. Close with a lessons-learned section of at least five findings with specific
recommendations for the next Brazos Valley system implementation.

### Challenge Step 3: Transition Plan Document

Write a complete transition plan for the Brazos Valley EHR system covering the handoff
from the project team to the hospital's IT operations group. The transition plan must
address all six components listed in Section 6.2 of the reading guide and additionally
include: a knowledge transfer schedule showing which project team members will brief
which operations team roles on which topics over a four-week period before the project
team disbands; a system architecture overview summary (one page maximum, written for
operations staff, not developers) covering how the cloud-hosted EHR connects to the
hospital network, where patient data is stored, and who the primary vendor contacts are;
a downtime procedure document addressing the Medical Records staff concern about system
unavailability — specifically, what clinical staff should do during a system outage and
how long the outage tolerance is before paper backup procedures activate; and a six-month
support model showing how support transitions from the dedicated project team floor
walkers in weeks one through four, to a shared IT help desk in months two through four,
to steady-state vendor support in months five and six. This exercise develops the
operational documentation skills that distinguish senior BAs from junior ones.
