### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 01 – Introduction to Systems Analysis and the SDLC**! This module establishes the foundational vocabulary and conceptual framework for everything that follows in the course. You will learn what a system is, why organizations invest in systems analysis, and how the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC) structures the process of building or improving information systems.

Understanding the SDLC phases and the business analyst's role within them is a direct requirement of the IIBA ECBA exam. BABOK® Guide v3 defines the BA as the person who facilitates communication between stakeholders to understand the need, analyze it, and guide the solution. Before diving into specific techniques in later modules, you need a firm grasp of where those techniques fit in the overall process.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Systems Analysis**: Systems analysis is the process of studying a business or organizational problem domain to identify improvement opportunities and define the requirements that a new or modified information system must satisfy. It bridges the gap between business stakeholders, who understand the problem, and developers, who build the solution. The deliverable of systems analysis is a clear set of requirements — not a design or code.

*   **Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC)**: The SDLC is a structured framework of phases that guides the planning, creation, testing, and delivery of an information system. Common phases include planning, systems analysis, systems design, implementation, and maintenance. Different methodologies (Waterfall, Agile, Spiral) sequence and repeat these phases differently, but all share the same core activities.

*   **Business Analyst (BA)**: A business analyst is a professional who identifies business needs, evaluates solutions, and elicits, documents, and manages requirements throughout a project. According to BABOK® Guide v3, the BA serves as the bridge between business stakeholders and technical teams, ensuring that the delivered solution addresses the root cause of the business problem rather than just its symptoms.

*   **Stakeholder**: A stakeholder is any individual, group, or organization that has an interest in or is affected by the outcome of a project or system change. Stakeholders include end users, sponsors, subject matter experts, regulators, and the development team. Identifying all stakeholders early is critical because missed stakeholders often mean missed requirements.

*   **Feasibility Study**: A feasibility study is a preliminary assessment conducted early in the SDLC to determine whether a proposed system is technically achievable, economically justified, operationally practical, and legally compliant. It provides decision-makers with evidence to approve, modify, or reject a project before significant resources are committed.

*   **Requirements**: Requirements are documented statements of what a system must do (functional requirements) or the quality characteristics it must exhibit (non-functional requirements). They represent the agreed-upon understanding between stakeholders and the development team and serve as the baseline for design, testing, and acceptance.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **ECBA Focus — BA Core Concept**: The IIBA ECBA exam tests whether you understand what business analysis *is* before testing techniques. Know the BABOK definition: business analysis is the practice of enabling change in an enterprise by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders. Expect a question that asks you to distinguish BA work from project management or software development.
*   **SDLC Phase Sequencing**: ECBA candidates are expected to recognize that requirements analysis comes *before* design. A common distractor on exam questions will suggest jumping from a business problem directly to design or technology selection — always anchor on: identify need → elicit requirements → analyze → design → build → test → deploy.
*   **Stakeholder Identification**: The BABOK Knowledge Area "Stakeholder Engagement" begins with stakeholder identification. On the exam, when a scenario describes a project that missed critical requirements, the root cause is almost always a stakeholder who was not identified or engaged early enough.
*   **Study Resource**: The IIBA publishes a free "ECBA Exam Prep" overview at [https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/](https://www.iiba.org/certification/ecba/) — review the exam blueprint to understand which BABOK knowledge areas receive the most questions before you begin the course content.
