## Scenario A: The Skipped Analysis Phase

A regional credit union decided to replace its loan origination software. Eager to meet a board-mandated go-live deadline, the project manager instructed the development team to begin building the new system immediately based on a two-page summary of the old system's features. No formal requirements gathering was conducted with loan officers, compliance staff, or branch managers. Eight months later, the system launched — and within two weeks, the compliance team discovered that the new system did not enforce any of the required federal lending disclosure rules. The system had to be taken offline for remediation, costing the credit union an estimated $1.2 million in rework and regulatory fines.

Sub-questions:

1. Identify which SDLC phase was skipped and explain specifically what deliverable from that phase would have caught the compliance gap before development began.
2. Using the BABOK definition, describe what the BA should have done during that phase and which stakeholder group was not engaged.
3. Evaluate the project manager's reasoning for skipping the phase — was the deadline justification valid? What would you recommend instead?

## Scenario B: Competing Stakeholder Definitions

A state government agency is building a new child welfare case management system. The BA is three weeks into elicitation and has uncovered a significant problem: the word "client" means different things to different departments. To the intake workers, a "client" is the child. To the investigators, a "client" is the parent or guardian. To the billing department, a "client" is the county government that reimburses the agency. All three groups are entering data into the same proposed system, and the requirements documents use the term without qualification throughout.

Sub-questions:

1. Explain why this terminology conflict is a systems analysis problem, not merely a communication problem, and what risk it poses to the project if left unresolved.
2. Describe at least two specific SDLC artifacts the BA should create to resolve this ambiguity before moving to the design phase.
3. Identify which BACCM concept is most directly threatened by this conflict and justify your choice.

## Scenario C: The "Build It or Buy It" Feasibility Decision

A mid-sized logistics company currently tracks driver routes and delivery confirmations using a combination of spreadsheets and phone calls. The operations director has proposed building a custom real-time route optimization system. The IT department estimates $800,000 in development costs and 18 months to build. A BA conducting a feasibility study discovers that three commercial SaaS platforms offer nearly identical functionality at $24,000 per year each, can be configured in 6–8 weeks, and have been validated by similar logistics companies. However, the operations director insists on custom development because "we need to own our own technology."

Sub-questions:

1. Identify which dimensions of feasibility favor the SaaS option over custom development in this scenario, and provide specific evidence from the case for each dimension you cite.
2. Describe the BA's appropriate role in this decision — is it the BA's job to make the final call, or to present an analysis? Justify your answer using the BABOK definition of business analysis.
3. How should the BA handle the operations director's preference for custom development while still fulfilling their professional obligation to present an objective analysis?

## A Note from Professor Nash

Systems analysis is fundamentally a human skill. The tools and diagrams we learn in this course are only as good as the conversations that inform them. Every scenario above involves a communication failure at least as much as a technical one. As you read your classmates' posts, pay attention to whether their recommended solutions address the human side of the problem — the stakeholders, the assumptions, the conflicting vocabulary — or whether they focus only on the artifact. The best BAs do both.
