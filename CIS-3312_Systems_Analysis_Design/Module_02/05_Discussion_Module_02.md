## Scenario A: The Invisible Stakeholder

A state agency is building a new benefits eligibility portal. The BA completed a thorough stakeholder analysis in week one and built a detailed stakeholder register. Eight weeks into elicitation, a compliance attorney from the state Attorney General's office attended a status briefing and immediately raised 14 new requirements related to ADA accessibility and public records law. None of these requirements had been discussed, and the attorney had not been included in the stakeholder register. The attorney has the authority to issue a legal hold that could stop the project.

Sub-questions:

1. Identify at least two specific ways the BA's initial stakeholder identification process could have caught this stakeholder earlier, referencing BABOK KA 2 techniques.
2. Using the Power-Interest Grid, classify the Attorney General's compliance attorney and describe the engagement strategy that should have been applied from the beginning.
3. The 14 new compliance requirements may require re-scoping the project. Describe how the BA governance plan should handle this situation, and who should have final approval authority.

## Scenario B: Approach Mismatch

A large insurance company assigned a BA to a project replacing its claims processing system for 2,000 claims adjusters. The system must integrate with a legacy mainframe that processes $4 billion in claims annually. Regulatory auditors require a fully signed-off requirements document before any development begins. The BA, fresh from a startup background, proposed an Agile Scrum approach with two-week sprints, no formal requirements document, and a product backlog updated weekly. The project sponsor approved the approach without review. Two months into the sprints, the regulatory audit team issued a stop-work notice because no baseline requirements document existed.

Sub-questions:

1. Identify at least three specific factors from this scenario that should have signaled that a predictive approach was required, and explain each factor using BABOK KA 2 approach selection criteria.
2. Describe what the BA should have included in the BA Approach document that would have prevented this stop-work order, referencing the KA 2 task "Plan Business Analysis Governance."
3. Is the project sponsor blameless in this situation? Discuss the BA's professional responsibility to recommend the correct approach even when the sponsor does not push back.

## Scenario C: BA Performance Off Track

A BA is leading requirements elicitation for a new warehouse management system. The BA plan committed to completing 25 stakeholder interviews in six weeks. After four weeks, only 9 interviews have been completed. The warehouse operations manager — a "Manage Closely" stakeholder — has cancelled three scheduled sessions. The BA has been substituting the missed interview time with document analysis of the old system's user manuals. The project manager has noticed the documentation milestones are slipping and has asked the BA for an explanation.

Sub-questions:

1. Apply the BABOK KA 2 task "Identify Business Analysis Performance Improvements" to this scenario. What specific steps should the BA take to analyze the variance and propose corrective action?
2. The warehouse operations manager is a "Manage Closely" stakeholder who keeps canceling. Describe two specific re-engagement strategies the BA could use, drawing on the stakeholder engagement approach from KA 2.
3. Document analysis of old user manuals can be a valid elicitation technique, but in this context it is being used as a substitute for stakeholder interviews. What risk does this substitution create, and how does it relate to requirements quality?

## A Note from Professor Nash

One of the most important insights from this module is that planning is not bureaucracy — it is respect. When you build a thorough stakeholder register, you are saying: I will not waste your time, and I will not forget that you exist. When you write a governance plan, you are saying: changes will be evaluated fairly, not just rubber-stamped or rejected arbitrarily. Good BA planning makes every downstream activity faster and more reliable. The scenarios this week all show what happens when that planning is skipped or done poorly.
