## Overview

In this lab you will practice the two most critical BA planning activities: stakeholder analysis and BA approach selection. You will read a provided case study, build a stakeholder register, position each stakeholder on a Power-Interest Grid, write an engagement strategy, and recommend a BA approach with justification. ## Case Study: Horizon Health Network — Patient Portal Replacement

Horizon Health Network (HHN) is a regional non-profit health system with 12 hospitals and 80 outpatient clinics. HHN's board has approved a project to replace the 11-year-old patient portal with a modern system that supports mobile access, appointment self-scheduling, lab result viewing, and secure provider messaging. The project has a $3.4 million budget, an 18-month timeline, and a regulatory requirement to comply with the federal patient data privacy and interoperability rules (assume HIPAA and HL7 FHIR standards apply). Go-live is fixed by a contractual commitment to HHN's largest insurer.

The following individuals have been identified during initial stakeholder interviews:

- Dr. Patricia Okonkwo — Chief Medical Officer. She approved the project and controls the budget. She has strong opinions about clinical workflow but is extremely busy and participates in monthly steering committee meetings only.
- Marcus Webb — VP of Information Technology. He owns the technical infrastructure and must approve all technology selections. He attends all project meetings and actively participates in requirements discussions.
- Aisha Reyes — Director of Patient Experience. She championed the project and has detailed knowledge of patient complaints about the old portal. She attends all working sessions.
- The Nursing Staff (represented by Sandra Park, RN charge nurse) — 2,400 registered nurses across HHN who will use the portal's provider messaging feature daily. Sandra represents this group but has no formal authority to approve requirements.
- Legal and Compliance Team (represented by Harold Metz, Compliance Officer) — reviews all requirements for HIPAA and FHIR compliance. Harold can issue a regulatory hold that stops the project if compliance gaps are found.
- HHN Patients — approximately 480,000 active patients who will use the portal. They are not represented by any single individual; patient feedback is available through an existing annual satisfaction survey.
- The EHR Vendor (Epic Systems, represented by a vendor project manager) — the current EHR system that the new portal must integrate with. The vendor must approve all integration specifications.
- Finance Department — will receive automated billing summary reports from the portal. Low engagement so far; has not attended meetings.

## Part 1: Stakeholder Register and Power-Interest Grid (60 points)

### Instructions

Step 1: Create a Stakeholder Register table with the following columns for each of the eight stakeholders listed above:

- Stakeholder Name / Role
- Stakeholder Type (user, sponsor, SME, regulator, vendor, etc.)
- Power Level (High, Medium, Low) — justify your classification in one sentence
- Interest Level (High, Medium, Low) — justify your classification in one sentence
- Power-Interest Grid Quadrant (Manage Closely / Keep Satisfied / Keep Informed / Monitor)
- Attitude Toward Change (Champion, Neutral, Resistant — infer from the case description)
- Recommended Engagement Approach (one to two sentences describing how the BA should engage this stakeholder)

Step 2: Draw a Power-Interest Grid (2x2 matrix) and place each stakeholder's name in the correct quadrant. You may draw by hand, use any diagramming tool, or use a table to represent the grid.

## Part 2: BA Approach Recommendation (40 points)

### Instructions

Based on the HHN case study, write a BA Approach Recommendation document of approximately 300–400 words addressing the following four questions:

Question 1: Approach Selection — Should HHN's BA use a predictive (Waterfall) approach, an adaptive (Agile) approach, or a hybrid? Identify the approach you recommend.

Question 2: Justification — List at least three specific factors from the case study that support your recommended approach. For each factor, explain how it favors your selection. Use vocabulary from the BABOK KA 2 approach selection framework.

Question 3: Governance Plan — Describe two governance rules that should be in place for this project. For each rule, specify: (a) what triggers the governance process, and (b) who has approval authority.

Question 4: Risks of the Alternative — Briefly describe what risks would arise if the opposite approach were chosen (e.g., if you recommended predictive, what would go wrong with adaptive, and vice versa).

### Rubric — Part 2

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---|
| Approach clearly identified and consistently applied throughout the response | 8 |
| Three or more case-specific justification factors, each correctly explained | 12 |
| Two governance rules with trigger and approval authority defined | 12 |
| Risks of alternative approach identified with case-specific reasoning | 8 |

Part 2 Total: 40 points
