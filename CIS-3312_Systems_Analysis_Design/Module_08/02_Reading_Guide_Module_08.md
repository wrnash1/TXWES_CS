### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 08 – Feasibility Analysis and Cost-Benefit Analysis**! Every systems project represents an organizational investment. Before committing significant resources, decision-makers need evidence that the proposed system is achievable and that its benefits will outweigh its costs. This module covers two critical early-SDLC activities: feasibility analysis (can and should we build it?) and cost-benefit analysis (does it make financial sense?).

These skills align directly with BABOK® Guide v3 Knowledge Area 3 (Strategy Analysis) and Knowledge Area 7 (Solution Evaluation), which examine the current state, define the future state, and assess whether a proposed solution delivers adequate value. For the ECBA exam, feasibility concepts and investment analysis vocabulary are regularly tested in scenario-based questions.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Feasibility Analysis**: Feasibility analysis is a structured investigation conducted early in the SDLC to determine whether a proposed system or project is viable from multiple dimensions before significant investment is made. The four primary dimensions are: technical (can we build it?), economic (does the financial return justify the cost?), operational (will users adopt it and does it fit organizational processes?), and schedule/legal feasibility. The output is a feasibility study report that informs the go/no-go decision.

*   **Technical Feasibility**: Technical feasibility assesses whether the organization possesses or can acquire the technology, infrastructure, and human skills required to build, deploy, and maintain the proposed system. It considers hardware, software, integration complexity, network requirements, and team expertise. A project may be economically attractive but technically infeasible if the required technology does not yet exist or if the development team lacks the necessary skills.

*   **Economic Feasibility**: Economic feasibility determines whether the expected benefits of a proposed system justify its costs — both one-time development costs and ongoing operational costs. It is the core of cost-benefit analysis. Economic feasibility is typically expressed through financial metrics such as Return on Investment (ROI), Net Present Value (NPV), and payback period. A positive NPV and acceptable payback period are standard decision criteria for approving an IT project.

*   **Net Present Value (NPV)**: Net Present Value is a financial metric that calculates the present-day value of all future cash flows (both costs and benefits) generated by a project, discounted at an appropriate rate (typically the organization's cost of capital). A positive NPV means the project is expected to generate more value than it costs; a negative NPV means the reverse. NPV accounts for the time value of money — a dollar received today is worth more than a dollar received three years from now.

*   **Return on Investment (ROI)**: Return on Investment is the ratio of net benefit (total benefits minus total costs) to total cost, expressed as a percentage. ROI = (Net Benefit / Total Cost) × 100%. It provides a simple, intuitive measure of investment efficiency but does not account for the time value of money or the timing of cash flows, making NPV a more rigorous financial metric for long-horizon projects.

*   **Payback Period**: The payback period is the time required for cumulative project benefits to equal (recover) the total project investment cost. A shorter payback period is preferred because it reduces financial risk. For example, a project with $500,000 in total costs and $125,000 in annual net benefits has a payback period of four years. Unlike NPV and ROI, the payback period does not measure profitability — it only measures how quickly the investment is recovered.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Four Feasibility Dimensions**: The ECBA exam often presents a scenario describing a project problem and asks which type of feasibility is at risk. Match: insufficient technology/skills → technical; poor financial return → economic; user resistance or process mismatch → operational; regulatory deadline impossible to meet → schedule/legal. Know all four.
*   **NPV Interpretation**: If NPV > 0, the project creates value and should generally be approved. If NPV < 0, the project destroys value. If NPV = 0, the project exactly breaks even at the discount rate. The ECBA exam may present a word problem with NPV result and ask for the correct interpretation.
*   **ROI vs. NPV**: ROI is simpler but ignores time value of money; NPV is more accurate for projects with costs and benefits spread across multiple years. The ECBA exam may ask which metric is more appropriate for a long-horizon project — the answer is NPV.
*   **Study Resource**: The Corporate Finance Institute offers a free online explanation of NPV, ROI, and payback period at [https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/) — the "Capital Budgeting" section provides the clearest explanations aligned to how these concepts appear on business analysis certification exams.
