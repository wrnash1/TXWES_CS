## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

This section is optional and not separately graded. It extends the final module into
advanced ECBA exam preparation and professional reflection practice.

### Challenge Step 1: Personal ECBA Study Plan

Using the IIBA ECBA Exam Blueprint and the knowledge area weight distribution provided in
the Module 16 reading guide, develop a personal structured study plan for ECBA exam
preparation. Your study plan must: create a two-week daily study schedule with specific
BABOK® knowledge areas or topics assigned to each day (fourteen days), allocate more
study time to higher-weighted knowledge areas (KA 4 Elicitation and KA 5 Requirements
Analysis at 18% each, KA 2 and KA 6 at 16% each), identify your three personally weakest
knowledge areas based on your performance across Modules 1–15 labs and quizzes, design
two targeted self-assessment activities for each weak knowledge area (such as re-reading
the reading guide, re-attempting lab exercises, or creating flashcards for key terms and
technique definitions), and specify the exam logistics you will need to complete: how to
register through the IIBA website, how to schedule at an authorized testing center, what
identification is required, and what to expect on exam day. This exercise develops the
metacognitive planning skills — assessing your own knowledge gaps and designing targeted
remediation — that distinguish candidates who pass certification exams from those who
study unfocused and underperform.

### Challenge Step 2: Cross-Module Integration Case Study

Select any two modules from Modules 11–15 and write a two-to-three page integrated case
study that demonstrates how the content of those two modules works together in a single
real-world business analysis project. For example, if you select Module 11 (Data Modeling)
and Module 14 (Testing and QA), your case study might show how an ERD produced during
design feeds directly into RTM traceability and data integrity test cases during UAT. Your
case study must: describe a realistic business scenario (fictional organization and system
are acceptable), show how a BA artifact produced in the first selected module becomes an
input to a BA activity in the second selected module, identify which BABOK® knowledge
areas govern each module's content and explain how the two knowledge areas intersect in
your scenario, produce one concrete example artifact (such as a partial ERD, a test case,
a training plan excerpt, or an ADKAR intervention design) that illustrates the integration
point, and write a one-paragraph reflection on how understanding the connections between
knowledge areas — rather than treating them as isolated topics — changes how a BA
approaches a project. This exercise prepares you for the ECBA's scenario-based questions,
which frequently require integrating knowledge across multiple knowledge areas to identify
the correct answer.

### Challenge Step 3: ECBA Practice Exam Self-Assessment

Using the fifteen questions from the Module 16 quiz as a mock exam, complete a full
timed self-assessment under exam conditions: allow yourself 18 minutes (simulating the
60-minute / 50-question ECBA pace), answer all fifteen questions without referring to
notes, then score your results and complete the following analysis. For each question you
answered incorrectly, write a one-paragraph post-mortem identifying: which BABOK®
knowledge area the question tested, which specific concept or distinction you missed
(for example, confusing Verify Requirements with Validate Requirements, or misidentifying
which KA owns a technique), and what you will do to close that gap before the real exam.
Then write an overall exam readiness assessment: rate your confidence across each of the
seven ECBA-tested knowledge areas on a scale of one to five, identify your single highest-
priority study focus, and set a target exam date that gives you at least two weeks of
focused preparation after completing the course. This structured post-assessment reflection
mirrors the professional practice of debriefing after each project milestone — a habit
that distinguishes strong BAs throughout their careers, not only during exam preparation.
