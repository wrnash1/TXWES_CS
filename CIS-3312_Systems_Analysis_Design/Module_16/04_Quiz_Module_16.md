**Question 1**
The IIBA ECBA exam consists of how many questions, and what is the time limit?
*   A) 100 questions, 120 minutes
*   B) 50 questions, 60 minutes
*   C) 75 questions, 90 minutes
*   D) 25 questions, 45 minutes
*   **Correct Answer:** B) 50 questions, 60 minutes
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* 100 questions / 120 minutes describes a longer certification exam format (such as the CBAP); the ECBA is shorter as an entry-level credential.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* 75 questions / 90 minutes is not the ECBA format; this could describe a mid-level certification structure, but not the ECBA specifically.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* 25 questions is too few to constitute a professional certification; the ECBA uses 50 questions to provide sufficient coverage of BABOK® knowledge areas.
    *   *Why B is correct:* The IIBA ECBA exam is 50 multiple-choice scenario-based questions with a 60-minute time limit (72 minutes with testing accommodations).

**Question 2**
In the context of the IIBA ECBA certification, which of the following is the most accurate definition of the **Business Analysis Core Concept Model (BACCM)**?
*   A) A project management framework that defines five process groups (Initiating, Planning, Executing, Monitoring, Closing) for managing BA projects
*   B) A foundational framework of six interrelated concepts — Change, Need, Solution, Stakeholder, Value, and Context — that frame all business analysis work in BABOK® v3
*   C) A competency assessment tool that rates BAs on 28 behavioral competencies across six competency categories for performance reviews
*   D) The seven BABOK® Knowledge Areas (KA 2–7 plus Perspectives) that organize all BA tasks and techniques for the ECBA exam
*   **Correct Answer:** B) A foundational framework of six interrelated concepts — Change, Need, Solution, Stakeholder, Value, and Context — that frame all business analysis work in BABOK® v3
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Five process groups (Initiating, Planning, etc.) describe the PMI PMBOK process framework, not the IIBA BACCM.
    *   *Why C is incorrect:* A 28-competency behavioral assessment describes the IIBA Business Analysis Competency Model, not the BACCM.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* The seven Knowledge Areas are the organizational structure of the BABOK®, not the BACCM. The BACCM is the conceptual foundation (six concepts) that underpins the KAs.
    *   *Why B is correct:* The BACCM defines the six core concepts (Change, Need, Solution, Stakeholder, Value, Context) that permeate all BA work in BABOK® v3 and provides the conceptual vocabulary for every knowledge area, task, and technique.

**Question 3**
A BA is managing a requirements list of 35 items for a system with limited development resources. The product owner wants to prioritize the list to ensure the most critical requirements are delivered first. Using MoSCoW prioritization, which category represents requirements that are absolutely essential for the system to function at go-live?
*   A) Should Have — important requirements that significantly improve the system but are not critical
*   B) Could Have — desirable requirements to include if schedule and resources permit
*   C) Must Have — requirements without which the solution would be unviable or fail its core purpose
*   D) Won't Have — requirements that are explicitly excluded from the current release
*   **Correct Answer:** C) Must Have — requirements without which the solution would be unviable or fail its core purpose
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* Should Have requirements are valuable but not critical; the solution can go live without them, though with reduced quality.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* Could Have requirements are nice-to-have items included only if resources allow; they are not essential for viability.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Won't Have requirements are explicitly deferred — they represent out-of-scope items, not essential requirements.
    *   *Why C is correct:* "Must Have" in MoSCoW denotes requirements that are non-negotiable — without them, the solution cannot meet its minimum viable purpose. They represent the highest priority category in MoSCoW and must be delivered in the first release.

**Question 4**
A BA is reviewing the BABOK® v3 Knowledge Area that covers tracing requirements to their source stakeholders and forward to test cases, maintaining requirements as they evolve, and obtaining formal stakeholder approval of the requirements baseline. Which Knowledge Area is this?
*   A) KA 4 — Elicitation and Collaboration
*   B) KA 5 — Requirements Analysis and Design Definition
*   C) KA 6 — Requirements Life Cycle Management
*   D) KA 3 — Strategy Analysis
*   **Correct Answer:** C) KA 6 — Requirements Life Cycle Management
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* KA 4 covers gathering and confirming information from stakeholders; it does not cover the ongoing management, tracing, and approval of requirements after they are documented.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* KA 5 covers specifying, modeling, verifying, validating, and defining design options for requirements; its focus is analysis, not lifecycle management and governance.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* KA 3 covers strategy analysis (current state, future state, gap analysis, change strategy); it does not manage the requirements lifecycle.
    *   *Why C is correct:* BABOK® KA 6 (Requirements Life Cycle Management) specifically covers the five lifecycle management tasks: Trace Requirements, Maintain Requirements, Prioritize Requirements, Assess Requirements Changes, and Approve Requirements — all activities described in the question.

**Question 5**
A BA has just completed the first year of their career in business analysis and wants to begin preparing for the IIBA ECBA certification. The BA has attended a 21-hour BA training course and regularly uses BABOK® Guide techniques on the job. What is the BA's most strategic first preparation step?
*   A) Register immediately for the ECBA exam without any additional study, relying entirely on on-the-job experience
*   B) Wait until accumulating two full years of experience before applying, since the ECBA requires two years minimum
*   C) Download the ECBA Exam Blueprint from the IIBA website to understand which knowledge areas are weighted most heavily, then align study effort accordingly
*   D) Study exclusively from the BABOK® chapter on Requirements Analysis (KA 5) since it contains the most techniques
*   **Correct Answer:** C) Download the ECBA Exam Blueprint from the IIBA website to understand which knowledge areas are weighted most heavily, then align study effort accordingly
*   **Distractor Analysis:**
    *   *Why A is incorrect:* While on-the-job experience is valuable, the ECBA exam tests specific BABOK® v3 terminology, task names, and technique definitions — scenario-based exam preparation is necessary.
    *   *Why B is incorrect:* The ECBA does not require two years of experience — it requires 21 hours of professional development, making it accessible to early-career BAs. Two years of experience is not a prerequisite.
    *   *Why D is incorrect:* Studying only one knowledge area ignores the exam's coverage of all seven knowledge areas; the ECBA exam blueprint shows that elicitation (KA 4), requirements analysis (KA 5), planning (KA 2), and lifecycle management (KA 6) all receive significant weight.
    *   *Why C is correct:* The ECBA Exam Blueprint is the definitive guide to what the exam covers and at what weight. Studying the blueprint first allows the candidate to allocate preparation time proportionally, ensuring the highest-weight knowledge areas receive the most study time — the most efficient preparation strategy.
