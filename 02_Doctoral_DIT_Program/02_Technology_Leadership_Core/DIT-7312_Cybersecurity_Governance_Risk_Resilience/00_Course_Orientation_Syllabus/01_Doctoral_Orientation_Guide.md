# DIT 7312: Doctoral Orientation & Course Delivery Guide
## Professional Expectations for Doctoral Scholars in Information Technology
**Texas Wesleyan University | School of Arts & Sciences**

---

### 1. The Nature of Applied Doctoral Inquiry
The Doctor of Information Technology (D.I.T.) is designed for experienced technology executives, enterprise architects, and senior cybersecurity leaders. Coursework emphasizes:
* **Evidence-Based IT Decision Making:** Moving beyond intuition to rigorously justify architectural and governance interventions through empirical evidence, peer-reviewed literature, and national standards.
* **Executive-Level Communication:** Deliverables must be concise, mathematically defensible, and structured for C-suite and Board of Directors audiences.
* **Regular & Substantive Interaction (RSI):** Consistent, high-touch engagement through asynchronous doctoral forums, detailed rubric evaluations, and bi-weekly synchronous colloquia.

### 2. Weekly Rhythm & Deadlines
* **Monday (12:01 AM):** Modular materials open (Reading Guide, Video Briefing, Case Prompt).
* **Thursday (11:59 PM CT):** Initial Doctoral Colloquium Contribution due (minimum 500 words, peer-reviewed/standards citations mandatory).
* **Sunday (11:59 PM CT):**
  * Substantive Peer Critiques (minimum 2 classmates).
  * Weekly Executive Deliverable / Technical Case Study.
  * Modular Synthesis Assessment.

### 3. Academic Integrity & Responsible AI Policy
Doctoral scholars are encouraged to study generative AI and algorithmic automation as technical artifacts. However, all submitted prose, case analyses, and architectural proposals must reflect original doctoral synthesis. AI tools used for literature discovery or grammatical editing must be transparently declared in an 'AI Acknowledgement Appendix' adhering to Texas Wesleyan Graduate Council standards.
