### Question 1
During a board-level risk review, the Audit Committee questions why the organization's cybersecurity policy adheres to NIST CSF 2.0 rather than relying strictly on annual third-party compliance audits. Which response provides the most defensible doctoral justification?

- A) Compliance audits evaluate historical controls at a single point in time, whereas NIST CSF 2.0 establishes a dynamic, risk-informed governance posture across the entire lifecycle. ✅
- B) NIST CSF 2.0 is legally mandated by the SEC for all domestic corporations, whereas commercial compliance audits are optional.
- C) Third-party compliance audits focus exclusively on physical security, whereas NIST CSF 2.0 focuses solely on application firewalls.
- D) Compliance audits require public disclosure of proprietary algorithms, whereas NIST CSF 2.0 maintains complete trade secret immunity.

**Correct Answer:** A
**Distractor Analysis:**
- *Why A is correct:* Compliance represents a point-in-time adherence to minimum standards; NIST CSF 2.0 creates continuous, outcome-based governance that aligns cybersecurity risk with enterprise mission goals.
- *Why B is incorrect:* NIST CSF is a voluntary guidance framework (unless mandated for federal agencies via Executive Order), not an SEC statutory rule.
- *Why C is incorrect:* Compliance audits cover logical and technical controls across the stack, not merely physical security.
- *Why D is incorrect:* Compliance audits do not necessitate public disclosure of proprietary code.
