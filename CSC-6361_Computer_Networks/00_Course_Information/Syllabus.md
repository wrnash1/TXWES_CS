## Course Overview

### Catalog Description
CSC-6361 Advanced Computer Networks is a graduate-level course designed for students pursuing careers in senior network engineering, architecture, and administration. The course examines enterprise-scale networking technologies at the depth and rigor aligned with the **Cisco Certified Network Professional (CCNP) Enterprise** certification track. Students will analyze, design, and troubleshoot complex multi-site routed and switched networks using industry-standard protocols, tools, and methodologies.

### Course Prerequisites
*   Undergraduate coursework in networking (e.g., CIS-3321 Network Administration or equivalent) **or** Cisco CCNA-level certification.
*   Familiarity with the OSI model, IP addressing, basic routing (RIP/OSPF), and switching concepts.
*   Graduate standing in the MS in Computer Science or MS in Information Technology program.

### Course Description
This course provides an in-depth exploration of advanced networking concepts including multi-area OSPF, EIGRP, eBGP/iBGP, MPLS, SD-WAN, campus network design, QoS, network automation with Python/Ansible, high-availability architectures, cloud networking, and enterprise security hardening. Emphasis is placed on applied configuration, analysis of real-world case studies, and development of graduate-level professional communication skills.

### Course Objectives / Student Learning Outcomes
By the end of this course, graduate students will be able to:
1. Design and implement enterprise-scale routing architectures using OSPF (multi-area), EIGRP, and BGP in a simulated production environment.
2. Analyze and configure campus network infrastructure including advanced switching (VLANs, STP variants, EtherChannel), WAN technologies (MPLS, SD-WAN), and VPN architectures.
3. Evaluate and apply Quality of Service (QoS) policies, High Availability (HA) mechanisms (HSRP, VRRP), and network automation frameworks (Python scripting, Ansible playbooks) to enterprise environments.
4. Synthesize knowledge of cloud-integrated networking architectures (hybrid cloud, SD-Access) and assess their operational and security implications.
5. Demonstrate proficiency in systematic network troubleshooting methodology through structured lab exercises, producing professional-grade documentation.
6. Critically evaluate advanced networking design decisions using CCNP Enterprise examination-level problem sets and justify architectural choices in graduate-level writing assignments.

## Graduate-Level Expectations

This is a graduate course. The expectations for depth of analysis, quality of writing, and rigor of lab execution are significantly higher than undergraduate-level courses.

*   **Technical Depth:** Lab reports must include not only screenshots of successful configurations but also written analysis explaining *why* the configuration works, what would happen if it failed, and how it would be monitored in a production environment.
*   **Graduate Discussion Board:** Posts must be substantive (400+ words), reference at least one external technical source (RFC, Cisco documentation, or peer-reviewed article), and include a meaningful response to at least two peers.
*   **Research Paper (Module 06):** Students will produce a 5–7 page technical paper on a self-selected advanced networking topic (e.g., SD-WAN deployment strategy, BGP security and RPKI, IPv6 migration planning). Paper must include at least 5 credible technical references.

## University & Departmental Policies

### Attendance & Participation Policy
Since this course is conducted 100% online asynchronously, attendance is measured by weekly engagement. Students must log in to Canvas and submit at least one required assignment (discussion post, lab, or quiz) each week to be marked "Present." Failure to submit work for **two consecutive weeks** will be flagged for departmental review and may lead to administrative withdrawal per Texas Wesleyan University catalog policy.

> **7-Week Session Note:** Because this is a compressed 7-week session, missing even one week represents a significant portion of the course. Students are strongly encouraged to contact the instructor immediately if personal circumstances may prevent timely submissions.

### Academic Integrity & Generative AI Policy
Texas Wesleyan University values academic honesty. Plagiarism, cheating, or any unauthorized collaboration will result in a zero for the assignment and potential disciplinary action, up to and including suspension. Refer to the *Texas Wesleyan Student Handbook* for full policies.

*   **Generative AI Guidelines (Graduate Level):** You are permitted to use AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot) as a research accelerator and for understanding complex error messages during lab work. However, **all submitted work — discussion posts, lab reports, and the research paper — must represent your own original analysis and writing.** AI-generated text submitted verbatim as your own work constitutes academic dishonesty. For the research paper, you must be able to defend any claim in your paper in a one-on-one discussion with the instructor.
