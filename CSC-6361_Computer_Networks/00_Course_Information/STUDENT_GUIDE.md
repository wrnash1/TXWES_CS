## Welcome, Graduate Student!

You are enrolled in a **7-week, graduate-level** networking course that covers content aligned with the **Cisco Certified Network Professional (CCNP) Enterprise** track. This is rigorous, applied material — and it is entirely achievable if you approach the course with consistency and professionalism.

This guide will walk you through everything you need to know to succeed from Day 1.

## Step 3 — Lab Submission Requirements

Each lab requires two deliverables:

**1. Packet Tracer File (.pkt):**
Save your completed Packet Tracer topology as a `.pkt` file and upload it to Canvas. This is the primary evidence that your configurations work.

**2. Lab Report (PDF):**
Your lab report must include:
- **Topology Diagram** — a screenshot or exported image of your Packet Tracer topology
- **Configuration Commands** — the key CLI commands you entered (copy/paste from the Packet Tracer CLI)
- **Verification Output** — screenshots of verification commands (e.g., `show ip ospf neighbor`, `show ip bgp summary`, `ping` results)
- **Analysis Section** — 2–3 paragraphs explaining why your configuration works, what would happen if a key component failed, and how you would monitor this in production
- **Troubleshooting Log** — a brief log of any errors you encountered and how you resolved them (graduate-level honesty and reflection)

## Step 4 — Discussion Board Expectations (Graduate Level)

Discussion boards in a graduate course are academic conversations, not social posts. Your initial post must:
- Be **400 words or more**.
- Address the specific prompt given in the module.
- Include at least **one citation** from a credible technical source (an IETF RFC, Cisco documentation, DevNet learning lab, or a peer-reviewed article).
- Demonstrate that you actually worked through the lab and can connect theory to your hands-on experience.

Your **peer responses** must:
- Be substantive (not "Great post! I agree.").
- Add new information, pose a challenging question, or offer an alternative perspective.

## Step 5 — Research Paper (Module 06)

The Graduate Research Paper is due **December 1, 2026** (extended deadline due to Thanksgiving).

**Requirements:**
- **Length:** 5–7 pages, double-spaced, 12pt font (not counting references or title page)
- **Topic:** An advanced networking topic of your choice (approved by instructor no later than November 13)
- **References:** Minimum 5 credible technical sources (RFCs, Cisco documentation, IEEE/ACM papers, or authoritative technical blogs)
- **Citation Format:** APA or IEEE (be consistent throughout)
- **Original Analysis:** Your paper should advance a clear thesis and provide your own analytical perspective — it should not simply summarize your sources.

**Suggested Topics:**
- SD-WAN vs. MPLS: cost, performance, and migration strategy
- BGP Route Origin Validation (RPKI) and internet routing security
- Network automation with Ansible: practical use cases and limitations
- Zero Trust Network Access (ZTNA) architecture design
- IPv6 enterprise migration planning

## Step 6 — Final Capstone (Module 07)

The Module 07 Final Capstone is your opportunity to demonstrate everything you have learned.

**Component A — Capstone Lab (60% of Final grade):**
You will receive a requirements brief describing an enterprise network and must build it in Packet Tracer. Requirements will include multi-area OSPF, BGP peering, VLANs, HA, and QoS. A professional lab report is required.

**Component B — Written Exam (40% of Final grade):**
A timed Canvas quiz with CCNP Enterprise-style scenario questions covering all 7 modules.
