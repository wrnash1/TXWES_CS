Course Introduction & Excitement

Welcome to DevSecOps CICD Pipelines! In this 3-credit-hour undergraduate course, you will develop practical and analytical competencies in devsecops cicd pipelines. This course is delivered in a 100% online asynchronous format, providing maximum scheduling flexibility for adult and working learners while delivering rigorous academic content.

Course Description & Prerequisites

DevSecOps shift-left automation, commit linters, GitHub Actions YAML workflows, Docker setups, SAST/DAST testing, SCA dependencies, Vault secret masking, and chaos injections.

Prerequisite: CIS-1310 or instructor approval.

Instructional & Learning Methods

The learning methods used in this online course include:

  • ☑ Direct Online Instruction: Structured interactive Reading Guides, visual architecture SVGs, and recorded video walkthroughs.
  • ☑ Hands-On Simulation Labs: Practical exercises, code implementation, and command-line diagnostics.
  • ☑ Discussion Forums: Peer-to-peer technical inquiry, real-world case analysis, and collaborative troubleshooting.
  • ☑ Auto-Graded Quizzes: Timed scenario assessments with instant distractor analysis and explanatory feedback.
  • ☑ Comprehensive Final Assessment: Proctored / comprehensive course examination.

Academic Integrity & Generative AI Policy

Cheating, plagiarism (submitting another person’s material as one’s own), or completing assignments for another person who will receive academic credit are strictly prohibited at Texas Wesleyan University in accordance with the University Academic Integrity Policy.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy: Generative AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot) may be utilized as supplementary tutors to explain complex technical concepts or brainstorm approaches. However, you may NOT submit AI-generated text, lab worksheets, discussion responses, or quiz answers as your own original work. Any authorized AI assistance must be explicitly cited. Submitting undisclosed AI-generated content is treated as plagiarism.

Class Attendance & Distance Education Engagement Policy

Students enrolled in distance education courses must demonstrate continuous academic engagement throughout the term to be counted present. Academic engagement requires active participation: submitting weekly labs, posting to discussion forums, completing quizzes, or communicating with the instructor. Simply logging into Canvas does not constitute academic engagement.

Students must demonstrate academic engagement at least once per week. During the first week of class, completion of the Introduce Yourself Discussion and Syllabus Quiz serves as the official census verification of enrollment and engagement.

Faculty-Initiated Student Withdrawal from a Course

When a student exceeds the permissible number of absences or fails to meet the course participation expectations, the instructor may drop the student using the Faculty Initiated Student Withdrawal process. The Registrar will record a grade of “DP” on the student’s transcript if dropped prior to the deadline, or “WF” (withdrawal failing) if dropped afterward. Note: The last day to receive a “DP” for Fall 2026 is Friday, November 20, 2026.
