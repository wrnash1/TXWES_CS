### Overview

This discussion asks you to apply dashboard design principles and KPI thinking to professional communication scenarios. Choose one of the three scenarios below, write an original post of 175–225 words, then respond to at least two classmates who chose different scenarios. Responses must be 75–100 words and add new reasoning rather than simply agreeing.

---

### Scenario A — The Vanity Metric Meeting

You are a data analyst at an e-commerce startup. The CEO insists that every weekly leadership meeting open with a slide showing three metrics: total registered users, total orders placed since launch, and total social media followers. Your analysis shows that these numbers grow every week regardless of business health because the company runs continuous discount promotions. The metrics have never prompted a single strategic decision.

Write a post that proposes replacing these three metrics with actionable KPIs. Address the following:

* What makes the current three metrics vanity metrics rather than KPIs?
* What three KPIs would you propose and how is each tied to a business outcome?
* How would you present this argument to a CEO who is proud of those growing numbers?
* What visualization format would you use to display your proposed KPIs in the leadership meeting?

---

### Scenario B — Dashboard for Two Audiences

A regional hospital has asked you to build a patient flow dashboard. The Chief Medical Officer wants to see monthly trends in patient admission rates, average length of stay, and readmission rates. The emergency department charge nurse wants to see real-time bed availability, current patients waiting longer than four hours, and staff-to-patient ratio per shift.

Write a post that argues for building two separate dashboards rather than one combined dashboard. Address the following:

* Why does a single combined dashboard fail both audiences?
* What specific design decisions differ between an executive dashboard and an operational dashboard?
* What update frequency is appropriate for each?
* How does the storytelling format differ between a monthly executive review and a real-time operations screen?

---

### Scenario C — Chart Type Dispute

A product analyst at a software company is preparing the quarterly business review. She wants to use a pie chart with eight slices to show revenue by product line because "it looks professional." A colleague argues the pie chart is unreadable and insists on a horizontal bar chart. Both cannot agree and have escalated to you as the senior analyst.

Write a post that resolves the dispute with a clear recommendation supported by design principles. Address the following:

* Why does a pie chart fail for eight slices?
* What chart type would you recommend and what are the specific advantages for this use case?
* What design principle from Module 13 directly supports your recommendation?
* How would you frame this recommendation to preserve the product analyst's confidence?
