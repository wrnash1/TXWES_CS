## Scenario A — Dashboard Design for a Hospital

A regional hospital wants to create an executive dashboard that displays four key operational metrics simultaneously:

- Daily patient admission count over the past 30 days
- Distribution of patient wait times in the emergency department (in minutes)
- Percentage of beds occupied by department (ICU, Medical/Surgical, Pediatrics, Maternity, Rehab)
- Comparison of average patient satisfaction scores across five attending physicians

In 175–225 words, address all three of the following:

1. For each of the four metrics, recommend the most appropriate chart type and justify your choice in one to two sentences each. Explain specifically what the chart needs to communicate for each metric.
2. One of the department heads asks to show bed occupancy as a pie chart. Another argues for a horizontal bar chart. Which argument is stronger, and why? Reference visualization principles (not just personal preference) in your answer.
3. The dashboard will be reviewed in a 10-minute executive meeting. What two design principles are most important when visualizing data for an executive audience with limited time, and how would you apply them to this dashboard?

## Scenario B — Identifying Misleading Visualizations

A financial analyst at a retail company prepares three charts for the quarterly earnings call:

Chart 1 is a bar chart comparing revenue across five regions. The y-axis runs from $4.8M to $5.4M. The largest region's bar is six times the height of the smallest, even though the actual values range only from $4.9M to $5.3M.

Chart 2 is a pie chart showing customer distribution across 9 customer segments. All slices are similar in size (8–14% each), rendered in similar shades of blue.

Chart 3 is a 3D bar chart comparing quarterly revenue for three product lines. The front bars appear taller than the back bars even when the values are equal, due to the perspective effect.

In 175–225 words, address all three of the following:

1. For each of the three charts, identify the specific design problem and explain precisely how it would mislead an investor or board member who viewed it.
2. Propose a corrected version of each chart. For Chart 1, specify the correct axis range and explain the visual result of the correction.
3. A colleague argues that making charts "look impressive" for investors is standard practice and that these visual enhancements are not misleading because "the numbers are all there in the footnotes." Construct a counter-argument based on the ethics of data visualization.

## Scenario C — Chart Type Selection for a Nonprofit Report

A nonprofit organization is preparing its annual report. The communications team has collected data on donations, program outcomes, and volunteer hours over the past five years. They need to decide on chart types for four key visualizations.

Visualization 1: Show that total annual donations grew from $1.2M in 2020 to $2.4M in 2024.

Visualization 2: Show that the distribution of individual donation amounts is heavily right-skewed — most donors give small amounts but a few give very large gifts.

Visualization 3: Show that there is a strong positive relationship between the number of volunteer hours contributed to a program and the program's community satisfaction rating.

Visualization 4: Show how the $2.4M in 2024 donations was allocated across six program areas.

In 175–225 words, address all three of the following:

1. For each of the four visualizations, name the chart type you would recommend, and explain in one sentence why it is the best fit for the specific data and message.
2. The communications director wants to use a pie chart for Visualization 2 to show the "typical" donor. Explain why a pie chart is wrong for this purpose, and describe what information the correct chart type would reveal that a pie chart cannot.
3. For Visualization 1, the team is debating whether to start the y-axis at zero or at $1M to "better show the growth." What is the correct choice and why? What risk does a non-zero y-axis create for a nonprofit's donors and supporters?
