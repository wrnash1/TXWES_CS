### Scenario A — The Re-identification Risk

A public health department wants to publish a community health dataset to help researchers study chronic disease patterns. The dataset includes ZIP code, age bracket (10-year ranges), gender, diagnosis category, and year of visit. No names or identifiers are included. A privacy officer argues the dataset is safe to publish. A data scientist on the team argues it is not.

Write a post taking the data scientist's side. Address the following:

* Why is the removal of names and IDs insufficient to guarantee anonymization?
* Which specific columns create re-identification risk and why?
* What anonymization technique would you recommend to reduce the risk while preserving enough utility for the research purpose?
* Under which regulation does the health department have an obligation to protect this data?

---

### Scenario B — The Biased Hiring Algorithm

A large technology company uses an automated resume screening tool to filter applicants before human review. An internal audit reveals the tool approves 74% of male applicants and 38% of female applicants. The model does not include gender as a feature. The company's legal team says the model is compliant because gender is not in the training data.

Write a post challenging the legal team's conclusion. Address the following:

* What is proxy discrimination and how could a model produce gender-based disparate impact without using gender directly?
* What additional features in a resume dataset could serve as proxies for gender?
* What analysis would you perform to confirm or rule out proxy discrimination?
* What is one change to the hiring process that would reduce algorithmic bias without completely removing the tool?

---

### Scenario C — The GDPR Request

A digital marketing agency based in Chicago runs campaigns for European clients and collects browsing data, email addresses, and location history from EU residents who visit its clients' websites. A German resident submits a written request to know what personal data the agency holds about them and asks for it to be deleted.

Write a post that describes how the agency must respond and what the consequences are for non-compliance. Address the following:

* Does GDPR apply to this US-based agency? Why or why not?
* What specific rights is the German resident exercising?
* What steps must the agency take within the GDPR response window?
* What penalty does the agency face if it ignores the request?
