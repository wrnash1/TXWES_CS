## Scenario: Cascade Regional Hospital IT Department

Cascade Regional Hospital has a 340-bed facility and employs 2,200 staff including physicians, nurses, administrative personnel, and technical staff. The IT department delivers 18 services including the electronic health record (EHR) system, clinical imaging, the nurse call system network, staff scheduling software, the patient portal, and general infrastructure services.

The hospital recently experienced a significant patient safety incident when the EHR system was unavailable for 4 hours during a busy shift. A root cause investigation revealed the following:

The EHR vendor had released a critical security patch three weeks earlier, but the IT team had not applied it because they were unsure who had authority to approve system changes. The patch contained a bug that the vendor had since corrected, but the correction was in a subsequent patch that the IT team also had not applied.

The nurse informatics specialist who normally serves as the liaison between clinical staff and IT had left the organization six months earlier and had not been replaced. Clinical staff had been reporting intermittent EHR slowdowns for four weeks, but the reports went to a personal email inbox that no one monitored after the specialist left.

The EHR is hosted and supported by a managed service provider (MSP) called CareHost Solutions. The contract with CareHost requires them to apply vendor-approved patches within 30 days. However, the IT director was unaware this clause existed and had been applying patches independently, sometimes duplicating CareHost's work and sometimes conflicting with their configurations.

The hospital's patch management procedure was last updated in 2019 and does not address cloud-hosted systems or third-party managed services. No workflow exists for triaging clinical staff reports of system slowdowns.

## Exercise 1: Four Dimensions Mapping (30 points)

For each of the five specific problems described in the scenario, identify the primary ITIL 4 dimension it belongs to, name the dimension, and explain in two to three sentences why that dimension is the primary one and what it would require to address the problem.

Problems to map:

1. No one had authority to approve system change (patch application) decisions.
2. The nurse informatics specialist role was vacant, and clinical feedback went unmonitored.
3. The contract with CareHost Solutions was not understood by the IT director.
4. The patch management procedure was outdated and did not cover cloud-hosted systems.
5. The EHR vendor had released two sequential patches, but the IT team had no structured process for monitoring vendor release communications.

## Exercise 2: Multi-Dimensional Improvement Plan (30 points)

Dr. Okonkwo, the newly appointed Chief Information Officer, has asked you to draft an improvement recommendation that addresses all four dimensions. Write a structured improvement plan (minimum 250 words) organized by dimension. For each dimension:

* Describe the specific gap that exists at Cascade Regional Hospital based on the scenario.
* Recommend two concrete actions the hospital's IT department should take to address that gap.
* Identify which ITIL 4 Guiding Principle supports each recommended action.

Your plan must address all four dimensions and reference at least six distinct Guiding Principles across the four sections. (You may use the same principle in more than one section if it genuinely applies.)

## Exercise 4: Dimension Interaction Analysis (20 points)

The scenario reveals that problems in one dimension created or worsened problems in other dimensions. Write a short analysis (minimum 125 words) that traces how the failure in the Organizations and People dimension (the vacant nurse informatics role) cascaded into failures in at least two other dimensions. Be specific about which dimensions were affected and how the cascade happened.
