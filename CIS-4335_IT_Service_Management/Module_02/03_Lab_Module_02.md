## Overview

This lab asks you to apply the Service Value System framework to a realistic IT organization scenario. You will map organizational activities to SVS components, trace a value stream through SVC activities, identify which practices are involved, and evaluate how well the organization's governance and improvement activities align with ITIL 4. No software tools or terminal commands are required. All work is analytical and written.

Submit all exercises as a single document to the Canvas assignment portal by the posted deadline.

## Scenario: Meridian Financial's IT Department Overhaul

Meridian Financial Services has 1,400 employees across three office locations. The company recently hired a new CIO, Dr. Rivera, who has been asked to modernize the IT department. Here is what she discovered in her first 30 days:

The IT department delivers twelve distinct services, including email, file storage, the core banking application, the HR portal, and the employee VPN. However, no service catalog exists and no one has formally documented what each service does or what performance levels are expected.

When users experience problems, they call a general IT phone number. There is no ticketing system. Tickets are logged in a shared spreadsheet that only two staff members can access. Resolution times vary wildly, and there is no formal escalation path.

The bank's board of directors recently approved a new data security policy requiring that all IT services be audited for compliance with financial data protection regulations. However, IT staff are unaware of this policy and have not changed any practices in response.

A software development team is building a new mobile banking application. They work in two-week Agile sprints and deploy code every sprint. However, no formal process exists for testing or approving deployments before they go live to customers.

Dr. Rivera wants to implement ITIL 4 as the framework for modernizing IT service management at Meridian Financial.

For each of the five SVS components listed below, identify one specific gap or problem in the Meridian Financial scenario that represents a failure or absence of that component. Write one to two sentences explaining what is missing and which component it corresponds to.

SVS components to address:

1. Guiding Principles
2. Governance
3. Service Value Chain
4. Practices
5. Continual Improvement

## Exercise 2: Value Stream Tracing (25 points)

Dr. Rivera wants to establish a formal process for handling user-reported service disruptions. Using the six SVC activities (Plan, Improve, Engage, Design and Transition, Obtain/Build, Deliver and Support), design a value stream for the following scenario:

A Meridian Financial employee calls the IT help desk to report that the core banking application is inaccessible from her workstation.

Write out the value stream step by step. For each step:

* Name the SVC activity involved.
* Describe what specifically happens in that step within the Meridian Financial context.
* Explain why this activity is necessary at this point in the value stream.

Your value stream must include at least four distinct SVC activities and must result in a resolved service disruption and a logged improvement opportunity.

## Exercise 3: Governance Analysis (25 points)

The board of directors has approved a new data security policy, but IT staff are completely unaware of it. Write a structured analysis (minimum 175 words) addressing all three of the following:

1. Using ITIL 4 definitions, explain what governance is and what role it plays in the SVS. Is this gap a governance failure, a management failure, or both? Justify your answer.
2. Describe two specific ITIL 4-aligned actions Dr. Rivera should take to close the gap between the board's security policy and the IT staff's daily practices. Reference at least one SVC activity and one Guiding Principle in your response.
3. Explain what risk Meridian Financial faces if this governance gap is not closed, connecting your answer to the Partners and Suppliers or Information and Technology dimension.

## Exercise 4: Practices Identification (30 points)

The Meridian Financial scenario reveals the absence of several ITIL 4 practices. For each situation described below, identify the ITIL 4 practice that should be implemented, name the practice category it belongs to (General Management, Service Management, or Technical Management), and write two to three sentences explaining how implementing that practice would improve the situation.

Situations to address:

1. No service catalog exists; users and staff do not know what services are available or what they are expected to deliver.
2. No ticketing system exists; incidents are tracked in a shared spreadsheet with no escalation path.
3. The mobile banking app is deployed every sprint with no formal testing or approval process.
4. No one tracks whether IT services are meeting performance targets; there is no measurement or reporting process.
5. The IT department has no formal relationship with the board of directors; the security policy reached no one in IT.
6. No one in IT tracks the hardware and software assets used to deliver services; there is no inventory.
