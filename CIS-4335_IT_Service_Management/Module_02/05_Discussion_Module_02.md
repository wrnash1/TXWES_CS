# Discussion Forum: Module 02 — The ITIL Service Value System (SVS)

**Course:** CIS-4335 IT Service Management — Texas Wesleyan University
**Instructor:** Professor Nash
**Total Points:** 10

---

## Overview

This discussion asks you to apply the Service Value System framework to realistic organizational situations. Choose one scenario from the three options below. Write an initial post responding to that scenario, then reply constructively to at least two classmates.

Use correct ITIL 4 terminology throughout. Vague responses that restate the prompt without applying framework concepts will not earn full credit.

---

## Scenario A: The Skipped Planning Activity

A healthcare IT department rolls out a new electronic health record (EHR) system. The project team is under pressure to meet a tight regulatory deadline and skips the Plan and Engage SVC activities, jumping directly to Design and Transition and Obtain/Build. The rollout completes on time, but within the first week clinical staff report that the system does not support the documentation workflow they actually use, and two physicians have submitted formal complaints that they were never consulted before go-live.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Identify which SVC activities were skipped and explain what each activity is designed to accomplish. What specific outcomes were lost by skipping them?
2. Identify which ITIL 4 Guiding Principle or Principles were violated in this scenario. Be specific about how the principle was violated, not just its name.
3. If you were the IT director asked to prevent this problem on the next EHR module rollout, which two SVS components would you prioritize strengthening, and what specific actions would you take?

---

## Scenario B: The Governance Gap

A regional logistics company has a mature IT department with well-functioning service management practices. The company's board of directors recently issued a new policy requiring all customer data to be encrypted at rest and in transit, with quarterly compliance audits. Six months later, the internal audit team discovers that three IT services still store unencrypted customer records. The IT director is surprised — she never received the policy document.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Using ITIL 4 definitions, explain what governance is and what it is supposed to accomplish within the SVS. Is this scenario a governance failure, a management failure, or both? Justify your answer carefully.
2. Identify which SVC activity, if properly executed, would have ensured that the board's policy was understood and translated into actionable requirements for IT service management.
3. Explain how the Collaborate and Promote Visibility Guiding Principle, if applied, could have prevented this gap. What would that principle require the organization to do differently?

---

## Scenario C: The Overcomplicated Value Stream

A university IT department creates a value stream for fulfilling standard software installation requests. The value stream has 23 steps, involves approvals from four different managers, takes an average of 14 business days to complete, and requires the requesting employee to submit three separate forms. Users have begun bypassing IT and installing software themselves, creating security vulnerabilities.

Respond to all three of the following prompts:

1. Using ITIL 4 definitions, describe what a value stream is and explain why this one appears to be failing. Identify the primary SVC activities involved in a software installation request value stream and describe what each should accomplish.
2. Identify which ITIL 4 Guiding Principle most directly applies to this situation and explain specifically how applying it would change the value stream design.
3. Explain how the Optimize and Automate principle could be used to reduce the 14-day fulfillment time. What specific steps might be automated, and what human judgment elements should remain?

## A Note from Professor Nash

The SVS is not an abstract diagram to memorize — it is a model of how real organizations actually function, and its components show up in real organizational failures every day. The three scenarios in this discussion are drawn from patterns that occur in IT departments across industries. When you write your response, think about what the organization should have done differently at each step, not just which component was missing. That practical reasoning is what separates a strong response from a textbook recitation.
