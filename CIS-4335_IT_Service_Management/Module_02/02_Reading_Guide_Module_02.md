### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 02 - The ITIL Service Value System (SVS)**! This module explores the overarching model that holds all ITIL 4 concepts together. The SVS describes how an organization's components and activities work together to enable value creation. Understanding the SVS is critical for the ITIL 4 Foundation exam and for applying ITIL in practice, because it is the mental model examiners use when writing scenario questions.

As a student, you will learn the five components of the SVS, how opportunity and demand enter the system and become value, and the role each component plays. Make sure to complete the checklist and review the glossary terms before beginning the lab activity.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam expects you to know these concepts precisely:

*   **Service Value System (SVS)**: The ITIL 4 model describing how all the components and activities of an organization work together as a system to enable value creation. It takes **opportunity and demand** as inputs and produces **value** as its output. The SVS has five components: Guiding Principles, Governance, Service Value Chain, Practices, and Continual Improvement.
*   **Opportunity and Demand**: The two inputs to the SVS. Opportunity represents options or possibilities to add value for stakeholders; demand represents the need or desire for products and services from internal and external customers. Both must be present for the SVS to function.
*   **Value (SVS output)**: The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. In ITIL 4, value is always co-created between the service provider and the service consumer — it is not unilaterally delivered. Value can be tangible (cost savings, faster processing) or intangible (satisfaction, brand trust).
*   **Guiding Principles**: Seven universal recommendations that guide an organization in all circumstances, regardless of changes in strategy or management structure. They are: Focus on Value; Start Where You Are; Progress Iteratively with Feedback; Collaborate and Promote Visibility; Think and Work Holistically; Keep It Simple and Practical; Optimize and Automate.
*   **Governance**: The means by which an organization is directed and controlled. Within the SVS, governance ensures that policies, roles, and accountabilities are defined so the organization operates within agreed boundaries. Governance is distinct from management.
*   **Continual Improvement**: An SVS component — and also a practice — that embeds a culture of ongoing incremental improvement across all areas. The Continual Improvement model has seven steps, from "What is the vision?" through to "How do we keep the momentum going?"

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **SVS Inputs and Output:** The exam frequently asks what the inputs and output of the SVS are. The inputs are **Opportunity and Demand**; the output is **Value**. Do not confuse "demand" with "incidents" or "requests" — those are inputs to specific practices, not the SVS as a whole.
*   **Five SVS Components:** Be able to list all five: Guiding Principles, Governance, Service Value Chain, Practices, and Continual Improvement. Questions may list four of the five and ask which is missing.
*   **Guiding Principles Are Universal:** The exam tests that guiding principles apply to every decision and action, at all levels, in all circumstances. They are not situational rules — they guide all work across the SVS.
*   **Governance vs. Management:** Governance directs and controls (sets direction, defines policy, ensures accountability). Management plans, builds, runs, and improves activities in accordance with governance direction. Do not conflate the two.
*   **Continual Improvement Is Embedded Everywhere:** The exam may present a scenario where a team claims "CI is the responsibility of the CI team only." This is incorrect — CI is everyone's responsibility and is embedded throughout the SVS.
