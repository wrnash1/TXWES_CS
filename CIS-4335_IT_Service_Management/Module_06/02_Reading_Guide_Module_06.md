### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 06 - General Management Practices: Continual Improvement**! Continual Improvement is both a component of the ITIL 4 Service Value System and a standalone general management practice. Every organization committed to quality must embed a culture of ongoing improvement at all levels. This module covers the Continual Improvement model, the Continual Improvement Register, and how to measure and track improvements using KPIs.

As a student, you will learn the seven steps of the Continual Improvement model, how to log and prioritize improvements, and how to define metrics that demonstrate progress. Make sure to complete the checklist and review the glossary terms before beginning the lab activity.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam expects you to know these concepts precisely:

*   **Continual Improvement Model**: A seven-step iterative framework used to structure and guide improvement initiatives. The steps are: (1) What is the vision? (2) Where are we now? (3) Where do we want to be? (4) How do we get there? (5) Take action. (6) Did we get there? (7) How do we keep the momentum going? Each step builds on the previous, ensuring improvements are purposeful and measurable.
*   **Continual Improvement Register (CIR)**: A documented list used to record, assess, and prioritize improvement opportunities and initiatives across the organization. The CIR ensures that no improvement idea is lost and that efforts are directed at the highest-value opportunities. It is maintained as a living document and reviewed regularly.
*   **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)**: Measurable values that demonstrate how effectively an organization is achieving its key objectives. In the context of Continual Improvement, KPIs are used in step 3 ("Where do we want to be?") to set targets and in step 6 ("Did we get there?") to evaluate outcomes. Effective KPIs are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **CI Is Both an SVS Component and a Practice:** The exam distinguishes between Continual Improvement as an SVS component (embedded throughout the entire SVS) and the Continual Improvement practice (a specific set of activities and tools for managing improvement work). Know both contexts.
*   **Seven-Step Model:** Be able to name the steps in order. A common exam format presents the steps out of sequence and asks you to identify the correct order or the missing step.
*   **CIR Is Not a Project Register:** The Continual Improvement Register tracks improvement opportunities, not active projects. It includes ideas at all stages — captured but not yet prioritized, in progress, and completed.
*   **Improvement Is Everyone's Job:** A frequent exam trap states that CI is managed only by a dedicated team. ITIL 4 explicitly states that all staff contribute to improvement, and the practice provides the structure to coordinate those contributions.
*   **Measurement Enables Improvement:** You cannot improve what you do not measure. The exam tests that defining metrics (step 3) and evaluating results (step 6) are essential — not optional — steps in the model.
*   **Study Resource:** The Axelos ITIL 4 Foundation resources at [https://www.axelos.com/certifications/itil-service-management/itil-4-foundation](https://www.axelos.com/certifications/itil-service-management/itil-4-foundation) include the official glossary definition of the Continual Improvement practice.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply these concepts in the following activities:
*   **Draft a Continual Improvement entry for a service desk backlog**: Using the CIR template, document an improvement opportunity identified from a mock service desk performance report, including priority, owner, and success criteria.
*   **Set target metrics**: Define KPIs for a given improvement initiative, specifying baseline measurements, target values, and measurement timeframes that align with step 3 and step 6 of the CI model.
*   **Define measurement loops**: Map each step of the Continual Improvement model to a specific activity in a case study organization, identifying what data is collected, who reviews it, and how decisions are made.
