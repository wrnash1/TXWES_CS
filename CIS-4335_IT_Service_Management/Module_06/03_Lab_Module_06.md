## Scenario: Parkview Health System IT Services

Parkview Health System operates four hospitals and twelve outpatient clinics. The IT department delivers 28 services including the electronic health record (EHR) system, clinical imaging, the patient scheduling platform, telemedicine infrastructure, and general administrative services.

IT leadership has received the following performance data from the past two quarters:

* EHR system availability: 97.1% (target: 99.9%)
* Average incident resolution time: 5.8 hours (target: 2 hours)
* First-contact resolution rate: 24% (target: 60%)
* Service request fulfillment average time: 11.2 business days (target: 3 business days)
* Patient scheduling platform uptime: 99.4% (target: 99.5% — barely meeting target)
* Number of open CIR items older than 90 days: 67 (no target defined)
* Staff satisfaction with IT services: 2.8 out of 5.0 (no formal target exists)
* Number of improvement ideas submitted by staff in the past 12 months: 0 (no formal submission channel exists)

## Exercise 1: Seven-Step Model Application (40 points)

Apply the seven-step Continual Improvement Model to the EHR system availability gap (current: 97.1%, target: 99.9%). Write out each of the seven steps in the context of this specific improvement challenge.

For each step, write three to five sentences describing:

* What specifically would be done in this step for the EHR availability improvement
* What information or output this step produces
* Why this step is necessary before proceeding to the next step

Your response should be written as a coherent improvement plan narrative organized by step, not as a bullet list.

### Rubric — Exercise 1

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 36–40 | All seven steps addressed with specific, realistic actions tailored to the EHR scenario; each step's output and necessity clearly explained |

| 20–27 | Five steps addressed; some generic rather than scenario-specific; outputs partially explained |

## Exercise 2: Continual Improvement Register Design (25 points)

Based on the performance data provided in the scenario, create a Continual Improvement Register with at least six entries. For each entry, complete all five fields:

Field 1 — Improvement description: What specifically needs to improve and why.

Field 2 — Source: What data or observation identified this as a gap.

Field 3 — Priority: High, Medium, or Low. Justify your priority assignment in one sentence.

Field 4 — Status: Assign one of: Idea / Under Evaluation / In Progress / Completed.

Field 5 — Expected benefit: What outcome is anticipated if this improvement is successfully implemented.

Present your CIR as a table. After the table, write a brief paragraph (75–100 words) explaining how you prioritized the items and what criteria you used.

### Rubric — Exercise 2

| Score | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 23–25 | Six or more entries with all five fields completed; priorities are logical and justified; prioritization rationale is clear |
| 18–22 | Six entries with most fields completed; one or two priority justifications thin; rationale paragraph present |
| 12–17 | Four or five entries; some fields missing; priority rationale absent or very brief |
| 0–11 | Fewer than four entries; CIR structure not demonstrated |

## Exercise 3: Practice vs. SVC Activity Distinction (15 points)

Write a structured analysis (minimum 150 words) addressing all three of the following:

1. In the Parkview scenario, 67 CIR items are more than 90 days old with no resolution. Using the distinction between the Continual Improvement practice and the Improve SVC activity, explain which of the two is failing here and why.
2. Describe one specific action Parkview's IT department should take at the practice level and one action at the SVC activity level to address this backlog.
3. Explain why having no formal staff improvement submission channel (resulting in zero improvement ideas in 12 months) is a failure of the Continual Improvement practice specifically, not just a management failure.

## Exercise 4: Guiding Principles Connection (20 points)

For each of the five situations described below, identify which Guiding Principle most directly applies to the continual improvement context, and explain in two sentences how applying that principle would change the organization's behavior.

Situations:

1. Parkview's IT team wants to improve the first-contact resolution rate. Before designing any training program, they should first analyze ticket data to understand why agents are escalating calls.
2. The improvement initiative for service request fulfillment is planned as a single large redesign project expected to take nine months. A consultant suggests a different approach.
3. The EHR availability improvement initiative is being designed by the IT infrastructure team in isolation. The clinical informatics team and the EHR vendor have not been consulted.
4. The IT director wants to eliminate the paper-based change log that was created in 2014, noting that no one has referenced it in three years.
5. The team is designing an automated alert system that will flag CIR items that have been open for more than 60 days without a status update, so no item is forgotten.

| 10–13 | Three correctly mapped; some explanations missing the principle application |
