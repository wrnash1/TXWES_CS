## Section 2: Exam Format and Rules [02:30 - 05:00]

[SHOW DIAGRAM: Exam format summary card — 40 multiple choice questions, 26 correct to pass (65%), 60 minutes, closed book, no negative marking, all questions equally weighted, available online proctored or at test centers]

The ITIL 4 Foundation exam has five key format facts you must know:

**40 questions.** Every question is multiple choice with four answer options. Only one answer is correct per question.

**65% pass mark.** You need 26 correct answers out of 40 to pass. This is not a high barrier — you can get 14 questions wrong and still pass. But it does mean you need solid understanding of the material, not just surface familiarity.

**60 minutes.** That is 90 seconds per question. Most questions can be answered in 30–45 seconds if you know the material. Time is rarely a problem for prepared candidates.

**Closed book.** No materials are permitted. The exam tests your recall and understanding, not your ability to look things up.

**No negative marking.** There is no penalty for wrong answers. If you are unsure of an answer, always guess — do not leave a question blank.

[PAUSE]

The exam is available through PeopleCert either as an online proctored exam (taken from your own computer with webcam monitoring) or at an authorized test center. The course fee may include an exam voucher — check with your institution.

## Section 3: Key Concepts Review — Service Value System [05:00 - 10:00]

[SHOW DIAGRAM: ITIL 4 SVS complete diagram — five components: Guiding Principles, Governance, Service Value Chain, Practices, and Continual Improvement — surrounding the central "Value" output]

### The Service Value System

The ITIL 4 Service Value System (SVS) describes how all the components and activities of an organization work together to enable value creation. The SVS has five components:

**Guiding Principles** — seven principles that guide decisions and actions in any circumstances regardless of changes in goals, strategies, or type of work. Know all seven: Focus on Value, Start Where You Are, Progress Iteratively with Feedback, Collaborate and Promote Visibility, Think and Work Holistically, Keep It Simple and Practical, Optimize and Automate.

**Governance** — the means by which an organization is directed and controlled. Governance evaluates, directs, and monitors organizational activities.

**Service Value Chain** — six interconnected activities that combine to create value. Plan, Improve, Engage, Design and Transition, Obtain/Build, Deliver and Support. Know the abbreviation: PIEDODS or simply: all six names.

**Practices** — sets of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective. ITIL 4 has 34 practices organized into three categories: General Management (14), Service Management (17), and Technical Management (3).

**Continual Improvement** — an ongoing effort to improve all aspects of the organization's products, services, and practices.

[PAUSE]

### The Four Dimensions

ITIL 4 defines four dimensions of service management that must be considered for every service and practice:

1. Organizations and People
2. Information and Technology
3. Partners and Suppliers
4. Value Streams and Processes

The exam may present scenarios and ask which dimension is being neglected. Remember: PESTLE (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental) factors form the external environment that surrounds all four dimensions.

## Section 4: Key Concepts Review — Practices [10:00 - 17:00]

[SHOW DIAGRAM: Practice reference grid — all 17 service management practices organized in a table with purpose statements visible for the most-tested ones]

The ITIL 4 Foundation exam tests a subset of practices more heavily than others. Know the purpose of every practice, but master these in detail:

### Incident Management

**Purpose:** To minimize the negative impact of incidents by restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible.

Key terms: Incident (unplanned interruption), Major Incident (highest impact category, dedicated team, separate procedure), Priority (based on impact + urgency).

Exam trap: Do not confuse Incident Management with Problem Management. Incidents restore service; Problem Management finds root causes.

### Problem Management

**Purpose:** To reduce the likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual and potential causes and managing workarounds and known errors.

Key terms: Problem (unknown root cause of one or more incidents), Known Error (identified root cause with workaround), Workaround (temporary fix that reduces impact without resolving the problem).

### Change Enablement

**Purpose:** To maximize the number of successful IT changes by ensuring risks have been properly assessed, authorized changes are prioritized, and the change schedule is managed.

Key terms: Standard change (pre-authorized), Normal change (requires individual assessment), Emergency change (expedited), Change Advisory Board (CAB), Change schedule (formerly Change Forward Schedule).

[PAUSE]

### Service Request Management

**Purpose:** To support the agreed quality of a service by handling all pre-defined, user-initiated service requests in an effective and user-friendly manner.

Exam trap: Service requests are not incidents. A request for access, a password reset, or a request for information is a service request — not an incident.

### Service Level Management

**Purpose:** To set clear, business-based targets for service levels and ensure delivery is assessed, monitored, and managed against those targets.

Key terms: SLA (Service Level Agreement — with customer), OLA (Operational Level Agreement — internal), UC (Underpinning Contract — external supplier), Watermelon SLA (green metrics, low satisfaction).

### Service Desk

**Purpose:** To capture demand for incident resolution and service requests.

Key terms: Single point of contact, shift-left (move resolution to lower-cost channels), omnichannel (multiple contact channels).

## Section 5: Key Concepts Review — Value and Outcomes [17:00 - 20:00]

[SHOW DIAGRAM: Value terminology map — Service, Product, Outcome, Output, Value, Utility, Warranty, Cost, Risk — with definitions and connecting arrows]

### Critical Terminology

**Service** — A means of enabling value co-creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.

**Value** — The perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something. Value is co-created — it requires both provider and customer.

**Utility** — The functionality offered by a product or service to meet a particular need. Utility = "fit for purpose." A service has utility if it does what the customer needs it to do.

**Warranty** — The assurance that a product or service will meet agreed requirements. Warranty = "fit for use." A service has warranty if it is available when needed, has sufficient capacity, is secure, and is continuous.

**Output vs. Outcome** — An output is a tangible or intangible deliverable. An outcome is a result for a stakeholder that is enabled by outputs. A new software feature is an output; the productivity improvement it enables for users is an outcome.

**Cost** — The amount of money spent on a specific activity or resource. Services remove costs from customers (they don't have to buy a server) but may impose costs (they must pay for the service).

**Risk** — A possible event that could cause harm or loss. Services remove risks from customers (they don't need to manage uptime) but may impose risks (the provider could fail to deliver).

[PAUSE]

### Value Co-Creation

ITIL 4 emphasizes that value is not delivered by the service provider alone — it is co-created through the relationship between provider and customer. The customer contributes to value creation by using the service effectively, providing feedback, and managing their own side of the service relationship.

## Section 6: Exam Strategy [20:00 - 22:30]

[SHOW DIAGRAM: Exam strategy checklist — five tactics listed with visual icons]

### Five Exam Tactics

**Tactic 1: Read the full question before reading the answers.** ITIL 4 questions often contain a scenario before the question. Read the scenario carefully — the question is testing specific knowledge, and misreading the scenario is the most common source of avoidable errors.

**Tactic 2: Identify what the question is actually testing.** Most questions are testing one of: a purpose statement, a key term definition, a relationship between two practices, or the application of a principle to a scenario. Identify the test before evaluating the answers.

**Tactic 3: Eliminate obvious distractors first.** Two of the four options are usually clearly wrong — they reverse relationships, describe a different practice entirely, or make a statement the exam has tested as incorrect. Eliminate those two first, then choose between the remaining two.

**Tactic 4: When in doubt, return to purpose statements.** Purpose statements are the most frequently tested items on the Foundation exam. If you know the purpose of every practice, you can answer a majority of questions correctly.

**Tactic 5: Answer every question.** There is no negative marking. If you are unsure, eliminate what you can and pick from the remaining options. Leaving questions blank guarantees zero points; guessing gives you a chance.

## Section 7: Career Pathways in ITSM [22:30 - 25:00]

[SHOW DIAGRAM: ITIL 4 certification pathway — Foundation → Managing Professional or Strategic Leader → Master. Alongside: ITSM career roles — Service Desk Analyst, ITSM Process Owner, Service Delivery Manager, IT Operations Manager, CISO, CIO]

### ITIL 4 Certification Pathway

ITIL 4 Foundation is the first step in a structured certification pathway.

**ITIL 4 Managing Professional** — builds on Foundation with four modules: Create, Deliver and Support; Drive Stakeholder Value; High Velocity IT; Direct, Plan and Improve. Targets practitioners who work in IT operations and delivery.

**ITIL 4 Strategic Leader** — two modules: Direct, Plan and Improve (shared with MP) and Digital and IT Strategy. Targets senior managers and executives.

**ITIL 4 Master** — the highest ITIL certification, requiring demonstrated application of ITIL principles in a real-world context.

### ITSM Career Roles

ITIL Foundation opens doors to roles including: Service Desk Analyst, Change Manager, Incident Manager, Problem Manager, IT Service Delivery Manager, IT Operations Manager, and senior positions including ITSM Director and CIO. Complementary certifications include ITIL Managing Professional, PMP, COBIT, CISSP, and Agile/Scrum certifications.
