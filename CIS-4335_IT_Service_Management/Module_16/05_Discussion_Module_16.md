## Overview

This is the final discussion of CIS-4335. Rather than analyzing fictional scenarios, this discussion invites you to reflect on your own learning, connect the course material to real-world practice, and engage with your classmates' perspectives on careers in ITSM. These conversations are designed to consolidate your understanding as you prepare for the ITIL 4 Foundation exam.

## Scenario 1: The Most Surprising Concept

Looking back across all sixteen modules, identify the single ITIL 4 concept or distinction that surprised you most — something that contradicted an assumption you held before the course, or a concept that reframed how you think about IT service management.

**Initial Post Prompt (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM):**

In 175–225 words, respond to the following:

- Identify the concept and explain what you assumed before the course versus what ITIL 4 actually says.
- Describe a real or plausible professional situation where your pre-course assumption would have led you to a worse decision than the ITIL 4 perspective.
- Explain how you will apply this concept in your future professional work.

**Peer Response Prompt (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM):**

Read at least two classmates' posts. In each reply (minimum 75 words):

- Share whether you had the same assumption before the course, or describe a different assumption you held that was also corrected by ITIL 4
- Add one specific exam-relevant implication of the concept they identified — how might this topic appear on the ITIL 4 Foundation exam?
- Describe one real-world scenario (different from theirs) where this concept would be consequential

## Scenario 2: Connecting ITSM to a Career Goal

ITIL 4 Foundation is a starting point, not a destination. The certification opens doors and provides vocabulary, but applying ITIL 4 in practice requires connecting its concepts to the specific role and industry you are pursuing.

**Initial Post Prompt (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM):**

In 175–225 words, respond to the following:

- Describe the role or industry you are targeting in your career. It does not have to be a traditional ITSM role — ITIL concepts apply across IT, healthcare, finance, government, and many other sectors.
- Identify two specific ITIL 4 practices from this course that are most directly relevant to that role or industry, and explain why.
- Describe one scenario in your target role where applying an ITIL 4 concept would give you a professional advantage over a colleague who is unfamiliar with ITIL.

**Peer Response Prompt (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM):**

Respond to at least two classmates' posts (minimum 75 words each):

- Affirm or challenge their practice selection — are those really the most relevant practices for their described role?
- Add one ITIL 4 concept they did not mention that would also be relevant to their career target
- Share whether your own career target overlaps with theirs and if so, what you see differently about which practices matter most

## Scenario 3: Advice to a Future CIS-4335 Student

You are now at the end of the course. Imagine a future student about to begin CIS-4335 who is wondering whether ITIL 4 is worth their time and how to succeed in the course and earn the certification.

**Initial Post Prompt (Due Wednesday at 11:59 PM):**

In 175–225 words, respond to the following:

- What is the single most important piece of advice you would give to a new student starting this course?
- What study strategy worked best for you in this course — and what would you do differently if you were starting over?
- What does earning the ITIL 4 Foundation certification signal to an employer, and why does it matter beyond the course grade?

**Peer Response Prompt (Due Sunday at 11:59 PM):**

Respond to at least two classmates' posts (minimum 75 words each):

- Agree with, challenge, or add nuance to their study strategy advice
- Add one piece of advice they did not include that you believe is equally important
- Reflect on whether the combination of advice from your discussion thread would have helped you succeed faster if you had it at the start of the course

## Discussion Rubric (10 Points Total)

**Initial Post (6 Points):**

- 5–6 pts: Thoughtful, substantive post that directly addresses all prompt questions, demonstrates genuine reflection on course material, and meets the word count requirement.
- 3–4 pts: Addresses most prompt questions but lacks depth, specificity, or connection to ITIL 4 concepts.
- 0–2 pts: Incomplete, off-topic, or missing initial post.

**Peer Responses (4 Points):**

- 4 pts: Substantive replies to at least two peers that add new insight, challenge assumptions, or build meaningfully on their posts.
- 2 pts: Replies to only one peer, or responses are superficial (e.g., "Great advice! I agree.").
- 0 pts: No peer responses submitted.
