## Overview

This lab requires you to apply the seven ITIL 4 Guiding Principles to real organizational scenarios. You will identify applicable principles, analyze situations where principles are violated, design a principle-aligned improvement approach, and evaluate how multiple principles interact. No software tools or terminal commands are required. All work is analytical and written.

Submit all exercises as a single document to the Canvas assignment portal by the posted deadline.

## Exercise 1: Principle Identification (20 points)

For each of the ten situations described below, identify the single ITIL 4 Guiding Principle most directly illustrated. Write the principle name and a one-sentence explanation of why it applies. If two principles seem equally applicable, name both and briefly explain why you consider one primary.

Situations:

1. A company discovers it has been generating a weekly performance report for three years that no manager has ever read.
2. An IT team evaluating a new monitoring platform decides to conduct a two-week pilot with the network operations team before purchasing enterprise licenses.
3. A service desk manager is redesigning the password reset workflow. She starts by sitting with front-line agents for a day to observe how the current process actually works.
4. An IT director deploys a chatbot to handle password resets without first mapping or simplifying the underlying password reset process.
5. The development team makes a significant change to the API used by five other systems without notifying or consulting the teams who own those systems.
6. An ITSM implementation team shares a weekly update dashboard with all stakeholders showing which practices have been implemented, which are in progress, and which are planned.
7. A new CIO wants to discard all existing IT processes and start fresh with pure ITIL 4. A consultant advises her to first assess which existing processes are already effective.
8. A service management team realizes that their incident categorization scheme has 47 categories, most of which are never used, and decides to reduce it to 12 categories that reflect how work actually flows.
9. A cloud migration team discovers that moving email to the cloud will affect the ticketing system integration, the security monitoring tool, and the employee onboarding workflow, and they pause to address each dependency before migrating.
10. An organization implementing a new ITSM tool decides to involve the service desk analysts who will use it daily in the requirements gathering and configuration workshops.

## Exercise 2: Principle Violation Analysis (25 points)

The following scenario describes an IT project that went wrong. Read it carefully, then answer the three questions below.

Scenario: A regional insurance company decides to implement a new IT service management platform. The project lead is given six months and a budget of $400,000. He assembles a team of three IT architects and begins designing the complete ITSM platform — all 34 practices, full workflow automation, a custom reporting suite, and integration with all 22 existing IT tools. The team works in isolation for four months. At the five-month mark, they hold a demonstration for the service desk staff who will use the platform daily. The staff immediately identify 15 significant usability problems. The project lead responds that the design is complete and changes would delay the launch. The platform launches on time. Within 90 days, service desk staff have created informal workarounds for 11 of the 15 problems, resolution times have increased by 40%, and two senior analysts have resigned citing frustration with the new system.

Question 1: Identify three specific Guiding Principles that were violated in this project. For each, describe precisely what action or inaction constituted the violation.

Question 2: If the project team had applied the Progress Iteratively with Feedback principle from the beginning, what would have been different about the project approach? Describe at least three specific changes to how the project would have been conducted.

Question 3: The project lead says: "We delivered on time and on budget. The problems are in how people are using the system, not in how we built it." Using ITIL 4 Guiding Principles, explain why this reasoning is flawed.

## Exercise 3: Principle-Aligned Improvement Design (30 points)

A university IT department wants to implement a self-service portal that allows students and staff to submit IT requests, reset passwords, report incidents, and check service status without calling the help desk. Currently, all requests come through a single phone number, handled by three staff members.

Design a phased implementation approach for this self-service portal using at least five of the seven Guiding Principles. For each principle you use:

* Name the principle.
* Describe the specific action or decision in your implementation plan that reflects this principle.
* Explain what would go wrong if this principle were not applied.

Your implementation plan should be presented as a coherent narrative or structured outline, not just a list of principle applications. The plan should be realistic and sequential.

Minimum 250 words.

## Exercise 4: Distinguishing Similar Principles (25 points)

For each of the five pairs of situations below, identify which Guiding Principle applies to each situation in the pair, then write two sentences explaining how the two principles differ in this context.

Pair 1:
A. An organization discovers that its change approval workflow requires signatures from six managers when two would be sufficient. They redesign it to require only two signatures.
B. An organization decides to automate the routine approval of standard changes (pre-approved change types) so analysts do not have to manually process them.

Pair 2:
A. An IT team wants to migrate to a new ITSM platform. Before beginning, they document all current workflows, tool configurations, and integrations to understand what exists.
B. An IT team decides to migrate to a new ITSM platform in three phases: first migrating incident management, then service request fulfillment, then change enablement — reviewing and adjusting after each phase.

Pair 3:
A. A DevOps team redesigns its release pipeline and discovers the change will affect the incident management escalation process, the monitoring alert routing rules, and the service desk knowledge base. They pause to address each dependency before proceeding.
B. An IT department redesigning its change enablement practice invites the development team, operations team, security team, and service desk manager to participate in the redesign workshops.

Pair 4:
A. An organization is debating whether to continue producing a detailed monthly compliance report. They investigate who reads the report and discover only one person ever requests it, and that person says it could be replaced by a simple dashboard.
B. An organization's incident management process has 28 steps. They review each step and eliminate 9 that add time without adding oversight or quality.

Pair 5:
A. Before choosing a new monitoring tool, the IT team evaluates its existing monitoring capabilities and finds that two tools already provide 80% of the needed functionality with minor configuration changes.
B. An IT director advocates using the existing ticketing system rather than purchasing a new one, arguing that it meets all current requirements and staff already know how to use it.

## Part 9 — Challenge Exercise

### Challenge 1: Principle Violation Audit

Review the following five organizational behaviors. For each one, identify which Guiding Principle (or principles) is being violated, name the principle(s), and write two sentences explaining the specific violation and its likely consequence.

1. An IT team designs a new self-service portal in complete secrecy and announces it to end users the day it launches.
2. A service manager proposes using a machine learning tool to triage all incident tickets, including complex multi-system outages that require experienced engineer judgment.
3. A healthcare IT department discards its existing change management documentation and starts the redesign entirely from a blank template purchased from a consulting firm.
4. After launching a new service, the IT team makes no adjustments for six months, stating they will review everything at the annual planning session.
5. An ITSM improvement team adds twelve new approval steps to the incident escalation process to ensure thorough oversight.

### Challenge 2: Principle-Based Process Redesign

A university IT department currently requires students who have forgotten their student ID number to submit a physical paper form to the registrar's office, wait three business days, and then call the IT help desk to have their account manually linked.

1. Apply a minimum of three Guiding Principles to redesign this process. For each principle applied, describe specifically what would change in the process and why.
2. Write a 100-word justification paragraph explaining how your redesigned process better serves the student (service consumer) and the university (service provider) simultaneously, using ITIL 4 value co-creation terminology.

### Reflection Questions

1. The principle "Think and Work Holistically" requires considering how a change in one area of a service affects other connected areas. Describe a real or hypothetical situation where an IT team applied a change in isolation and caused an unintended problem elsewhere. Identify which principle was violated and what should have been done differently.
2. Of the seven Guiding Principles, which one do you find most difficult to apply consistently in a real work environment, and why? Support your answer with a specific scenario where that principle would be challenging to follow under time pressure or budget constraints.
