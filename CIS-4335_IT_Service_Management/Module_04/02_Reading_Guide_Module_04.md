### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 04 - ITIL Guiding Principles**! The seven ITIL 4 Guiding Principles are universal recommendations that guide an organization's decisions and actions in all circumstances, regardless of changes in goals, strategies, or management structure. They are among the highest-yield topics on the ITIL 4 Foundation exam — expect scenario questions that test your ability to identify which principle is being applied or violated.

As a student, you will learn each principle's name, definition, and practical application. You will also learn how the principles interact and reinforce each other. Make sure to complete the checklist and review the glossary terms before beginning the lab activity.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam expects you to know these concepts precisely:

*   **Focus on Value**: Everything the organization does must link, directly or indirectly, to value for itself, its customers, and other stakeholders. When applying this principle, always ask: "How does this contribute to value for our stakeholders?"
*   **Start Where You Are**: Do not start from scratch without first assessing what already exists. Existing services, processes, data, and tools should be measured and evaluated as a baseline. Reuse and improve rather than replace by default.
*   **Progress Iteratively with Feedback**: Do not attempt to do everything at once. Organize work into smaller, manageable sections that can be executed and completed in a timely manner. Use feedback loops at each iteration to identify and respond to changes.
*   **Collaborate and Promote Visibility**: Work done in silos rarely produces good outcomes. Collaboration across teams, with suppliers, and with customers produces better results. Making work visible — sharing information and progress — reduces risk and builds trust.
*   **Think and Work Holistically**: No service, practice, process, or department works in isolation. A holistic approach considers how all parts of the organization and its ecosystem work together to produce value. Changes in one area can affect others.
*   **Keep It Simple and Practical**: Eliminate unnecessary steps, procedures, and documentation. If a process, service, or metric does not add value, remove it. Always use the minimum number of steps needed to accomplish an objective.
*   **Optimize and Automate**: Before automating, optimize. Automating a broken or inefficient process only produces broken results faster. Identify and remove waste first, then apply automation to standardized, well-understood activities to free human effort for judgment-intensive work.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Know All Seven by Name:** The exam frequently presents a scenario and lists four guiding principles — you must identify which one best applies. Knowing precise names matters; "Keep It Simple" without "and Practical" can be marked wrong.
*   **Scenario Matching:** Common exam traps: a team discards all existing tools and rebuilds from zero = violates "Start Where You Are." A team automates a poorly designed process = violates "Optimize and Automate" (optimize first). A team works in secret without stakeholder input = violates "Collaborate and Promote Visibility."
*   **Principles Are Not Situational Rules:** All seven apply universally and simultaneously. An exam question may require you to identify the single most relevant principle for a given scenario.
*   **Optimize Before You Automate:** This sequencing — optimize first, then automate — is a common exam question. Automating before optimizing is explicitly identified as a mistake in ITIL 4.
*   **Principles Apply Across the SVS:** The guiding principles apply to every component of the SVS, every practice, and every level of the organization. They are not limited to service management staff.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply these concepts in the following activities:
*   **Apply guiding principles to a failed IT rollout scenario**: Given a case study of a failed service deployment, identify which guiding principles were violated and explain how applying each principle correctly would have changed the outcome.
*   **Analyze system dependencies**: Map the dependencies of a sample IT service to demonstrate the "Think and Work Holistically" principle — showing that changes to one component affect others.
*   **Design an automation plan**: Given a process with identified inefficiencies, first apply "Keep It Simple and Practical" to eliminate waste, then design an automation approach that reflects "Optimize and Automate" — in the correct sequence.
