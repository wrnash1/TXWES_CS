### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 07 - Technical Management Practices: Change Enablement**! Change Enablement is one of the most frequently tested ITIL 4 practices on the Foundation exam. Every organization must manage changes to services and infrastructure in a way that maximizes successful changes, minimizes risk, and avoids disrupting live services. This module covers the purpose of Change Enablement, the types of changes, the role of the Change Advisory Board, and how change relates to other ITIL 4 practices.

As a student, you will learn the difference between standard, normal, and emergency changes, understand what triggers and authorizes each type, and explore how Change Enablement connects to Deployment Management and Release Management. Make sure to complete the checklist and review the glossary terms before beginning the lab activity.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam expects you to know these concepts precisely:

*   **Change**: The addition, modification, or removal of anything that could have a direct or indirect effect on services. Changes must be assessed and authorized to protect service stability.
*   **Change Enablement**: The ITIL 4 practice whose purpose is to maximize the number of successful IT and service changes by ensuring that risks are properly assessed, authorizing changes to proceed, and managing the change schedule. In ITIL 4, this replaces the ITIL v3 "Change Management" process.
*   **Standard Change**: A pre-authorized, low-risk, well-understood change that follows a documented procedure. Because the risk and steps are already assessed and approved, standard changes do not require individual review each time. Examples include adding a new user account or applying a routine security patch.
*   **Normal Change**: A change that requires assessment and authorization before implementation. The assessment is performed by the appropriate change authority, which may be an individual, a group, or the Change Advisory Board (CAB), depending on risk and impact.
*   **Emergency Change**: A change that must be implemented as quickly as possible to resolve a major incident or prevent a critical service failure. Emergency changes still require authorization, but the process is accelerated, often through an Emergency CAB (ECAB) or a single senior authority.
*   **Change Advisory Board (CAB)**: A group that provides advisory support to change authorization by reviewing, discussing, and making recommendations on high-risk or high-impact normal changes. The CAB does not replace the change authority — it advises it.
*   **Change Schedule**: A document that lists all authorized changes and their planned implementation dates, used to plan and coordinate changes across the organization and minimize conflicts.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Practice, Not Process:** ITIL 4 uses the term "practice" — not "process." Change Enablement is a practice. Using "Change Management process" on the exam is technically ITIL v3 language, though the conceptual difference is the key distinction to know.
*   **CAB Advises, Doesn't Authorize:** A common exam trap states that the Change Advisory Board authorizes changes. The CAB only provides advisory input — the change authority holds the authorization power. Know this distinction.
*   **Three Change Types and Their Authorization:** Memorize the authorization model: standard changes are pre-authorized; normal changes require individual assessment; emergency changes are expedited but still authorized.
*   **Change Enablement Is Not Deployment:** A frequent exam scenario distinguishes Change Enablement (assessing and authorizing the change) from Deployment Management (physically moving the change into the live environment). Change Enablement approves; Deployment Management executes.
*   **Change Schedule Prevents Conflicts:** The exam tests that the change schedule is used to coordinate timing across the organization — for example, avoiding changes during peak business periods or during other high-risk windows.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply these concepts in the following activities:
*   **Classify a change backlog**: Given a list of ten proposed IT changes, classify each as standard, normal, or emergency, and identify the required authorization level for each based on risk and impact.
*   **Draft a change record**: Using a provided change record template, document a normal change for a server OS upgrade — including scope, risk assessment, rollback plan, and proposed implementation window.
*   **Identify CAB involvement criteria**: For a set of change scenarios, determine which changes would require CAB advisory review versus those that can be authorized by a single change authority, and explain your reasoning.
