## Scenario 1: The Siloed University (175–225 words)

Ramona is the new Director of IT at a mid-sized university. After her first month, she
observes the following pattern: the infrastructure team builds and upgrades systems
without consulting the service desk about how changes affect users; the service desk
resolves tickets without feeding patterns back to the infrastructure team; and the
project management office launches new systems without coordinating with operations.

Each team is working hard, but the university's IT experience remains poor and users
frequently complain that "IT doesn't communicate."

Using the ITIL 4 Service Value Chain model, explain what is happening in structural
terms. Which SVC activities appear to be disconnected? Which activity, if strengthened,
would most directly address the communication breakdown across teams? Provide at least
one concrete recommendation Ramona could implement, grounded in the SVC model.

Your response should use correct ITIL 4 terminology and demonstrate understanding of
how the six activities are intended to interconnect.

## Scenario 2: Demand vs. Value (175–225 words)

A large hospital system's IT department tracks the following monthly metrics: 4,200
service desk contacts, 98% ticket closure rate, 94% first-call resolution, and an
average resolution time of 8 minutes.

The CIO reviews these numbers and says, "Our IT team is delivering tremendous value."
A patient safety officer reviews the same numbers and says, "I still don't know if
IT is actually helping our nurses and doctors do their jobs better."

Both statements reference the word "value," but they are not describing the same thing.

Using the ITIL 4 concept of co-creation of value and the flow of demand through the
Service Value Chain, explain the difference between what the CIO is measuring and what
the patient safety officer is asking about. What would a more complete picture of value
look like for a hospital IT environment? What role does the Engage activity play in
surfacing that fuller picture?

## Scenario 3: Value Stream Design Decision (175–225 words)

Your organization is implementing a new IT onboarding process for new employees. On
day one, every new hire should have a working laptop, email account, VPN access, and
access to the three business applications relevant to their role — all fully configured
and ready before they arrive.

Currently, each of these steps is handled by a different team with no coordinated
handoff, and new employees regularly wait two to four days before they are fully
productive.

Design a brief value stream (you do not need full detail — a logical sequence of SVC
activities with a sentence of explanation for each step is sufficient) that would
address this problem. Then explain: why is a value stream a more useful tool for
solving this problem than simply telling each team to "do their part faster"? What
does the value stream concept add that individual team optimization cannot provide?
