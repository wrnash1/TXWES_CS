## Scenario: Rampart University Help Desk Launch

Rampart University is preparing to launch a new student IT Help Desk service. The university's Chief Information Officer (CIO) has approved a budget of $120,000 for the first year. The academic department heads have outlined their requirements: students must be able to report technical problems with the learning management system (LMS), campus Wi-Fi, and student email accounts. The CIO's office wants the service available 8 AM–8 PM Monday through Friday and 10 AM–4 PM on Saturdays.

Three IT staff members will be assigned to the help desk. They will use a ticketing system to log, route, and track all requests. A managed service provider, TechCare Solutions, will provide after-hours email support as a backup channel.

The Director of Student Affairs has expressed concern that students from non-technical backgrounds may not know how to describe their problems clearly, which could slow resolution times. The Head of IT Infrastructure notes that the ticketing system vendor has a reputation for poor documentation.

Using the scenario above, identify the ITIL 4 role each stakeholder plays. For each stakeholder listed below, write the correct ITIL 4 role (customer, user, sponsor, or service provider) and provide a one-sentence justification explaining why that role applies.

Stakeholders to classify:

1. The CIO who approved the $120,000 budget
2. The academic department heads who defined the service requirements
3. Students who will call the help desk when their LMS stops working
4. The three IT staff members operating the ticketing system

## Exercise 3: Value Co-Creation Mapping (25 points)

ITIL 4 states that value is co-created between the service provider and the service consumer — it does not flow in one direction only.

Using the Rampart University scenario, write a structured analysis (minimum 200 words) that addresses all four points below:

1. Identify two specific actions the IT Help Desk (as service provider) must take to enable value.
2. Identify two specific actions students (as service consumers) must take to enable value.
3. Explain what outcome the students are trying to achieve through this service. Be specific — do not say "get help." Describe the academic or professional result they are pursuing.
4. Identify one way in which value could fail to be co-created even if the service is technically functional (i.e., the technology works but value still does not emerge).

## Exercise 4: Four Dimensions Assessment (30 points)

The scenario describes several potential problems. For each problem listed below, identify which of the ITIL 4 Four Dimensions it primarily belongs to, name the dimension, and explain in two to three sentences how that dimension's principles would guide the organization's response.

Problems to assess:

1. Students cannot describe their problems clearly, slowing ticket resolution.
2. The ticketing system vendor has poor documentation, raising concerns about effective staff use.
3. TechCare Solutions' contract has not been finalized and their service-level commitments are unclear.
4. The three IT staff members have never worked in a formal help desk environment and have no escalation process.
5. The university has not determined how ticket data will be stored, retained, or used to measure performance.
6. The IT team plans to implement the service by copying a process manual from another institution without reviewing whether it fits Rampart's environment.
