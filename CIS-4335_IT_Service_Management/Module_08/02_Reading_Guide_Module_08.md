### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 08 - Service Management Practices: Incident Management**! Incident Management is one of the core ITIL 4 practices and among the highest-frequency topics on the Foundation exam. Every organization that delivers IT services will experience disruptions — Incident Management provides the structured practice for restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible while minimizing impact on the business.

As a student, you will learn the purpose of Incident Management, key definitions including incident versus service request versus problem, the importance of incident categorization and prioritization, and how incidents relate to other ITIL 4 practices such as Problem Management and Service Desk. Make sure to complete the checklist and review the glossary terms before beginning the lab activity.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam expects you to know these concepts precisely:

*   **Incident**: An unplanned interruption to a service or a reduction in the quality of a service. An incident is distinguished from a service request (a planned, expected action) and a problem (the underlying cause of one or more incidents). Restoring service from an incident is the immediate priority, even before the root cause is identified.
*   **Incident Management**: The ITIL 4 practice whose purpose is to minimize the negative impact of incidents by restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible. It focuses on speed of recovery, not on identifying root cause — that is the role of Problem Management.
*   **Service Request**: A formal request from a user for something to be provided — such as a new user account, a password reset, or information. Service requests are planned, expected, and handled through Service Request Management — not Incident Management.
*   **Incident Categorization**: The process of classifying an incident by type (for example: hardware, software, network, security) so that it can be routed to the correct team, tracked consistently, and analyzed for trends over time.
*   **Incident Prioritization**: The process of determining how urgently an incident should be addressed, typically based on impact (the effect on the business) and urgency (how quickly the situation will worsen). Priority drives the response time and resource allocation for incident resolution.
*   **Major Incident**: An incident with significant business impact that requires a coordinated response from multiple teams. Major incidents typically trigger a separate, escalated response process and require a post-incident review after resolution.
*   **Workaround**: A temporary solution that reduces or eliminates the impact of an incident or problem for which a full resolution is not yet available. Workarounds are documented in the Known Error Database.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Incident vs. Service Request vs. Problem:** This three-way distinction is among the most tested ITIL 4 Foundation topics. Incidents are unplanned disruptions; service requests are planned, expected requests; problems are the root causes of incidents. Know these precisely.
*   **Purpose Is Speed, Not Root Cause:** A common exam trap says that Incident Management must identify the root cause before closing the ticket. ITIL 4 is explicit: the goal is to restore service quickly. Root cause analysis belongs to Problem Management.
*   **Prioritization = Impact + Urgency:** The exam will present scenarios and ask how an incident should be prioritized. Always apply the impact-plus-urgency model. A VIP user being affected may increase urgency; a system supporting 500 users increases impact.
*   **Major Incidents Have a Separate Process:** The Foundation exam tests that major incidents — those with high business impact — warrant an escalated, coordinated response distinct from normal incident handling.
*   **Workarounds Enable Closure:** A workaround does not mean the problem is solved — it means normal service is restored sufficiently. The underlying problem record remains open in Problem Management.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply these concepts in the following activities:
*   **Classify a set of IT tickets**: Given ten realistic IT tickets, classify each as an incident, service request, or problem record, and justify your classification using ITIL 4 definitions.
*   **Prioritize a queue of incidents**: Using impact and urgency ratings provided in a mock incident queue, assign priority levels (P1–P4) to each incident and determine the order in which they should be addressed.
*   **Draft an incident record**: Complete a sample incident record for a realistic scenario — including category, description, affected users, impact and urgency scores, priority, assigned team, and resolution steps — using a provided template.
