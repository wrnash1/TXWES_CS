### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 10 - Service Management Practices: Service Desk**! The Service Desk is one of the most visible and high-impact ITIL 4 practices in any organization. It serves as the single point of contact between service providers and users, handling incidents, service requests, and general communications. This module covers the purpose and value of the Service Desk practice, the key activities performed, staffing models, and how the Service Desk connects to other ITIL 4 practices.

As a student, you will learn why a single point of contact matters, how Service Desk channels and staffing structures vary across organizations, and what makes an effective Service Desk in the context of ITIL 4. Make sure to complete the checklist and review the glossary terms before beginning the lab activity.

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam expects you to know these concepts precisely:

*   **Service Desk**: The ITIL 4 practice that serves as the single point of contact between the service provider and users. The Service Desk captures demand for incident resolution and service requests, and communicates status updates and service information to users. It is a critical link between the service provider and the people who rely on services.
*   **Single Point of Contact (SPOC)**: A centralized channel through which users access IT support and report all issues and requests. The SPOC model ensures consistent logging, routing, and follow-up across all user interactions, and gives users a reliable, predictable place to go for help.
*   **Service Desk Channel**: The communication method through which users contact the Service Desk. Common channels include phone (voice), email, self-service portal, chat, and walk-in. Modern Service Desks typically offer multiple channels to meet varying user needs and preferences.
*   **Shift-Left**: A Service Desk strategy that moves resolution capability closer to users by providing self-service options, knowledge articles, and first-line resolution, reducing escalations to higher-cost specialist teams. Shift-left improves efficiency and user satisfaction when implemented effectively.
*   **Omnichannel Support**: An approach where multiple communication channels are integrated so that interactions are seamless and consistent regardless of which channel the user chooses. Omnichannel differs from multichannel in that context and history transfer across channels.
*   **Service Desk Staffing Models**: The structural approaches organizations use to staff their Service Desks, including local (desk staff at each site), centralized (all staff at one location), virtual (geographically distributed but appearing unified through technology), and follow-the-sun (24/7 coverage by shifting work across global time zones).

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Service Desk Is a Practice, Not a Team:** ITIL 4 defines the Service Desk as a practice — a set of activities, competencies, and tools — not just a group of people. The exam may test this distinction.
*   **Single Point of Contact Is the Core Value:** The key value proposition of the Service Desk is that users have one reliable place to go for help. Questions about the Service Desk often hinge on this concept.
*   **Service Desk vs. Incident Management vs. Service Request Management:** The Service Desk practice handles the user-facing interaction. Incident Management and Service Request Management are the practices that govern how those interactions are processed and resolved. Know which practice owns which responsibility.
*   **Shift-Left Reduces Costs:** The exam tests that shift-left strategies — self-service portals, knowledge bases, and automated ticket handling — reduce volume at higher-cost support tiers and improve resolution speed for common issues.
*   **Staffing Model Scenarios:** Expect scenario questions where a company's geographic or operational profile maps to a staffing model. Global 24/7 needs map to follow-the-sun; cost consolidation maps to centralized; local site needs may require local staffing.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply these concepts in the following activities:
*   **Design a Service Desk channel strategy**: Given an organization profile (size, geography, user types, and budget), recommend a Service Desk staffing model and channel mix, and justify your recommendations using ITIL 4 Service Desk practice principles.
*   **Evaluate shift-left opportunities**: Review a provided Service Desk ticket volume report categorized by ticket type. Identify which ticket categories are candidates for shift-left (self-service or knowledge article) and estimate the potential reduction in escalated tickets.
*   **Draft a Service Desk interaction record**: Complete a sample interaction record for a realistic scenario — including the channel used, user details, issue description, classification, and handoff point — to practice the logging standards of the Service Desk practice.
