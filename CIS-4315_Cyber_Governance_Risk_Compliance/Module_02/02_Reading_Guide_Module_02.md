# Reading Guide: Module 02 - Security Strategy and Business Alignment
## Course: CIS-4315_Cyber_Governance_Risk_Compliance (ISACA Certified Information Security Manager (CISM))

### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 02 - Security Strategy and Business Alignment**! This module explores how organizations translate governance direction into a coherent information security strategy that drives the development and maintenance of security policies and standards. Strong policy frameworks are the foundation of a defensible, auditable security program.

The CISM exam approaches policies and standards from a management perspective — focusing on lifecycle management, business justification, and the hierarchy of policy documents rather than specific technical configurations.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Security policies**: High-level, management-approved statements that define the organization's security intent, expectations, and accountabilities. Policies are mandatory and represent the organization's position on protecting information assets; they do not prescribe specific technical implementations.
*   **Standards**: Mandatory, specific requirements that define the minimum acceptable security configurations, specifications, or behaviors across the organization. Standards operationalize policy by providing measurable, enforceable criteria (e.g., "all passwords must be at least 12 characters").
*   **Guidelines**: Non-mandatory recommendations that provide additional context, best practices, or suggested approaches for implementing policies and standards. Guidelines offer flexibility and are used where prescriptive requirements would be impractical.
*   **Procedures**: Step-by-step, operational instructions that describe exactly how to perform a specific security task or process in compliance with policies and standards. Procedures are the most granular level of the policy hierarchy.
*   **Policy life cycle**: The ongoing management process for security policies, including creation, review, approval, communication, enforcement, exception handling, and periodic revision. A mature policy program maintains version control, assigns ownership, and schedules reviews at least annually or after significant organizational change.

---

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Policy Hierarchy Matters:** The CISM exam frequently tests the distinction between policies, standards, guidelines, and procedures. Policies = intent (mandatory), Standards = specifications (mandatory), Guidelines = recommendations (optional), Procedures = steps (operational).
*   **Business Justification:** CISM questions often present scenarios where a security manager must justify a new policy to business leadership. The correct answer typically involves demonstrating risk reduction and business alignment — not purely technical merit.
*   **Exception Management:** The exam tests knowledge of policy exception processes. When a business unit cannot comply with a standard, a formal exception with documented compensating controls and management approval is the correct governance response.
*   **Study Resource:** [NIST SP 800-12 Rev. 1: An Introduction to Information Security](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-12/rev-1/final) is a free publication that provides an accessible overview of security policy principles and program components directly relevant to CISM Domain 1.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply policy framework concepts through the following activities:
*   **Draft an Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) template**: Create a one-page AUP covering approved uses of organizational IT resources, prohibited activities, and acknowledgment requirements.
*   **Differentiate standards from guidelines**: Given a set of 10 security requirements, classify each as a mandatory standard or an optional guideline and provide a business justification for each classification decision.
*   **Write standard operating procedures for password reset**: Document a step-by-step procedure for an IT help desk technician to perform a user password reset in compliance with the organization's identity management policy.
