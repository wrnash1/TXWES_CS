## Scenario A: The Misaligned CISO

A regional hospital system hires a new CISO who spent the previous decade at a financial services firm. In their first 90 days, the CISO drafts a comprehensive three-year security roadmap focused on fraud prevention controls, transaction monitoring, and financial system integrity — the priorities of the previous employer. The hospital's board accepts the roadmap without modification. Eighteen months later, a ransomware attack shuts down patient scheduling, radiology, and pharmacy systems for six days. Post-incident analysis reveals that availability of clinical systems and medical device security were never addressed in the strategy.

For your initial post, address the following: What governance failure allowed a misaligned security strategy to be approved without challenge? Which business context analysis step was skipped or performed inadequately? What should the board's role have been in reviewing the strategic alignment of the roadmap? Use CISM Domain 1 principles to support your analysis.

---

## Scenario B: The Unfunded Strategy

A telecommunications company's CISO develops an ambitious five-year information security strategy covering identity and access management transformation, zero-trust architecture, and advanced threat intelligence integration. The strategy document is professionally produced and clearly written. However, it contains no resource requirements section, no cost estimates, and no budget request. The board reviews and praises the document, then allocates the same flat security budget as the prior year. Two years later, none of the Year 1 initiatives have been completed.

For your initial post, address the following: What critical component of the security strategy document was missing, and how did that omission lead directly to the program failure? How should a CISO connect strategy to financial planning to ensure initiatives receive funding? What governance accountability mechanism should have caught this gap before the strategy was approved? Use Module 02 concepts to structure your answer.

---

## Scenario C: The Invisible Risk

A mid-size manufacturing company maintains a 40-item security risk register reviewed quarterly by the security team and the CIO. The register has never been shared with the enterprise risk management committee or presented to the board. The company's CFO and COO — who sit on the risk committee — have no visibility into the security program's risk findings. When a sophisticated supply chain attack compromises the company's OT environment, the CEO asks the risk committee why this risk was never on their radar. The security team responds that it has been in the security risk register for two years.

For your initial post, address the following: What integration failure allowed a known, documented risk to remain invisible to enterprise governance? What is the CISM principle that addresses this gap? How should security risk findings be structured and communicated to ensure they reach the appropriate governance bodies? Propose one concrete change the CISO could implement immediately to prevent this from recurring.

Peer Responses — 4 points: A 4-point response replies constructively to at least two peers with substantive additions — an alternative recommendation, an additional principle, or relevant context — with each reply at least 50 words. A 2-point response replies to only one peer or responses are superficial and do not advance the discussion. A 0-point response submits no peer replies.

## Professor Nash Note

The three scenarios in this discussion represent real failure modes in professional security programs. The most common trap is the "strategy without context" failure — a CISO who produces a technically excellent document that solves the wrong problem because they did not invest enough time understanding the business before writing. The CISM exam will test your ability to recognize this failure mode and recommend the correct first action. Use these scenarios to practice that reasoning now.
