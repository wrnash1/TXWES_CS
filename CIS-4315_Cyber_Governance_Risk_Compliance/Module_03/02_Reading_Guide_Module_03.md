# Reading Guide: Module 03 - Risk Management Frameworks (NIST RMF, ISO 27005)
## Course: CIS-4315_Cyber_Governance_Risk_Compliance (ISACA Certified Information Security Manager (CISM))

---

### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 03 - Risk Management Frameworks**! This module introduces the structured methodologies organizations use to identify, assess, respond to, and monitor information security risk. Risk management is the foundation of CISM Domain 2 and represents approximately 20% of the exam.

The CISM exam emphasizes risk management as a business discipline. Candidates must understand how frameworks like NIST SP 800-37 (RMF) and ISO/IEC 27005 provide structured approaches to risk decision-making that support organizational objectives.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Risk management frameworks**: Structured methodologies that provide organizations with a repeatable, systematic process for identifying, assessing, treating, and monitoring information security risks. Frameworks like NIST RMF and ISO 27005 ensure risk management activities are consistent, documented, and integrated with organizational governance.
*   **NIST SP 800-37 (RMF)**: The NIST Risk Management Framework — a six-step (plus Prepare) process for integrating security and privacy risk management into the system development life cycle. The steps are: Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, and Monitor. Originally developed for U.S. federal agencies, it is widely adopted across sectors.
*   **Risk categorization**: The process of determining the impact level (Low, Moderate, or High) of an information system based on the potential adverse effects a security breach would have on organizational operations, assets, or individuals. In NIST RMF, categorization uses FIPS 199 and NIST SP 800-60 as guidance.
*   **Control selections**: The process of choosing appropriate security and privacy controls from a catalog (such as NIST SP 800-53) based on a system's risk categorization and tailoring guidance. Control selection ensures that the strength and scope of implemented safeguards are proportionate to the risk level of the system being protected.

---

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **RMF Step Order Matters:** CISM candidates should know the NIST RMF steps in sequence: Prepare → Categorize → Select → Implement → Assess → Authorize → Monitor. The exam may present scenario questions about which step should occur next.
*   **Risk Framing Is Strategic:** The exam distinguishes between risk identification (finding threats and vulnerabilities) and risk framing (setting the organizational context — appetite, tolerance, and constraints — within which risk decisions are made). Risk framing is a governance activity.
*   **ISO 27005 vs. NIST RMF:** ISO 27005 provides a process framework for information security risk management aligned with ISO 27001; NIST RMF provides prescriptive steps for U.S. federal-style system authorization. Both support the same core risk management principles but differ in structure and prescriptiveness.
*   **Study Resource:** [NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 2: Risk Management Framework for Information Systems and Organizations](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-37/rev-2/final) is free from NIST and is the authoritative RMF reference. Review the executive summary and Chapter 2 (Fundamentals) for exam preparation.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply risk management framework concepts through the following activities:
*   **Map the NIST SP 800-37 RMF steps**: Create a flowchart or table documenting each RMF step, its key tasks, and the primary output artifact (e.g., System Security Plan, Authorization Package).
*   **Categorize a mock system using FIPS 199**: Given a scenario describing a healthcare patient records system, determine the appropriate impact level (Low/Moderate/High) for Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability, and justify your selections.
*   **Draft a security control baseline selection justification**: Using the NIST SP 800-53 moderate baseline, select three controls relevant to the categorized system and explain why each is applicable.
