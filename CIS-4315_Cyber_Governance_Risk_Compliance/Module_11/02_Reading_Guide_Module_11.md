### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 11 - Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Planning**! This module covers industry compliance frameworks — specifically PCI DSS and ISO/IEC 27001 — that define security requirements for specific sectors and provide internationally recognized certifications of security program maturity.

CISM candidates must understand these frameworks as risk management tools, not merely compliance checklists. The exam tests your ability to advise organizations on how these standards integrate with their broader security governance programs.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS)**: A set of security requirements established by the PCI Security Standards Council (major card brands: Visa, Mastercard, Amex, Discover, JCB) that any organization processing, storing, or transmitting cardholder data must comply with. PCI DSS defines 12 core requirements organized around protecting the cardholder data environment, including network controls, access management, vulnerability management, and regular testing.
*   **ISO/IEC 27001**: An international standard published by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) that defines the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an Information Security Management System (ISMS). ISO 27001 certification is voluntary and requires a third-party audit; Annex A contains 93 security controls organized into 4 themes.
*   **Security controls certification**: The process through which an organization demonstrates to an independent third-party auditor or assessor that its security controls meet the requirements of a specific standard or regulation. Certifications (ISO 27001, SOC 2) and attestations (PCI DSS QSA report) provide evidence of program maturity to customers, regulators, and partners.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **PCI DSS is Mandatory; ISO 27001 is Voluntary:** PCI DSS compliance is contractually required by card brands for any entity handling cardholder data — it is not optional. ISO 27001 certification is voluntary but increasingly expected in enterprise contracts and by regulators.
*   **PCI DSS 12 Requirements:** Know the six control goals and 12 requirements of PCI DSS at a conceptual level. The exam may present a scenario about card data and ask which PCI DSS requirement applies.
*   **ISMS Scope in ISO 27001:** ISO 27001 certification applies to a defined ISMS scope — an organization can certify part of its operations. The exam may test whether a breach outside the certified scope affects certification.
*   **Study Resource:** [PCI Security Standards Council Website](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/) — PCI DSS v4.0 documentation and summaries are available free from the PCI SSC. The "At a Glance" summary documents provide exam-ready overviews of all 12 requirements.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply industry standards concepts through the following activities:
*   **Review PCI DSS 12 core requirements**: Using the PCI DSS At a Glance document, create a summary table mapping each requirement number to its goal, primary control type (preventive/detective/corrective), and an example of a specific control that satisfies it.
*   **Map ISO 27001 Annex A controls to organizational policies**: Given a sample organization with 5 existing security policies, identify which Annex A control objectives each policy addresses and note any gaps.
*   **Determine PCI DSS scope**: Given a network diagram of a retail organization, identify which systems are in scope for PCI DSS (those that store, process, or transmit cardholder data or are connected to the cardholder data environment) and which are out of scope.
