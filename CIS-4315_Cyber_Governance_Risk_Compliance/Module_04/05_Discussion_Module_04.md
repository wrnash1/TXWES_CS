## Scenario A — Choosing Between Qualitative and Quantitative Analysis

Redwood Financial Credit Union serves 85,000 members across North Texas and manages $1.4 billion in assets. The credit union's new CISO has inherited a backlog of 60 unassessed risks across core banking, digital banking, lending systems, and back-office operations. The board has given the CISO 30 days to present a risk prioritization report. The CISO has a team of two analysts and limited financial asset valuation data for most systems. One analyst advocates strongly for quantitative analysis because "real numbers are more credible to the board." The other analyst recommends qualitative analysis for the initial pass.

In 175–225 words, respond to the following questions in complete sentences.

Which analyst's recommendation is more appropriate for this specific situation, and why? Identify at least three characteristics of the credit union's situation that support your answer. Then explain what the CISO should communicate to the board about the limitations of whichever approach is chosen, so that board members interpret the results with appropriate confidence. Finally, describe how the CISO might use both approaches together — using one method for the initial 30-day deliverable and the other method afterward — to build a mature, credible risk analysis program over time.

## Scenario B — Business Impact Analysis Prioritization

Sunrise Health System is a regional hospital network operating five hospitals and 22 outpatient clinics in the Dallas-Fort Worth area. The CISO is preparing a BIA for presentation to the executive leadership team. During stakeholder interviews, the following disagreements arose.

The Director of Emergency Services argues that the emergency department patient tracking system should have an MTD of 30 minutes and an RTO of 10 minutes. The Chief Financial Officer argues that the revenue cycle management system — which processes insurance claims and manages $800 million in annual revenue — is the most critical system and should have the lowest RPO. The Chief Nursing Officer argues that the clinical documentation system used by nurses for medication administration records must have an RPO of zero because any data loss creates patient safety risk.

In 175–225 words, respond to the following questions in complete sentences.

Are all three executives' arguments reasonable? For each argument, explain whether you agree or disagree with their position and why, using BIA terminology precisely. Explain how conflicting MTD, RTO, and RPO demands across departments should be resolved — who has final decision-making authority over BIA metrics, and how should trade-offs be handled when recovery targets cannot all be met within the available IT budget? Conclude with a brief statement about how the BIA process itself — not just the final metrics — creates value for the organization even when the targets are not perfectly achievable.

## Scenario C — Threat Modeling in Practice

ClearPath Software is a startup that has just launched a B2B SaaS application for small business accounting. The platform handles payroll data, bank account information, and tax documents for 3,200 small business clients. The founding team built the application quickly to meet a market window and did not conduct formal threat modeling during development. Following a security review, the security consultant recommended conducting a STRIDE threat model of the application.

The CTO pushed back: "We already passed our SOC 2 Type 1 audit. Our code has been reviewed by our developers. Why do we need a separate threat modeling exercise? This feels like redundant work."

In 175–225 words, respond to the following questions in complete sentences.

How would you respond to the CTO's argument? Explain specifically what STRIDE threat modeling reveals that a SOC 2 audit and developer code review do not. Use at least two concrete examples of STRIDE threat categories (for instance, Repudiation or Elevation of Privilege) to illustrate what the CTO might be missing. Then address the timing concern: given that ClearPath built the application without threat modeling, is it still worth conducting the exercise after deployment, or is threat modeling only valuable during the design phase? Conclude by explaining how the outputs of a STRIDE analysis would feed into ClearPath's existing risk management activities.

## A Note from Professor Nash

The scenarios this week are designed to reflect the kinds of judgment calls you will face in real security roles — and the kinds of scenario questions that appear on the CISM exam. Notice that in Scenario A, neither analyst is entirely wrong. In Scenario B, all three executives have legitimate concerns. In Scenario C, the CTO is raising a fair efficiency question that deserves a genuine answer, not dismissal.

Good security professionals can acknowledge the legitimate points in a counterargument while still explaining clearly why a particular course of action is the right one. That ability — to engage seriously with opposing views and reason through them — is what separates strong communicators from technical experts who struggle to influence decisions.

Bring that same quality of reasoning to your peer responses. The best discussions this week will involve students genuinely engaging with each other's positions, not simply affirming them.
