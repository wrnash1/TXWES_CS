# Reading Guide: Module 04 - Risk Assessment and Treatment
## Course: CIS-4315_Cyber_Governance_Risk_Compliance (ISACA Certified Information Security Manager (CISM))

---

### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 04 - Risk Assessment and Treatment**! This module covers the methodologies organizations use to identify information assets, assess the risks those assets face, and select appropriate risk treatment strategies. These skills sit at the heart of CISM Domain 2 (Information Risk Management).

Understanding asset identification and valuation enables security managers to apply proportionate controls — protecting high-value assets more rigorously while avoiding over-investment in low-risk areas.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Information assets**: Any data, systems, processes, or knowledge that have value to the organization and require protection. Information assets include databases, intellectual property, financial records, software applications, and the hardware that processes them. Identifying assets is the prerequisite to any meaningful risk assessment.
*   **Asset inventory**: A complete, maintained catalog of all organizational information assets, including their owners, classification levels, locations, and business dependencies. An accurate asset inventory is foundational to risk management because you cannot protect what you have not identified.
*   **Classification tiers (Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted)**: A structured hierarchy used to assign sensitivity levels to information assets based on the potential harm their unauthorized disclosure, modification, or destruction would cause. Classification drives the selection of appropriate controls for each tier.
*   **Asset valuation metrics**: Quantitative or qualitative measures used to determine the business value of an information asset, including its replacement cost, revenue dependency, regulatory exposure, and reputational impact. Valuation informs risk prioritization and justifies security investment decisions.

---

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Asset Owner Accountability:** The CISM exam frequently tests the concept of asset ownership. The asset owner (typically a business unit manager, not IT) is responsible for classifying the asset, defining access requirements, and accepting residual risk. IT is the custodian, not the owner.
*   **Classification Drives Controls:** A key exam theme is that classification tiers directly determine the required controls. When a scenario presents a decision about access controls, the correct answer usually depends on the asset's classification level.
*   **Valuation Methods:** Know the difference between quantitative valuation (dollar amounts — SLE, ALE) and qualitative valuation (High/Medium/Low ratings). Quantitative is more precise but resource-intensive; qualitative is faster but less defensible to auditors.
*   **Study Resource:** [NIST SP 800-60 Vol. 1: Guide for Mapping Types of Information and Information Systems to Security Categories](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-60/vol-1-rev-1/final) is a free publication that provides the categorization guidance used in NIST RMF, directly applicable to asset classification concepts.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply asset management concepts through the following activities:
*   **Build an asset inventory schema**: Create a spreadsheet template with columns for Asset Name, Asset Type, Owner, Classification Level, Business Criticality (1-5), Location, and Primary Dependency.
*   **Classify assets into security tiers**: Given a list of 12 sample assets (payroll database, public marketing website, HR performance reviews, building access logs, etc.), assign each to a classification tier and document the justification.
*   **Assign business value scores**: For three sample databases, use a qualitative scoring rubric (Revenue Impact, Regulatory Exposure, Reputational Damage) to calculate an overall business value score and rank them by risk priority.
