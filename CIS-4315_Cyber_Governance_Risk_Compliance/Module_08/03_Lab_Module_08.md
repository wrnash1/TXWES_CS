# Lab Activity: Module 08 — Security Awareness and Training Programs

## Course: CIS-4315 Cyber Governance, Risk, and Compliance

**Certification Alignment:** ISACA CISM — Domain 3: Information Security Program

---

## Lab Overview

In this lab you will design a comprehensive, role-based security awareness and training program for a fictional organization. You will conduct an audience analysis, write measurable learning objectives, select and justify delivery methods, plan a phishing simulation campaign, and build a program measurement plan.

This lab develops the practical skills of program design and management that CISM Domain 3 requires. The deliverables are realistic program documents appropriate for an actual organizational security program.

**Estimated Time:** 90–120 minutes

**Submission:** Upload all deliverables as a single PDF or Word document to the Canvas LMS assignment portal.

---

## Scenario

**Cascadia Health Network** is a fictional regional healthcare organization with 3,200 employees across six hospitals, twelve outpatient clinics, and a central administrative office. The organization is subject to HIPAA Security Rule training requirements.

The workforce includes the following groups:

- 1,400 clinical staff (nurses, physicians, medical technicians) — primarily patient care roles with limited desk time; access ePHI daily
- 800 administrative staff (billing, scheduling, HR, finance) — desk-based; access ePHI and financial systems
- 400 IT staff (systems administrators, network engineers, help desk, developers) — privileged access to systems and patient data
- 200 executives and managers (C-suite, department directors, clinic managers) — high-value social engineering targets; make decisions that affect security funding and policy
- 400 support staff (facilities, food service, security officers) — physical access to facilities; minimal system access

The current state of security awareness at Cascadia:

- An annual online compliance module covering HIPAA basics was last updated in 2021
- Completion rate is 78 percent organization-wide; clinical staff completion rate is 52 percent
- No phishing simulation program exists
- No role-based training differentiation — all employees receive identical content
- Two significant security incidents in the past 18 months were directly attributable to employee behavior: one credential theft via phishing and one misdirected fax containing patient records

## Part A: Audience Analysis and Behavior Gap Assessment (25 points)

### Task Instructions

For each of the five workforce segments listed in the scenario, complete a behavior gap analysis. Identify the two or three most significant security risks specific to that segment, describe the current behavior (what employees in that group currently do that creates risk), and describe the target behavior (what they should do instead).

Present your analysis as a table with these columns: Workforce Segment, Key Security Risks, Current Behavior (Gap), and Target Behavior.

After completing the table, write a paragraph of 3–5 sentences identifying which workforce segment you assess as the highest-priority target for immediate program investment, and justify your choice based on the scenario details.

### Part A Assessment Criteria

| Criteria | Points |
|---|---|
| All five segments analyzed with specific, accurate risks | 10 |
| Current and target behaviors are specific and behavioral (not generic) | 10 |
| Priority segment justification is evidence-based and well-reasoned | 5 |

## Part B: Learning Objectives and Curriculum Outline (25 points)

### Task Instructions

Design a role-based training curriculum for three of the five workforce segments: clinical staff, administrative staff, and IT staff. For each segment, write exactly three measurable behavioral learning objectives and outline the training topics that would achieve those objectives.

A measurable behavioral learning objective must describe an observable action (not "understand" or "be aware of") and must be specific enough that you could test whether an employee achieved it.

For each of the three segments, also specify:

- Recommended total training time per year (in hours or minutes, broken into delivery events)
- Whether the segment requires any specialized regulatory training (reference HIPAA specifically for healthcare)
- One topic that should NOT be included for this segment because it would be irrelevant or inappropriate for their role

Present your curriculum outline as three separate subsections, one per segment, each with the objectives listed, topic outline, time recommendation, regulatory note, and exclusion.

### Part B Assessment Criteria

| Criteria | Points |
|---|---|
| Three measurable behavioral objectives per segment (nine total) — all are observable and specific | 12 |
| Topic outlines are relevant to the segment's actual risk profile | 7 |
| Time recommendations and regulatory notes are accurate | 3 |
| Exclusion justifications demonstrate role-relevance thinking | 3 |

## Part C: Delivery Method Selection and Phishing Simulation Plan (25 points)

### Task Description

For the organization-wide awareness program (all 3,200 employees), recommend a mix of at least three delivery methods. For each method, explain why it is appropriate for this healthcare workforce, identify which workforce segment(s) it primarily serves, and identify any limitations that the program manager must manage.

Then design a phishing simulation campaign plan for the first year. Your plan must specify:

- Campaign frequency (how many simulations per year)
- Template variety strategy (what types of lures will be used and why — reference current healthcare-relevant threats)
- How you will handle employees who fail a simulation (click or submit credentials)
- The three metrics you will track and how you will report them
- One safeguard against the simulation being used punitively or harmfully

Present the delivery method recommendation as a table and the phishing simulation plan as a structured section with numbered sub-components.

### Part C Assessment Criteria

| Criteria | Points |
|---|---|
| Three or more delivery methods selected with healthcare-appropriate justification | 10 |
| Phishing simulation plan addresses all five required components | 12 |
| Anti-punitive safeguard is specific and realistic | 3 |

## Part D: Measurement Plan (25 points)

### Task Description

Design a measurement plan for the Cascadia security awareness program using the Kirkpatrick four-level model. For each Kirkpatrick level, identify at least one specific metric, describe how it will be collected, and state the target or goal for that metric.

Then build a sample 12-month measurement calendar that shows when each major measurement activity occurs (baseline, quarterly, mid-year, annual assessment).

Finally, write a 4–6 sentence executive summary of the measurement plan that you would present to the Chief Medical Officer at Cascadia. The CMO is not a technical person — frame the measurement plan in terms of risk reduction and patient data protection, not technical security metrics.

### Part D Assessment Criteria

| Criteria | Points |
|---|---|
| All four Kirkpatrick levels addressed with specific metrics and collection methods | 12 |
| 12-month measurement calendar is complete and realistic | 7 |
| Executive summary is written for a non-technical audience in business and patient safety terms | 6 |
