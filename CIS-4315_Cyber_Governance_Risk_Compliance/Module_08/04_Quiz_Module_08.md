## Question 1

A security manager wants to reduce phishing susceptibility across all 5,000 employees of a manufacturing company. Which program component is MOST appropriate for achieving this goal?

- A) Security training, because it develops the specific technical skills employees need to analyze email headers
- B) Security education, because employees need deep conceptual knowledge of how phishing attacks work
- C) Security awareness, because the goal is behavioral change across the entire workforce, not role-specific skill development
- D) Security certification, because employees who hold credentials are more accountable for their behavior

**Correct Answer:** C

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. Security training is role-specific and builds defined skills. Analyzing email headers is a technical skill relevant to IT staff, not a behavioral change needed by all 5,000 employees. The scenario describes an organization-wide behavioral goal.
- B — Incorrect. Security education develops deep theoretical and conceptual foundations through formal programs. Teaching all 5,000 manufacturing employees the conceptual mechanics of phishing is neither necessary nor practical for the behavioral goal described.
- C — Correct. Security awareness programs target the full workforce and aim to change daily behaviors — recognizing phishing lures and reporting them — without requiring role-specific skill development. This is precisely the scope and goal described in the scenario.
- D — Incorrect. Security certification is an education-level investment for security professionals. It does not describe a program component for a general workforce population and would not be practical or relevant for manufacturing employees.

## Question 2

Which of the following is a well-written, measurable behavioral learning objective for a security awareness module on phishing?

- A) Employees will understand the threat of phishing and the importance of email security
- B) Employees will be aware of phishing tactics used by cybercriminals
- C) Employees will identify the three most common phishing indicators and report suspicious emails using the company's one-click reporting button
- D) Employees will appreciate why clicking unknown links creates risk for the organization

**Correct Answer:** C

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. "Understand" is not observable or measurable. This objective describes an internal cognitive state with no behavioral test. A manager cannot assess whether an employee "understands" without observable evidence of that understanding.
- B — Incorrect. "Be aware of" is similarly unmeasurable. Awareness without a behavioral outcome produces no evidence of change and cannot be assessed. This is a common but ineffective objective structure.
- C — Correct. This objective specifies observable actions (identify three indicators, report using the reporting button) that can be tested. A quiz can verify indicator knowledge; click-through metrics can verify use of the reporting tool. This is a properly structured behavioral learning objective.
- D — Incorrect. "Appreciate why" describes an attitudinal outcome. While attitude change supports behavior change, it is not itself observable or directly measurable, and does not describe what the employee will do differently.

## Question 3

An organization's annual security awareness training has a 96 percent completion rate and post-training survey scores averaging 4.4 out of 5. However, the phishing simulation click rate has remained unchanged at 22 percent for three consecutive years. How should the security manager interpret this situation?

- A) The program is effective because completion and satisfaction rates are excellent indicators of behavior change
- B) The program is producing compliance but not behavior change — Levels 1 and 2 Kirkpatrick measurements are passing while Level 3 behavior is unchanged
- C) The phishing simulation is flawed because a 22 percent click rate is within normal industry ranges and should not be considered a failure
- D) The training content should be updated to include more information about the history of phishing attacks

**Correct Answer:** B

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. Completion rate (Level 1 adjacent) and satisfaction scores (Level 1) measure whether employees attended and liked the training, not whether they changed their behavior. The scenario provides explicit evidence that behavior has not changed — the click rate is flat.
- B — Correct. This is a textbook Kirkpatrick model scenario. The organization measures well at Levels 1 (reaction via satisfaction survey) and Level 2 (implied learning through completion), but the Level 3 behavior measure — phishing click rate — shows no improvement. The program produces compliant attendance, not behavioral change.
- C — Incorrect. Whether 22 percent is "normal" for the industry is irrelevant to the question of program effectiveness. The critical finding is that the rate has not improved over three years despite ongoing training investment. A flat metric after three years signals program failure regardless of the benchmark.
- D — Incorrect. Historical information about phishing origins does not address behavioral change. The issue is not content knowledge — employees completing a 4.4/5 training presumably acquired the knowledge. The issue is that the training design does not produce behavioral application of that knowledge.

## Question 4

A security manager is designing a phishing simulation program. After the first campaign, several employees who clicked the simulated link are publicly named in a department meeting by their manager as "security risks." What is the MOST significant problem with this outcome?

- A) Public identification violates GDPR if any identified employees are European residents
- B) The simulation did not use sufficiently realistic phishing templates to produce valid results
- C) Public shaming destroys psychological safety, reducing the likelihood that employees will report real suspicious emails in the future
- D) The manager should have been exempt from the simulation as a leadership figure

**Correct Answer:** C

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. While privacy concerns may be relevant, they are not the most significant problem identified. The core issue is behavioral: the punitive response will damage the security culture in a specific, measurable way.
- B — Incorrect. Template realism has no bearing on the management response to simulation results. Whether the templates were realistic or not, the punitive handling of results is the critical problem here.
- C — Correct. Psychological safety is the foundation of a healthy security reporting culture. When employees fear punishment for mistakes, they stop reporting — real incidents go unreported, near-misses are concealed, and the security team loses visibility. Public shaming is one of the most damaging responses to phishing simulation failure.
- D — Incorrect. Excluding managers or executives from simulations undermines the program's integrity and cultural message. Leaders should participate without exemption, which is the opposite of what this distractor suggests.

## Question 5

In the ADDIE instructional design model applied to security training, which phase ensures that the program is designed for the actual audience and their specific behavior gaps rather than generic security content?

- A) Develop — because content quality determines whether behavior change occurs
- B) Implement — because delivery scheduling affects which employees receive the training
- C) Analyze — because understanding the audience, their risks, and current behavior gaps must precede all design decisions
- D) Evaluate — because post-training measurement reveals whether the audience was correctly targeted

**Correct Answer:** C

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. Develop is the content creation phase. High-quality content built on the wrong audience analysis still misses the mark. Content quality cannot compensate for a skipped or inadequate analysis phase.
- B — Incorrect. Implement covers deployment logistics. Scheduling affects reach but does not address the audience understanding that prevents generic, irrelevant content from being developed in the first place.
- C — Correct. Analyze is the first ADDIE phase and the one that prevents the single most common awareness program failure: building generic content for an imagined average employee rather than content designed for the actual roles, risks, and behavior gaps of the real workforce.
- D — Incorrect. Evaluate assesses outcomes after the program runs. By the time evaluation data is available, the program has already been designed and delivered. Evaluation informs the next cycle — it cannot retroactively fix a design that was built without adequate audience analysis.

## Question 6

A Chief Information Security Officer reports to the board that the security awareness program is highly effective, citing a 100 percent annual training completion rate. A board member with a risk management background pushes back, asking for evidence that behavior has actually changed. The board member is applying which evaluation principle?

- A) The board member is applying Kirkpatrick Level 1 evaluation by asking about participant reaction to training
- B) The board member is applying Kirkpatrick Level 3 evaluation by requiring evidence of on-the-job behavior change rather than completion data
- C) The board member is applying the ADDIE evaluation phase by requesting a needs assessment report
- D) The board member is applying a compliance audit standard by verifying that training records match regulatory requirements

**Correct Answer:** B

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. Kirkpatrick Level 1 measures participant reaction — satisfaction and perceived value. Completion rate is not even a Level 1 measure; it is a logistics metric. The board member is asking for something substantively more meaningful than this.
- B — Correct. Kirkpatrick Level 3 measures whether participants changed their behavior on the job as a result of training. The board member correctly recognizes that completion rate (a logistics metric) provides no evidence of behavioral impact, and is asking for the Level 3 evidence that demonstrates the program's actual value.
- C — Incorrect. The ADDIE Evaluate phase is a training design concept describing ongoing program measurement. The board member is not asking about design documentation; they are applying an evaluation framework to assess program impact claims.
- D — Incorrect. Compliance audit standards verify that training occurred and was documented — essentially confirming the completion rate the CISO already cited. The board member is explicitly asking for something beyond compliance documentation.

## Question 7

A security awareness manager observes that phishing simulation click rates have declined from 28 percent to 9 percent over two years of a targeted program. Simultaneously, the number of real phishing emails reported by employees through the company's reporting tool has increased from 40 per month to 210 per month. How should the manager interpret these two trends together?

- A) The increased reporting rate indicates the phishing simulation program is failing because more employees are encountering attacks
- B) Both trends indicate program success: fewer employees are falling for phishing while more are actively reporting it, indicating a shift toward security culture
- C) The decline in click rate is the only meaningful metric; reporting volume is affected by too many uncontrolled variables to be useful
- D) The program should be discontinued now that click rates are low, to avoid training fatigue

**Correct Answer:** B

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. Increased reporting volume does not indicate program failure — it indicates a healthier security culture. More reporting means more employees are recognizing threats and taking the correct action. Conflating more reports with more attacks misunderstands the metric.
- B — Correct. Both trends are positive and reinforce each other. Declining click rates demonstrate reduced susceptibility (fewer employees falling for phishing). Rising reporting rates demonstrate active engagement (more employees recognizing and reporting suspicious activity). Together they are strong evidence of a shifting security culture.
- C — Incorrect. Dismissing reporting rate as too variable to measure is incorrect. While some volume variation exists, a five-fold increase in reporting concurrent with a sustained click rate decline is a meaningful signal that cannot be explained by uncontrolled variables alone.
- D — Incorrect. Discontinuing a successful program to avoid training fatigue would reverse the behavioral gains achieved. Security behaviors are maintained through ongoing reinforcement, not a one-time intervention. Click rates would likely climb again without continued program activity.

## Question 8

A security manager is designing role-based training for the finance department at a retail company. Which training topic is MOST relevant to this specific audience and should be prioritized in their curriculum?

- A) SQL injection prevention and secure database query design
- B) Business email compromise and wire transfer fraud verification procedures
- C) Network packet analysis and firewall rule interpretation
- D) Physical security officer patrol route planning

**Correct Answer:** B

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. SQL injection prevention is a developer training topic. Finance department employees do not write database queries and would not apply this knowledge in their work. Including it wastes training time and signals that the program is not designed for the audience.
- B — Correct. Business email compromise and wire transfer fraud are the dominant social engineering threats targeting finance personnel. BEC attacks specifically impersonate executives or vendors to redirect payments. Finance employees who understand verification procedures are the primary defense against these high-dollar fraud attempts.
- C — Incorrect. Network packet analysis and firewall interpretation are IT staff competencies. Finance personnel have no operational need for these skills and would not benefit from this training.
- D — Incorrect. Patrol route planning is a physical security officer function. It is neither relevant to the finance department's risk profile nor to any information security control they would apply.

## Question 9

An organization's CISO presents the board with the following security culture observation: "Our employees complete all required training on time, but they never voluntarily report suspicious emails and consistently push back on security policies that slow down their work." Which stage of the security culture maturity continuum does this description represent?

- A) Non-Existent — security is completely ignored across the organization
- B) Compliance-Driven — employees do the minimum required but have not internalized security values
- C) Proactive — employees are actively engaged in security decisions
- D) Optimizing — the organization continuously improves based on employee feedback

**Correct Answer:** B

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. Non-Existent culture is characterized by widespread violations and no awareness activity. This organization completes training and presumably follows some requirements — it is not absent of security culture, just immature.
- B — Correct. Compliance-Driven culture is the precise description here. Employees do what is required (training completion) to avoid consequences, but they exhibit no voluntary security behavior (no reporting), and they resist security that creates friction. This is the defining characteristic of compliance without commitment.
- C — Incorrect. Proactive culture is characterized by employees who voluntarily participate in security, report anomalies, and consider security in new processes. The scenario describes the opposite of these behaviors.
- D — Incorrect. Optimizing culture represents the highest maturity — employees advocate for security and the organization continuously improves. The scenario describes an organization that struggles to get basic voluntary compliance, far from optimizing.

## Question 10

A CISM candidate is reviewing a proposal for a new security awareness program. The proposal states: "All employees will complete a 90-minute annual security awareness course covering all security topics." Which program design principle does this proposal most clearly violate?

- A) The principle of executive sponsorship — the board has not formally approved the program
- B) The principle of role-based relevance — a single generic 90-minute course does not address the different risk profiles and learning needs of distinct workforce segments
- C) The principle of regulatory alignment — a 90-minute course does not meet HIPAA minimum training hour requirements
- D) The principle of technical depth — 90 minutes is insufficient to develop the security skills employees need

**Correct Answer:** B

**Distractor Analysis:**

- A — Incorrect. The proposal does not describe a governance process failure. Board approval is important for the program charter, not the training design itself. There is no information in the proposal suggesting executive sponsorship is absent.
- B — Correct. The most fundamental violation is the one-size-fits-all design. A 90-minute generic course treats a developer, a nurse, a finance analyst, and a facilities worker as if they have identical security risk profiles, identical learning needs, and identical technical backgrounds. This design produces generic content that is partially relevant to nobody and fully relevant to nobody.
- C — Incorrect. HIPAA does not prescribe specific minimum training hours. This distractor invents a regulatory requirement that does not exist. Regulatory requirements typically specify topics and frequency, not exact durations.
- D — Incorrect. The goal of an awareness program is behavioral change in the general workforce, not technical skill development. 90 minutes may be appropriate for an awareness module, and the insufficient depth concern misframes the problem. The design failure is relevance and segmentation, not duration.
