### Introduction
Welcome to **Module 06 - Information Security Policies and Standards**! This module deepens your understanding of risk treatment — the decisions organizations make after identifying and assessing risks. Knowing when to mitigate, avoid, accept, or transfer risk is a critical CISM Domain 2 competency.

The CISM exam presents these concepts in management scenarios: a CISO advising executives, a risk manager responding to new threats, or a security team presenting treatment options. The correct answer is always grounded in the organization's risk appetite and business priorities.

---

### 1. High-Yield Glossary
Review these essential definitions carefully. The certification exam expects you to know these concepts inside and out:

*   **Risk treatment plans**: Documented action plans that specify how an identified risk will be addressed, including the chosen treatment option (mitigate, avoid, accept, or transfer), the responsible owner, required resources, timeline, and residual risk level after treatment. Treatment plans are the output of the risk assessment process and feed into the organization's risk register.
*   **Risk acceptance limits**: The thresholds established by senior management that define which risks can be tolerated without additional treatment based on their likelihood and impact level. Risks that fall within acceptance limits are acknowledged, documented, and monitored but not actively mitigated. These limits reflect the organization's formal risk appetite.
*   **Risk avoidance**: A risk treatment strategy in which the organization eliminates the threat entirely by discontinuing or not engaging in the activity that creates the risk. Avoidance is appropriate when a risk exceeds the organization's tolerance and cannot be cost-effectively mitigated (e.g., deciding not to launch an e-commerce platform due to inability to meet PCI DSS requirements).
*   **Risk mitigation**: A risk treatment strategy in which the organization implements controls to reduce the likelihood, impact, or both of a risk event to an acceptable level. Mitigation is the most common treatment option; it does not eliminate risk but brings it within the organization's risk appetite.
*   **Risk sharing/transfer**: A risk treatment strategy that shifts some or all of the financial consequences of a risk to a third party, typically through cyber insurance, contractual indemnification clauses, or outsourcing arrangements. Transfer does not eliminate the risk; it redistributes financial exposure.

### 2. Certification Exam Tips
*   **Four Treatment Options:** Memorize the four risk treatment options and their distinguishing characteristics: Mitigate (implement controls), Avoid (stop the activity), Accept (formally acknowledge within tolerance), Transfer (shift financial consequence). The exam tests your ability to select the most appropriate option for a given scenario.
*   **Residual Risk After Treatment:** After any treatment option is applied, residual risk remains. Senior management must formally accept residual risk — this acceptance cannot be delegated to IT. The CISM exam tests this accountability concept.
*   **Risk Appetite vs. Risk Tolerance:** Risk appetite is the broad level of risk the organization is willing to pursue; risk tolerance is the acceptable variation around that appetite for specific risk categories. Know the distinction — the exam uses both terms.
*   **Study Resource:** [NIST SP 800-39: Managing Information Security Risk](https://csrc.nist.gov/publications/detail/sp/800-39/final) — Section 3.4 (Risk Response) covers the four treatment options from a NIST perspective and is freely available as a ZTC resource.

### Lab & Command Integration
In this week's hands-on lab, you will apply risk treatment concepts through the following activities:
*   **Draft a risk treatment plan**: For three risks identified in a provided scenario, document the selected treatment option, responsible owner, implementation timeline, compensating controls (if applicable), and estimated residual risk level.
*   **Evaluate treatment options for five risk scenarios**: Given five business scenarios, select the most appropriate treatment option (mitigate, avoid, accept, transfer) and justify your decision based on cost-benefit analysis and the organization's stated risk appetite.
*   **Build a risk register entry**: Using a provided template, enter three risks into a risk register format including risk description, likelihood, impact, treatment decision, owner, and current status.
