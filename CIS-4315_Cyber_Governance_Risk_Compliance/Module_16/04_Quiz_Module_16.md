### Question 1

An organization's board of directors asks the CISO to present the company's security posture. The CISO responds by showing a 500-line firewall ruleset and a list of patched CVEs. How should the board's response to this presentation be characterized?

- A) The board should be fully satisfied — detailed technical data demonstrates the security team's thoroughness.
- B) The presentation is appropriate since the board is responsible for approving all technical security configurations.
- C) The board should request a governance-level presentation showing risk exposure, security investment alignment to business objectives, and key risk indicators — not technical operational details.
- D) The CISO should distribute the firewall ruleset to all board members for independent review.

Correct Answer: C

Distractor Analysis:

- Why C is correct: CISM Domain 1 emphasizes that security managers must communicate with executives in business and risk language, not technical operations language. Board-level governance communication requires reporting on risk, strategy alignment, and program performance.
- Why A is incorrect: Technical detail overwhelms boards with information they cannot act on; governance presentations must translate security into business risk terms.
- Why B is incorrect: Boards do not approve individual firewall rules; that is an operational management function. Boards set direction and oversee risk at a strategic level.
- Why D is incorrect: Distributing firewall configurations to board members creates confidentiality risk and provides no actionable governance value.

- C) The governance structures, board-level oversight, and strategic alignment activities that direct the overall information security program.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: Domain 3 is the "build and run" domain — it covers security architecture, program design, resource management, and security metrics.
- Why A is incorrect: That describes Domain 2 — Information Risk Management.
- Why C is incorrect: That describes Domain 1 — Security Governance.
- Why D is incorrect: That describes Domain 4 — Incident Management.

### Question 3

- C) Perform a risk-based analysis identifying which security investments provide the greatest risk reduction, present the business impact of reducing each, and provide the board with a risk-informed recommendation.

Correct Answer: C

Distractor Analysis:

- Why C is correct: CISM emphasizes that security managers must frame budget discussions in risk terms, enabling the board to make explicit, informed trade-off decisions.
- Why A is incorrect: Blind acceptance of budget cuts without risk analysis fails the security manager's governance responsibility.
- Why B is incorrect: Refusing all budget discussions is not a governance option; security programs must be justified and competed for resources like any other business function.
- Why D is incorrect: Budget discussions are internal governance matters, not regulatory violations; escalating to a regulator is inappropriate and disproportionate.

### Question 4

An organization implements a comprehensive set of technical security controls but has no security policies, no defined risk appetite, and no governance oversight structures. What is the most significant risk this situation creates?

- A) The technical controls are likely to be misconfigured without vendor guidance.
- B) Without governance direction, the technical controls are not connected to business objectives, risk priorities, or accountability — creating an ineffective security program despite the technology investment.
- C) The organization will fail all regulatory compliance audits since technical controls alone satisfy all requirements.
- D) The security team will lack job satisfaction without formal policy documentation.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM's core thesis is that governance must precede and direct technology — tools deployed without governance are uncoordinated and disconnected from business risk.
- Why A is incorrect: Configuration is a management issue separate from governance; well-configured controls can still be misaligned without governance.
- Why C is incorrect: Most regulations explicitly require governance elements (policies, risk assessments, management accountability) in addition to technical controls; technology alone does not satisfy compliance.
- Why D is incorrect: Team satisfaction is not a governance risk; the strategic risk is program ineffectiveness and misalignment.

### Question 5

A CISM candidate taking a practice exam reads this scenario: "A newly hired security manager discovers that the organization has no documented incident response plan. A significant intrusion is detected two days after the manager starts. What should the manager do FIRST?" Which answer best reflects CISM principles?

- A) Immediately shut down all network connections to contain the breach.
- B) Contact law enforcement before taking any internal action.
- C) Activate available response capabilities while simultaneously beginning to document and improve the response process for future incidents.
- D) Inform the board that the organization is unprepared and request emergency budget for an incident response platform.

Correct Answer: C

Distractor Analysis:

- Why C is correct: CISM Domain 4 emphasizes using the Preparation phase to build capabilities — but when a real incident occurs, response proceeds with available resources while the post-incident review drives future improvement.
- Why A is incorrect: Immediately shutting down all connections may be a containment step but is not the first action; assessment of the situation precedes containment decisions, and wholesale shutdown may destroy evidence or disrupt business.
- Why B is incorrect: Law enforcement contact may be appropriate but is not universally the first step; initial response, assessment, and internal escalation typically precede external notification.
- Why D is incorrect: Board escalation may be required for a significant incident, but it is not the first action; technical response and management assessment come first.

### Question 6 (5 points)

An information security manager is developing the organization's risk appetite statement for presentation to the board. The board asks what distinguishes risk appetite from risk tolerance. Which answer is most accurate?

- A) Risk appetite and risk tolerance are interchangeable terms describing the same concept in CISM.
- B) Risk appetite is the amount of risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of its objectives; risk tolerance is the acceptable variance around risk appetite thresholds before escalation is required.
- C) Risk appetite applies only to financial risk; risk tolerance applies to operational and technology risk.
- D) Risk tolerance is set by the board; risk appetite is set by the CISO and applies only to the security program.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM Domain 2 defines risk appetite as the broad level of risk an organization is willing to accept overall, while risk tolerance defines the specific boundaries of acceptable variation around that level. Risk appetite is strategic; risk tolerance is the operational implementation of that appetite.
- Why A is incorrect: The terms are distinct. Using them interchangeably leads to imprecise governance communication and prevents the board from expressing nuanced preferences about which risks are acceptable and to what degree.
- Why C is incorrect: Risk appetite and risk tolerance apply to all categories of organizational risk — including information security, operational, financial, and strategic risk. There is no subject-matter restriction in the CISM framework.
- Why D is incorrect: Both risk appetite and risk tolerance are ultimately board-level governance decisions. The CISO may propose and inform them, but setting the organization's risk appetite is a board responsibility, not a security function prerogative.

### Question 7 (5 points)

A security manager is classifying information assets as part of a Domain 2 risk assessment. The organization has three proposed classification tiers: Confidential, Internal Use, and Public. A legal brief containing attorney-client communications and settlement discussions is being classified. Which tier is most appropriate, and what is the primary governance reason?

- A) Internal Use — because legal documents are for employees only.
- B) Public — because court proceedings eventually become public record.
- C) Confidential — because the document contains attorney-client privileged information and unauthorized disclosure could cause severe legal and financial harm.
- D) Internal Use — because the CISO, not Legal, determines the classification of all documents.

Correct Answer: C

Distractor Analysis:

- Why C is correct: Information classification under CISM Domain 2 is based on the sensitivity of the information and the potential business impact of unauthorized disclosure. Attorney-client privilege represents a legally protected category of information where unauthorized disclosure could waive the privilege, expose the organization to litigation risk, and cause direct financial harm. The Confidential tier is required.
- Why A is incorrect: Internal Use is insufficient for information requiring legal protection. Not all employee-accessible documents have the same sensitivity level; classification must reflect potential harm from disclosure, not just the intended audience.
- Why B is incorrect: That a document may eventually become public does not reduce its current classification requirement. Classification reflects current sensitivity and required protection — not eventual public availability.
- Why D is incorrect: Data owners — in this case Legal or the executive responsible for the matter — classify data based on their understanding of its sensitivity and business value. The CISO administers the classification program and enforces controls; they do not unilaterally determine classification for all documents.

### Question 8 (5 points)

An organization's security metrics program reports that its mean time to detect (MTTD) threats has improved from 21 days to 8 days over the past year. The CISO presents this as evidence of program improvement. A board member asks whether this metric alone demonstrates that the security program is achieving its governance objectives. What is the most accurate response?

- A) Yes — MTTD is the single most important security metric and its improvement confirms program effectiveness.
- B) MTTD improvement is a meaningful operational metric, but governance effectiveness requires a broader set of indicators including risk reduction, control coverage, policy compliance rates, and alignment to business objectives — no single metric confirms program governance effectiveness.
- C) No — MTTD is a technical metric irrelevant to governance; the board should only review financial metrics.
- D) MTTD improvement is sufficient evidence because it demonstrates that the security team is working harder.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM Domain 3 requires security managers to develop comprehensive metrics programs that align security performance to organizational objectives. MTTD measures one operational dimension — detection speed — but does not reflect control effectiveness, policy compliance, risk reduction, or business alignment. Governance requires a balanced set of key performance indicators and key risk indicators.
- Why A is incorrect: No single metric can capture program governance effectiveness. Overreliance on one metric can mask deficiencies in other dimensions — for example, fast detection of low-severity events while missing high-severity ones.
- Why C is incorrect: MTTD is relevant to governance — detection speed has direct implications for breach cost, regulatory notification timelines, and business impact. Dismissing operational metrics as irrelevant to governance misunderstands the relationship between operations and governance outcomes.
- Why D is incorrect: Metrics measure outcomes, not effort. An improved MTTD might reflect better tooling, better threat intelligence, or process improvement — or it could reflect a shift in threat types rather than genuine program improvement. Effort is not an outcome measure.

### Question 9 (5 points)

During an incident response exercise, the team lead asks which phase of the incident response lifecycle is most frequently neglected in organizations with immature programs, and why that neglect creates recurring incidents. Which answer best reflects CISM Domain 4 guidance?

- A) The Detection phase — because most organizations have mature SIEM deployments that eliminate detection gaps.
- B) The Containment phase — because containment is technically complex and requires specialized skills.
- C) The Post-Incident Review and Lessons Learned phase — because organizations close incidents without systematically converting findings into program improvements, allowing root causes to persist.
- D) The Eradication phase — because organizations always prioritize recovery speed over thorough threat removal.

Correct Answer: C

Distractor Analysis:

- Why C is correct: CISM Domain 4 consistently identifies post-incident review as the phase most commonly skipped or performed superficially. Without a structured lessons-learned process that produces documented findings, assigned owners, and tracked remediation, the root causes of incidents remain unaddressed. Organizations that skip post-incident review repeatedly encounter the same vulnerability classes, process failures, and detection gaps.
- Why A is incorrect: Detection gaps remain significant in most organizations — MTTD measured in days or weeks is common. Detection is not a neglected phase; it receives substantial investment precisely because organizations recognize detection failures.
- Why B is incorrect: Containment receives significant focus during active incidents because its failure directly extends the blast radius of an event. While containment can be technically challenging, it is not the most neglected phase.
- Why D is incorrect: Eradication thoroughness is a well-recognized concern, particularly for advanced persistent threats, but it is not the most broadly neglected phase. The post-incident review is specifically identified in CISM materials as the phase where organizational learning most frequently fails.

### Question 10 (5 points)

A security manager at a financial services firm receives a request from the CFO to implement a new automated payment processing system within 30 days to capture a market opportunity. The security manager's preliminary review indicates that the system would require a significant change to the network architecture and would introduce three unassessed risks. Which CISM-aligned response best balances business agility with security governance?

- A) Refuse the implementation until a full security assessment is completed, regardless of business impact.
- B) Approve the implementation immediately to demonstrate that security supports business objectives.
- C) Conduct an expedited risk assessment covering the three identified risks, present quantified risk findings and proposed mitigating controls to senior management, and recommend a go/no-go decision with documented risk acceptance if management chooses to proceed.
- D) Delegate the risk decision entirely to the CFO since the request originated from the business.

Correct Answer: C

Distractor Analysis:

- Why C is correct: CISM Domain 2 requires risk-informed decision-making — not risk elimination. An expedited risk assessment surfaces the material risks, and presenting them with proposed mitigations enables management to make an informed decision. If they choose to accept residual risk, that decision is documented and owned at the appropriate authority level. This approach supports business agility without abandoning governance.
- Why A is incorrect: Refusing implementation without providing a risk-informed alternative positions security as a business obstacle rather than a business enabler. CISM emphasizes that security managers must support organizational objectives while managing risk — not block progress.
- Why B is incorrect: Approving implementation without assessing identified risks abandons the security manager's core responsibility. Business support does not mean unconditional approval; it means enabling informed decisions.
- Why D is incorrect: Delegating the risk decision to the CFO without providing risk analysis removes the security manager's governance contribution entirely. The CFO cannot make an informed risk decision without the security manager's assessment. Delegation without analysis is an abdication of the security management role.

### Question 11 (5 points)

An organization has an information security policy, a data classification standard, an access control procedure, and a password standard. A new regulation requires organizations to document their approach to third-party vendor security risk management. In which policy hierarchy tier does the new requirement most appropriately belong, and why?

- A) The information security policy (Tier 1) — because all regulatory requirements must be reflected in the top-level policy.
- B) A topic-specific policy on vendor risk management (Tier 2) — because third-party risk management is a subject-matter area requiring dedicated policy coverage with defined requirements, roles, and governance structure.
- C) A procedure (Tier 4) — because regulatory requirements are always operational in nature.
- D) A standard (Tier 3) — because vendor requirements are configuration specifications.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM Domain 1 and the NIST policy hierarchy place topic-specific policies at Tier 2 for subject areas that require dedicated governance coverage — including vendor risk management. A vendor risk management policy establishes the program's scope, requirements, roles, and oversight mechanisms. Procedures (Tier 4) would then specify how vendor assessments are conducted; standards (Tier 3) would specify minimum security requirements for vendors.
- Why A is incorrect: The Tier 1 information security policy provides high-level direction for the entire security program. It would reference vendor risk management as a required program area, but the substance of vendor risk requirements belongs in a topic-specific policy, not in the top-level document.
- Why C is incorrect: Regulatory requirements that establish a governance program are policy-level requirements. Procedures implement policies — they do not establish governance frameworks. Placing a program requirement at the procedure tier inverts the hierarchy.
- Why D is incorrect: Standards specify measurable configuration or control requirements. Vendor risk management requirements include governance structures, roles, and program elements that go beyond configuration specifications and belong at the policy tier.

### Question 12 (5 points)

A security manager is building the annual security program business case for budget approval. The CFO asks for evidence that last year's security investments produced measurable return. Which type of metric best supports the business case from a CISM governance perspective?

- A) The number of security patches applied to production systems during the year.
- B) The reduction in mean time to detect and mean time to respond compared to the prior year, correlated with estimated avoided breach costs based on industry loss data.
- C) The number of security awareness training completions recorded in the LMS.
- D) The total number of firewall rule changes reviewed and approved by the security team.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: A business case requires demonstrating value in terms the CFO can evaluate — financial impact. Correlating operational security improvements (MTTD/MTTR reduction) with avoided breach cost estimates translates security performance into business value language. Industry datasets such as IBM Cost of a Data Breach Report provide the cost-per-day figures needed to quantify avoided losses.
- Why A is incorrect: Patch count is an activity metric — it records effort, not outcome. The CFO cannot determine from patch counts whether security risk was reduced or whether the patching effort was applied to the highest-risk systems.
- Why C is incorrect: Training completion counts measure program participation, not behavior change or risk reduction. High completion rates with no corresponding reduction in phishing susceptibility or policy violations do not demonstrate security program value.
- Why D is incorrect: Firewall rule review counts are operational workload metrics. They record that the security team was busy reviewing changes, not that the organization became more secure as a result.

### Question 13 (5 points)

During a CISM exam scenario, a candidate reads: "A security manager discovers that a business unit has been operating a shadow IT system containing customer personal data for 18 months without security review, IT approval, or data classification." Which CISM Domain is most directly implicated by this finding, and what is the security manager's primary obligation?

- A) Domain 4 — Incident Management: the shadow IT system should be treated as a security incident and immediately shut down.
- B) Domain 3 — Information Security Program Development and Management: the finding reveals a gap in the security program's asset discovery, classification, and risk assessment processes that must be addressed systematically.
- C) Domain 1 — Security Governance: the security manager should report the business unit to the board for disciplinary action.
- D) Domain 2 — Information Risk Management: the security manager should immediately perform a full penetration test of the system.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: Shadow IT is primarily a Domain 3 program gap — the security program lacks processes to discover, classify, and assess assets that are not formally registered. The security manager's obligation is to assess the system's risk, bring it into the security program's scope, and close the process gap that allowed 18 months of unmanaged risk. Systematic program improvement prevents recurrence.
- Why A is incorrect: Immediate shutdown without assessment may destroy business value and is disproportionate before the risk is understood. While an investigation is appropriate, treating every shadow IT discovery as a shutdown-first incident ignores the business context and CISM's emphasis on risk-informed decisions.
- Why C is incorrect: Escalation may be appropriate, but reporting to the board for disciplinary action is not the security manager's primary obligation. The primary obligation is risk management — assessing and addressing the risk — not enforcement. Governance structures handle policy violations through appropriate channels.
- Why D is incorrect: Penetration testing is one risk assessment technique, but it is not the automatic first response to a shadow IT discovery. A risk assessment covering asset inventory, data classification, access controls, and integration risks should precede any technical testing decision.

### Question 14 (5 points)

An organization's security awareness training program has been in place for three years and consists of annual online modules covering phishing, password hygiene, and data handling. The CISO proposes measuring program effectiveness. Which measurement approach best aligns with CISM Domain 3 program management principles?

- A) Count the number of employees who completed the training each year and report completion percentage to the board.
- B) Measure behavior change through simulated phishing campaign click rates, policy compliance audit results, and reported security incident rates attributable to human error — tracked before and after training interventions.
- C) Increase training module length from 30 minutes to 90 minutes and measure whether employees report the modules as more valuable.
- D) Require employees to retake the training until they score 100% on the assessment and report the average retake count.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM Domain 3 requires security managers to measure program outcomes, not just activities. Behavior change — evidenced by phishing simulation performance, policy compliance, and human-error incident rates — directly measures whether training is reducing the risk it was designed to address. Before-and-after comparisons isolate the training's contribution to risk reduction.
- Why A is incorrect: Completion percentage is an activity metric. A program with 100% completion that has no effect on employee behavior has not demonstrated value. Completion measures participation, not risk reduction.
- Why C is incorrect: Module length and employee satisfaction ratings measure content delivery preferences, not security behavior change. A longer module that employees find valuable may still produce no improvement in phishing susceptibility or policy compliance.
- Why D is incorrect: Assessment retake counts measure quiz performance under conditions that may not reflect real behavior. Employees who score 100% on a multiple-choice quiz about phishing may still click phishing links in practice. Assessment scores are a weak proxy for actual behavior change.

### Question 15 (5 points)

A security manager is reviewing the organization's risk register and notes that a critical application vulnerability has been in "risk accepted" status for 14 months. The original risk acceptance was approved for 90 days pending a vendor patch. The vendor has since released the patch, but the application team has not applied it. What is the security manager's most appropriate action?

- A) Leave the risk acceptance in place since it was formally approved by management.
- B) Escalate to the risk acceptance authority that the approval conditions have changed — the vendor patch is available — and that the continued acceptance requires re-evaluation and either re-approval with updated justification or immediate remediation.
- C) Apply the patch without notifying the application team to close the risk.
- D) Remove the vulnerability from the risk register since it has been accepted for over a year.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: Risk acceptances are time-bound and condition-dependent governance decisions. When the conditions under which a risk was accepted materially change — in this case, the vendor patch becoming available removes the primary justification for acceptance — the acceptance must be re-evaluated. CISM Domain 2 requires risk managers to maintain the currency of risk register entries and escalate when accepted risks require reassessment.
- Why A is incorrect: A risk acceptance approved under conditions that no longer exist is no longer a valid governance decision. Leaving a stale acceptance in place without review creates accountability gaps and potentially exposes the organization to risks that management did not knowingly accept.
- Why C is incorrect: Applying a patch to a production application without coordinating with the application team ignores change management processes and could disrupt business operations. The security manager does not have unilateral authority to modify production systems.
- Why D is incorrect: Removing a risk from the register because it is inconvenient or stale is a governance failure. The risk register must accurately reflect the organization's risk posture. Deletions require documented justification — remediation, transfer, or formal re-acceptance — not simply the passage of time.

### Question 16 (5 points)

An organization experiences a significant ransomware incident and the response team successfully contains it within six hours. The CISO declares the incident closed after eradication and recovery are confirmed. Two weeks later, the same ransomware variant is detected on a different segment of the network that was not included in the initial remediation scope. Which gap in the incident management process does this scenario most directly illustrate?

- A) The containment phase was too aggressive and disrupted normal operations.
- B) The eradication phase was incomplete — the scope of attacker presence was not fully validated before recovery began, and post-incident review did not identify the missed segment as a residual risk.
- C) The detection tools failed and should be replaced with a newer platform.
- D) The incident response team was not large enough to handle a network-wide threat.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM Domain 4 defines eradication as the systematic removal of all attacker footholds from the environment. Declaring eradication complete without validating scope across all network segments is an eradication phase failure. Additionally, the post-incident review should have identified whether the scope assessment was thorough. Both phases contributed to the recurrence.
- Why A is incorrect: Containment aggressiveness is not indicated as a problem in the scenario. The issue is not that containment was too aggressive — it is that eradication was incomplete because the scope was not fully assessed.
- Why C is incorrect: The detection tools identified the ransomware variant in the second segment — they functioned. The failure was in the eradication process and scope validation, not in detection technology.
- Why D is incorrect: Team size may be a contributing factor, but the root cause described in the scenario is a process failure — incomplete scope validation during eradication — not a resource capacity issue. Adding more people to a flawed eradication process would not guarantee complete remediation.

### Question 17 (5 points)

A security manager is presenting the annual security program performance report to the board. The report shows that 94% of critical vulnerabilities were remediated within the 30-day SLA, 98% of employees completed security awareness training, and the organization passed its PCI-DSS annual assessment with zero exceptions. A board member asks whether these metrics indicate that the organization's residual risk is within its defined risk appetite. Which response is most accurate?

- A) Yes — passing PCI-DSS with zero exceptions confirms that residual risk is within the risk appetite.

A separate risk posture assessment comparing identified risks against appetite thresholds is required.
- Why A is incorrect: PCI-DSS compliance certifies that specific controls were in place during the assessment period. It does not assess all organizational risk categories, threat landscape changes, or whether risk exposure aligns with the board's appetite across all business dimensions.
- Why C is incorrect: Vulnerability remediation rate is an important operational metric but measures only one risk vector. Residual risk encompasses all identified risks across all domains — not just unpatched vulnerabilities.
- Why D is incorrect: Compliance certifications address regulatory obligations — they are not equivalent to risk appetite management. An organization can maintain all compliance certifications while carrying residual risk that exceeds its board-defined appetite in areas not covered by those certifications.

### Question 19 (5 points)

An organization's information security program has been operating for three years. The CISO proposes conducting a comprehensive security program maturity assessment using the CMMI (Capability Maturity Model Integration) framework. At the conclusion of the assessment, the program is rated at Level 2 — Managed. The board asks what Level 2 means and what Level 3 would require. Which explanation is most accurate?

- A) Level 2 means the program has no documented policies; Level 3 requires implementing a SIEM.

- C) Level 2 means the organization has passed at least one external compliance audit; Level 3 requires passing audits for three different frameworks simultaneously.
- D) Level 2 and Level 3 are equivalent in practice; the difference is administrative labeling only.

Correct Answer: B

- Why B is correct: CMMI Level 2 (Managed) indicates that processes are planned, performed, monitored, and controlled at the individual project or team level, but they are not yet standardized organization-wide. Each team may manage their security activities differently. Level 3 (Defined) requires that the organization establish standard processes used consistently across all projects and teams, with an organizational process asset library supporting continuous improvement.
- Why A is incorrect: Level 2 does not mean the program has no documented policies — it means processes exist but are managed at the local rather than organizational level. SIEM implementation is a specific technical control, not a CMMI maturity requirement.
- Why C is incorrect: CMMI maturity levels are not defined by audit outcomes. Compliance certifications and maturity levels measure different dimensions — compliance measures adherence to external requirements at a point in time; maturity measures the organizational capability of processes over time.
- Why D is incorrect: The difference between Level 2 and Level 3 is material and directly affects program repeatability, consistency, and scalability. An organization at Level 2 with inconsistent practices across business units cannot reliably ensure that security requirements are met uniformly — a significant governance gap.

### Question 20 (5 points)

A CISM exam scenario presents: "The security manager has just completed an organization-wide risk assessment. The assessment identified 47 risks. Senior management asks the security manager to eliminate all 47 risks within the next fiscal year." Which response best reflects CISM risk management principles?

- A) Accept the challenge and plan to remediate all 47 risks within the fiscal year regardless of cost or feasibility.
- B) Explain that risk elimination is not the goal of risk management — the goal is to bring risk within the organization's risk appetite through treatment strategies including mitigation, transfer, acceptance, and avoidance — and present a prioritized treatment plan for the highest-residual-risk items.
- C) Inform management that 47 risks is too many to manage and recommend reducing the scope of the next risk assessment to identify fewer risks.
- D) Transfer all 47 risks to the organization's cyber insurance provider and close the risk register.

Correct Answer: B

Distractor Analysis:

- Why B is correct: CISM Domain 2 establishes that the objective of risk management is not to eliminate all risk but to bring risk within acceptable levels defined by the organization's risk appetite. The four risk treatment options — mitigate, transfer, accept, avoid — provide a framework for making deliberate, business-aligned decisions about each identified risk. A prioritized treatment plan focused on the highest-residual-risk items is the governance-appropriate response.
- Why A is incorrect: Committing to eliminate all 47 risks regardless of cost creates an unrealistic expectation and misunderstands the nature of risk management. Some risks are accepted by design; others cannot be fully eliminated at any cost. A security manager who promises to eliminate all risk has either misrepresented what is possible or plans to conceal risks that cannot be addressed.
- Why C is incorrect: Reducing assessment scope to identify fewer risks does not reduce actual risk — it only reduces visibility. A risk assessment's value is its completeness.
